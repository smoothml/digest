## High-Level Summary
The Westminster Hall debate considered e-petition 767687, which called for a new UK-wide briefing on climate and nature risks, introduced by the Prime Minister, delivered by independent experts and broadcast on television, radio and online platforms. Members discussed the effects of climate and nature risks on health, food security, infrastructure, national security, economic resilience and communities, while differing over whether a single broadcast would be sufficient and how best to reach audiences without causing fear or disengagement. The Minister for Climate Transition, Katie White, said that she and DEFRA counterparts would meet the national emergency briefing team the following week and that she would explore further opportunities for public engagement. The question was agreed to, the House resolved that it had considered the petition, and the sitting was adjourned; no division is recorded in the transcript.

## Detailed Summary
### Petition for a UK-wide climate and nature briefing
Roz Savage moved the motion on e-petition 767687 and said that more than 138,000 people had signed it. She clarified that the petition sought a new Government-organised briefing, rather than a screening of the existing film, introduced by the Prime Minister, delivered by independent scientists and broadcast on television, radio and online platforms: “The petition calls for a national briefing introduced by the Prime Minister and delivered by independent scientists”. Several Members supported the principle of a national briefing, often comparing it with the Covid-19 briefings. Caroline Voaden said that such briefings could give people “agency to make decisions about their lives”.

Alex Mayer supported wider access to reliable information but opposed compelling broadcasters to show a briefing, warning that a future Government might declare an emergency on an issue with which people disagreed. Other Members argued that the initiative should reach people beyond those already attending local screenings and should involve independent experts, and some suggested including devolved leaders or mayors. No decision was taken on the proposed format during the debate; the final question on the petition was agreed to at the conclusion.

### Climate and nature risks: impacts on security, food and communities
Members described current and potential effects of climate and nature risks, including heat-related deaths, flooding, drought, wildfires, coastal erosion, pressure on food production and risks to infrastructure and emergency services. Roz Savage argued that the public needed information to support preparedness rather than panic: “This is an argument not for panic but for better preparedness, which begins with knowledge and understanding”. Members also referred to local examples from their constituencies, including flooding, wildfires, overheating and disruption to farming and water supplies.

Several speakers connected these risks with national security and economic resilience. James MacCleary said that food shortages, population displacement and competition for resources could contribute to conflict and instability, and asked how the Government’s nature security assessment was influencing policy. He described climate resilience and nature loss as “a national security issue that should, as such, be integrated into our security strategy”. Shockat Adam called for a resilience plan for the public estate and statutory adaptation targets covering areas including flood protection and heat-safe homes: “we need to put adaptation on a statutory footing”. Aphra Brandreth accepted that climate change was happening, but argued that mitigation had to be affordable for families and workable for British businesses, alongside adaptation and nature restoration: “The way we cut emissions has to be affordable for families and workable for British businesses”. No specific adaptation or resilience measure was agreed during the debate.

### Public communication, hope and practical action
Members differed over how the risks should be communicated. Supporters of a national briefing argued that local screenings often reached people who were already concerned and that a national initiative could provide a wider shared basis for action. They also said that information should be accompanied by practical guidance, community support and an explanation of available solutions. Charlotte Cane said that a Government briefing could provide “consistent information from independent experts”. Alex Mayer similarly argued: “We need to give a message of hope, not just fear”.

Other Members cautioned that a single televised event would not be sufficient. Martin Rhodes called for “something broader than a one-off event”, using schools, workplaces, libraries, social media, local journalism and community organisations. Aphra Brandreth said that some people at a screening had used words including “anxious”, “scared” and “depressed” to describe their reactions, and argued that public communication should give people confidence that they and their communities could act. The debate also covered misinformation, youth participation, community resilience, support for farmers, home retrofitting, clean energy and adaptation. No specific national communications programme was announced in the transcript.

### Ministerial response, commitments and outcome
The Minister for Climate Transition, Katie White, praised the grassroots campaign and said that the Government should communicate clearly about climate and nature risks. She said that she had met the national emergency briefing leadership in June and that, the following week, she and her DEFRA counterparts would meet the team again “to discuss the issues raised today and explore opportunities to work together”. She also said: “I will work with the climate and nature taskforce to explore further opportunities for public engagement”, and undertook to keep Members updated on progress.

White described Government action on nature, clean energy, climate security and electrification, including the climate and nature security taskforce and the appointment of a UK climate security envoy. She did not commit to the precise format requested by the petition—namely a live, prime-time briefing introduced by the Prime Minister. Roz Savage welcomed the planned meeting with the briefing team. The question was agreed to, followed by the resolution that the House had considered e-petition 767687: “Question put and agreed to”. The sitting was then adjourned.
