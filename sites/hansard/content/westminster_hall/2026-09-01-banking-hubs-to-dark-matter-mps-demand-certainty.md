## High-Level Summary
Westminster Hall debates covered five themes: access to face‑to‑face banking in rural areas; funding pressures and priorities for UK science facilities, notably dark‑matter research at Boulby; the future of UK financial services; local government reorganisation in Cambridgeshire; and aluminium recycling by local authorities. MPs urged stronger protections for in‑person banking and broader criteria for banking hubs, with Ministers signalling an imminent review and ongoing hub roll‑out. Science speakers warned of proposed savings across physics facilities; Ministers cited record planned R&D, and gave assurances on Boulby and continuing engagement with the XLZD project. Financial services discussions ranged from mobilising domestic capital to AI and tokenisation, with the Government setting out timelines for a digital gilt (DIGIT) and T+1 settlement. Further debates sought clarity and timelines on Cambridgeshire’s reorganisation and highlighted lost value and exports in aluminium recycling, alongside Government waste‑system reforms and targets.

## Detailed Summary
### Face‑to‑Face Banking: Rural Areas
Caroline Voaden said rural banking services have been “decimated” and urged protection of in‑person services: “Banking services in rural areas have been decimated.”. She argued many still rely on branches, noting, “81% of UK adults say that they have accessed in‑person banking services”, and criticised narrowed hub criteria: “the Financial Conduct Authority substantially tightened the rules, placing the focus solely on access to cash.”. Members highlighted poor connectivity, transport barriers and small‑business cash needs; one observed a need to “legislate for access to face‑to‑face banking” following access‑to‑cash laws.

Replying, the Economic Secretary, Lucy Rigby, underlined ongoing demand for in‑person banking: “Around 3.3 million current account holders did not bank online or through a mobile app”. She confirmed the Lloyd review “will be published next month”, and that “More than 280 banking hubs have been announced, and 240 are already open.”. She added, “Decisions about the location of banking hubs are made independently by Link”. Outcome: “Motion lapsed (Standing Order No. 10(6)).”.

### Science Facilities and Laboratories
Alison Hume described Boulby Underground Laboratory as vital to dark‑matter research: “Boulby underground laboratory… is the UK’s only deep underground science facility.”. She warned that the STFC prioritisation review proposed “£162 million in cumulative savings by 2030.”, and said Jodrell Bank “faces closure as early as 2028.”. MPs sought equitable treatment, certainty for facilities and staff, and recognition of regional impacts.

Ministers highlighted planned investment and specific reassurances: “a record £86 billion to be invested between 2026‑27 and 2029‑30.”. They said “Boulby will remain an internationally significant research facility.”, confirmed support to “sustain the UK’s continued engagement with the… XLZD dark matter experiment… beyond… 2027.”, and stated “no staff exits are expected this financial year”. The Minister agreed to meet the Boulby team. Outcome: “Question put and agreed to.”.

### UK Financial Services
Callum Anderson proposed measures to channel more domestic capital and support innovation, including asking that “default pension funds [adopt] a UK‑weighted approach to listed equity”, publishing “a clear road map for DLT‑enabled capital markets”, and noting that “AI can transform productivity, investment management, fraud detection and customer service”. Jeremy Hunt urged tax stability—“do no harm with additional taxes in October’s Budget”—and a proportionate regime, warning, “Compliance now costs the sector more than £39 billion a year”. Other contributions covered ring‑fencing, pensions consolidation and global competitiveness.

Minister Lucy Rigby reaffirmed the 10‑year strategy and recent delivery: “We have delivered the Pension Schemes Act 2026”. On capital markets, she cited PISCES, which “saw Wayve and Moneybox sell more than £100 million of shares”, and confirmed timetables: “issuing DIGIT… no later than quarter 1 2027” and “moving to a T+1 settlement cycle from quarter 4 2027”. She also referenced inclusion measures and transition finance. Outcome: “Resolved, That this House has considered the future of UK financial services.”.

### Local Government Reorganisation: Cambridgeshire
Ben Obese‑Jecty opposed splitting Huntingdonshire (“Option D”) and advocated a single unitary: “I back a Huntingdonshire unitary authority”, arguing that “Option D rips Huntingdonshire in half”. He raised concerns about financial viability and alleged pre‑briefings; another Member disputed those claims. He sought clarity on process, criteria and timing.

Local Government Minister Jim McMahon said the earlier delay was because “more time was needed to consider which option, if any, to implement”, and set out a timetable to “make decisions… on or before October this year,” enabling elections in May 2027 and go‑live in April 2028. He announced transition funding: “£63 million will be made available to support the transition”, with per‑unitary support for leadership in key services. He noted disaggregation complexities would be considered but did not prejudge options. Outcome: “Question put and agreed to.”.

### Local Authorities: Aluminium Recycling
Tessa Munt called aluminium “the most valuable material in every household recycling bin”, citing FOI‑based disparities and opacity—e.g., “Cambridgeshire county council made about £1.6 million” while others made little or nothing—and warned “about £1 billion‑worth of scrap aluminium is sold abroad each year”. With domestic capacity expanding, she argued that by next year “there should be no need whatsoever… to send… aluminium cans abroad.”. MPs pressed for transparency, UK processing, and alignment with critical‑minerals and circular‑economy aims.

Environment Minister Emma Hardy backed the deposit return scheme, which “will play a vital role in increasing the capture of drinks containers”, referred to a recent “consultation on reforming the packaging waste recycling note system”, and noted “we publish a monthly market overview” to aid councils. She set targets—“65% of municipal waste… and 67% of aluminium packaging”—and highlighted investment: Novelis has “committed… £66.5 million to upgrading” its plant; reforms are expected to underpin “£10 billion… and… 25,000 jobs.”. Outcome: “Resolved, That this House has considered aluminium recycling and local authorities.”.
