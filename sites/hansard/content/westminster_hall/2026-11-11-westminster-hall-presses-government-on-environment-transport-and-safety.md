## High-Level Summary
Westminster Hall considered motions on hunting trophy imports, the possible appointment of a Minister for Neurodiversity, Government policy on the Rosebank oilfield, and support for the West London Orbital rail project. Members supporting a ban on trophy imports pressed for immediate action, but the Government gave no firm date and said that primary legislation was required. The Rosebank debate set out opposing arguments on climate change, energy security, jobs and taxation; the Minister said that the regulatory process was continuing and declined to prejudge the decision. The final debate concerned violence, intimidation and other risks affecting emergency workers; the Minister described existing legal and policing powers, while the motion lapsed when the sitting ended.

## Detailed Summary
### Safety of Emergency Workers
Amanda Martin opened a debate on violence, intimidation and wider safety risks affecting police officers, ambulance staff, firefighters, NHS workers, coastguard personnel and rescue volunteers. She described assaults and intimidation connected with protests in Portsmouth and Gosport, including damage to emergency vehicles, abuse directed at the Royal National Lifeboat Institution (RNLI), doxxing and threats. Members also discussed reported violence against ambulance workers, the effect on recruitment and morale, mental-health support, face coverings, intelligence sharing, online harms and firefighters’ exposure to carcinogens. Martin said that the message from emergency-services representatives was: “This is not what we signed up for”.

Minister Sarah Jones distinguished peaceful protest from criminal conduct and referred to the Assaults on Emergency Workers (Offences) Act 2018, the maximum two-year sentence for common assault or battery against an emergency worker, and police powers concerning conditions on protests, dispersal and face coverings. She said Hampshire and Isle of Wight Constabulary had investigated the Portsmouth disorder, and referred to the Government’s consideration of Lord Macdonald’s review. On online intimidation, she said that the Online Safety Act 2023 applied “up to a point” and that the Government were considering whether the criminal law adequately addressed the malicious publication of personal information. She also said that the Government intended to strengthen the policing covenant through legislation to support police officers’ mental health. The motion lapsed when the sitting ended, without a question being put: “Motion lapsed, and sitting adjourned without Question put”.
