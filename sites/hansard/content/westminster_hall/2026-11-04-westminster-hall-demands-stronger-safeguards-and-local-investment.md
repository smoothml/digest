## High-Level Summary
Westminster Hall considered five issues in sequence: smart glasses and violence against women and girls; a national lifelong learning strategy; heating oil prices in rural areas; illegal off-road bikes in the North East; and potholes and road maintenance in Suffolk. Members generally supported stronger safeguards, better access to learning, additional protection for off-grid households, enhanced policing powers and sustained road investment, while differing over the scope and timing of regulation and funding. Ministers outlined work on smart-glasses restrictions and safety by design, lifelong-learning provision, heating-oil regulation, policing responses and highways funding. Each motion was agreed to; a separate Division briefly suspended the heating-oil debate, and the sitting adjourned after the Suffolk debate.

## Detailed Summary
### Potholes and road maintenance in Suffolk
Patrick Spencer (Central Suffolk and North Ipswich) raised the deterioration of Suffolk’s roads, attributing it to extreme weather, heavy construction traffic, the de-trunking of the A12 and historic funding arrangements. Members discussed repeated failed repairs, rural road safety, road-maintenance technology such as the JCB Pothole Pro and Dragon Patcher, contractor accountability, disruption from roadworks, skills shortages and the management of strategically important roads. There was disagreement about the respective responsibility of central government and Suffolk County Council for the condition of the network. Spencer said he would continue discussions with the Department for Transport and write further, adding: “I feel there is more that can be done beyond simply talking about funding.”

Justin Madders, the Minister, said £7.3 billion would be provided for local highways maintenance over four years, including more than £219 million for Suffolk, with the annual amount for Suffolk expected to reach £64 million by 2029–30. He said red-rated authorities, including Suffolk, had received peer reviews and tailored practical advice, and that updated ratings would be published in early 2027. The Government would review the funding formula before the next funding cycle, require transparency reports and use incentives linked to best practice, prevention and training. The Minister confirmed that the Department did not hold data on how long individual potholes took to repair, but collected information on reporting and the response provided. The motion was agreed to, and the sitting was adjourned.
