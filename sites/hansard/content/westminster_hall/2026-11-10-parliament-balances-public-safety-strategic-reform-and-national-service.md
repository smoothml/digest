## High-Level Summary
The sitting considered antisocial behaviour, National Highways’ traffic management on the M5 in Somerset, a proposed fishing and seafood sector strategy, school-building conditions in Rushcliffe, and the 125th anniversary of the submarine service. On antisocial behaviour, Members discussed reporting confidence, policing, enforcement, prevention, accommodation, youth services and multi-agency working; the motion lapsed under Standing Order No. 10(6). The M5, fishing and Rushcliffe debates ended with questions put and agreed, with Ministers making commitments or indicating further consideration on specific matters. The submarine-service debate concluded with a resolution marking its 125th anniversary.

## Detailed Summary
### Antisocial behaviour
Ayoub Khan (Birmingham Perry Barr), who secured the debate, described antisocial behaviour as widespread and under-reported, citing street drinking, begging, threatening behaviour, fly-tipping, criminal damage, noise nuisance and vandalism. He argued that victims lacked confidence because agencies did not co-ordinate effectively, civil justice was slow and enforcement resources were insufficient. His proposals included a national agreement between agencies, a dedicated housing court, standardised and anonymous reporting, sustained funding for community safety partnerships, early intervention and protection for youth and community services. He also raised concerns about poorly managed supported exempt accommodation in Birmingham and called for the Government to implement existing regulatory powers and support the SEA pilot scheme. He said that “new powers on paper do not make our streets safer”.

Members raised local examples involving shoplifting, vehicles, e-bikes and e-scooters, town-centre disorder, emergency accommodation, drug and alcohol problems, public transport and abandoned buildings. Contributions differed in emphasis: some Members called for more visible neighbourhood policing and tougher enforcement, while others stressed youth services, addiction and homelessness support, civic investment and co-ordinated agency responses. Minister Sarah Jones said that respect orders had come into force and could include treatment conditions, and also referred to higher fines, longer dispersal periods, improved data collection, work on prolific shoplifters, e-bike enforcement and prevention. She stated that “Respect orders can be put on anybody, anywhere, not just in our town centres”. The transcript records that the motion lapsed under Standing Order No. 10(6).

### National Highways: M5 traffic management in Somerset
Ashley Fox (Bridgwater) raised repeated closures and congestion on the M5, particularly between junctions 22 and 24, where diversions place pressure on the A38 and surrounding communities. He cited increasing numbers of days on which traffic on the northbound section between junctions 23 and 24 moved at below 30 mph for at least an hour, and 24 full closures between junctions 20 and 25 from January 2025 to August 2026. He asked whether National Highways could improve the planning of scheduled closures, consider variable speed limits, reduce disruption following accidents and examine strategically located crossover points to enable controlled contraflows. He described the effect on residents, including a constituent whose disabled son was delayed returning from school: “My son returned home over 2 hours late tonight.”

Justin Madders, responding for the Government, acknowledged the disruption but said that motorway renewal was necessary to maintain safety and the condition of the network. He cited £8.4 billion of investment over five years, a target for National Highways to reduce delays, a requirement for 97.5% of the network to be free from traffic restrictions caused by roadworks, and a target for at least 88% of motorway incidents to be cleared within one hour. He said that the customer and delay action plan was expected before the end of March the following year and that the Office of Rail and Road would hold National Highways to account. He undertook to write to Fox about flexibility in the central-reservation barriers and to ask the Home Office to discuss whether incident-response arrangements could reduce the effects of closures. The question was put and agreed to.

### Fishing and seafood sector strategy
Daniel Zeichner (Cambridge) introduced an all-party parliamentary group action plan calling for a long-term strategy for UK fishing and the wider seafood sector within the next 12 months, as part of the sustainable ocean plan that the Government had committed to developing. He argued that fisheries policy needed to be connected with food security, health, climate, energy, marine space, skills, trade and devolved responsibilities. The proposed strategy has four pillars: sustainable management and access to space and quota; fleet modernisation, infrastructure and technology; workforce and labour; and supply chains and domestic consumption, supported by 38 recommendations. Zeichner described “the consequence of a fragmented policy landscape and the absence of a strategic view for the sector”.

Members supported the case for a strategy while differing on priorities, including fuel costs, crewing and visas, fish-stock recovery and science-based catch limits, quota allocation, spatial displacement, processing, aquaculture and the needs of coastal communities. The official Opposition spokesman supported a cross-government strategy but criticised the level of fuel support, the EU access arrangements and the handling of the fishing and coastal growth fund. Stephen Morgan, Minister, said he would meet representatives of the APPG, continue discussions across Government on workforce issues, and consider the case for a national strategy while recognising devolution and the diversity of the sector. He said: “I will consider the case for a national strategy.” He also said that the Government would seek the best possible deal for UK fishermen in negotiations on fishing opportunities for 2027. The question was put and agreed to, and the motion was resolved.

### School buildings in Rushcliffe
James Naish (Rushcliffe) welcomed more than £120 million of education investment in the constituency but identified six continuing issues: the rebuilding of St Peter’s junior school after structural deterioration; the wider risks associated with CLASP and Derwent buildings; ageing temporary classrooms at Edwalton and Lady Bay; delayed capacity-related works; the future of Ash Lea special school; and falling rolls at small rural schools. He sought a timetable and funding clarity for St Peter’s, retention of its special educational needs and disabilities provision, a strategic assessment of CLASP and Derwent buildings, a route to assess individual temporary buildings, and a national approach to the sustainability of rural schools. He said: “We should not wait for buildings to fail before acting.”

Josh MacAlister, Minister, said that overall capital investment in education had been increased to about £38 billion through to 2029-30 and outlined the school rebuilding, maintenance, renewal and retrofit programmes. St Peter’s had been confirmed as part of the school rebuilding programme, with its delivery timeline dependent on feasibility and design work; Ash Lea’s nomination was still being assessed. The Minister said that Edwalton’s temporary buildings had not been shortlisted for the first phase of the renewal and retrofit programme, but that officials had been asked to visit the school. He also said that the programme would expand to all regions of England from the following year. The question was put and agreed to.

### 125th anniversary of the submarine service
John Cooper (Dumfries and Galloway) led tributes to 125 years of the Royal Navy Submarine Service, covering its history from Holland 1 to the nuclear-powered fleet and the continuous at-sea deterrent. Members remembered submariners killed on operations, described the demands placed on crews and their families, and highlighted links with Portsmouth, Northern Ireland, Rosyth, Devonport and the wider defence-industrial base. Several Members also raised concerns about the availability of Astute-class attack submarines and the length of deterrent patrols. Cooper said: “A century and a quarter of silent service must not be torpedoed by defence spending cutbacks.”

Calvin Bailey, Minister, paid tribute to submariners, their families, engineers, dockyard workers and industrial partners. He attributed infrastructure and availability problems to earlier defence reductions and said that operational security limited what could be discussed about submarine movements and readiness. Mark Francois pressed for an explanation of why no Astute-class submarine had reportedly been at sea for months; the Minister urged responsibility in discussing submarine operations and argued that earlier cuts and delays had caused lasting damage. The debate concluded with remembrance of submariners on “eternal patrol”. The question was put and agreed to, and the motion was resolved.
