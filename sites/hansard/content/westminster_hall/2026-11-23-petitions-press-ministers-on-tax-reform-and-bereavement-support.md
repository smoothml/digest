## High-Level Summary
The sitting comprised two Petitions Committee debates: one on an independent review of council tax and Stamp Duty Land Tax, and one on reform of Bereavement Support Payment. In the first debate, Members discussed the fairness of property taxation, possible alternatives and the effects of stamp duty on housing mobility and local-government finance. The Government Minister described existing revenue, funding and enforcement arrangements, including a surcharge on properties worth more than £2 million, but did not announce an independent review. In the second debate, Members called for longer-lasting and uprated bereavement support and better notification, while the Minister said there were no current plans to extend the payment and outlined work to identify eligible claimants.

## Detailed Summary
### Independent review of Council Tax and Stamp Duty Land Tax
Jamie Stone introduced e-petition 754331, which had attracted more than 100,000 signatures and called for the Government to “commission a full, independent review of Council Tax and Stamp Duty to deliver the fundamental, evidence-based reform”. Members argued that council tax in England remains based on 1991 property valuations, producing regional differences in effective tax burdens, and that stamp duty can discourage moving, downsizing, upsizing and relocation for work. Proposals included a proportional property tax, a land value tax, revaluation, abolition of stamp duty and a wider review that could also include business rates. Some Members also highlighted transitional risks, including the possibility that valuable homes could be owned by people who are “asset rich but cash poor”, and that a review could create uncertainty in the housing market.

The Government Minister, Jim McMahon, said UK property taxes raised more than £75 billion a year and described council tax as a stable source of local-government revenue, with a 95.6% collection rate. He referred to the fair funding review, council tax support and enforcement changes, and said the new surcharge would apply to properties worth more than £2 million and affect less than 1% of properties across England. He did not announce an independent review or a timetable for one. The question was agreed, with no division recorded in the transcript: “Question put and agreed to.”

### Bereavement Support Payment
Irene Campbell introduced e-petition 752501, which had more than 108,000 signatures and called for Bereavement Support Payment to continue beyond 18 months and to be uprated annually. Members described cases in which widowed parents lost a substantial part of their household income while caring for grieving children. They argued that the financial effects of bereavement do not end after 18 months, that the three-month period for claiming the full entitlement can be missed, and that eligible people are not always told about the payment. Suggestions included extending support while a youngest child remains dependent, reviewing the 2017 reforms, restoring the payment’s value in line with inflation, commissioning research on the longer-term effects on bereaved children and using the Tell Us Once service to improve notification. Amanda Hack cited the Work and Pensions Committee’s conclusion that 18 months was “not long enough for many bereaved families to come to terms with the emotional and financial implications of their new circumstances.”

Minister Lilian Greenwood explained that Bereavement Support Payment is contributory, non-means-tested and tax-free. The standard rate provides £4,300 in total and the higher rate £9,800. She said it was intended as transitional support rather than ongoing income replacement, while acknowledging that “universal credit will not replace a late partner’s earnings in every case”. The Government had “no current plans to extend the bereavement support payment beyond its current duration”, and the rates would remain unchanged in 2027–28 following the latest review. The Minister said officials had met Caroline Booth and Widow’s Fight, that the Department was exploring the use of data to identify eligible people, and that it would consider whether more could be done through Tell Us Once. She also confirmed that people with no recourse to public funds could qualify for the standard rate. The question was agreed, with no division recorded in the transcript: “Question put and agreed to.”
