## High-Level Summary
Westminster Hall hosted five debates on community safety, public health, foreign affairs, crime and media policy. MPs pressed for sustained protection and education measures to counter antisemitism; the Minister set out new police funding, training and reporting on extremism. On World Suicide Prevention Day, cross‑party speakers called for early intervention and local support, while the Government highlighted capital investment and a forthcoming cross‑government mental health strategy. The Sudan debate centred on humanitarian access, external arms flows and sanctions; the Minister confirmed £146 million in UK aid, export‑licensing scrutiny and backing for widening a UN arms embargo. Further sessions examined vehicle theft trends and the Media Green Paper; Ministers outlined pending enforcement, system reform and a preference for voluntary prominence agreements while consulting on the future of terrestrial TV.

## Detailed Summary
### Safety of the Jewish Community
Catherine West opened a debate on rising antisemitism, recent attacks and online harms, welcoming extra protection funding but urging effectiveness online and in schools. She cited research that “eight in 10 chatbots would assist users to plan violent attacks”. MPs raised local experiences, pressures on policing, incidents in Northern Ireland and on campuses, and called for clear measures on Ofcom’s Online Safety Act duties and the Bell review on schools (not yet reported).
Home Office Minister Sarah Jones described tackling antisemitism as a continuing “journey”, setting out actions under Protecting What Matters, including an annual State of Extremism report and expanded disruption capability. She confirmed: “we announced over £250 million of additional funding for the police to tackle antisemitism over the next three years”; the Met will receive “£85.8 million” and “Antisemitism training will be rolled out across policing as part of that work”. The Bell report on schools will be published “this autumn”. The motion was agreed: “That this House has considered the safety of the Jewish community”.

### World Suicide Prevention Day
Liz Twist led a debate marking World Suicide Prevention Day, urging a shift from stigma to openness and early intervention. She noted: “The World Health Organisation calls suicide a ‘major public health challenge’”, and cited analysis that “calculates the economic cost of a single suicide as roughly £1.46 million”. MPs highlighted risks for men, construction workers, autistic people, and rural and student populations, and called for walk‑in hubs, workplace standards and education.
Minister Alison McGovern reaffirmed suicide prevention as a priority, referencing NHS reform capital—“investing £343 million in capital funding to make that change”—and that “we are developing a cross-Government mental health strategy for England”. She stressed multi‑agency opportunities outside mental health services and sport’s role in engaging men. The question was put and agreed to.

### Conflict in Sudan
Naushabah Khan described Sudan as entering its fourth year of war and “the world’s worst humanitarian crisis”, citing famine risk, widespread sexual violence and regional spillovers. She reported that Egypt hosts “about 1.5 million” Sudanese refugees, and MPs urged action to curb external arms supplies, expand the UN arms embargo beyond Darfur, disrupt illicit gold networks, support survivors and scrutinise UK export licences.
Minister Kirsty McNeill stated: “the UK has committed £146 million in support for Sudan” this year, including a “£20 million programme to support survivors of sexual and gender-based violence”. On exports, she said, “We have so far reviewed over 3,000 export licences” and “have thus far found no evidence of UK weapons or ammunition being used in Sudan”. The UK “is full-throatedly supporting efforts to extend” the UN arms embargo to the whole of Sudan, convened an urgent Human Rights Council debate on El Obeid and will push to extend the fact‑finding mission’s mandate. She undertook to update Parliament proactively. The motion was agreed: “That this House has considered the conflict in Sudan”.

### Vehicle Theft
Liz Jarvis highlighted increased vehicle theft and evolving criminal methods, citing “115,448 instances of vehicle theft … since the start of 2025” in England and Wales. She explained keyless‑entry ‘relay’ theft and other techniques, welcomed new offences in the Crime and Policing Act 2026—“Anyone caught with one now must prove they have a legitimate reason or face up to five years in prison”—and raised concerns about online tutorials, right‑to‑repair misuse, non‑attendance—“police did not even attend the scene in over 70% of car theft cases”—and limited national analytical capacity.
Minister Sarah Jones noted trends—vehicle crime “has dropped over the last two” years—reiterated the neighbourhood policing commitment, “we are committed to putting 13,000 more police in our neighbourhoods”, and said the new offences have “not commenced” yet. She undertook to raise removal of online ‘how‑to’ content with platforms—“I will definitely take it away as something to raise with the tech companies”—and outlined work with manufacturers, ports, international partners and a national reform to tackle organised networks. No resolution was required in this 30‑minute debate.

### Media Green Paper
John Whittingdale welcomed much of the Green Paper but warned that any 2034 digital terrestrial television (DTT) switch‑off should proceed only with universal broadband and protections for radio. He said, “The proposal can go ahead only if we reach the position … [of] universal access to high-speed broadband”, and, with others, highlighted concerns that radio costs could rise if TV carriage ends, “potentially increasing transmission costs by more than 50%”. He supported PSB prominence on connected TVs but opposed mandating ‘trusted’ news prominence on social media as “a very dangerous extension of Government interference in the freedom of the press”.
Media Minister Ian Murray said the aim is to adapt to online viewing while protecting UK content and news. On third‑party platforms, “our strong preference remains an industry-led voluntary agreement” on discoverability, with legislation only if needed. Noting platform dynamics, he observed “it is delivered by the algorithm”. He confirmed, “We will have a permanent charter” for the BBC, and on DTT said the key decision is timing—“Is it 2034? Is it 2044?”—adding that the transition “should be the beacon that we aim for to deliver on digital inclusion”. The consultation has closed and further proposals will follow; the motion to consider the Green Paper was agreed.
