## High-Level Summary
The sitting began with the Business, Innovation, Science and Trade Committee’s report on the UK-China economic relationship. Liam Byrne advocated continued engagement with China alongside clearer sectoral safeguards, supply-chain resilience and stronger governmental expertise, while also supporting closer cooperation with European partners. Members then debated suicide prevention training for fire and rescue services; the Government did not commit to mandatory national training, but undertook to assess provision and identify any material gaps with sector partners. The final debate considered hospitality-sector VAT, with Members proposing reductions or targeted relief and the Government citing the fiscal cost while highlighting business-rates and employment-support measures; both motions were agreed, with no division recorded in the transcript.

## Detailed Summary
### China and the UK economy: the case for risk-managed growth
Liam Byrne, Chair of the Business, Innovation, Science and Trade Committee, introduced the Committee’s 10th report, “China and the UK economy: the case for risk-managed growth”, HC 124. He argued that the UK should continue engaging with China but needed a published strategy to guide government and business. He said the Government’s stated approach of “compete, collaborate and confront” was not sufficiently operational for businesses: “it is not a strategy and not something the business community, in particular, can operationalise to any useful effect”. Byrne proposed clearer “green” sectors in which trade should be encouraged and “red” sectors in which trade or dependence would be unwise, with clearer sovereign-capability priorities.

Byrne also advocated closer work with European partners on Chinese subsidies and possible tariffs, stronger protection for technologically sensitive infrastructure, supply-chain stress-testing and diversification, action to exclude forced labour from supply chains, and the rebuilding of China expertise across Whitehall and regional government. In response to Perran Moon, he said Cornwall’s tin, lithium and tungsten resources could contribute to sovereign capabilities and supported updated geological surveys and investment in mining, processing and recycling. On electric vehicles, he said his discussions with the British automotive industry indicated greater support for tariffs on over-subsidised Chinese electric vehicles and argued for closer alignment with the European Union. He summarised the approach as “precision derisking” rather than wholesale decoupling. The sitting was suspended; no decision or vote on the report was recorded in the transcript.

### Suicide prevention training for fire and rescue services
Luke Akehurst moved that the House consider suicide prevention training for fire and rescue services and supported the Samaritans’ call for funded, mandatory training for every firefighter in England. He cited variation between services, including mandatory, optional and absent provision, and argued for a national standard covering recognition of suicidal crisis, non-judgemental conversations and safe intervention. Liz Twist and Danny Chambers raised the importance of support for firefighters themselves, while Robbie Moore and Marie Goldman supported a consistent national approach. David Simmonds argued that any training should be proportionate and should draw on effective programmes already used in other public services, noting the costs and operational effects of mandatory training.

Matthew Pennycook, the Minister responding for the Government, acknowledged the increase in suicide-related incidents attended by fire and rescue services but said the available data did not establish that current training was inadequate or that mandation was the appropriate response. The Government will seek advice from the cross-sector firefighter health and wellbeing task and finish group, work with the National Fire Chiefs Council and other partners to identify material gaps, and consider the role of standards and inspection. Pennycook said, “we are not ruling out mandatory national training in future”, but no commitment to introduce it was made. He also referred to the National Fire Chiefs Council’s toolkit, Fire Fighters Charity and Samaritans support, cross-Government work on firefighter wellbeing, and the planned college of fire and rescue in 2029. Akehurst welcomed the proposed evidence-gathering work and the possibility of future national requirements if justified by the evidence. The question was agreed and the motion was resolved; no division was recorded in the transcript.

### Hospitality-sector VAT, local economies, employment and growth
Tonia Antoniazzi opened the debate on behalf of Chris Webb, who had not arrived when the Chair called the motion. Antoniazzi highlighted the pressures facing hospitality and tourism businesses and Northern Ireland’s competition with the Republic of Ireland, where she said lower VAT rates applied to food, catering and accommodation. She proposed a four-year Northern Ireland pilot reducing VAT to 10% on food, excluding alcohol, and on accommodation, arguing that it would provide evidence about effects on investment, employment and economic activity. Other Members supported a UK-wide reduction or targeted relief, with some favouring measures focused on smaller or rural businesses so that a blanket cut did not primarily benefit large chains. The case for a targeted approach was expressed as follows: “a universal cut in VAT for hospitality would cost about £12 billion and give McDonald’s about £400 million”.

James Murray, the Minister responding and responsible for the tax system, said a VAT reduction could benefit businesses but set out estimated annual costs of about £11 billion for reducing VAT on accommodation and food and beverage services from 20% to 10%, about £5 billion for a 15% rate and about £8 billion for a 12.5% rate. He said Treasury costings included behavioural effects and that wider economic effects would generally offset only part of the direct cost: “these effects typically offset only part of the direct cost of a tax reduction”. He pointed instead to business-rates measures, including a real-terms freeze for retail, hospitality and leisure properties, a 20% reduction for pubs and higher small-business rates relief thresholds, as well as the increased employment allowance and protected employer National Insurance relief for employees under 21 and apprentices up to 25. No VAT reduction was announced. The question was agreed and the motion was resolved; no division was recorded in the transcript, after which the sitting was adjourned.
