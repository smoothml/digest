## High-Level Summary
The sitting covered Royal Assent, departmental questions, an Immigration: Earned Settlement statement, two Bills presented, Budget resolutions and debate, committee changes, and an adjournment debate on hair and beauty regulation. During questions, Ministers addressed fire and rescue co-ordination, housing supply, local government standards and finance, leasehold and renting, high streets, devolution and constituency cases. The Home Secretary proposed settlement periods linked to contribution, with transitional arrangements for certain care workers and longer periods for some refugees. The Budget debate considered cost-of-living measures, devolution, high streets, energy, housing, public services, welfare, defence and regional investment, and was adjourned to resume the following day.

## Detailed Summary
### Royal Assent
The Speaker notified the House that His Majesty had signified Royal Assent to the Sovereign Grant Act 2026: “Sovereign Grant Act 2026.” No further decision was recorded in this item.

### Housing, Communities and Local Government questions
Questions covered fire and search and rescue services, house building, Homes for Ukraine, local government standards, housing in Chesterfield, park homes, Pride in Place, accommodation for vulnerable households in Northern Ireland, houses in multiple occupation (HMOs), high streets, commonhold and leasehold reform, first-time buyers, private renters and rural local-authority funding. Angela Rayner said military aircraft would support wildfire operations and that Ministers and officials would work with the devolved Governments; she described volunteer search and rescue teams as “an indispensable part of Britain’s emergency response system.” On house building, Matthew Pennycook referred to an increase in housing starts and reaffirmed the 1.5 million-home target. He also said a package of build-out transparency measures would include a right for local authorities to refuse permission in some cases where developers repeatedly obtained consent without building: “You will not have to wait long for a package of build-out transparency measures.”

The Government said the commonhold and leasehold reform Bill would be introduced this year, that the final position on the park-home sales commission would be set out by the end of the year, and that legislation to strengthen the local-government standards framework would be introduced when parliamentary time allowed. On Ashfield district council, Jim McMahon said: “I have asked the Department to conduct an urgent best-value assessment of the local authority.” Ministers offered follow-up on several constituency matters, including a meeting on Shropshire’s exceptional financial support, continued official engagement concerning evacuated buildings in Canning Town, and a meeting on out-of-borough temporary accommodation. No divisions were recorded.

### Immigration: Earned Settlement
Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood announced a proposed settlement system linking indefinite leave to remain to contribution and specified minimum requirements, including no criminal record, a high standard of English, no unpaid debt to the British taxpayer and three years of National Insurance contributions. She set out a five-year route for higher earners and specified health and education workers, a three-year route for the highest earners and people on the highest talent schemes, and a default 10-year route for others. She stated: “Settlement should not be automatic; it should be earned.”

The proposed standard qualifying period for refugees who had not used a safe and legal route would be 20 years, with a further 10 years for those who entered by an illegal method; people who worked or studied could qualify earlier. Transitional arrangements would retain a five-year route for care workers who arrived before May 2025 and met their visa conditions, while some who had not met those conditions would be able to apply for a further five-year care visa. The shadow Home Secretary argued that the changes reversed earlier policy and alleged a £10 billion cost. The Home Secretary said that about half a million people would settle later and that the Department for Work and Pensions expected the reforms to reduce the benefits bill by billions of pounds. She said the changes would begin to be laid through the December immigration rules and would be implemented during 2027: “They will be implemented over the course of 2027.” No division was recorded.

### Animal Shelters (Licensing) Bill
Mr Richard Holden, supported by Members from across the House, presented a private Member’s Bill to provide for the licensing of animal shelters and connected purposes. The Bill was read the First time and ordered to be read a Second time on Friday 15 January 2027: “Bill read the First time; to be read a Second time on Friday 15 January 2027.”

### Submarine Recycling Strategy Bill
Graeme Downie sought leave to introduce a Bill requiring the Secretary of State to prepare and publish a strategy for recycling decommissioned submarines. He argued that continuing the work at Rosyth could preserve specialist skills, support long-term employment and maintain sovereign nuclear capability, while criticising the delay in recycling retired submarines. He said the proposed strategy would include a timetable for submarines at Rosyth and Devonport, skills and supply-chain planning, safety and waste arrangements, and regular reports to the House: “We do not need any more open-ended promises; we need a published plan.”

The motion for leave was agreed to, and the Bill was presented and read the First time. It was ordered to be read a Second time on Friday 4 December and to be printed as Bill 170.

### Budget resolutions: income tax charge and wider Budget debate
The resumed debate considered the Budget’s proposals on cost-of-living support, local and regional devolution, high streets, housing, public services, energy, welfare, defence and industrial policy. Angela Rayner defended measures including £100 energy payments for eligible households, the removal of gas standing charges, high-street support, additional funding for social and affordable housing, increased business-rates retention and expanded devolution. She said the announced agreements would mean that “over 90% of England’s population is covered by a devolution agreement”. Opposition Members argued that the Budget did not provide sufficient growth or support for employers, and criticised the tax burden, welfare policy and delayed defence decisions. Andrew Bowie described it as “a total missed opportunity.”

Members also debated rural transport and infrastructure, council housing, energy costs and the warm homes programme, manufacturing, research, justice, the Mineworkers’ Pension Scheme, and funding for Scotland and Northern Ireland. The Government defended the Budget’s fiscal position, energy measures, business-rate changes and industrial support. Dan Tomlinson said the Government remained committed to the Acorn carbon-capture project and would pursue the full transfer of the Mineworkers’ Pension Scheme guarantor’s fund. The debate was adjourned and ordered to resume the following day: “Debate to be resumed tomorrow.”

### Business without debate and committee changes
The House ordered changes to two select committees. Olivia Blake was discharged from the Environmental Audit Committee and Cat Eccles was added: “That Olivia Blake be discharged from the Environmental Audit Committee and Cat Eccles be added.” Laurence Turner was discharged from the Transport Committee and Joe Morris was added: “That Laurence Turner be discharged from the Transport Committee and Joe Morris be added.” Both orders were made on behalf of the Committee of Selection.

### Hair and Beauty Sector Regulation
Vikki Slade argued for mandatory registration for hairdressers and barbers, citing consumer safety, unfair competition, bogus self-employment, declining apprenticeships and concerns about illicit activity associated with some businesses. She argued that the existing statutory framework could be strengthened rather than replaced and referred to a sector survey indicating support for mandatory registration. The Minister, Kate Dearden, agreed that safety, employment rights, apprenticeships and enforcement were important, but said that registration alone would not inspect premises, identify business ownership or resolve employment-status issues: “It would not create a single new apprenticeship place.”

The Minister pointed to existing health-and-safety and local-authority enforcement powers, proposed regulation of high-risk cosmetic procedures, employment-status reforms, the Fair Work Agency, HM Revenue and Customs enforcement and targeted support for apprenticeships and law enforcement. She remained open to further evidence but said she was “not yet persuaded that a mandatory register would deliver those things.” The adjournment motion was agreed to and the House adjourned.
