## High-Level Summary
The Commons centred on culture, sport and local growth, with Ministers questioned on short‑term lets, sporting participation, cultural access, AI risks, tourism levies and support for lidos. Urgent Questions covered the Jackdaw and Rosebank oil and gas consents (no decisions yet) and the bluetongue outbreak (vaccination supply and testing capacity being increased). The Government outlined plans to revive high streets, expand social housing and end rough sleeping, while the Sporting Events Bill [Lords] passed Second Reading to bolster delivery of major events. Business for early September was announced, a petition on police front‑desk access in Redditch was presented, and an adjournment debate pressed for masterplans on very large housing sites.

## Detailed Summary
### Speaker’s Statement: Tribute to Sir Billy Boston
The Speaker paid tribute to rugby league legend Sir Billy Boston, noting he was a “world cup winner for his country and the first ever rugby league player to receive a knighthood” and acknowledging the racism he faced.

### Oral Answers: Digital, Culture, Media and Sport
Short‑term lets: The Secretary of State said regulation work had been accelerated and that “the register will be up and running in full by March,” arguing this would help communities—including rural areas—balance tourism and local housing needs.

Access to sport: Ministers highlighted targeted funding, including school sport and grassroots facilities, stating, “£1 billion will go in over the next few years … We are investing £400 million into community facilities”. On women’s sport eligibility, the Government reiterated that national governing bodies set policies, prioritising “fairness, safety and competition” while balancing inclusion.

Access to culture: The Government cited the “arts everywhere programme” and “investing £1.5 billion into arts, culture and heritage across England,” and referred to City/Town of Culture competitions. Challenges of rural access, including transport, were acknowledged. On Notting Hill Carnival, Ministers said policing “is the responsibility of the Mayor of London”.

Frontier AI and online harms: Citing the AI Safety Institute report, Ministers stressed companies “cannot be allowed to mark their own homework,” and agreed to meet campaigners on press harms.

Overnight visitor levy: Ministers emphasised the measure is permissive and locally decided; one Minister said, “we are cutting taxes, but we are also introducing an overnight levy,” adding it makes sense for funds to be reinvested in tourism (for local decision).

Lidos and grassroots facilities: A forthcoming announcement to support lidos was trailed—“we will have more to say about that shortly”—alongside “£400 million … into grassroots sports facilities”.

Tourism: The Government referenced the Great British summer savings scheme’s temporary VAT cut for attractions and VisitBritain’s “Starring GREAT Britain” campaign. Ministers reaffirmed commitment to a short‑let register to protect hotels and communities.

Digital inclusion: The Department highlighted the £11.9 million digital inclusion innovation fund and agreed to visit to hear rural connectivity concerns.

Topical: Government asked the Chartered Trading Standards Institute to clarify digital ownership rights; will meet TikTok on dangerous driving content; and said the Sporting Events Bill would be introduced “later today” to help keep ticket prices affordable. The Prime Minister was said to want “a change of leadership at FIFA”. Plans to strengthen Charity Commission powers against extremist abuse of charities were restated. The Speaker confirmed DCMS questions will be for an hour after recess.

### Church Commissioners, House Authorities and Electoral Commission: Oral Questions
Support for Palestinian Christians was affirmed; the Second Church Estates Commissioner cited UN‑reported figures and said, “The settler terrorism that we are witnessing must come to an end”. Training for MPs on domestic and sexual abuse casework was outlined, including “a day‑long training course … [and] an e‑learning module on safeguarding”.

On parish finances, it was clarified: “No parish is required to make payments to the national Church institutions” and that parish share funds diocesan ministry. Global persecution of Christians remained a concern. Church support for youth holiday clubs was praised, and partnership with local authorities on family hubs encouraged, backed by “a £1 billion investment over the next nine years”. A working group is addressing misuse of Christian nationalism; Christianity’s message “to love our neighbour as ourselves” was stressed.

The Electoral Commission said it translates voter materials into various languages and is in discussion locally on Cornish (Kernewek). Project Spire will proceed and “is being funded out of the commissioners’ own income”. On the Places of Worship Renewal Fund, engagement with Historic England was noted, including using at‑risk and deprivation indices to prioritise grants.

### Urgent Question: Jackdaw and Rosebank Oil and Gas Fields
The Energy Minister set out the context: original consents were quashed in January 2025; supplementary guidance implementing the Finch judgment was issued in June 2025; public notice periods closed in August; and “The Secretary of State will consider the representations … and will make separate decisions … in due course”. She stressed that the quasi‑judicial process must be respected to avoid re‑running it.

Members pressed for clarity on timelines and jobs. The Government emphasised a “pragmatic approach,” with oil and gas having “an important role” alongside renewables, and confirmed Scope 3 emissions will be assessed in EIAs. No decisions or dates were announced, with a decision to come “in due course”.

### Urgent Question: Bluetongue Virus in Livestock
The Farming Minister reported 795 confirmed cases since 10 July, stressed there is “no risk to human health or food safety,” and said vaccination is the “most effective … control measure,” with testing capacity being enhanced; he highlighted close working with devolved Governments and industry.

Concerns were raised about testing delays, vaccine supply and fallen stock costs. The Minister said Government had “acted quickly to secure increased vaccine supply,” was expanding capacity at the Pirbright Institute, reviewing processes to expedite confirmations, and is “considering … further financial support”. Collaboration with Scotland and other devolved Administrations was confirmed.

### Business of the House
The Leader announced business for 7–15 September, including the remaining stages of the Health Bill, Second Readings of the Civil Aviation (Consumer Protection and Regulatory Reform) Bill [Lords] and the Social Housing Bill [Lords], and the Sovereign Grant Bill. “The House will rise for the conference recess … and return on Monday 12 October”. He also noted recent wildfires and paid tribute to two police officers who died in Cleveland.

On defence spending and Restoration & Renewal, he said the Prime Minister would set spending in the forthcoming Budget, and that R&R proposals would come to the House “not … much longer,” given delays carry costs. On No.10 North, he described it as “the biggest transfer of power and decision making out of Whitehall in a generation,” while acknowledging scrutiny arrangements require consideration.

### Statement: Building Homes and Renewing Communities
The Deputy Prime Minister set out high street measures, including support to block new betting shops and vape stores, doubling closure orders, and improving shopfronts, alongside “our Pride in Place programme, which is giving local communities £6 billion”. She pledged to “end rough sleeping,” promising, “By Christmas this year, everyone sleeping rough will be offered a route off the streets,” backed by “£442 million for a new rough sleeping programme” and more than “£4 billion over the next three years” for homelessness and rough sleeping services.

On housing, she announced the first wave of more than 70,000 social and affordable homes, “nearly two‑thirds … for social rent,” £46 million to rebuild council capacity, NPPF changes to unlock homes near stations, and new mayoral planning and housing powers. Wider devolution will give mayors a share of income tax revenue and new powers, aiming to end “the begging‑bowl culture”. A wildfire summit will be held in the autumn. In response to challenges, she cited “a £30 million boost to police and Trading Standards” and “strengthened closure orders,” and said mandatory housing targets and plans are being applied.

### Sporting Events Bill [Lords]: Second Reading
The Culture Secretary presented the Bill as a framework to boost the UK’s major‑event hosting, confirming 2028 Euros co‑hosting, a bid for the 2029 World Athletics Championships, interest in the Ryder Cup, and that “we are continuing to explore at pace a bid for an Olympic and Paralympic games in the north of England”. The Bill tackles ticket touting, controls ambush marketing and unauthorised trading, enables tailored transport measures, and provides funding powers. She undertook to address including major recurring domestic events during passage.

The Opposition supported Second Reading and preserving Lords changes allowing recurring events (e.g. Wimbledon, the Open) to access the framework, while seeking safeguards for existing traders and clarity on legitimate sponsorship. The Government confirmed a forthcoming major events strategy, said ticket resale reforms for music/culture would be a “high priority,” and that domestic events’ inclusion would be taken up in Committee. The Bill passed Second Reading without division; programme and money motions were agreed.

### Petition: Redditch Police and Fire Station
Christopher Bloore presented a petition objecting to a new station “that … has neither a permanent front‑counter service nor holding cells,” and calling for a “publicly accessible front desk” in Redditch.

### Adjournment: Large‑scale Development Masterplans
Allison Gardner argued that very large housing allocations should be guided by comprehensive masterplans to coordinate infrastructure, green space and accessibility, warning against piecemeal applications and citing the need for accessible homes and water infrastructure upgrades. The Housing Minister agreed on the value of masterplanning, noting the new NPPF defines strategic sites (≥1,500 homes) and such developments “require a masterplan,” alongside strengthened infrastructure policy and a new £5 billion national housing delivery fund.
