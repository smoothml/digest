## High-Level Summary
The Commons held Work and Pensions questions on pensions, disability employment, Access to Work delays, skills and child poverty. An Urgent Question addressed the North Sea oil and gas sector after Petrofac’s holding company entered administration, with ministers stressing the UK arm continues to trade. The Justice Secretary made a statement on a prisoner released in error, announcing strengthened release checks and an independent investigation. MPs then completed Report and Third Reading of the Victims and Courts Bill, adopting provisions on restricting parental responsibility where a child is conceived by rape and voiding certain non-disclosure agreements, before passing the Bill. Royal Assent was notified for five Acts, and the day concluded with a debate urging regulation of funeral services.

## Detailed Summary
### Business before Questions and Speaker’s statement
The House agreed an amendment to Standing Order No. 224 on examination of public bills by Examiners; the motion was recorded as “Ordered,” [ref: c1.2/1]. The Speaker announced publication of the Speaker’s Conference second and final report on security for Members, candidates and elections: “today we publish our second and final report” [ref: c1.4/1].

### Oral Answers to Questions: Work and Pensions
The Secretary of State confirmed: “The state pension age will rise to 67 from 2028… Consideration of the future of the state pension age is already under way” [ref: c1.10/3]. On Women Against State Pension Inequality compensation, he declined to comment on litigation but said, “we will take all relevant factors into account” [ref: c3.1/1].

On Access to Work, ministers said policy had not changed but that guidance had been applied “more scrupulous[ly]” over the last year [ref: c4.2/1], noted spending increases of 17% and 32% in the past two years [ref: c4.4/1], and accepted that “delays are still a problem” pointing to the “need for reform” [ref: c5.3/1]. On skills, ministers highlighted a skills White Paper and employer engagement [ref: c5.7/1; c6.4/1], and agreed to explore a single portal for academic and technical routes: “I have already asked the Department to begin work in this area” [ref: c6.2/1]. On supporting people with health conditions, the Government said the Keep Britain Working review would be published soon and assured “there will be no changes to the eligibility conditions for the mobility component of the personal independence payment… until the conclusion of the review” [ref: c7.3/1; c7.7/1].

On child poverty, ministers referred to a forthcoming child poverty strategy and stated that extending free school meals to all families on universal credit would lift “100,000 children out of poverty” [ref: c11.5/1]. On pre‑1997 pension indexation, the Pensions Minister acknowledged challenges and pointed to surplus‑sharing reforms in the Pension Schemes Bill [ref: c14.1/1]. In Topical Questions, the Secretary of State confirmed that means‑testing the state pension “is not the Government’s policy” [ref: c17.6/1].

### Urgent Question: North Sea oil and gas and Petrofac
The Energy Minister said Petrofac Ltd (the top‑level holding company) had entered administration after a failed restructuring, but stressed that “the UK arm of Petrofac has not entered administration and is continuing to operate as normal” [ref: c25.2/4]. He described the UK business as “an in-demand business with a highly skilled workforce” and said there was “reason to be optimistic about a commercial resolution” [ref: c25.2/5].

He cautioned against remarks that might undermine a sale, calling it “deeply irresponsible” to “talk down a business” [ref: c26.0/1], and noted the sector’s transition predated the change of government [ref: c26.0/2]. He added, “we are doing everything that we can… All the signs are that it is a growing, successful business” [ref: c28.0/1; c28.0/2]. The session also touched on licensing policy (with a response to consultation due) and support for clean energy jobs and skills. No policy changes were announced; ministers committed to continued engagement to support a buyer or other resolution for the UK arm.

### Ministerial Statement: Prisoner release in error (HMP Chelmsford)
The Justice Secretary reported that Hadush Kebatu, released in error from HMP Chelmsford, had been apprehended: “He is back where he belongs: behind bars” [ref: c43.1/2]. Immediate actions included an urgent review to strengthen release checks—“These are the strongest release checks that have ever been in place” [ref: c43.1/6]—a requirement for the duty governor to be physically present for Early Removal Scheme discharges, and a pause on ERS removals from HMP Chelmsford for the rest of the week [ref: c43.1/7].

He announced an independent investigation by Dame Lynne Owens to examine the causes, training and technology, and to hear from victims, noting releases in error had risen “from an average of nine per month in 2023 to 17 per month in the period spanning January to June 2024” [ref: c43.1/8]. He linked systemic pressures to the inherited state of prisons [ref: c43.1/10] and outlined longer‑term capacity and deportation measures [ref: c43.1/12; c43.1/13]. In exchanges, he contrasted current steps with the lack of prior independent probes in similar incidents under the previous Government [ref: c46.0/4]. Outcome: strengthened processes and an independent inquiry; temporary pause on ERS removals from Chelmsford.

### Victims and Courts Bill: Report Stage and Third Reading
Ministers introduced measures to void non‑disclosure agreements (NDAs) that purport to silence victims or witnesses of crime: “NDAs must not be used to silence victims or witnesses of crime” [ref: c73.1/2]. The House added New Clause 14 to restrict parental responsibility where a child was conceived as a result of rape; as the Member for Bolsover said, “rapists will no longer have access to children conceived by their crime” [ref: c84.0/1]. Government Amendment 10 broadened Clause 3 so courts must restrict parental responsibility where an offender with parental responsibility is sentenced to at least four years for serious child sexual offences against any child [ref: c125.1/4].

Opposition proposals to broaden victim personal statements and extend the Unduly Lenient Sentence scheme were defeated: New Clause 4 (victim personal statements) Ayes 152, Noes 337 [ref: c113.3/1]; New Clause 7 (extending the Victim Contact Scheme) Ayes 165, Noes 323 [ref: c117.3/2]; New Clause 12 (Victims’ Code abroad) Ayes 166, Noes 322 [ref: c122.1/2]; Amendment 5 (ULS time limit to one year) Ayes 153, Noes 332 [ref: c127.3/2]. The Bill was then read the Third time and passed [ref: c133.2/3].

### Royal Assent
The House was informed, in accordance with the Royal Assent Act 1967, that Royal Assent had been signified to five measures, including the Bus Services Act 2025 and the Renters’ Rights Act 2025 [ref: c61.2/1; c61.2/2; c61.2/4].

### Adjournment Debate: Regulation and inspection of funeral services
Members called for statutory licensing and inspection of the funeral sector, citing recent scandals. The Minister said, “People deserve dignity in death, and families deserve the comfort of knowing that their loved ones have been safely laid to rest” [ref: c141.0/2], and confirmed a timetable for responding to the Fuller Inquiry (phase 2): “an interim update before the end of this year, followed by a full response in summer 2026” [ref: c141.0/9]. No immediate policy changes were announced; ministers committed to cross‑departmental work on potential regulation.
