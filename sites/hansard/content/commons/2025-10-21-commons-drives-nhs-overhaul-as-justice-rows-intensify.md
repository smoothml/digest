## High-Level Summary
The House concentrated on health policy during Oral Questions, covering vaccine injury payments, NHS performance management, maternity and neonatal safety, children’s hospices, digitisation, and shifting care into communities. Ministers then answered an Urgent Question on the statutory national inquiry into grooming gangs, addressing concerns about victim engagement, scope and the chairing process. A Ten Minute Rule Bill proposed mandatory reporting of cyber extortion and ransomware attacks. The Committee of the whole House scrutinised and amended the Sentencing Bill, with divisions on suspended sentences, early release exclusions and deportation provisions. The day concluded with an adjournment debate on rural social housing in the South Cotswolds, alongside routine delegated legislation, committee membership changes and a public petition.

## Detailed Summary
### Oral Questions: Health and Social Care
On vaccine injury, the Minister stated: “The vaccine damage payment scheme is not a compensation scheme but a one-off payment” and said campaigners’ input was shaping reform consideration [ref: d767.7/1]. On NHS trust performance, the Secretary of State cited investment and accountability tools, saying “We will not tolerate failure” and highlighting league tables, leadership support and potential board restrictions [ref: d768.0/1]. On maternity and neonatal safety, he announced an independent investigation chaired by Baroness Amos and a separate inquiry into Leeds Teaching Hospitals, stating: “Families deserve truth and justice” [ref: d770.2/1; d770.2/2].

Further exchanges noted: a three‑year £80 million settlement for children’s hospices and a £12 million capital allocation to the sector [ref: d771.5/1; d772.0/1]; commitment to deliver schemes under the new hospital programme [ref: d772.5/1]; plans to abolish NHS England and other bodies as part of reorganisation [ref: d773.2/1]; the NHS Online hospital aiming for up to 8.5 million appointments in its first three years [ref: d774.6/1]; progress on the cancer 28‑day standard with “135,000 more patients” diagnosed or ruled out compared with the previous year [ref: d775.6/1]; GP access improvements including recruiting “2,000” GPs and online requests [ref: d778.3/1]; assurance that the NHS will remain free at the point of use while exploring neighbourhood health services [ref: d779.2/1]; efforts to cut agency spending “by a third” with plans to abolish it [ref: d779.9/1]; menopause policy and HRT supply measures [ref: d781.0/1]; virtual wards expansion [ref: d782.0/1]; and shifting care into the community through neighbourhood health centres [ref: d782.3/1]. In Topical Questions, the Secretary of State listed actions such as NHS Online, hospice funding and league tables [ref: d783.3/1], and stated: “waiting lists are lower now than when Labour took office” [ref: d784.1/1].

### Urgent Question: Statutory inquiry into grooming gangs
The Safeguarding Minister confirmed delivery of Baroness Casey’s recommendations via “a statutory national inquiry under the Inquiries Act 2005”, with powers to compel evidence, time‑limited to three years, focused on grooming gangs, and running alongside police operations [ref: d794.2/2; d794.2/4]. Members raised concerns about resignations from the victims and survivors liaison panel and fears that the remit might downplay religious or ethnic factors [ref: d794.3/5–d794.3/11]. The Minister clarified the process of meeting prospective chairs was managed by the independent NWG Network—“The Government have not handled the process; it has been handled by a grooming gang charity” [ref: d795.0/1]—and undertook to keep listening to victims, stating “I will listen completely and utterly to the feedback from the victims” [ref: d798.0/1]. She expressed regret about resignations [ref: d799.0/1] and reaffirmed: “it will be three years long … and it will be a grooming gangs inquiry” [ref: d803.7/1].

### Ten Minute Rule Bill: Cyber Extortion and Ransomware (Reporting)
Bradley Thomas sought leave to bring in a Bill requiring larger companies and critical national infrastructure operators to report cyber extortion or ransomware attacks within 72 hours, and to report any subsequent payments within 72 hours [ref: d809.2/7]. He cited a “50% increase in British cyber‑incidents deemed ‘highly significant’ over the past year” [ref: d809.2/3] and research that “59% of companies targeted by ransomware chose to pay, with an average cost of £870,000” [ref: d809.2/6]. Leave was granted; the Bill was read a First time and set down for Second Reading on 29 May 2026 [ref: d809.2/16; d809.2/20].

### Sentencing Bill: Committee of the whole House
The Committee debated extensive amendments. Proposals to narrow the new presumption to suspend sentences of 12 months or less (e.g., amendment 46) were defeated (Ayes 105, Noes 381) [ref: d902.5/1]. Clause 1, as amended by Government technical changes (including exclusions related to supervision orders and breaches closely connected with the index offence), was added (Ayes 389, Noes 102) [ref: d907.1/1; d902.5/4–d902.5/14]. An amendment to exclude specified serious offences (including rape and grievous bodily harm) from early release provisions (amendment 24) was defeated (Ayes 182, Noes 307) [ref: d913.1/1]. Government new clause 1 was agreed, clarifying that for deportation purposes “a sentence of imprisonment includes a suspended sentence order” [ref: d917.1/1; d917.1/3]. 

Other outcomes included rejection of: a lifetime driving ban for causing death by dangerous or careless driving (new clause 6) (Ayes 167, Noes 313) [ref: d917.5/2]; a duty to collect and publish detailed sentencing data (new clause 9) (Ayes 104, Noes 317) [ref: d921.3/1]; and a resentencing exercise for those serving imprisonment for public protection sentences (new clause 30) (Ayes 77, Noes 390) [ref: d925.3/2]. The original clause 42 was disagreed to, with its effect substituted by Government new clause 1 [ref: d913.1/4; d917.1/3]. The Bill, as amended, was reported and set down for further consideration the next day [ref: d934.1/5].

### Delegated legislation
The House approved the draft Compensation for Miscarriages of Justice (Alteration of Overall Compensation Limits) Order 2025: “Question agreed to.” [ref: d934.6/2].

### Committee membership changes
The House agreed to changes in select committees: Ellie Chowns was discharged and Carla Denyer added to the Environmental Audit Committee; Peter Fortune and James Murray were discharged and Rupert Lowe and Dan Tomlinson added to the Committee of Public Accounts; Danny Kruger was discharged and Joy Morrissey added to the Work and Pensions Committee [ref: d934.10/1; d934.12/1; d934.14/1].

### Public petition: Wordsworth House and Garden
A petition from Penrith and Solway urged Government to work with the National Trust to keep Wordsworth House in Cockermouth “fully operational and open to visitors” [ref: d935.1/2]. The petitioner noted the Trust’s assurance not to close the site while expressing concern about its long‑term sustainability [ref: d935.1/1].

### Adjournment debate: Social Housing in the South Cotswolds
The Member highlighted rural housing pressures, stating “the need for genuinely affordable, safe and well‑maintained housing has never been greater” and that “around 80% of Cotswold district lies within a designated national landscape” [ref: d936.2/2; d937.0/2]. She called for protection of rural exception sites and more flexible national funding. The Minister replied that Government aims to “build 1.5 million homes during this Parliament” [ref: d941.3/2] and announced “a new 10‑year, £39 billion social and affordable homes programme,” hoping to deliver “around 300,000 new homes, with at least 60% for social rent” [ref: d942.2/6; d942.2/7].
