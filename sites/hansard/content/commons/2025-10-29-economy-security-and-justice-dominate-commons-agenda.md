## High-Level Summary
The Commons day covered Wales questions on farming inheritance tax, economic growth, living costs, energy infrastructure, asylum accommodation and cross‑border rail. Prime Minister’s Questions focused on tax, the economy, defence exports and Gaza, with the Prime Minister updating on hurricane aid for Jamaica and the Renters’ Rights Act. Ministers answered Urgent Questions on Gaza’s fragile ceasefire and aid access, and on plans to use two MOD sites for asylum accommodation. The Government announced an £8 billion UK–Türkiye Typhoon export deal. A Ten Minute Rule proposal to withdraw from the ECHR was defeated, the Sentencing Bill completed Commons stages (several proposed new clauses were rejected) and a Privileges motion to assist the Omagh Bombing Inquiry was agreed before an adjournment debate on the Tyneside offshore wind supply chain.

## Detailed Summary
### Wales Oral Questions: Farmers and Inheritance Tax
Minister Anna McMorrin said she had recently met the Farmers’ Union of Wales and would meet the National Farmers’ Union to discuss inheritance tax (IHT), adding, "I will be meeting with the National Farmers’ Union later today to discuss important matters for its members, including inheritance tax" [ref: a291.7/2]. She argued reforms would strike a balance and that most farmers would be unaffected: "The vast majority of farmers will not be affected by this change, and they will be able to pass the family farm down to their children" [ref: a291.9/1]. Opposition Members warned of wider impacts and affordability, citing a claim that 85% of active farms would face IHT exceeding income; McMorrin reiterated that "the majority of those claiming agricultural property relief will not be affected" [ref: a292.1/1]. Outcome: ministerial assurances; no decisions.

### Wales Oral Questions: Economic Growth
Secretary of State Jo Stevens said growth was the Government’s top priority: "Economic growth is the No. 1 priority for this Government. The UK was the fastest growing economy in the G7 in the first half of this year" [ref: a293.4/1]. She outlined "pride in place" investments and stated "record inward investment in Wales, with a 23% increase on the previous financial year" [ref: a294.1/1]. On Plaid Cymru’s independence plans, she said they would cost £21.5 billion annually [ref: a293.8/1]. Asked to preview Budget measures, she replied, "She will have to wait and see what the Chancellor says on 26 November" [ref: a295.0/1]. Outcome: statements; no decisions.

### Wales Oral Questions: Cost of Living
Jo Stevens said the Government had increased minimum and living wages for 160,000 Welsh workers, frozen fuel duty and extended the warm home discount: "We have already taken action by increasing the national minimum wage and the national living wage for 160,000 workers across Wales. We have frozen fuel duty and extended the warm home discount" [ref: a295.6/1]. She linked place‑based funds and programmes with growth and jobs: "Taken together, they will boost the economy, draw in further investment, create thousands of jobs and raise living standards across Wales" [ref: a296.2/1]. Outcome: statements; no decisions.

### Wales Oral Questions: Energy Infrastructure and Pylons
On undergrounding powerlines, Jo Stevens said overhead lines are much cheaper and quicker: "undergrounding costs an estimated four and a half times more than overhead lines. Overhead lines are also quicker to build, cause less environmental damage, and are much easier to maintain" [ref: a296.5/1]. She said communities would benefit from new funds and bill discounts near transmission upgrades [ref: a296.7/1]. Asked about Plaid Cymru’s approach, she said, "Labour is the only party that is committed to investing in renewable energy... Plaid and Reform are against renewable infrastructure" [ref: a297.1/1]. Outcome: statements; no decisions.

### Wales Oral Questions: Asylum Seeker Accommodation
Minister Anna McMorrin said the Government would end hotel use for asylum seekers as soon as possible and before the end of the Parliament: "We are committed to ending the use of hotels as asylum accommodation as soon as possible and before the end of this Parliament" [ref: a297.7/1]. She stressed providing sanctuary while noting progress: "Hotel use has nearly halved since the last election, and we have removed 30,000 people who have no right to be here" [ref: a298.2/1]. Outcome: statements; no decisions.

### Wales Oral Questions: Cross‑border Rail Connectivity
Anna McMorrin announced investment of at least £445 million in Welsh rail to improve cross‑border links: "We are investing at least £445 million in Welsh rail... That will mean new stations and more and faster trains" [ref: a299.3/1]. She cited funds to progress the five Burns stations: "Cardiff East, Newport West, Somerton, Llanwern and Magor and Undy" [ref: a299.5/1]. Outcome: statements of funding and plans.

### Prime Minister’s Questions
The Prime Minister updated on support for Jamaica after Hurricane Melissa: "HMS Trent and specialist rapid deployment teams are pre-positioned in the region, and we stand ready to provide humanitarian support" [ref: a300.4/1], and said the Renters’ Rights Act 2025, including Awaab’s law, was now in force [ref: a300.4/2]. Exchanges focused on manifesto tax pledges, with the PM saying Budget plans would be set out on 26 November but that the Government would "build a stronger economy" [ref: a301.1/1]. On defence exports, he said, "Just on Monday of this week, the Turkish Government signed an £8 billion deal for Typhoons" [ref: a302.3/1]. On Gaza, he called for de‑escalation and aid access: "All sides need to uphold President Trump’s peace plan... The immediate priority remains getting aid in" [ref: a303.3/1]. Outcome: questions to the PM; no decisions.

### Urgent Question: Gaza and Hamas
Minister Hamish Falconer reported overnight strikes in Rafah and called for de‑escalation and adherence to the ceasefire agreement: "we echo the Americans in calling for urgent de-escalation and for all parties to keep the commitments they have made in the ceasefire agreement" [ref: a311.2/2]. He pressed for unrestricted aid access and opening crossings (Rafah and Allenby bridge) [ref: a311.2/2; a312.0/3] and said, "We are clear that there can be no role for Hamas in the future governance of the strip" [ref: a311.2/3]. He stated, "no British weaponry is used either in Gaza or in the west bank, other than subject to the messages relating to F-35s that we have discussed previously" [ref: a315.0/1]. Outcome: ministerial update; no vote.

### Urgent Question: Asylum Seekers – Use of MOD Housing
Home Office Minister Alex Norris announced plans to use two MOD sites temporarily—Cameron Barracks (Inverness) and Crowborough training camp (East Sussex)—to accommodate around 900 asylum seekers to help close hotels: "plans are under way for the temporary use of Ministry of Defence sites at Cameron barracks in Inverness and at Crowborough training camp in East Sussex... a total of around 900 people will be housed across both sites" [ref: a326.2/3]. He apologised for communication issues and committed to engage locally: "the hon. Gentleman should not have heard in the way he did" [ref: a327.0/1]. He said closing hotels was essential to break the "pull factor" and illegal working model [ref: a327.0/3]. Outcome: policy announced; engagement promised.

### Statement: UK–Türkiye Typhoon Export Deal
Defence Minister Luke Pollard announced an agreement for Türkiye to purchase 20 British‑built Typhoons worth £8 billion: "he finalised an agreement with President Erdoğan for Türkiye to purchase 20 British-built Typhoon fighter jets. That deal is worth £8 billion" [ref: a338.1/2]. He said it would support 20,000 jobs across 330 UK companies [ref: a338.1/3] and strengthen NATO deterrence and interoperability [ref: a338.1/5]. He also cited a recent £10 billion Type‑26 frigate export to Norway and outlined defence industrial strategy, including a consultation on offsets [ref: a338.1/6; a338.1/9]. Outcome: statement; no division.

### Ten Minute Rule Motion: European Convention on Human Rights (Withdrawal)
Nigel Farage sought leave to bring in a Bill for UK withdrawal from the ECHR, arguing that sovereignty required it: "We are not sovereign all the while we are part of the European convention on human rights" [ref: a353.0/4]. Sir Edward Davey opposed, highlighting the convention’s protections and Churchill’s role: "Britain helped to create the European convention on human rights, championed by Winston Churchill himself" [ref: a354.2/4]. Outcome: The House negatived the motion on division, Ayes 96, Noes 154 [ref: a356.1/1; a356.1/2].

### Sentencing Bill: Consideration and Third Reading
Debate covered proposals including removing anonymity for serious young offenders, whole‑life orders where police/prison officer murders are linked to duty, and a child‑cruelty offender notification regime. Proponents cited deterrence and victim focus; Ministers emphasised balancing reform with capacity and highlighted the new domestic abuse identifier, under which "once the court is satisfied that an offence involves domestic abuse, it must declare that is the case" [ref: a390.0/5; see also a413.1/1]. Divisions: New Clause 12 (rehabilitation access for those on remand) was defeated (Ayes 82, Noes 314) [ref: a423.1/1; a423.1/2]; New Clause 19 (whole‑life orders for murders connected to police/prison duties) was defeated (Ayes 173, Noes 323) [ref: a426.3/1; a426.3/2]; New Clause 20 (child‑cruelty notification regime) was defeated (Ayes 182, Noes 311) [ref: a431.1/1; a431.1/2]. The Bill passed Third Reading on division (Ayes 321, Noes 103) [ref: a437.1/1–a437.1/3]. Next steps: the Bill proceeds to the House of Lords (not stated in the transcript).

### Privileges: Omagh Bombing Inquiry
The House agreed to a motion to provide the Omagh Inquiry with an unpublished 2009 Northern Ireland Affairs Committee transcript and to grant the Inquiry power to disclose or publish it following advice: the motion "orders that the unpublished transcript... be provided to the Omagh Bombing Inquiry and that the Inquiry shall have power to disclose or publish it if it thinks fit to do so, after taking relevant advice" [ref: a441.1/6]. The Privileges Committee Chair said there was "an overwhelming public interest in our helping the Omagh bombing inquiry" [ref: a441.3/2]. Outcome: motion agreed to.

### Adjournment Debate: Offshore Wind Supply Chain – Tyneside
Mary Glindon urged further Government support for domestic fabrication, citing risks from lower‑cost overseas competition and physical constraints (Tyne power cables) limiting bids for large structures, and she queried the adequacy of Allocation Round 7 [ref: a445.7/2; a445.7/6]. She asked for acceleration of cable works and support to bridge a pipeline gap [ref: a445.7/7; a445.7/10]. Minister Katie White highlighted the AR7 budget and clean industry bonus, and wider support including a £1 billion supply chain package and UK Infrastructure Bank/port investments: "a total budget of £900 million is available for fixed-bottom offshore wind in allocation round 7" [ref: a448.1/6]; "a package of support, worth up to £1 billion, for offshore wind supply chains" [ref: a449.1/3]. On the Tyne cable, she said the Government were open to discussions with National Grid, with Ofgem as decision maker [ref: a449.1/6]. Outcome: assurances and willingness to engage; no decision.
