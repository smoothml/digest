## High-Level Summary
The Commons held Scotland questions, Prime Minister’s Questions, a Transport statement on Heathrow expansion, consideration of Lords amendments to the Renters’ Rights Bill, and debates and motions on life sciences funding, NHS procurement and slavery, Scottish devolution, and hoarding disorder. Ministers confirmed a review of the Airports National Policy Statement to keep Heathrow expansion on a 2035 runway‑opening ambition, while the House agreed Lords changes to the Renters’ Rights Bill and approved modern slavery due‑diligence regulations for NHS procurement. MPs also authorised higher‑value awards under the Life Sciences Innovative Manufacturing Fund. The Prime Minister announced repeal of the family courts’ presumption of parental involvement and took questions on the national grooming inquiry, the Crown Estate, and public services. Angela Rayner made a personal statement; a Ten Minute Rule Bill on perinatal mental health assessments was introduced.

## Detailed Summary
### Oral Answers: Scotland
On inheritance tax relief for agriculture, the Parliamentary Under‑Secretary of State, Kirsty McNeill, said the Government is balancing support for farmers with fiscal repair, stressing, “Only the richest estates will be asked to pay, not small family farms” [ref: b945.6/1], later adding, “the majority—almost three quarters—of those claiming the relief will not be affected” [ref: b946.5/1]. She pointed to extra funding since the election for devolved services, noting “an extra £5.2 billion to the Scottish Government” and a further £9.1 billion in the spending review [ref: b946.3/1].

On North Sea energy security, the Secretary of State, Douglas Alexander, said oil and gas “will remain part of our energy system for decades to come” alongside investment in clean power and a “fair and orderly transition” [ref: b947.9/1; b948.1/1]. On the Pride in Place programme, McNeill highlighted “our £292 million pride in place investment” to revitalise high streets and empower communities [ref: b949.5/1; b950.1/1]. On energy prices, Alexander said the Government is considering “mandating the provision of community benefit funds for low‑carbon energy infrastructure” with plans “later this year” [ref: b951.4/1], and confirmed automatic Warm Home Discount eligibility for those on pension credit [ref: b951.6/1]. On an independence referendum, Alexander restated that the Government “do not support independence or another referendum” and recalled the 2014 result [ref: b951.10/1; b953.5/1]. On the cost of living, McNeill said a child poverty strategy is forthcoming and reiterated the Government’s commitment to reducing poverty [ref: b954.1/1; b954.3/1]. No divisions were held.

### Prime Minister’s Questions
The Prime Minister set out announcements on clean‑energy jobs, new “V‑level” qualifications, investment secured at a regional summit, and tougher penalties for water polluters [ref: b954.9/1]. He confirmed reform to family courts: “we will repeal the presumption of parental involvement, putting children’s safety first” [ref: b954.9/2].

The Leader of the Opposition (Kemi Badenoch) pressed on the national grooming inquiry; the Prime Minister said its scope “will not be watered down” and announced, “Dame Louise Casey will now support the work of the inquiry” [ref: b956.1/2; b956.1/3]. He explained it would not be judge‑led to avoid delaying parallel criminal investigations and to maintain speed [ref: b958.0/1‑2]. In response to Edward Davey, he supported proper scrutiny of the Crown Estate: “there is proper scrutiny. I certainly support that” [ref: b960.0/1]. A wide range of constituency issues were raised (housing, potholes, inequality, maternity safety, accessibility, local elections, events, hospices, flooding, pharmacies, youth clubs, business rates, dentistry, and knife crime). No votes were taken.

### Transport Statement: Heathrow – Airports National Policy Statement review
The Secretary of State for Transport (Heidi Alexander) launched a review of the Airports National Policy Statement to reflect environmental and travel changes since 2018, while keeping expansion on track: “We are committed to making a decision on a third runway at Heathrow within this Parliament, and we are clear in our ambition to see flights taking off on a new runway in 2035” [ref: b967.1/3]. The review will test any proposal against four criteria—economic growth, air quality, noise and climate obligations—and seek advice from the Climate Change Committee [ref: b967.1/5‑6]. Two schemes remain under consideration (Heathrow Airport Limited and the Aurora Group) with a single scheme to be identified by end‑November; consultation on any ANPS amendments is expected by next summer. Supporting reforms include streamlined consenting, judicial review timing work with the judiciary, airspace modernisation and a CAA review of economic regulation [ref: b967.1/7‑11].

The Shadow Secretary of State (Richard Holden) warned of delay, costs being passed to passengers and judicial review risks, and pressed on competition and surface access [ref: b969.1/2‑1/7]. Alexander said the Government is “accelerating Heathrow expansion… setting out this swift and robust review of the ANPS”, while insisting the scheme must be privately financed and compliant with climate law [ref: b970.0/1; b967.1/4; b972.0/1]. No division occurred; the review proceeds on the stated timeline.

### Personal Statement: Angela Rayner
Angela Rayner addressed the House regarding her departure from office, acknowledging a tax error linked to a family trust and committing to pay any tax due: “There is no excuse not to pay taxes owed, and I will do so” [ref: b984.1/4]. She reflected on priorities she had advanced, including the forthcoming Employment Rights Bill—“a game changer for millions stuck in insecure and low‑paid work” [ref: b984.1/6]—plans for a Hillsborough‑style duty of candour [ref: b984.1/7], investment in social and affordable housing, and the Renters’ Rights Bill [ref: b984.1/8]. No motion followed.

### Ten Minute Rule Motion: Perinatal Mental Health Assessments Bill
Laura Kyrke‑Smith introduced a Bill to require structured mental health assessments within NHS antenatal care, enhanced training and supervision for professionals, clear referral pathways (including to NHS Talking Therapies), and accountability through data capture and CQC oversight [ref: b989.2/2; b989.2/11‑2/15]. She highlighted that suicide is the leading cause of maternal death in the year after birth and that one in four women experience perinatal mental ill‑health [ref: b989.2/4‑2/5]. The House granted leave; the Bill was read a First time with Second Reading set for Friday 31 October [ref: b989.2/21].

### Renters’ Rights Bill: consideration of Lords message
Housing Minister Matthew Pennycook asked the House to agree Lords amendments creating a limited exemption for shared owners from the 12‑month “no re‑let” period under new ground 1A (subject to safeguards), and introducing a statutory requirement for annual reporting on the condition of Service Family Accommodation against the Decent Homes Standard [ref: b993.9/3; b995.1/1]. He said the Government had been “more than willing to make sensible changes in response to the legitimate concerns that have been raised,” and that the Bill was now “within touching distance” of becoming law [ref: b993.9/2; b993.5/1].

The Shadow Secretary of State (Sir James Cleverly) supported the amendments while cautioning that new costs could reduce rental supply, and he sought clarity on court reform and local enforcement [ref: b996.1/1‑1/6]. Liberal Democrats welcomed the SFA provision and broader tenant protections [ref: b998.2/1‑2/6]. The House agreed the Lords amendments without a division [ref: b1003.1/7‑1/8].

### Life Sciences Innovative Manufacturing Fund (LSIMF)
The Minister (Kanishka Narayan) sought authority for payments above £30 million to LSIMF projects, up to £520 million in total, describing life sciences as “a jewel in the crown of our economy” [ref: b1005.6/4]. He said the fund is projected to attract “nearly £4 billion in private investment, creating more than 7,000 jobs and safeguarding more than 5,000 existing ones” and announced the first two grant awards [ref: b1006.3/2; b1006.5/2].

The Opposition supported the motion (originating under the previous Government) but criticised wider policy uncertainty and called for clarity on medicines pricing (VPAG), leverage and regional spread [ref: b1007.5/1‑5/10]. The motion was agreed to without a division, authorising higher‑value awards [ref: b1016.3/3‑3/5].

### NHS Procurement, Slavery and Human Trafficking Regulations 2025
The Government introduced regulations requiring all public bodies procuring goods and services for the NHS in England to assess and address modern slavery risks throughout procurement and contract management, building on the Health and Care Act 2022, the Modern Slavery Act 2015 and the Procurement Act 2023 [ref: b1017.1/9‑1/11]. The Minister (Zubir Ahmed) emphasised the ethical imperative and the scale involved: “The NHS is one of the largest public sector procurers in the world” [ref: b1017.1/8]. The aim is to move to a single, enforceable risk‑management approach across the NHS.

The Opposition backed the intent but sought assurances on burdens, guidance, consistency, SME impact and cross‑Government co‑ordination [ref: b1019.1/1‑1/9]. The House approved the regulations without a division [ref: b1020.0/5].

### Backbench Business: Devolution in Scotland
Jamie Stone led a reflection on 25 years of the Scottish Parliament, calling for stronger scrutiny and devolution within Scotland. He recalled the Parliament as “the settled will of the Scottish people” [ref: b1022.2/7] and argued that after 2011 “the failure of some Committees to show any real teeth meant that some bad legislation came to be” [ref: b1023.1/1‑1/2]. He also warned that Scotland has become overly centralised, with too few decisions taken close to communities [ref: b1023.1/5‑1/6].

Patricia Ferguson highlighted early cross‑party achievements and Donald Dewar’s founding vision [ref: b1025.1/3‑1/5], while Members debated whether focus on constitutional questions had crowded out delivery on health, education, housing and local government. The motion that the House had considered the matter was agreed without a division [ref: b1073.0/4].

### Adjournment: Mental Health and Hoarding
Paulette Hamilton raised hoarding disorder as a recognised mental health condition affecting an estimated 2% to 5% of the population, stressing it is “not a lifestyle choice” but a “complex mental health condition” and calling for a national strategy and guidelines to replace punitive or inconsistent approaches [ref: b1075.1/2]. She argued that reliance on outdated legal powers can worsen trauma and risk, and urged a co‑ordinated multi‑agency framework [ref: b1075.1/8‑1/10].

Health Minister Zubir Ahmed said there are “no plans for a specific strategy” but outlined wider mental health reforms, including 8,500 new staff, expanded access to Talking Therapies, safeguarding duties under the Care Act 2014 (self‑neglect including hoarding), and pilots of 24/7 neighbourhood mental health centres to support holistic care [ref: b1078.0/2‑0/4; b1078.0/6; b1078.0/4‑0/5]. No division was held.
