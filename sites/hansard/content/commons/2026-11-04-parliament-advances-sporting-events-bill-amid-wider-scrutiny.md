## High-Level Summary
The sitting began with Welsh oral questions on NHS waiting lists, the autumn Budget, economic growth, defence, the Union and cross-border transport. The First Secretary of State then answered Prime Minister’s Questions on welfare and a range of domestic, international and constituency matters. The House presented two Bills, granted leave for the Fashion Supply Chain (Code and Adjudicator) Bill, and considered and passed the Sporting Events Bill [Lords], rejecting amendments on ticket resale, industrial action and single-sex protections. It also agreed carry-over extensions for the Railways Bill and the Cyber Security and Resilience (Network and Information Systems) Bill before petitions and an adjournment debate on London strategic licensing concluded proceedings.

## Detailed Summary
### Welsh affairs: NHS waiting lists and the 2026 Budget
During Welsh oral questions, Anna McMorrin said NHS waiting lists were rising under the Welsh Government and called for action; Shivani Raja asked about patients from Wales receiving treatment in England and the capacity implications for services on both sides of the border. McMorrin said Welsh Labour had previously reduced waiting lists for “10 consecutive months” and said discussions with the Welsh Government took place regularly. The transcript records no specific new cross-border arrangement.

On the autumn Budget, Llinos Medi argued that Wales faced real-terms reductions in public services because inflation had not been fully compensated. Stephen Kinnock disputed that assessment, citing an additional £450 million for Welsh public services and saying that “the Welsh Government settlement continues to grow in real terms over the spending review period”. The exchanges also covered Cardiff Parkway, the M4 relief road, rail funding, Welsh broadcasting and the Cynnal child-payment pilot. Kinnock said the Government were open to exploring the pilot and would continue engaging with the Welsh Government; no further decision is stated in the transcript.

### Wales: economic growth, defence, the Union and cross-border transport
Stephen Kinnock said the UK Government’s growth measures included “£2.6 billion to bring nuclear power back to Wylfa, £500 million to secure the future of Welsh steel, and £50 million to support defence jobs across Wales”. He also confirmed that Ruabon was among 23 stations selected to progress to the next stage of the Access for All programme. On defence, Anna McMorrin said the UK Government would be ready to act if the Welsh Government did not commit to a defence technical excellence college, while questions also covered the Deep Space Advanced Radar Capability project and employment at General Dynamics.

Questions on the Union included the Cynnal pilot and the Welsh Government’s possible approach to free school milk. On transport, Kinnock said at least £445 million was being invested in Welsh rail. In response to a question about the A5 Watling Street bridge, he invited the Member to write to him so that he could follow up with officials and the Transport Secretary: “I would be happy to follow up with officials and the Secretary of State for Transport”. The transcript records no final decision on further devolution, broadcasting or the M4 relief road.

### Prime Minister’s Questions: welfare, the economy and international affairs
Louise Haigh answered on behalf of the Prime Minister, who was accompanying His Majesty the King at the Commonwealth Heads of Government meeting in Antigua. The principal exchange with Alex Burghart concerned working-age welfare spending and whether the benefit bill would be reduced. Burghart stated that spending would be £183 billion; Haigh did not give a competing figure in the transcript but said, “the benefit bill will come down”. She cited changes to Universal Credit, increased face-to-face assessments, a right to try work for disabled people and a youth guarantee, and said the Milburn report and Timms review would report shortly.

Other questions covered a possible European Union sanitary and phytosanitary agreement and youth experience scheme, de-escalation in the Middle East, Northern Ireland’s budget and institutions, satellite capability, special educational needs and disabilities reform, rail projects, domestic-abuse legal aid, online scams, racism, local government finance, NHS estates, flooding and knife crime. Haigh offered or confirmed ministerial follow-up on several constituency matters, including Burnley station, Haughley junction, the DCVax-L treatment, St Helier Hospital and violent crime. A point of order about statements concerning Budget votes was not debated; Lindsay Hoyle said the Member had corrected the record by putting his view of the facts on the record.

### Bills presented and Fashion Supply Chain Bill
The Energy Independence Bill and the Visitor Levy (England) Bill were presented and read the First time. Both were ordered to be printed and scheduled for Second Reading on Tuesday 10 November.

Liz Twist then sought leave to introduce the Fashion Supply Chain (Code and Adjudicator) Bill. She proposed a mandatory code of practice and an adjudicator with confidential reporting channels, proactive investigations, financial penalties and powers to require retailers to reimburse suppliers. She said the regulator would be funded through a statutory levy on large fashion retailers rather than by the taxpayer. Leave was granted, the Bill was presented, and its Second Reading was scheduled for Friday 5 March 2027.

### Sporting Events Bill [Lords]: ticketing, industrial action and single-sex protections
The House considered amendments to the Sporting Events Bill [Lords]. Anna Sabine and Liz Jarvis supported measures to limit resale prices and platform fees and to prohibit surge pricing. Sabine argued that new clause 1 would implement the Government’s stated commitment because “it stops tickets being resold at above face value”. Stephanie Peacock said the Government were committed to a separate ticket-touting Bill and that “this Bill is not the place to do so”. New clause 1 was rejected on division, with 62 votes in favour and 286 against.

The Opposition also proposed restrictions on industrial action during designated events and provisions concerning single-sex competitions and facilities. Rebecca Paul said the restrictions should not be optional and described the proposed measures as protections for competitors, officials, spectators and staff. Lisa Nandy opposed the industrial-action proposals, saying that no Member had identified a major sporting event derailed by strike action, and said that “participation in all events within the scope of the Bill is already based on biological sex”. The industrial-action amendment was rejected, 91 votes to 298, and the single-sex protections amendment was rejected, 93 votes to 287.

### Sporting Events Bill [Lords]: Third Reading and outcome
Members from different parties supported the Bill’s broad purpose of creating a standing framework for major sporting events, citing economic, community and participation benefits. The Government said the framework would apply across the United Kingdom, including to Euro 2028 and certain recurring events, and referred to future sporting opportunities and a feasibility study for a possible Olympic and Paralympic bid in the north of England. Lisa Nandy said that “the benefits and rewards from hosting these events are shared across all four nations”.

The Opposition supported the Bill’s fundamental purpose but criticised the rejection of its amendments on industrial action and single-sex protections. Rebecca Paul said the Opposition would not seek a division on Third Reading. Third Reading was agreed, and the Bill was read the Third time and passed, with amendments.

### Railways Bill: carry-over extension
Simon Lightwood moved to extend the period for proceedings on the Railways Bill from 4 November 2026 to 29 April 2027 because the legislation was still being scrutinised in the House of Lords. He described the proposed Great British Railways framework as supporting passengers, disabled passengers, rail freight and partnerships with local authorities, and said, “The motion simply ensures that Parliament has sufficient time available to scrutinise the Bill”.

Richard Holden opposed the extension and criticised the Bill’s delay and its proposed effects on rail organisation, fares and open-access services. Olly Glover supported the carry-over motion while questioning the gaps in the parliamentary timetable. The motion was agreed on division, with 279 votes in favour and 139 against.

### Cyber Security and Resilience (Network and Information Systems) Bill: carry-over extension
Ian Murray moved to extend proceedings on the Cyber Security and Resilience (Network and Information Systems) Bill to 29 April 2027. He said the Bill targeted essential and critical services, including drinking water, health, transport, energy and digital services, and included a vendor risk management framework. Alex Ballinger supported the motion, citing recent cyber-attacks and the need to strengthen incident reporting and Government powers.

Victoria Collins also supported carry-over but called for consideration of local authorities, digital sovereignty, high-risk suppliers, artificial-intelligence-related threats and support for small and medium-sized enterprises. She quoted the National Cyber Security Centre’s warning that “The UK’s cyber resilience is not yet where it should be”. Murray encouraged businesses to use the Centre’s free tools and said cyber-security should be addressed in every boardroom. The carry-over motion was agreed without a division.

### Petitions: A140 junctions, Reedswood Park and Palantir
Ben Goldsborough presented a petition from residents of South Norfolk concerning safety at junctions on the A140 in Newton Flotman. He said more than 500 residents had signed it and that the petition sought “upgrading the junctions on the A140 with Flordon Road, Short Street, Old Street and Cargate Lane”. Valerie Vaz presented a petition concerning a proposed secondary school on Reedswood Park, citing green-space, environmental, transport, parking and road-safety concerns and asking that alternative sites be considered. The petition sought that “full weight is given to the environmental, transport and community impact of development at Reedswood Park”.

Zarah Sultana presented a petition about Coventry City Council’s contract with Palantir for children’s services. She referred to concerns about data handling, public-sector privatisation and the renewal of the contract, and said the petitioners wanted the council to “terminate its contract with Palantir, to consult residents and trade unions”. The petitions were presented; no Government response or further decision is stated in the transcript.

### Strategic Licensing Regulations: London
Rachel Blake questioned how the new strategic licensing arrangements would balance London-wide economic and cultural interests with local democratic control and residents’ concerns about crime, noise and public nuisance. She said the final regulations were narrower than earlier proposals and asked how many applications would be eligible for call-in, how many would be called in and how decisions would be made. She also proposed annual transparency reporting, published reasons and meaningful opportunities for resident participation. Blake stressed that “being eligible for a call-in is not the same thing as being called in”.

Jim McMahon said the mayor’s role was intended to complement, not replace, borough decision-making. He confirmed that the four statutory licensing objectives would continue to apply, that residents would retain their existing ability to make representations and that “local licensing authorities will remain the default decision makers”. He said the Government would conduct a full new-burdens assessment for local authorities and confirmed that the arrangements would be reviewed within two years and at least every five years thereafter. The House agreed the adjournment motion and then adjourned.
