## High-Level Summary
The Commons day opened with the swearing‑in of a new Member and a Speaker’s statement declaring three Select Committee Chair vacancies and setting election timetables. Foreign Office questions covered UK export licensing to Israel, West Bank settlements, Iran’s threat to shipping, China, Ukraine, climate security, EU co‑operation and the UK response to the Nepal‑China border floods. The Prime Minister’s statement set out priorities on security, migration, criminal justice, devolution via “No. 10 North”, utilities and housing, followed by a Justice statement on prison capacity, tagging, foreign national offenders and plans to end IPP sentences. The Home Secretary updated the House on the Cleveland tragedy; the Government reset the Sovereign Grant framework and introduced a Bill; MPs passed a Backbench motion urging comprehensive anti‑SLAPP legislation; and the Adjournment debate pressed for improvements on sudden cardiac death in young people, with ministerial commitments on meeting the family and expanding CPR training.

## Detailed Summary
### New Member and Speaker’s Statement
Nigel Paul Farage (Clacton) took and subscribed the Oath: “Nigel Paul Farage, for Clacton.”. The Speaker announced resignations of three Select Committee Chairs and declared the posts vacant: “I therefore declare those Chairs vacant.”. He set the timetable: nominations close “at 2.30 pm on Monday 7 September… If there is more than one candidate… the ballot will take place on Monday 14 September between 3 pm and 6 pm”, and reminded Members of new campaigning rules endorsed on 28 April 2026.

### Foreign Office Questions: Export Licensing to Israel and West Bank Settlements
Ministers stressed controls on exports and ongoing review: “We categorically do not export any bombs or ammunition for use in military operations in Gaza. We suspended 29 licences in September 2024, and approximately 50 licences have been refused since then.”. They reiterated, “we keep all our licences under review.”. On illegal settlements, the Foreign Secretary said, “Israeli settlements are illegal under international law… This Government will not acquiesce in the destruction of the two-state solution—we will act. I will set out a comprehensive set of measures in the coming weeks.”. He said the Government was working on “measures to ban the trading of goods with illegal settlements”.

### Foreign Office Questions: Chagos/Diego Garcia and Falklands
On the UK‑Mauritius treaty and Diego Garcia, Ministers said base security is a top priority and any funding would be within existing departmental settlements: “Maintaining the long-term operational control and security of the base remains a top priority… A financial element was a crucial part of that deal.”; “Funding for the treaty will be provided within the existing spending review settlements for the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office and the Ministry of Defence.”. The UK reaffirmed Falklands sovereignty and Islanders’ self‑determination.

### Foreign Office Questions: Ukraine
The Foreign Secretary underlined long‑term support: “stand shoulder to shoulder with the people of Ukraine against Russian aggression”, emphasising urgency of deliveries: “this winter is crucial… we must do everything we can… in a speedy way.”. He said the Government would work on Russian disinformation, including Meta issues, and affirmed the UK would not be intimidated by threats.

### Foreign Office Questions: Strait of Hormuz and Iran
The UK pushed for a diplomatic reopening and restated sanctions pressure: “The strait of Hormuz should be open to safe, secure and unimpeded transit”; “We have already imposed 240 sanctions on Iran” and keep measures under review.

### Foreign Office Questions: China and Disinformation
Ministers said China poses multiple threats: “China poses a series of threats to UK national security”. On reported incidents at the Chinese embassy, the Minister said there is “a live investigation by the Metropolitan police” and Members should await its outcome. The Government also highlighted counter‑disinformation efforts, including BBC World Service funding.

### Foreign Office Questions: Detention of UK Nationals Abroad
The Minister highlighted “the appointment of the first dedicated envoy for complex detentions, our former colleague the right honourable Alistair Burt”. Updates included sustained advocacy in Jagtar Singh Johal’s case, support for clemency applications for Ryan Cornelius and Charles Ridley, and condemnation of the “politically motivated prosecution of Jimmy Lai” with calls for his release.

### Foreign Office Questions: Climate Security, EU Co‑operation, Nepal Floods
Ministers linked climate impacts to national security and pledged UN‑level leadership, outlined international climate and nature partnerships and the Foreign Secretary’s role as UK governor to the World Bank on development and climate finance. On EU co‑operation, Ministers cited the new security and defence partnership and joining the EU’s Ukraine loan initiative. On the Nepal‑China border floods, the Foreign Secretary reported “987 confirmed deaths and 3,916 people still missing, of whom 36 are British nationals” and £5 million in UK humanitarian assistance.

### Prime Minister’s Statement: Direction of Government
The Prime Minister reaffirmed support for Ukraine: “We do not seek escalation, but instead will stand firm in providing what help we can to enable Ukraine to defend itself from this illegal invasion.”. He reported small‑boat arrivals had fallen: “numbers arriving over the summer halved on last year, making this year, to date, the lowest for crossings since 2021.”. On criminal justice, he confirmed early‑release exemptions extended to serious offences, including “manslaughter, death by dangerous driving or indecent assault”. Domestically, he pledged “the biggest redistribution of power this country has seen” with No. 10 North “to oversee the rewiring of the British state”, stronger public control of utilities, and “the biggest council home building programme since the post-war period”. He said, “I cut tax twice in my first week” and claimed “significant progress has indeed been made” on small boats.

### Justice Statement: Prison Capacity, Tagging, FNOs and IPP
The Justice Secretary said prisons had been close to running out of space: “They were regularly at 99% capacity… We were one bad day away from running out of space”. He outlined the earned progression model and expanded controls: “Every single offender leaving prison can now be tagged” with curfews, exclusion zones and “world‑first ‘restriction zones’” for the most serious offenders. Early‑release exclusions were widened to “all unlawful killing offences… as well as indecent assault”. He announced “more than £110 million” to create headroom, said that next year the UK would “begin removing foreign national offenders serving determinate sentences immediately after sentencing”, and pledged legislation to end IPP sentences “by the end of this Parliament”. The Shadow highlighted risks from releasing dangerous offenders and sought timelines and data. In reply, the Justice Secretary cited “374 places” added since the election and a programme for “14,000 places”, with legislation on IPP reform and capacity acceleration to follow.

### Statement: Policing in Cleveland
The Home Secretary paid tribute to PCs Matthew Blades and Tom Clough, noted that “27 arrests have been made” in related investigations, and confirmed mutual aid and “an additional £2 million” for Cleveland Police, alongside a wider review of the police funding formula. She condemned “lawlessness of any kind” and said offenders would face the law.

### Ways and Means: Sovereign Grant; and Sovereign Grant Bill Presented
The Minister said the royal trustees recommended that, with the Buckingham Palace reservicing nearing completion, “the overall level of funding provided through the sovereign grant should reduce from next year from £137.9 million to £99.9 million in 2027-28”. The Shadow welcomed a mechanism to reduce the grant year‑to‑year and noted the reference percentage would rise to 20.5% to calibrate to expected costs. The House ordered that a Bill be brought in; the Sovereign Grant Bill was presented with Second Reading set “tomorrow”.

### Backbench Business: Strategic Lawsuits Against Public Participation (SLAPPs)
The House debated the growing use of SLAPPs to chill public‑interest speech, with a motion that “notes the critical threat that Strategic Lawsuits Against Public Participation (SLAPPs) pose to press freedom… [and] the urgent need for the Government to find a legislative vehicle for simple, comprehensive anti‑SLAPP provisions”. Sir John Whittingdale confirmed he would introduce a private Member’s Bill in November. The Minister said “freedom of expression and investigative journalism must never be undermined by abusive litigation” and that the Government would work with sponsors in both Houses. The motion was agreed to without division.

### Adjournment Debate: Sudden Cardiac Death in Young People
An MP raised the case of 17‑year‑old Adam Ankers, who collapsed during football and later died, pressing for improvements to emergency call‑handling, CPR, NHS Pathways, genetic information‑sharing and screening. He said “no one recognised that Adam was in sudden cardiac arrest. No cardiopulmonary resuscitation was started.”. He reported the coroner found about eight minutes without oxygen to the brain before CPR began. The Minister offered to meet the family—“would of course be happy to meet with… Adam’s parents”—outlined recent call‑script changes, confirmed the Care Quality Commission assesses call‑handling, and announced that from April 2027 “all NHS staff, including those in A&E” will receive CPR training.
