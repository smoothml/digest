## High-Level Summary
The sitting covered departmental questions on culture, sport, heritage, local journalism, online safety, volunteering and connectivity, followed by statements on military installation security, Gaza, obesity and defence in the High North. Ministers announced or confirmed measures including £5 million of additional UNICEF funding for Gaza’s winter response, £470 million over four years for military-site security and a dedicated fund for lidos within a public leisure facilities programme. Backbench debates considered the 110th anniversary of the Battle of the Somme and the Nolan principles; both motions were agreed to. The sitting also included parliamentary business announcements, a planning Bill and petitions before concluding with a debate on animal cruelty and domestic abuse, in which the Government outlined existing disclosure arrangements and further work planned for 2027.

## Detailed Summary
### Digital, Culture, Media and Sport oral questions
Members questioned Lisa Nandy, Ian Murray, Stephanie Peacock and Vicky Foxcroft on participation in sport, regional cultural funding, girls’ grassroots facilities, heritage, local journalism, volunteering, online safety, antisemitism, creative-industry access and rural connectivity. On physical activity, Nandy said that “this has to be a whole-of-Government endeavour”. Ministers referred to investment in grassroots facilities, cultural infrastructure, local journalism and volunteering, and to proposed online-safety measures including a consultation on social-media access for under-16s and work on smart-glasses risks.

The exchanges also covered the Independent Football Regulator, women’s representation in football, the proposed Swindon theatre, local museums and cultural venues, mobile notspots, working-class pathways into the creative industries and protections against misconduct in media, entertainment and sport. Nandy said that she would meet representatives from those sectors and that “if those protections are insufficient, the Government will not hesitate to take further action”. Ministers offered meetings or official follow-up on a number of constituency matters. The Milburn review on young people not in education, employment or training was expected the following week; the Minister for Media said that the media White Paper would be published soon and that he would not pre-empt it.

### Protection of United Kingdom military installations
In response to Mark Francois’s urgent question, Luke Pollard described the protection of military sites as layered and intelligence-led, involving physical protection, access controls, surveillance, policing, aerial monitoring and counter-drone capabilities. Following incidents at RAF Fairford and RAF Molesworth, he said that protection had been strengthened and that “there have been no further reported breaches since those incidents”. The Government identified £470 million over four years for security technology and infrastructure, more than £750 million for short-range counter-drone protection, and additional powers in the Armed Forces Bill.

Members asked about United States aircraft, an espionage arrest at Imjin Barracks, drone incursions, climate-related risks and the defence readiness Bill. Pollard confirmed that a member of the US Army had been arrested under sections 1 and 3 of the National Security Act 2023 for assisting a foreign intelligence service in connection with Russia, but said that the investigation was live. He said that the base-security review was ongoing and that the Government were preparing the defence readiness Bill for later in the Parliament. No decision was recorded.

### Business of the House and other parliamentary business
Alan Campbell announced business for the weeks beginning 16 and 23 November, including the Second Reading of the Visitor Levy (England) Bill and Finance Bill, remaining stages of the Immigration and Asylum Bill, consideration of Lords amendments to the Armed Forces Bill and the Cyber Security and Resilience (Network and Information Systems) Bill, Opposition days, and general debates on International Men’s Day, advanced artificial intelligence, inequalities in cancer care and lifelong sex education. Bob Blackman also listed forthcoming Westminster Hall debates and asked for protected time for the advanced-AI debate; Campbell said he would consider how best to protect the time.

The questions that followed covered veterans’ protections under the Northern Ireland Troubles Bill, constituency-office connectivity, rail reliability, miners’ pensions and requests for ministerial meetings or debates. Campbell said that amendments to the Troubles Bill provided protection from repeated investigations, and undertook to obtain a Treasury update on miners’ pensions. A Bill presented by Ben Maguire would require planning permission in England to change a primary residential home to a short-term let or secondary residential home; it was read the First time and ordered for Second Reading on 26 February 2027. Petitions were then presented on housing in Cornwall, illegal e-bikes and motorbikes, and NHS dentistry.

### Humanitarian situation in Gaza and the wider region
Stephen Doughty reported severe humanitarian conditions in Gaza after flooding, including damage to displacement sites, hospitals and schools. He said that tents, tarpaulins, timber and other equipment were outside Gaza awaiting entry, and called on the Israeli authorities to open crossings, reduce dual-use restrictions and allow humanitarian access, with the direct message: “let the aid in”. He announced a further £5 million for UNICEF’s winter response, in addition to existing UK support, including provision of winter clothing, tarpaulins, family tents and blankets. He also reported £104 million of humanitarian support for Yemen and work on sanctions concerning illegal settlement activity and obstruction of humanitarian aid.

Members expressed differing views about the responsibilities of Hamas, Israel and other regional actors, and asked about sanctions, arms exports, accountability for attacks on healthcare facilities and further diplomatic action. Doughty said that Hamas must disarm and have no role in Gaza’s future, while also stating that Israel had legal and moral responsibilities as the occupying power. He declined to identify future sanctions designations but said that “we do not take powers without intending to use them”. The Government’s stated next steps were diplomatic engagement with Israel and international partners, further work on a settlement-trade-ban regime—which the Foreign Secretary had said would take six to nine months—continued review of arms exports and additional humanitarian assistance.

### Health and Social Care Committee statement: tackling obesity
Layla Moran presented the Health and Social Care Committee’s report on an obesity “moonshot”, describing obesity as “the relapsing, remitting condition that is obesity” and arguing that it should not be treated as a personal failing. She criticised the absence of measurable targets in the Government’s plan. The Committee recommended prevention measures including implementation of the 2018 nutrient-profiling model, healthy-sales reporting, front-of-pack labelling, restrictions on advertising foods high in fat, sugar and salt, and planning measures concerning fast-food outlets.

On treatment, Moran highlighted limited access to GLP-1 medicines and bariatric surgery and called for a modern service framework, a simplified person-centred pathway and improved professional training. The Committee also recommended a cross-Cabinet committee chaired by the Prime Minister because responsibility is spread across departments and local government. Members supported a joined-up approach, attention to health inequalities and the use of lived experience. No Government decision was recorded during the statement.

### Defence Committee statement: defence in the High North
Tan Dhesi presented the Defence Committee’s report on the High North, describing it as a strategically important and increasingly contested region. He said that “Russia is seeking to dominate and exploit the High North”, and identified capability gaps including 11 frigates and destroyers in service against a stated requirement of 19, and limited towed-array sonar capability for anti-submarine warfare. The Committee called for no further withdrawal of towed-array-equipped Type 23 frigates until they were replaced by Type 26 vessels, and for under-ice submarine capability to be re-established.

Members discussed defence spending, submarine availability, Arctic training, the Joint Expeditionary Force, Scotland and NATO. Dhesi said that Royal Marines’ Arctic expertise could not compensate for shortages in naval platforms and that the UK needed to invest “at pace”. The report also called for the Joint Expeditionary Force’s contribution to European security to be reassessed and for consideration of a greater focus on the High North. No decision was recorded.

### Backbench Business: 110th anniversary of the Battle of the Somme
Rebecca Smith opened a debate marking the 110th anniversary of the Battle of the Somme, focusing on its scale, local impact and the work of the Commonwealth War Graves Commission. Members described losses from constituencies across the United Kingdom and highlighted the contributions of Commonwealth troops, Irish and Ulster divisions, Pals battalions, miners, medics, nurses and women working in munitions. Smith said that “behind every one of these enormous figures is a name, and behind every name is a story”. Members also debated artillery tactics, the absence of surprise and the development of combined-arms warfare, with some cautioning against reducing the history to the “lions led by donkeys” narrative.

The debate stressed education, commemoration and the preservation of individual stories, including those of Indian soldiers and local service personnel. Members linked remembrance to support for veterans and to the prevention of future conflict. The motion was agreed to: “That this House has considered the 110th anniversary of the battle of the Somme”.

### Backbench Business: Nolan principles
Susan Murray introduced a debate on the seven Nolan principles—selflessness, integrity, objectivity, accountability, openness, honesty and leadership—and linked them to public trust. She used cases involving school records and safeguarding to argue that public bodies should maintain transparent records of decisions and amendments, and called for stronger Electoral Commission powers and a statutory, more independent role for the adviser on ministerial standards. Other Members discussed second jobs, local-government accountability, lobbying transparency, political donations, misinformation and the need to address culture as well as rules.

Bernard Jenkin argued that regulation should support Members in handling conflicts of interest rather than becoming an end in itself, proposing a clearer separation between advice and adjudication. Joe Powell and Phil Brickell called for more comprehensive lobbying disclosure. The Government said that the Ethics and Integrity Commission’s recommendations were under consideration, supported the Standards Committee’s work on second jobs and committed to reforming the local-government standards framework. The Minister said that the Government would present their response to the Commission’s report “in the not-too-distant future”. The motion was agreed to.

### Animal cruelty and domestic abuse
John Hayes argued for “Holly’s law”, proposed after the murder of his constituent Holly by her husband, who had previously abused and killed pets. Hayes said that animal cruelty can form part of coercive control and domestic abuse, and called for more consistent police information-sharing, potentially including a disclosure scheme or register. He argued that relevant information should include incidents as well as convictions.

Jenny Riddell-Carpenter said that convictions under the Animal Welfare Act 2006 are recorded on the Police National Computer and that existing disclosure mechanisms allow police to share relevant information for safeguarding, licensing and public protection. She said that the Government were reviewing how the domestic violence disclosure scheme operates across police forces, working with the Royal Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals and other organisations, and considering stronger statutory guidance. Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs research is due to report in 2027, while related Home Office work was expected in early 2027. The adjournment motion was agreed to and the House adjourned.
