## High-Level Summary
The Commons focused on electoral modernisation and constitutional change. The First Secretary of State outlined plans to "rewire" government, expand devolution, overhaul procurement, and reviewed the summer wildfire response. The Representation of the People Bill, including a ban on cryptoasset donations and tighter political finance rules, completed Commons stages, with several Opposition new clauses defeated. Wales Questions and PMQs covered Union investment, wildfires, cross‑border services, fiscal rules, defence, and UK‑EU relations. The adjournment debate examined defects delaying the opening of the M49 Severnside junction.

## Detailed Summary
### Business Before Questions: Committee of Selection
The House agreed without division to changes in membership of the Committee of Selection: "That Deirdre Costigan and Lilian Greenwood be discharged from the Committee of Selection and Jade Botterill and Emma Foody be added.".

### Oral Answers to Questions: Wales
Opening, the Secretary of State said, "A stronger Wales in a stronger United Kingdom" will guide his approach, highlighting UK Government support for steel, Wylfa and growth funds. On wildfires, he confirmed a MACA request was "authorised by the Ministry of Defence" and paid tribute to responders. On connectivity, he noted that Loganair’s routing is a commercial matter but offered to engage on proposals, backed conditional support for the WSMR open‑access application provided it did not compromise network performance, and supported progressing Cardiff Parkway, which "could unlock up to 6,000 jobs". On devolution and policing, he said, "Our priority will always be ensuring the safety and security of the people of Wales. What matters is what works.". On housing costs, Ministers pointed to removing VAT from energy bills to ease pressures from 1 October, and, on homelessness, a programme to "give everyone a route off the streets by Christmas" with additional funding and a summit. On child poverty, the Government cited ending the two‑child limit and a target to lift 550,000 children out of poverty UK‑wide by 2030. For farming, Ministers highlighted raising relief thresholds to £2.5 million for family farms and praised emergency services’ wildfire efforts. No divisions were held; issues are to be pursued with the Welsh Government and agencies.

### Prime Minister’s Questions
The Prime Minister cited Franco‑British cooperation and recent cost‑of‑living measures, saying Government actions included removing VAT from electricity bills and "a cut in business rates for pubs, social clubs and live music venues". He restated fiscal rules—"No borrowing to fund day‑to‑day spending, debt falling as a percentage of GDP by the end of the Parliament"—and confirmed defence commitments, with a funded plan and a pathway to meet NATO aims by 2035. He announced a wildfire summit, noting capability gaps: "Our fire and rescue services are not set up properly for the changing climate". On Europe, he wanted a closer UK‑EU relationship short of rejoining the single market, prioritising steel and farming in an upcoming summit: "It is imperative… that we work hard to get a good deal for British steel and for British farming". Other answers included looking at North Devon’s maternity services, ambitions for mass‑transit in big cities, and balancing green belt protection with a brownfield‑first approach.

### Ministerial Statement: Rewiring the State (First Secretary of State)
Louise Haigh set out the creation of the Office of the Prime Minister and Cabinet and "rewiring the machinery of government" to strengthen the centre and devolve power, including "the establishment of No. 10 North" and a national devolution blueprint. Procurement reforms will focus £90 billion of central contracts on UK jobs and skills, with the defence exemption from social value removed. On wildfires, she reported "47 wildfire incidents" and "close to 12,000 999 calls… in a single day" and announced a £100m uplift for fire services and £65m support for farmers. She confirmed a Cabinet‑level Minister for AI and stronger intergovernmental relations roles. In questions, she said No. 10 North is funded from existing budgets, the central civil service is expected to shrink with capability built locally, and a wildfire summit will be convened. She said "more public control" of the water industry is "absolutely necessary" with reforms to follow, committed to restore service levels for civil service pensions and to use "all our commercial levers" with Capita, and trailed a National Economic Council meeting with devolved leaders on devolution. No votes were taken; multiple updates were promised.

### Representation of the People Bill: Consideration and Third Reading
The Minister said cryptoasset transactions create "a new and clear route for untraceable money" and introduced a prohibition on party donations in cryptoassets, with retrospective effect from 25 March 2026 (Government NC72; transitional provisions also adopted). Government NC116 imposed an annual £100,000 cap on donations and regulated transactions from overseas contributors, with the threshold set "at the lowest recommended" level and supporting declarations and transitional rules. Company and LLP donations moved to a profit‑based permissibility test, with associated aggregation and reporting changes agreed via Government amendments. The Electoral Commission was given enhanced information‑sharing powers and the ability to prescribe standardised return formats (NC78; NC114). Postal and proxy‑postal voting durations were extended from three to five years. Opposition new clauses for an overseas electors participation review (NC65) and a ban on foreign‑language campaign material (NC67) were defeated (Ayes 166, Noes 346; Ayes 105, Noes 410). The Bill passed Third Reading (Ayes 411, Noes 102). The Secretary of State said the Bill "strengthens our elections" and tightens political finance. The Bill now proceeds to the Lords.

### Other Business: Programme Motion, Bill Presented, Delegated Legislation and Petitions
The House agreed a programme variation for the Representation of the People Bill so that consideration would conclude five hours after debate commenced and Third Reading six hours after commencement. A Private Member’s Bill to repeal the Climate Change Act 2008 was presented and read the First time, with Second Reading due on 26 February 2027. Several statutory instruments were approved on civil registration, energy prices, international development capital subscriptions and armed forces complaints without division. Petitions were presented on the Stoke‑on‑Trent to Manchester Airport rail link, local government reorganisation in Devon, a similar rail link from Newcastle‑under‑Lyme, and food security and cosmetically imperfect produce.

### Adjournment Debate: National Highways—M49 Severnside Junction
Claire Young (Thornbury and Yate) highlighted that, despite completion in 2020, the M49 junction remains unopened and unconnected, with newly discovered defects requiring remedial works. The Minister said defects "require further assessment and remedial work before the junction can be safely opened to traffic", noted Government support "including £10 million of Government funding" for the local link road, and asked National Highways to provide monthly stakeholder updates. He added that a "significant quality incident investigation" is under way to establish lessons learned. No timetable was set; dates will follow conclusion of technical and commercial discussions.
