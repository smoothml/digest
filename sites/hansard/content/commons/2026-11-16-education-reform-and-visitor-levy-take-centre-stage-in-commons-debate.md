## High-Level Summary
## High-Level Summary
The day’s proceedings included extensive Education questions on technical and vocational pathways, the Ukrainian GCSE, special educational needs and disabilities (SEND), youth opportunities, school transport, online safety and school funding. An urgent question examined planning permission and feasibility work for asylum accommodation at MOD Bicester, with the Government defending the planning process and the move away from asylum hotels while Members raised local concerns. Statements covered the Microsoft 365 outage and a new procurement process for the NHS federated data platform, including questions about resilience, supplier concentration and Palantir. The Visitor Levy (England) Bill passed Second Reading by 296 votes to 96 and was committed to a Public Bill Committee; the day concluded with an adjournment debate on support for victims of high-control religious groups.

## Detailed Summary
### Education questions: technical and vocational pathways, the Ukrainian GCSE and SEND reform
Lucy Powell said the Government intended to expand technical and vocational routes alongside academic education, including vocational GCSEs, V-levels, T-levels and A-levels. She described the objective as providing “more routes to post-16 education”. Members discussed University Technical Colleges, youth hubs, employer-led training, apprenticeship provision, funding for smaller schools and the timing of course information. Powell said the first V-levels were planned for September 2027, with course details already available, and that further V-levels would follow in 2028. In response to concerns that choices could be narrowed at 14, she said the reforms were “expanding choices”. When asked about implementation costs, specialist teachers and measures of success, she said that “there will be more information very soon”.

Georgia Gould confirmed that the Ukrainian GCSE would be introduced with AQA, with first teaching planned for September 2028 and assessments from summer 2029. She offered discussions with the Scottish and Northern Irish Governments and with relevant community groups. On SEND, Gould reported £4 billion of investment over this year and the next two years, £3.7 billion of high-needs capital funding to create 60,000 specialist places, and plans to publish the consultation response and bring forward legislation early in the new year. Members raised accountability, NHS involvement, joint commissioning, attendance penalties and local capacity. The Minister said local authorities and integrated care boards were preparing reform plans and that the Government would “set out more on that in due course” regarding stronger joint commissioning.

### Education questions: youth opportunities, transport, online safety and school funding
On young people not in education, employment or training, Lucy Powell referred to “almost 1 million young people not in employment, education or training”. She said the Government would work on data-sharing, transition points and accountability. Questions also covered the effect of attendance, SEND and mental health on participation, rural transport, the Mission Coastal programme and the national funding formula for Suffolk. Josh MacAlister said Mission Coastal would bring local partners together to address persistent absence; he reported that 115,000 fewer children had been persistently absent since the Government took office.

Paul Waugh said the Government were working with the Department for Transport on school-minibus driver licensing and offered to meet Sarah Edwards to update her. On online safety, he referred to measures including raising the social-media access age to 16, restricting contact from unknown adults, screen-use guidance and restrictions on mobile phones in classrooms. In response to questions about artificial-intelligence chatbots, he said that existing guidance stated that they “should not be used in schools”.

### Urgent question: MOD Bicester asylum accommodation
Anna Turley said that planning permission for MOD Bicester site A had been granted by the Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government on 12 November through the urgent Crown development procedure. She said that feasibility assessments were exploratory and did not predetermine future use, adding: “No final decision will be taken until all necessary arrangements, assessments and approvals are in place and have been properly considered.” The Government’s stated rationale was to reduce reliance on asylum hotels; the Minister reported that fewer than 160 hotels were operating, compared with a peak of more than 400 in 2023, and that 44 hotels had closed during the year. She also said engagement with local authorities, police, health partners and other stakeholders would continue.

Calum Miller and other Members raised the 3,177 objections and one response in support recorded during the consultation, as well as concerns about infrastructure, policing, transport, community cohesion, site condition and cost. Turley said that the final cost could not yet be stated and compared existing large-site costs of £135 and £138 per night with hotel costs of £199. Members also discussed crime, detention, the Rwanda policy and other possible sites. In response to questions about Linton-on-Ouse, Turley clarified: “There has been no decision on Linton-on-Ouse; that is under consideration.” She said local schools, amenities and play areas would be considered, and that commercial sensitivities could limit publication of some material while the work continued.

### Statements: Microsoft 365 outage and NHS digital capability
Ian Murray reported that the Microsoft 365 outage on 12 and 13 November had disrupted email and other services worldwide, but said that 999, accident and emergency departments, transport, borders and major telecommunications remained operational. He said there was no indication of malicious activity and that Microsoft was expected to share lessons learned. The Government’s Cyber Security and Resilience (Network and Information Systems) Bill was described as strengthening requirements for cloud providers and bringing data centres and managed service providers into scope. Members questioned NHS disruption, supplier concentration, contingency planning, open-source alternatives and analogue back-up systems. Murray said that “More than 90% of the civil service uses some kind of Microsoft product” and that a comprehensive audit of Government resilience had been undertaken. The Bill was expected to return to the House before the end of the year.

James Frith announced a new procurement process for the NHS federated data platform. Engagement with patients, partners and the market was due to begin in January, formal procurement was planned for the second half of the following year, and the full process was expected to take around 18 months. The current contract would continue for two years and end in March 2029, while the next arrangement would emphasise interoperability, common standards, supplier diversity and public trust. Members raised concerns about Palantir, performance data, transparency and data sovereignty. Frith said the process would be open to UK and international suppliers, that the product, intellectual property and data were NHS-owned, and that “we are not locked in”. He also confirmed that exit and transition provisions existed and that the data would be transferred.

### Visitor Levy (England) Bill: Second Reading and programme
Angela Rayner presented the Bill as a devolved power allowing strategic authorities to introduce an overnight visitor levy, calculated as a percentage of accommodation costs, after local consultation and the publication of a prospectus and impact assessment. She said that authorities would decide whether to introduce the levy and how to use the receipts. The Government stated that “the levy will not replace Government grants” and that authorities would report annually on revenue, expenditure and future spending plans. Sally Jameson said that strategic authorities could exempt categories such as camping pitches, educational trips and groups such as scouts, and that criminal sanctions would be reserved for deliberate fraudulent evasion rather than honest mistakes.

Opponents argued that the levy could increase the cost of domestic holidays, business travel and medical stays, add administrative burdens for smaller providers and reduce tourism and employment. They called for a statutory cap, ringfencing of receipts for tourism, protections for children, carers and scout camps, and a national impact assessment. Supporters argued that visitors place additional demands on local infrastructure and that local authorities should have the power to respond; Peter Lamb said the levy could help meet “the community’s infrastructure needs”. The Liberal Democrats supported devolution in principle but tabled a reasoned amendment “declining to give the Bill a Second Reading”. The House divided with 296 Ayes and 96 Noes, agreed to Second Reading and committed the Bill to a Public Bill Committee. Committee proceedings were scheduled to conclude on 10 December 2026.

### Adjournment debate: high-control religious groups and support for victims
Sam Carling raised alleged abuse in cults and high-control religious groups, including coercive control, financial exploitation, mandated shunning, alleged forced labour and lack of secular education. He distinguished these concerns from ordinary religious teaching and asked the Government to consider extending coercive-control law beyond family or intimate relationships, supporting specialist counselling, responding to the Bloom review and assigning cross-Government responsibility. Describing the current legal position, he said: “coercive control can take place only in a family or in a romantic relationship”. Other Members supported stronger protection while stressing freedom of religion and the need to target harmful coercion rather than religious belief.

Alex Davies-Jones said victims and survivors should be recognised and supported. She referred to more than £550 million for victim and witness support services over three years, modern-slavery support and child-trafficking guardians. She undertook that the safeguarding Minister would meet Carling and the Family Survival Trust to discuss possible legal reform, including the concerns about coercive control outside domestic or intimate relationships. She also said that the draft conversion-practices Bill was intended to criminalise harmful practices while undergoing pre-legislative scrutiny. The question was agreed to and the House adjourned.
