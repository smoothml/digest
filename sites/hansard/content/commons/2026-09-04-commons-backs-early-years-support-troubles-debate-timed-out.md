## High-Level Summary
The House rejected a motion to sit in private by 79 votes to 1 before devoting most of the sitting to the Second Reading of the Infants, Parents and Carers Bill, which would place early‑years support on a statutory footing. The Bill received Government support, passed its Second Reading and was committed to a Public Bill Committee. A Second Reading debate on the Northern Ireland Troubles (Criminal Investigations etc) Bill began but was adjourned due to lack of time, to be resumed on a later date. Several other Private Members’ Bills were taken without debate—one passed Second Reading while others were objected to and rescheduled. The day concluded with an Adjournment debate on social care and mental health services in Hornsey and Friern Barnet, focusing on delayed discharges, housing and service coordination.

## Detailed Summary
### Motion: House to sit in private
Ruth Jones moved that the House sit in private. The Question was put forthwith under Standing Order No. 163 and, after a short division, the House rejected the motion: “The House divided: Ayes 1, Noes 79.”. The Chair announced, “Question accordingly negatived.”.

### Infants, Parents and Carers Bill: Second Reading
Sir Desmond Swayne introduced the Bill, explaining that it aims to give statutory underpinning to infant and early‑years support so it is better understood and more accountable: “The purpose of the Bill is to shine the legislative spotlight on those services and to ensure that they are systematically understood and are known about and scrutinised by Parliament.”. He outlined duties on the Secretary of State: to define services via regulations (clause 1) — “Clause 1 lays a duty on the Secretary of State to make regulations to define these services” — to assess need with parental input (clause 2), to make provision for needed services (clause 3) — “Clause 3 lays a duty on the Secretary of State to then make provision for the services that she has assessed are needed.” — and to report annually to Parliament (clause 4).

Members across the House stressed the evidence for the first 1,001 days and the importance of universal, non‑stigmatising, face‑to‑face help. Helen Hayes highlighted “the importance of these services being available on a universal basis”. Sharon Hodgson argued for legislative protection so support cannot be dismantled, saying, “We need legislative protection so that history can never repeat itself.”. Summing up, the Minister stated, “the Government are supporting the Bill this afternoon.”. Outcome: “Bill accordingly read a Second time; to stand committed to a Public Bill Committee.”.

### Northern Ireland Troubles (Criminal Investigations etc) Bill: Second Reading (debate adjourned)
Lincoln Jopp introduced this Private Member’s Bill seeking to limit repeat investigations into historic Troubles‑era cases involving veterans: “there could be no further investigations, inquiries, inquests or prosecutions of Northern Ireland veterans unless and until there was new compelling evidence in the view of a Supreme Court judge.”. He also criticised the scheduling, noting veterans in the Gallery had expected time “to hear a debate in support of our veterans.”. Mark Francois said the Bill aimed “to try to defend those who defended us.”.

The debate could not be completed within the time available: “The debate stood adjourned (Standing Order No. 11(2)).”. Next steps: “Ordered, That the debate be resumed on Friday 11 September.”.

### Business without Debate: Private Members’ Bills
Several Bills were progressed formally. The Child‑like Sexual Abuse Dolls (Offences) Bill advanced: “Bill read a Second time; to stand committed to a Public Bill Committee.”. Three further Bills met objections and were rescheduled: the Multi‑Storey Car Parks (Safety) Bill (“Object.” … “Bill to be read a Second time on Friday 11 September.”); the Ponds (Permitted Development) Bill (“Object.” … “Friday 16 October.”); and the Sale and Supply of Glue Traps (Prohibition) Bill (“Object.” … “Friday 11 September.”).

### Adjournment: Social care and mental health services in Hornsey and Friern Barnet
Catherine West raised pressures at the interface of social care, housing and mental health, citing delayed discharges and lack of suitable accommodation. She reported that from January to July 2026 “a total of 846 occupied bed days were unavailable” due to discharge delays, and “In July alone, the trust had an average of 10 in-patients… clinically ready to leave but could not be discharged”. She called for better coordination and earlier intervention and requested ministerial engagement and visits to local services.

Replying, the Minister said the Social Care Minister “is happy to meet”, noted Haringey’s CQC rating as “requires improvement”, and set out national measures: “making £473 million of capital funding available… including £343 million to establish 100 community-based mental health centres and 59 mental health emergency departments”, adding “there is already a unit at Highgate mental health centre”. She highlighted support through the Better Care Fund of “over £9 billion in ’26-27” and “more than £4.6 billion of additional funding for adult social care in 2028-29”. The House then adjourned.
