## High-Level Summary
The day included Women and Equalities questions on child poverty, period poverty, trans people’s access to services, disability, violence against women and girls, and related topical matters. Prime Minister’s Questions covered Stormont, the Government’s proposed 10-year plan, energy and defence, welfare, Gaza, public services and regional issues. Opposition day debates considered working-age welfare and employer National Insurance contributions; the welfare motion was replaced by the Government amendment after divisions, while the employers motion was rejected without an amendment. The House also presented two Bills, approved appointments and committee changes, and held an adjournment debate on Charity Commission powers and resources.

## Detailed Summary
### Women and Equalities questions
Bridget Phillipson said that the Government’s child-poverty measures included removing the two-child limit, expanding free school meals and rolling out Best Start family hubs. She said the child poverty strategy was expected to lift more than half a million children out of poverty in 2029-30: "Measures in the child poverty strategy are expected to lift more than half a million children out of poverty in 2029-30." Michelle Welsh discussed connecting Sure Start services with maternity services, while Chris Murray raised child poverty and homelessness in Scotland and asked about discussions with Scottish Ministers. Rebecca Smith questioned the replacement of the independent Social Mobility Commission with a Government-run class unit; Phillipson said class disadvantage should be addressed across Government: "I do not believe that that should be outsourced."

On period poverty, Phillipson referred to measures to make work pay, support women at work and provide period products in schools and health settings. She agreed to meet Jonathan Brash and Lyndsay Hogg, and said she would respond to Christine Jardine on the additional cost of essential products. Simon Lightwood said the Equality and Human Rights Commission’s code continued to protect trans people and that providers should consider alternatives so that trans people were not left without facilities: "The code is clear that trans people should not be left without facilities to use." Stephen Timms described free off-peak bus travel for eligible older and disabled people and said disabled passholders would be able to travel free at any time of day across England from 1 April. He said he would reflect on making companion travel mandatory. Satvir Kaur referred to the Government’s target of halving violence against women and girls in a decade, specialist policing and courts, domestic abuse protection orders and a misogyny taskforce. She said the Employment Rights Act 2025 would prevent employers using non-disclosure agreements to silence workers about workplace harassment or discrimination, and agreed to meet Members on related issues. Ministers also agreed to meetings on sexual-assault referral services on the Isle of Wight and cross-party work concerning allegations of abuse and attempts to silence victims; Timms said he was in discussions on pavement parking and hoped to make progress.

### Women and Equalities topical questions
Phillipson said the misogyny taskforce would address misogyny in workplaces, communities and online and was backed by £15 million, which the Government were seeking to increase with businesses and philanthropists. She said the Government would respond shortly to the Women and Equalities Committee’s report on homelessness among black people. On socioeconomic disadvantage, she said the Government intended to enact the socioeconomic duty. On conversion practices, she said a draft Bill had been published for pre-legislative scrutiny and that the Government would consider the Joint Committee’s recommendations, with the aim of delivering a comprehensive, trans-inclusive ban: "I look forward to the Joint Committee’s recommendations and to working across the House to finally deliver that long-overdue ban."

Other questions concerned community partnerships on mental health, rape-trial directions on myths and misconceptions, menopause action plans and a national summit on men and boys. Phillipson said large employers would have to produce action plans explaining how they would support women going through the menopause. She also said the Government would hold a summit on men and boys and that the relevant Minister would update Ben Obese-Jecty on its date: "We will be holding a summit, because we recognise that there is more to do to support men and boys in our country."

### Prime Minister’s Questions
Under the Prime Minister heading, Andy Burnham marked Armistice Day, said he had written to all English councils to ask that accommodation be offered to all ex-service personnel before the winter break, and said the Government would work to restore Stormont and help settle its budget. Kemi Badenoch challenged the Government over its proposed 10-year plan, the timing of the spending review, defence funding and the Rosebank oilfield. Burnham said the plan would be presented before the Christmas recess, described Rosebank as a quasi-legal decision to be taken in due course, and referred to both gas and renewable-energy projects. He also said that a proposal for a national care service and a plan to reduce the welfare bill would feature in the 10-year plan: "We will present our plan for Britain to this House before it rises for the Christmas recess."

Other Members raised bus services, ethnicity options in the census, youth work and welfare, defence procurement, Gaza, hospital investment, social-care capital limits, e-bike regulation, small-boat arrivals, Down syndrome guidance, special educational needs and disabilities, heating oil, immigration, Russia’s military threat and reindustrialisation. Burnham agreed to meetings or further work on several constituency matters, including hospital provision in Durham and Watford, adult social care and reindustrialisation; on the Skynet programme, he said that no final decision had been made and that the decision should support jobs and reindustrialisation. On Russia, he said that current threats differed from those during the Cold War but were multifaceted: "the threat is different; it is multifaceted, and we will address it on every level."

### Online Platforms (Regulation) Bill and Compulsory Purchase (Fees) Bill
Manuela Perteghella, supported by Caroline Voaden and Sorcha Eastwood, presented the Online Platforms (Regulation) Bill. It would impose duties on providers concerning harm arising from user-generated content and interactions, transparency in content moderation, algorithms and advertising, and would establish a regulator with powers concerning complaints, investigations, penalties and redress. The Bill was read the First time, ordered to be printed as Bill 180, and scheduled for Second Reading on Friday 19 March 2027: "Bill read the First time; to be read a Second time on Friday 19 March 2027 and to be printed (Bill 180)."

Leigh Ingham secured leave to introduce the Compulsory Purchase (Fees) Bill. She argued that, where an owner’s neglect had contributed to the need for compulsory purchase, a local authority should not automatically have to pay that owner’s legal and professional fees. She distinguished the proposal from a blanket removal of property owners’ rights. The motion was agreed to, the Bill was presented and ordered to be printed as Bill 179, with Second Reading scheduled for Friday 5 February 2027. Ingham summarised the principle as follows: "The principle behind my Bill is simple: there should be no reward for neglect."

### Point of Order on named-day questions
Roger Gale asked the Speaker to ensure that named-day questions received timely replies, referring to an answer that had arrived after the requested date. Lindsay Hoyle said the delay was unacceptable and said he would raise the matter with the Leader of the House: "With a named day question, it should be answered, as in its title, by that named day." The Speaker then declined to allow further argument on the matter.

### Opposition day: welfare
Helen Whately moved that the Government commit to reducing working-age welfare spending during the Parliament. She argued that the bill was rising and that the Government had provided neither a timetable nor a sufficiently detailed plan. She set out Conservative proposals said to deliver £36 billion of savings through changes involving sickness and disability benefits, immigration-related eligibility, the benefit cap, housing benefit, fraud and error, the two-child limit and long-term unemployment. She said welfare should be a safety net rather than a lifestyle choice and proposed replacing some cash payments with support: "Without a date or plan, it is just empty words."

Stephen Timms moved the Government amendment. He argued that the previous Government had overseen increases in welfare spending and youth economic inactivity, while saying that employment and economic activity had increased since the General Election. He referred to the forthcoming Milburn and Timms reviews, face-to-face assessments, the youth guarantee, youth hubs, Connect to Work and Pathways to Work, and said that support would remain for people unable to work: "The social security system should always provide security for those who cannot work." Liberal Democrat speakers supported reform but opposed what they described as punitive or stigmatising cuts, highlighting the number of people in work who receive Universal Credit and the possible effects on child poverty, homelessness and costs elsewhere. Other Government Members questioned the Conservatives’ savings estimates, particularly those concerning disability benefits, foreign nationals and housing. The original motion was negatived by 92 votes to 279. The Government amendment was agreed by 268 votes to 93, and the amended motion was resolved. It noted the previous Government’s record, recognised that one million more people were in work and 397,000 more were economically active since the General Election, welcomed the imminent Milburn and Timms reviews, and supported welfare reforms intended to reduce barriers to employment and bring down the benefits bill: "The House divided: Ayes 268, Noes 93."

### Opposition day: employers
Julia Lopez moved a motion stating that employers are working people, condemning the increase in employers’ National Insurance contributions and calling for those contributions to be halved for under-25s. Opposition speakers argued that the increase from 13.8% to 15%, together with the lower payment threshold and other employment costs, had increased costs for businesses, reduced recruitment and particularly affected young people’s access to first jobs. They proposed reducing employer National Insurance for younger workers and wider measures concerning business rates, apprenticeships, hospitality and regulation. Lopez emphasised the hours and financial risks associated with running a business: "Employers are not just working people; they are uber-taxpayers."

Government speakers accepted that employers were working people but defended the National Insurance increase as necessary to fund public services, particularly the National Health Service. They pointed to the increased employment allowance, relief for under-21s and apprentices under 25, business-rates measures, energy support, investment and youth hiring grants. James Murray said that the Government had maintained their manifesto commitments on income tax, employee National Insurance and value-added tax, while Blair McDougall said employment was up and inactivity down. Liberal Democrat speakers attributed business pressures to a combination of Brexit, the pandemic, the mini-Budget, energy costs and recent tax and regulatory decisions, and argued for stronger economic growth and closer European trade links. The motion was rejected by 105 votes to 293: "The House divided: Ayes 105, Noes 293." No amendment had been selected, so no alternative motion was considered or agreed.

### Independent Expert Panel and committee changes
The House appointed Alison Abu, Tom Crowther, Justine Davidge and Elizabeth Dux as members of the Independent Expert Panel for six years from 25 November 2026, under Standing Order No. 150C: "Alison Abu, Tom Crowther, Justine Davidge and Elizabeth Dux be appointed as members of the Independent Expert Panel."

It also ordered changes to the Joint Committee on the National Security Strategy and to the Northern Ireland Affairs, Procedure, Public Accounts, Science and Technology, and Work and Pensions Committees. The changes were made without debate.

### Charity Commission powers and resources
Ashley Dalton raised concerns about the Charity Commission’s ability to investigate and intervene where charities were poorly governed, using Scarisbrick village hall as a constituency example. She described disputes over the management of the hall, village green, playground and war memorial, alleged governance and accounting failures, and the length of the Commission’s ongoing regulatory-compliance investigation. She also referred to wider committee concerns about the Commission’s powers, resources and approach, and asked what more the Government could do to support prompt investigations. Her central point was: "Ultimately, this is about ensuring that the Charity Commission has the resources, powers and support it needs to fulfil its responsibilities, protect charitable assets and maintain public confidence in the charitable sector."

Vicky Foxcroft said that the Commission had opened a regulatory-compliance case in February 2026, that it remained ongoing, and that the Commission intended to meet the parties at the premises. She stressed that opening a case was not a finding of wrongdoing and that the Commission could act only where there was misconduct or mismanagement. She said the Commission had experienced a 50% increase in concerns raised between August 2025 and 2026, received a 25% funding uplift in the 2025 spending review, and was keeping its powers under review. The Government had also published a consultation on expanded trustee-disqualification powers and would consider a wider review proposed by the Public Administration and Constitutional Affairs Committee. Foxcroft agreed to meet a delegation concerning the Coal Industry Social Welfare Organisation: "I would be happy to meet a delegation." The adjournment question was agreed to and the House adjourned.
