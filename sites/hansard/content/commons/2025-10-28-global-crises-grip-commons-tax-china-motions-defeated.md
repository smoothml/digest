## High-Level Summary
The House of Commons sat for Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office questions on Sudan, the Gaza ceasefire and peace plan, Ukraine, China, Gibraltar, Hong Kong, organised immigration crime, the BBC World Service, Gaza humanitarian access and the UK–Mauritius agreement on the Chagos Archipelago. Topical Questions centred on Hurricane Melissa preparations alongside updates on sanctions, climate finance and cultural institutions. An Opposition Day motion proposing the abolition of Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) on primary residences was defeated (Ayes 103, Noes 329). A later motion seeking publication of documents relating to the collapsed China spying case was also defeated (Ayes 174, Noes 327). Proceedings concluded with an adjournment debate on support for disabled veterans.

## Detailed Summary
### Speaker’s Statement: 75th anniversary of the rebuilt Chamber
The Speaker marked the 75th anniversary of the Chamber’s rebuilding after wartime destruction, quoting Churchill’s description of the Commons as “the citadel of British liberty” [ref: b145.1/2], and invited Members to a commemorative photograph at 11 am the following day [ref: b145.1/3].

### Sudan
The Foreign Secretary condemned escalating violence in El Fasher, warning of grave risks to civilians and urging a ceasefire and humanitarian access. She said the UK was pressing for a UN Security Council meeting on Sudan “as soon as possible” [ref: b146.4/1], and that while UK aid for Sudan had been doubled, delivery was impeded by fighting [ref: b147.1/1]. She supported safe exit routes: “getting civilian pathways out of the city to safety are urgently needed” [ref: b147.3/1]. On export concerns, she stressed the UK’s “strong controls on arms exports” and the need for regional actors to press both sides, noting Quad engagement (US, Saudi Arabia, UAE and Egypt) and calls to end external support [ref: b146.2/1-2].

### Gaza ceasefire and peace plan
Ministers described the ceasefire resulting from President Trump’s initiative as “a profound moment of peace and hope”, while stressing the need to “flood” unconditional aid into Gaza and advance phase 2 measures, including Hamas disarmament and transitional governance [ref: b147.8/1]. They backed monitoring compliance—“support an effective monitoring arrangement so that… all sides hold to the ceasefire” [ref: b148.3/1]—and reaffirmed that Gaza cannot be divided and that the pathway is to two states, including Gaza, east Jerusalem and the West Bank [ref: b148.5/1]. On Hamas, the Government said the group “can play no role in the future governance of Gaza or of Palestine” [ref: b149.4/1]. Queries covered hostages’ remains, water infrastructure, and concerns about an Israeli “yellow line”, while the Government condemned West Bank annexation proposals and reiterated commitment to a two‑state solution [ref: b150.3/1].

### Ukraine
The Government highlighted new sanctions against Rosneft and Lukoil to “tighten the economic vice” on Russia [ref: b151.0/1], work with partners to mobilise Russian sovereign assets for Ukraine [ref: b151.2/1], and continued military and economic support coordinated with allies [ref: b151.4/1]. They underscored the importance of security guarantees: “Security guarantees remain an important part of our support for Ukraine” [ref: b152.2/1], and urged reductions in third‑country imports and processing of Russian crude, noting the UK has sanctioned designated refineries “across the world” [ref: b152.5/1].

### China: assessment of threat level
The Foreign Secretary set out “the full spectrum of threats” posed by China—“espionage, cyber-attacks, transnational repression and support for Russia”—while noting China is also a major trading partner, requiring the UK to “challenge… on security and compete and co-operate on economic and global affairs” [ref: b152.8/1]. On embassy planning, she said the process is independent [ref: b153.1/1], and on the collapsed spy case, she was “extremely frustrated”, saying such activity should face “the full force of the law” [ref: b153.6/1].

### Gibraltar: UK‑EU agreement
Ministers said the June UK‑EU agreement would bring “jobs, investment and stability” to Gibraltar and the region [ref: b154.2/1]. They committed to bring the treaty to Parliament “as soon as it is finalised” under normal processes, allowing debate [ref: b154.4/1], and noted potential tourism benefits, including “more flights… from elsewhere in Europe” [ref: b154.6/1].

### Palestinian state and aid
The Government reaffirmed recognition of the State of Palestine, announced on 21 September, to support a two‑state solution and confirmed £116 million of aid this year [ref: b155.2/1]. On trade relating to settlements, Ministers said they “keep these questions under close review” [ref: b155.4/1]. Aid flows into Gaza have increased but remain insufficient; “vital crossings remain unopened”, and Ministers said they would travel to the region to press for access [ref: b155.6/1].

### Hong Kong: human rights
Ministers said the “continued erosion of democratic rights and freedoms” in Hong Kong is “deeply concerning”, with national security legislation diminishing autonomy [ref: b156.2/1]. They pledged to protect BNO visa holders and oppose transnational repression [ref: b156.4/1], and confirmed the UK “continue[s] to oppose the national security law” given its impact on freedoms, including press freedom [ref: b156.6/1].

### Organised immigration crime
The Government said it is “strengthening co-operation” across borders, including via an illegal migration sanctions regime targeting people‑smuggling leaders [ref: b157.2/1]. Following the west Balkans summit, new sanctions were announced and collaboration enhanced, including taskforces and deployments [ref: b157.4/1]. The Minister added that over 5,000 foreign national offenders were deported in the Government’s first year, a 14% increase [ref: b157.6/1].

### BBC World Service
Ministers called the World Service “the world’s most-trusted international news source” and noted a 31% increase in Government funding this year [ref: b157.9/1]. They said next year’s charter review will consider sustainable funding and innovation to extend reach [ref: b158.1/1], and voiced support for journalists facing repression [ref: b158.3/1].

### Gaza: humanitarian situation
The UK is working to “open more crossings and remove all restrictions on aid”, with £74 million of humanitarian funding announced this year, including £20 million at the Egypt peace summit [ref: b158.7/1]. Ministers said Rafah must be “fully opened” and that international NGOs need unimpeded access [ref: b159.1/1], and confirmed they had raised with Israel reports of aid delivery refusals, calling for full implementation of the agreement [ref: b159.3/1].

### UK–Mauritius agreement on the Chagos Archipelago
Ministers said payments under the agreement will be split between the FCDO and the MoD and set out in departmental annual accounts [ref: b159.6/1, b160.1/1]. Responding to cost criticisms, it was stated that “the cost… in today’s money is £101 million, and the net present value over 99 years is £3.4 billion,” verified by the Government Actuary’s Department [ref: b160.4/1].

### Topical Questions
The Foreign Secretary outlined support for Jamaica ahead of Hurricane Melissa: “We are prepared to mobilise resources… We have set up the crisis centre in the Foreign Office… [and are] positioning specialist rapid deployment teams” [ref: b160.7/2]. Other topics included condemning China’s “non-compliance with the joint declaration” on Hong Kong [ref: b161.2/1], the rationale for de‑proscribing HTS (“no longer assesses HTS to be an alias of al-Qaeda” [ref: b161.5/1]), increased pressure on Russia via sanctions [ref: b162.1/1], commitment to £11.6 billion in international climate finance by 2025‑26 [ref: b163.1/1], a £1.25 billion pledge to Gavi [ref: b163.3/1], British Council support (£160 million grant aid) [ref: b167.7/1], and humanitarian corridors and medevacs for injured children from Gaza: “we need to be able to get humanitarian corridors in place… we are also medevacing children to the UK” [ref: b167.3/1].

### Points of Order
One Member apologised for not following the Chair’s direction the previous day [ref: b169.1/1]. In response to a bid for parliamentary time regarding the Duke of York, the Speaker reiterated that “scheduling of time for debates is not a matter for the Chair” [ref: b169.6/1].

### Opposition Day: Stamp Duty Land Tax
The Opposition proposed abolishing SDLT on primary residences, funded by reductions in public expenditure, to boost ownership, growth and mobility [ref: b170.3/2]. Supporters argued SDLT “gums up” transactions and mobility, affecting first‑time buyers and downsizers [ref: b172.3/1-3, b172.5/1], and would stimulate related economic activity. The Government declined pre‑Budget speculation and noted SDLT raised £13.9 billion in 2024‑25, calling the motion “half-baked… built on… unfunded tax cuts” [ref: b174.2/1-2, b175.1/1]. After debate on housing supply, fiscal costs, and distributional effects, the motion was defeated: Ayes 103, Noes 329 [ref: b237.3/1-2].

### Debate: China spying case (transparency motion)
The Opposition sought publication of minutes, correspondence and advice related to the collapsed prosecution of two alleged Chinese spies, arguing for transparency [ref: b241.2/2-3]. The Government outlined the deputy National Security Adviser’s statements and a 1 September officials’ meeting on UK–China relations, insisted CPS decisions were independent, and called allegations of political interference “absolutely baseless” [ref: b247.2/2, b248.1/2]. Ministers refused to release sensitive material publicly—“Asking for open publication is completely different from the appropriate parliamentary scrutiny” [ref: b250.3/3]—but noted cooperation with parliamentary committees. The motion was defeated: Ayes 174, Noes 327 [ref: b276.3/1-2].

### Business without Debate and Committee changes
The House approved the draft Financial Services and Markets Act 2023 (Mutual Recognition Agreement) (Switzerland) Regulations 2025 without division [ref: b280.4/1-2], agreed adjournment dates for November, Christmas and February [ref: b280.6/2-3], and ordered changes to the Education Committee’s membership [ref: b280.8/2].

### Adjournment Debate: Support for disabled veterans
Contributions highlighted difficulties with benefits access, transfer of medical records, mental health provision, housing adaptations, and treatment of military compensation. One veteran’s experience captured the stakes: “I fought for this country… And now because I was abroad for 18 months I am made to suffer” [ref: b281.4/3-4]. Speakers called for improved Op COURAGE pathways, timely digitised transfer of service medical records, and for military compensation to be disregarded in means‑tested benefits. The Minister praised charities, outlined upcoming Valour hubs and reaffirmed that “we are extending the armed forces covenant into law” to reduce postcode variation [ref: b287.3/2]. She also noted an £86 million annual boost to the Disabled Facilities Grant [ref: b289.2/4-5], and invited Members to write in with specific cases for follow‑up.
