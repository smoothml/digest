## High-Level Summary
The sitting covered questions on Northern Ireland, Prime Minister’s Questions, statements on nuclear test veterans and violence against women and girls, a private Member’s Bill, an Opposition day debate, delegated legislation, a petition and an adjournment debate. The Prime Minister announced an independent panel on nuclear test veterans, while the Government set out measures including domestic abuse protection orders, changes to new non-disclosure agreements and a national conversation on consent. The House rejected the Opposition motion on Restoration and Renewal by 102 votes to 291, agreed the Government’s amendment and approved the amended Question. The final debate considered safeguarding reforms following abuse at Medomsley detention centre, with the Minister reporting measures already taken and further work under way.

## Detailed Summary
### Northern Ireland questions
Questions covered violence against women and girls, economic growth, foreign investment, legacy issues, events at Drumcree and the Northern Ireland Executive’s budget. Sarah Owen said the Government would support work with the Executive and Northern Ireland organisations, and welcomed a pilot of domestic abuse protection notices and orders in Greater Belfast, which would draw on experience from England and Wales: “the pilot will draw on insights gained from England and Wales”. She also said that service providers needed “the certainty that they need for the future”.

On the economy, Chris Bryant referred to the Government’s offer of £1.5 billion over three years and said a further £631 million was available in the current year if the Executive agreed a budget. Sarah Owen said £1 million had been announced for an investment summit and described the UK–EU sanitary and phytosanitary agreement as providing “certainty for Northern Ireland cross-border traders”. Jim Allister disputed the benefits of dual-market access. On legacy arrangements, Bryant said the Northern Ireland Troubles Bill had completed its Commons stages and was scheduled for Second Reading in the Lords on 2 December. He said the Government’s understanding was that “essential” represented a high bar, and said the UK would not implement its legislation until the Irish legislation was ready: “we will not be implementing our legislation until such time as it is”. On Drumcree, Bryant said events had passed peacefully and that court-order enforcement and breaches were matters for the courts and prosecuting authorities. On the budget, he warned that the Executive could run out of expenditure around the beginning of February if it did not act.

### Prime Minister’s Questions
Prime Minister Andy Burnham said the Government would bring forward to January the date by which non-disclosure agreements could no longer be used to silence victims of sexual harassment. In exchanges with Kemi Badenoch, he said the proposed arrangements with the European Union did not include a concluded agreement on tuition fees; he described that issue as “a separate piece of paper” and defended the wider package on the grounds of growth, reduced paperwork and opportunities for young people. He also said he would go further in supporting care leavers: “I can promise him today that I will go further as Prime Minister”. In response to Nadia Whittome, he agreed to establish an expert group on rape myths in courts as part of a wider national conversation.

Other questions concerned energy bills, steel in Wales, resilience and cyber-security, Northern Ireland’s consent principle, individual constituency cases, special educational needs, sport, sodium valproate compensation and MenB vaccination. Burnham said that the Government would remove the standing charge from gas bills next year and seek to do so as quickly as possible. He reaffirmed that the Good Friday agreement is based on consent and urged Northern Ireland’s parties to agree a budget. He also offered or arranged ministerial meetings on several constituency matters, including diagnostic capacity, railway infrastructure, Gurkha veterans and MenB vaccination.

### Nuclear test veterans independent panel
Andy Burnham announced the immediate creation of a non-statutory independent panel, chaired by Bishop James Jones, to examine the experiences of nuclear test veterans, the disclosure of documents, what the state knew and when, and the treatment of veterans and their families. The panel will operate on a “families first” basis, undertake a listening exercise, agree its membership by the end of the year and agree its terms of reference by January. Burnham said: “The panel will operate on the ‘families first’ principle”. An interim report is expected by 3 October 2027, and the Government will begin developing plans for a national memorial service on that date.

Kemi Badenoch supported the panel and asked about ministerial misinformation, compensation, records and the powers of a non-statutory process. Burnham said the panel would examine whether Ministers had been misled, but that compensation questions should be addressed after the truth had been established. In response to Edward Davey, he said he wanted to make a full apology once the findings were known and that national security and legal privilege should not create unjustified barriers. Members also raised access to medical records, the inclusion of descendants, merchant mariners, aircrews and ground crews, and a possible national oversight mechanism. Burnham said the scope was not intended to exclude those affected, committed to consulting the wider community and agreed to meet families affected by the Chinook crash.

### International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women and Girls
Bridget Phillipson announced measures in three principal areas. The Government would roll out domestic abuse protection notices and orders across England and Wales: “We are rolling out domestic abuse protection notices and domestic abuse protection orders across England and Wales”. It would bring forward the date from which new non-disclosure agreements could not restrict victims of crime or workers reporting harassment and discrimination; from the new year, “any victim of crime who signs a new NDA will have no limits on who they can speak to”. It would also begin a national conversation on consent, including whether further changes were needed concerning relationships between older men and younger women, to be launched in the new year. Phillipson additionally announced £15 million for a misogyny prevention fund and restated the objective of halving violence against women and girls within a decade.

Joy Morrissey, the shadow Minister for Women and Equalities, criticised the Government over early release, grooming-gang recommendations, data and an incident involving an influencer at Downing Street. Phillipson defended the early-release decision by referring to prison-capacity problems and said violence against women and girls must be tackled regardless of perpetrators’ backgrounds. Other Members raised stalking, strategic lawsuits against public participation, freelancers, older women, police funding, education and the Metropolitan Police. Phillipson said the Government would provide an update on its violence against women and girls strategy and referred to new relationships, sex and health education guidance on healthy relationships, consent and misogyny.

### Point of order: correction of the record
James Naish corrected earlier references to the death of his constituent Amanda Walker. He said that, following the inquest, the coroner had recorded a conclusion of “misadventure”, rather than the description he had previously used. The Deputy Speaker thanked him for correcting the record.

### Sale of Electric Personal Vehicles Bill
Lola McEvoy sought leave to introduce a Bill creating offences and penalties concerning the sale of certain electric personal vehicles. She proposed obligations for companies and online marketplaces to take reasonable steps to prevent illegal misuse, and referred to off-road electric bikes, modified battery-assisted bikes and tuning kits. She also referred to the death of Gloria Stephenson while using a zebra crossing and to fires involving modified batteries.

Leave was granted: “Question put and agreed to”. The Bill was presented by McEvoy and the named sponsors, read the First time and ordered to be read a Second time on Friday 5 February 2027. It was ordered to be printed as Bill 192.

### Restoration and Renewal of the Palace of Westminster
Jesse Norman, the shadow Leader of the House, argued that the projected cost range of £11.1 billion to £39.2 billion lacked an agreed budget, defined scope and sufficiently clear governance. He proposed a 90-day independent review led jointly by the Speakers of both Houses, distinguishing essential safety works from discretionary enhancements and producing “a clear budget, scope and schedule for a scaled-back programme”. Other Members supporting the Opposition motion questioned the proposed decant, the £3 billion phase-one package and whether Parliament was applying financial disciplines to itself. Some proposed a shorter, time-limited decant or work carried out in zones, while accepting that urgent safety repairs were necessary. Lee Dillon said the Liberal Democrats would abstain, seeking value for money, transparency and a clear timetable.

Alan Campbell, the Leader of the House, argued that delay increased cost and risk. He cited National Audit Office and Public Accounts Committee conclusions, including that “The costed options provide enough information for a decision on the way forward”. He said the Government would provide a full day of debate on 8 December. The proposed phase-one package would last seven years and be “capped at a maximum of £3 billion”, with updated costs by 2029 and a final decision on decanting by the middle of 2030. Campbell also said governance would be reviewed and streamlined within six months if phase one proceeded. The House rejected the original motion by 102 votes to 291, agreed the Government amendment and agreed the amended Question.

### Delegated legislation and petition
The House approved two draft instruments without debate: the Environmental Permitting (England and Wales) (Amendment) (England) Regulations 2026 and the Social Security (Persons Detained in Hospital) (Amendment) Regulations 2026. The questions on both instruments were agreed.

Rupa Huq presented a petition from residents of The Collective Old Oak. It asked the Government to ensure that the building’s management would “engage meaningfully with residents”, make decisions fairly and transparently, provide sufficient support and avoid evictions over the Christmas period.

### Medomsley detention centre: youth custody safeguarding
Mary Glindon described physical and sexual abuse of young men at Medomsley detention centre between 1961 and 1987, failures by authorities to intervene and the long campaign by survivors. She asked about implementation of Isabelle Trowler’s review, including the reporting of corruption allegations to local-authority-designated officers, independent scrutiny, trusted adults’ ability to make complaints and social workers from children’s home authorities. She said the complaints process needed reform because young people had been required to complain to people implicated in their abuse.

Jake Richards, the Minister, apologised on behalf of the Government, saying: “The state failed these young men”. He reported that heads of safeguarding were now involved in counter-corruption meetings, new guidance required local-authority-designated officers to be informed of allegations, a safeguarding board had been established, mandatory safeguarding training was required for staff working with children in custody, and enhanced criminal-record checks would be renewed every three years. He said work was under way to reform complaints, strengthen independent oversight and provide every child in youth custody with a dedicated social worker from their home local authority; legislation would be introduced when parliamentary time allowed. A forthcoming youth custody transformation plan would bring the reforms together, and the debate ended with the motion for adjournment agreed and the House adjourned.
