## High-Level Summary
The Commons spent the day on education policy and national security. Education questions covered International Baccalaureate funding, free school meals and breakfast clubs, support for pupils with special educational needs and disabilities, and the Government’s ambition for higher‑level learning ahead of a post‑16 White Paper. MPs then pressed Ministers on the collapsed alleged China spying case and debated the policing decision to exclude Maccabi Tel Aviv supporters from a match at Aston Villa. The Government unveiled its post‑16 skills strategy, and the House completed Committee and Third Reading of the Diego Garcia/Chagos treaty Bill, rejecting amendments and passing the Bill. A petition on Grove Park station improvements was presented, and an adjournment debate sought upgrades to the A50/A500 corridor.

## Detailed Summary
### Oral Answers – International Baccalaureate (IB) funding
Minister Josh MacAlister said 16–19 providers decide their provision within core funding and confirmed overall funding had increased, with the large programme uplift prioritised for students taking multiple STEM A‑levels. He stated that the IB "can still be studied" within providers’ 16–19 allocations and that institutions remain free to choose whether to offer it [ref: d602.0/1]. Pressed by Members that state IB provision may become unviable, he said the Government was prioritising support for "the vast majority of students who are studying A‑levels" while still recognising the IB as "an important course" [ref: d602.2/1]. 

Responding to accusations that the IB was being undermined, he said colleges are funded to make choices locally and that Ministers were "being frank and honest about that choice" to prioritise STEM large programmes, while the IB "can be chosen by colleges where that is right for students" [ref: d603.0/1]. No further policy change beyond the uplift prioritisation was announced.

### Oral Answers – Free School Meals (FSM) and breakfast clubs
The Secretary of State said expansion of FSMs will reach an extra half a million children, lift 100,000 out of poverty and save families £500, alongside wider early years measures [ref: d603.3/1]. On funding pressures, she said the expansion is "backed up with an extra £1 billion of funding through the spending review", adding that 750 breakfast clubs are open with a further 2,000 due from April next year "reaching half a million more children" [ref: d604.2/2]. 

She said the Department is working with schools, local authorities and the sector to notify families about the changes [ref: d603.5/1]. She also agreed to consider information on maths schools places and provide an update [ref: d603.7/1]. No new commitments were made beyond the stated rollout and funding.

### Oral Answers – SEND support in mainstream and specialist settings
Schools Minister Georgia Gould emphasised earlier identification, mainstream inclusion, teacher training and additional specialist places. She cited investment in early intervention and "£740 million to support schools to provide more specialist places" [ref: d604.7/1], alongside "an extra £1 billion into the high‑needs block" [ref: d606.6/1]. She highlighted the Partnerships for Inclusion of Neurodiversity in Schools (PINS) programme and changes to core teacher training to include SEND content [ref: d606.6/1; d620.6/1]. 

MPs sought timelines for a schools White Paper and clarity on Education, Health and Care Plans. Gould said reforms were urgent but did not provide a date: "I can assure the hon. Gentleman that this is urgent for me" [ref: d607.6/1]. She said the Department is working with councils to improve EHCP timeliness: "We are working closely with councils with waiting lists" [ref: d608.5/1]. No publication dates for SEND reforms were given.

### Oral Answers – Higher‑level learning target and pathways
The Secretary of State trailed a post‑16 White Paper later in the day and a new ambition for two‑thirds of young people to participate in higher‑level learning, with clearer technical routes alongside university [ref: d609.9/1]. She announced sectoral investments and reforms to pathways, including additional construction apprenticeships and new technical excellence colleges working with business [ref: d610.3/1]. On standards, she said the Office for Students will have powers to ensure quality and good outcomes [ref: d611.5/1]. 

On campus antisemitism, she stated there is "no place" for it and that vice‑chancellors must act: "Freedom of speech does not mean people have a right to harass or intimidate Jewish students" [ref: d612.2/1]. No decisions were taken at questions; further detail followed in the subsequent White Paper statement.

### Oral Answers – Free schools application decisions
Minister Josh MacAlister said the Government is taking a value‑for‑money approach to paused free school applications and committed to an "update… later this year" for affected areas [ref: d612.7/1]. He said investment decisions are possible "because of the decisions made at fiscal events" [ref: d613.4/1]. No immediate decisions were announced.

### Oral Answers – British Sign Language GCSE
Minister Georgia Gould said Ofqual has consulted on assessment arrangements for a new GCSE in British Sign Language and "expects to publish its final qualification rules this autumn" [ref: d613.7/1]. She noted that adult skills funding is available for BSL up to level 2 and offered to meet on early years BSL access [ref: d613.9/1]. No GCSE start date was given.

### Oral Answers – Under‑19 vocational training and NEETs
Minister Josh MacAlister said the Government had "cut red tape, put in record investment" and that apprenticeship "starts, participation and achievement are up, up, up" [ref: d614.2/1]. He argued the system can be "pro‑further education, pro‑technical education and pro‑higher education" without trade‑offs, citing "120,000 new training opportunities, up to 30,000 foundation apprenticeship starts" and £3 billion invested in apprenticeships [ref: d614.4/1]. 

He said tackling the "moral stain" of one million young people not in education, employment or training sits at the heart of the skills strategy, with further plans to be set out later the same day [ref: d614.6/1]. No new measures were announced at questions.

### Oral Answers – Childcare affordability and school‑based nurseries
New Children’s Minister Olivia Bailey said the Government has delivered a "record expansion of childcare, saving working parents £7,500 a year", and is supporting new school‑based nurseries [ref: d615.4/2]. She said 5,000 places have been created at school‑based nurseries this year with 7,000 more due next September, and that resources would not be the barrier to families receiving their entitlement [ref: d615.6/1; d616.1/1]. 

She cited overall funding of "£8 billion this year and £9 billion next year" for expanded childcare and family hubs [ref: d616.3/1]. She invited details of cases where providers were restricting funded hours so they could be examined [ref: d616.1/1]. No new policies were announced.

### Topical Questions – Education
The Secretary of State highlighted the Best Start family hubs rollout: "Labour is giving local authorities the funding they need to open Best Start family hubs" [ref: d616.6/1]. Members raised issues from year‑abroad visa costs to mobile phones in schools—where the Secretary of State said, "Phones should not be out in schools—it is as simple as that" [ref: d618.6/1]—and breakfast clubs, which "drive improvements in behaviour, attendance and attainment" [ref: d619.1/1]. There were also questions on SEND funding pressures and guidance on single‑sex spaces. No formal decisions were taken.

### Urgent Question – Home Office involvement in alleged China spying case
Security Minister Dan Jarvis reiterated disappointment that the case would not be heard and emphasised Crown Prosecution Service independence. He stated that "no member of this Government, nor any special adviser, was in any way involved" in the evidence or decision to discontinue [ref: d628.5/1], and confirmed the Government will cooperate with inquiries by the Joint Committee on the National Security Strategy and the Intelligence and Security Committee [ref: d625.6/3; d629.1/1]. 

He pointed to the deputy National Security Adviser’s statements describing Chinese espionage threats—"the Chinese intelligence services are highly capable and conduct large scale espionage operations against the UK" [ref: d632.0/4]—and said there was "no note to the Prime Minister" after a 1 September meeting [ref: d634.3/1]. He characterised China as posing "a series of threats to UK national security", while noting the UK must be "clear‑eyed" about economic realities [ref: d637.3/1]. Outcome: no decisions; next steps are JCNSS and ISC inquiries taking evidence.

### Urgent Question – Decision to exclude Maccabi Tel Aviv FC away supporters at Aston Villa
Culture Secretary Lisa Nandy said safety decisions are operationally for police and the safety advisory group (SAG), but argued the proposed exclusion "chooses exclusion" and is "wrong". She said the Government will support West Midlands Police and Birmingham City Council with any resources needed and that "resources will not be the determining factor" [ref: d641.2/2; d641.2/5]. 

She added that if the risk assessment is revised, the SAG will meet again to consider options: "If the assessment is revised, the safety advisory group will meet again" [ref: d641.2/5]. Members raised concerns about antisemitism, community cohesion and policing capacity. Outcome: no reversal announced; next steps are further risk assessment work by West Midlands Police and, if revised, SAG review.

### Statement – Post‑16 Education and Skills White Paper
The Secretary of State set out reforms to create clearer academic and technical pathways, including new V‑levels at level 3 alongside A‑levels and T‑levels. She announced targeted investments—"£187 million for our ‘Techfirst’ digital skills and AI learning, £182 million for engineering, £182 million for the defence talent pipeline and £625 million to train 60,000 more construction workers"—and 29 new technical excellence colleges [ref: d659.1/8]. She set an ambition for two‑thirds of young people to enter higher‑level learning and confirmed inflation‑linked increases to undergraduate fee caps for the next two years, with plans to legislate so future cap rises are linked to quality [ref: d659.1/11; d659.1/13]. 

Answering questions, she said V‑levels would streamline around 900 existing level 3 qualifications, with most existing funding kept until new V‑levels are introduced, and argued against an "endless cycle of unnecessary resits" in English and maths [ref: d665.2/1; d663.0/6]. Outcome: statement made; next steps include consultation on qualification transition and legislation on fee cap linkage "when parliamentary time allows" [ref: d659.1/13].

### Diego Garcia Military Base and British Indian Ocean Territory Bill – Committee and Third Reading
Debate focused on the costs of the treaty with Mauritius, national security safeguards (including any role of third countries), the marine protected area and the rights of Chagossians (including proposals for a referendum). Ministers argued the treaty secures the base’s operation with "full operational control of Diego Garcia" and a "strict ban on any foreign security forces across the archipelago" [ref: d731.3/2], and that costs average £101 million per year with a net present value of £3.4 billion [ref: d731.3/3]. 

Amendments seeking publication of legal advice and a Chagossian referendum were defeated: Amendment 7 (legal obligations memorandum) Ayes 174, Noes 321 [ref: d737.2/1]; Amendment 9 (right of return/referendum) Ayes 83, Noes 319 [ref: d741.1/1]; and New Clause 1 (Commons approval for payments) Ayes 172, Noes 322 [ref: d748.3/1]. Clause 2 stand part was agreed (Ayes 318, Noes 174) [ref: d744.3/1]. The Bill was reported without amendment and passed Third Reading (Ayes 320, Noes 171) [ref: d757.1/2; d757.1/3]. Next steps: further stages are in the House of Lords (not stated in the transcript).

### Petition – Grove Park station improvements
Janet Daby presented a petition calling for improvements at Grove Park station, including step‑free access, better lighting and CCTV, more shelter and seating, and better access to toilets: "Declares that the Station needs improvement… Enhanced Accessibility… Improved Safety… Better Shelter and Seating… Better access to toilets" [ref: d760.1/9; d760.1/12; d760.1/13; d760.1/14; d760.1/15]. Outcome: petition presented; no Government response was recorded during this sitting (not stated in the transcript).

### Adjournment Debate – A50/A500 corridor upgrades
Jacob Collier urged inclusion of A50/A500 upgrades in the next Road Investment Strategy (RIS3), citing congestion, safety concerns at Uttoxeter roundabouts and economic benefits along the corridor. The Roads Minister referenced record road maintenance funding and said the A50 central section near Uttoxeter and M6 junction 15 remain in the pipeline "subject to a supportive business case", potentially for RIS4 or late in RIS3 if funding allows [ref: d765.1/4; d765.1/5]. 

Outcome: no commitment to a start date; National Highways will continue scheme development and consideration through RIS planning.
