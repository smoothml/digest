## High-Level Summary
The Commons held Wales questions, Prime Minister’s Questions, an urgent question on Gaza and Hamas, an urgent question on asylum accommodation using MOD sites, and a defence statement on the UK–Türkiye Typhoon export deal. MPs then considered numerous amendments to the Sentencing Bill, rejected several high‑profile new clauses, and passed the Bill at Third Reading. The House agreed to share unpublished 2009 evidence with the Omagh Bombing Inquiry. The day concluded with an adjournment debate on strengthening the offshore wind supply chain on Tyneside.

## Detailed Summary
### Oral Answers: Wales – farming inheritance tax; economic growth; cost of living; pylons; asylum accommodation; cross‑border rail
On inheritance tax and farms, Wales Minister Anna McMorrin said she had met the Farmers’ Union of Wales and would meet the NFU, adding Government had ensured “the full £337 million has been allocated to the Welsh Government” [ref: a291.7/2]. She said “The vast majority of farmers will not be affected by this change” [ref: a291.9/1], later reiterating there would be “no change and no U‑turn on inheritance tax” [ref: a293.0/1].

On growth, Secretary of State Jo Stevens said, “The UK was the fastest growing economy in the G7 in the first half of this year” [ref: a293.4/1], and said Pride in Place funding would “help revitalise high streets” [ref: a293.6/1]. She criticised Plaid Cymru’s independence plans as costing Wales £21.5bn annually [ref: a293.8/1], and rejected claims about harm from business property relief changes, saying “It is certainly not” true [ref: a294.1/1]. On cost of living, she cited actions including increasing the minimum and living wages and freezing fuel duty [ref: a295.6/1], adding that nationalist policies would “cost Wales £21.5 billion every single year” [ref: a295.8/1].

On pylons, Stevens said undergrounding lines costs about “four and a half times more than overhead lines” [ref: a296.5/1], while pointing to new community funds and bill discounts near major projects [ref: a296.7/1]. On asylum hotels, McMorrin said use would end “as soon as possible and before the end of this Parliament” [ref: a297.7/1], noting hotel use had nearly halved and 30,000 people with no right to remain had been removed [ref: a298.2/1; a299.0/1]. On rail, she said Government was investing “at least £445 million in Welsh rail” and listed work on stations including “Cardiff East, Newport West, Somerton, Llanwern and Magor and Undy” [ref: a299.3/1; a299.5/1].

### Prime Minister’s Questions
The Prime Minister updated the House on support for Jamaica, stating “HMS Trent and specialist rapid deployment teams are pre‑positioned” [ref: a300.4/1]. He highlighted renters’ reforms and a Typhoon export deal, saying the UK is “a leading and trusted member of NATO” [ref: a300.4/3]. Pressed on tax pledges, he said plans would come at the 26 November Budget, adding, “no Prime Minister or Chancellor will ever set out their plans in advance” [ref: a301.3/1].

On Gaza, he called for all sides to uphold the ceasefire and said, “All sides need to uphold President Trump’s peace plan” [ref: a303.3/1]. On Russian interference, he warned Reform would be “Putin‑friendly” and “a real threat to our defence” [ref: a304.0/2]. He referenced closer EU ties via “10 strands” agreed at a UK‑EU summit [ref: a304.2/1]. Elsewhere, he confirmed banking hub expansion: “we have committed to rolling out 350 banking hubs… over 180 are already open” [ref: a307.3/1].

### Urgent Question: Gaza and Hamas
Foreign Office Minister Hamish Falconer reported on strikes in Rafah with estimates of “up to 100 people in Gaza” killed [ref: a311.2/1], urging “urgent de‑escalation” and adherence to the ceasefire [ref: a311.2/2]. He said, “We are clear that there can be no role for Hamas in the future governance of the strip” [ref: a311.2/3], and declined to give a “running commentary” on individual incidents given the ceasefire’s fragility [ref: a312.0/1].

On aid, he noted an “increase… through Kerem Shalom” but said volumes require Rafah and the Allenby Bridge to reopen [ref: a312.0/3; a313.1/1]. He highlighted sanctions against settler violence [ref: a314.3/1], clarified that “no British weaponry is used either in Gaza or in the west bank” save prior F‑35 messaging [ref: a315.0/1], and confirmed ongoing evacuations of students and medically vulnerable children [ref: a320.1/1]. He stressed close coordination with the US and regional partners [ref: a313.4/1].

### Urgent Question: Asylum seekers – use of MOD barracks
Home Office Minister Alex Norris said hotel use had fallen from a peak of 400 to “around 200” and confirmed temporary use of Cameron Barracks (Inverness) and Crowborough Training Camp (East Sussex) for “around 900 people” [ref: a326.2/1‑a326.2/3]. He apologised for communication lapses with MPs, noting both sites had supported Afghan resettlement, and argued the need to break the model that arrivals expect to “live in a hotel and work illegally” [ref: a327.0/1‑a327.0/3].

He said removals were “over 35,000 since we took office” and system costs were down £1 billion, including £500 million on hotels [ref: a328.0/2; a329.0/1]. Accommodation would be “functional… humane, but… basic” [ref: a335.0/1], with resistance to wider HMO use [ref: a332.3/1]. On the Kebatu deportation escort payment, he said £500 was an operational decision to avoid higher costs and delay [ref: a335.4/1].

### Statement: UK–Türkiye Typhoon export deal
Defence Minister Luke Pollard announced an “£8 billion” deal for 20 British‑built Typhoon jets for Türkiye, supporting “20,000 jobs across 330 UK companies” and strengthening NATO interoperability [ref: a338.1/2‑a338.1/3; a338.1/5]. He linked it to a recent “£10 billion” Type 26 frigate deal with Norway [ref: a338.1/6], and to a defence industrial strategy including an offsets consultation [ref: a338.1/9].

He confirmed “all Eurofighter nations have indeed signed off this export, including Germany” [ref: a341.0/2], and said capability investment timings (e.g. E‑Scan radar and P4E upgrades) would be set out in the defence investment plan [ref: a341.0/4]. He framed the deal as “defence as an engine for growth”, preserving sovereign skills [ref: a338.1/3].

### Ten Minute Rule Bill: European Convention on Human Rights (Withdrawal)
Nigel Farage argued that “we are not sovereign all the while we are part of the European convention on human rights” and cited the 2022 Rwanda flight injunction by “a single judge in Strasbourg” [ref: a353.0/4; a353.0/5]. Sir Edward Davey opposed withdrawal, noting “Russia under Vladimir Putin is the only country to have withdrawn from the European convention on human rights” [ref: a354.2/1].

Outcome: On division, the motion for leave was negatived (Ayes 96, Noes 154) [ref: a356.1/1‑a356.1/2].

### Sentencing Bill – Consideration and Third Reading
Backbench new clauses included NC1 (assessment of parenting and financial orders) and NC14 (lifting anonymity at sentencing for under‑18s with 4+ year sentences), advanced by Peter Bedford [ref: a359.3/1‑a359.3/7; a377.0/1]. Kieran Mullan proposed NC19 to expand whole‑life orders for murders of police/prison officers motivated by their duties, and NC20 to create child‑cruelty notification requirements akin to the sex offenders register. Ministers expressed sympathy but said it would be “wrong to pre‑empt” the homicide review on NC19 [ref: a414.2/1], and “wholly irresponsible to legislate” on NC20 without delivery‑capacity assessment [ref: a410.1/1].

Opposition speakers warned that many serious offenders on standard determinate sentences would be released earlier under the Bill. The Minister said “Judges will always have discretion to send offenders to prison” [ref: a414.0/1], and argued capacity was being stabilised, stating that under Labour “there will be more criminals in our prisons than ever before” [ref: a408.0/2]. Divisions: NC12 (rehabilitation access for remand prisoners) Ayes 82, Noes 314 [ref: a423.1/1‑a423.1/2]; NC19 Ayes 173, Noes 323 [ref: a426.3/1‑a426.3/2]; NC20 Ayes 182, Noes 311 [ref: a431.1/1‑a431.1/2]. Third Reading passed (Ayes 321, Noes 103) [ref: a437.1/1‑a437.1/3].

### Privileges: Omagh Bombing Inquiry – unpublished evidence
The House agreed to provide an unpublished 2009 Northern Ireland Affairs Committee transcript to the Omagh Bombing Inquiry, allowing the Inquiry to disclose or publish it subject to advice [ref: a441.1/1‑a441.1/6]. The Leader said this “will enable the unpublished transcript… to be provided” [ref: a441.1/7]. The Privileges Committee Chair underscored the atrocity’s gravity: “a 500 lb car bomb exploded, taking the lives of 29 people and two unborn children” [ref: a441.3/2]. The motion was agreed without division [ref: a444.0/3].

### Adjournment Debate: Offshore wind supply chain on Tyneside
Mary Glindon urged stronger support for UK fabrication and warned of foundation structures being imported from lower‑cost regions. The Energy Minister said “a total budget of £900 million is available for fixed‑bottom offshore wind in allocation round 7” [ref: a448.1/6], and outlined a supply‑chain package “worth up to £1 billion” via Great British Energy, the Crown Estate and industry [ref: a449.1/3].

She cited investment at the Port of Tyne and said Government is open to discussions on undergrounding a Tyne transmission cable, though Ofgem would decide: approval must show “benefits to consumers” [ref: a449.1/6].
