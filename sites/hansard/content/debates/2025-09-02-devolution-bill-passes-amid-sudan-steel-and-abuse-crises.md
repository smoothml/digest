## High-Level Summary
The House dealt with a Speaker’s privilege ruling, extensive Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office questions (including on Sudan and Gaza), a business statement on the insolvency of Speciality Steel UK, and a Home Office statement on tackling group‑based child sexual exploitation. MPs then debated the English Devolution and Community Empowerment Bill at Second Reading; an Opposition reasoned amendment was defeated and the Bill was read a Second time and programmed for Committee. Bills were presented, including a Government Sentencing Bill and a Ten‑Minute Rule Bill on babies’ and infants’ allergy guidance. In the Adjournment, large‑scale solar schemes in Newark were examined alongside Ministers’ policy on planning safeguards, community benefits and rooftop/floating solar.

## Detailed Summary
### Speaker’s statement on parliamentary privilege
The Speaker informed the House that he had granted precedence to a privilege matter arising from a legal case that could impede the Parliamentary and Health Service Ombudsman laying a report. He ruled: “I have decided that this is indeed a matter for which I should allow the precedence accorded to issues of privilege.” [ref: d121.4/2]. He added that the motion “will be taken after any urgent questions or statements and before Government business” and would be tabled before the rise of the House. [ref: d121.4/2].

### FCDO oral questions: ODA, Sudan, Gaza and other diplomacy
Ministers said detailed Official Development Assistance allocations would follow, informed by impact assessments: “we will set out the detailed allocations in due course; they will be informed by impact assessments.” [ref: d122.6/1]. On Sudan, the Foreign Secretary described “its worst humanitarian crisis on record. Over 30 million people need aid, 12 million have been displaced,” and said the UK was leading UN Security Council efforts on access and civilian protection. [ref: d123.3/1]. 
On Gaza, the Minister announced “a further £15 million of aid and medical care for Gaza and the region” (taking the year’s total to £75 million), and highlighted that the IPC assessment found “500,000 people in Gaza are facing famine.” [ref: d124.10/1; d125.1/1]. Other items included the strategic importance of Diego Garcia in Chagos discussions — “The Diego Garcia military base is essential to the security of the United Kingdom” [ref: d127.3/1] — and continued tightening of sanctions on Russia: “Since coming to office we have sanctioned more than 680 individual entities and ships.” [ref: d134.9/1].

### Speciality Steel UK: insolvency statement
The Business Secretary updated the House following a High Court winding‑up order: “the company has now entered liquidation.” [ref: d145.1/1]. She said the official receiver had been appointed and the Government “have agreed to funding for the official receiver, who will now stabilise operations.” [ref: d145.1/4; d145.1/5]. She stressed there would be “no immediate changes to the current operation of the business, including to employees’ jobs,” with sites secured and “employee payroll processed within 24 hours,” while options are assessed with unions, local leaders and potential investors. [ref: d145.1/3].

### Group‑based child sexual exploitation: national inquiry and policing operation
The Home Office announced a new national policing operation — “Operation Beaconport has been established.” [ref: d160.2/4] — and reported that “1,273 cases have now been identified for formal review,” with 216 rape‑related, highest‑priority cases being expedited. [ref: d160.2/6]. On the national statutory inquiry, the Minister said the chair appointment was in its final stages and that it “will place victims and survivors firmly at its heart.” [ref: d160.2/12]. She also committed to improved suspect data (“making it a requirement to collect ethnicity and nationality data”) [ref: d160.2/11] and emphasised a time‑limited, victim‑centred process. [ref: d160.2/16].

### Bills presented and Members’ measures
The Government presented a Sentencing Bill “to make provision about the sentencing, release and management after sentencing of offenders” and it was read a First time (Bill 299). [ref: d176.4/2; d176.4/3]. A Ten‑Minute Rule Bill — Babies and Infants (Allergy Guidance) — was introduced; the sponsor noted that “On average, two children in every classroom are affected by food allergies.” [ref: d177.2/5; d177.2/21]. In addition, the Vehicle Registration Marks (Misuse and Offences) Bill was presented and set down “to be read a Second time on Friday 12 September.” [ref: d176.6/3].

### English Devolution and Community Empowerment Bill: Second Reading and outcomes
Moving Second Reading, the Deputy Prime Minister said the Bill aims to “drive the biggest transfer of power in a generation out of Whitehall to our regions and communities.” [ref: d180.3/2]. The House debated mayoral powers, neighbourhood governance, local government reorganisation and community asset protections. The Opposition’s reasoned amendment was defeated (Division 272: Ayes 167, Noes 367) [ref: d251.1/1], and the Bill was then read a Second time (Division 273: Ayes 365, Noes 164). [ref: d255.1/1]. Programme, Money and Ways and Means motions were agreed and the Bill was committed to a Public Bill Committee. [ref: d259.1/14; d259.3/6; d259.5/5].

### Adjournment: Solar development in Newark and Government policy
The right hon. Member for Newark raised three large proposed solar projects, saying that “Between them, they would cover at least 10,000 acres,” and focused on cumulative impacts, food security, flood and fire risk, supply chains and landscape effects. [ref: d262.2/3]. The Minister said solar is central to clean‑power goals and public support is high — “solar energy is supported by 86% of the public” — and highlighted planning safeguards, community engagement and a forthcoming change to double the threshold for nationally significant projects to 100 MW “to return control over a greater number of those projects to local authorities.” [ref: d267.0/3; d267.0/4]. She also outlined measures to boost rooftop and floating solar, community benefits and biodiversity requirements. [ref: d268.1/2; d268.1/8].
