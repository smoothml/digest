## High-Level Summary
The House of Commons held Women and Equalities oral questions on domestic abuse, support for trans people, violence against women and girls (VAWG), maternity care, and labour‑market inequalities for young disabled people. Prime Minister’s Questions covered the economy, a £10 billion Norway shipbuilding export, the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR), and Gaza. MPs granted leave for a Ten Minute Rule Bill on methanol poisoning in travel advice, considered and defeated two Opposition Day motions (property taxes: 98–335; hospitality sector: 158–334), and noted Royal Assent to two Acts. Several statutory instruments were approved, a petition on recognising Palestinian statehood was presented, and the day concluded with an adjournment debate on diabetes in sport.

## Detailed Summary
### Women and Equalities: Domestic abuse aggravated offences
Ministers said they are implementing “a domestic abuse identifier at sentencing to ensure consistent recognition of domestic abuse offenders” and that this will deliver “on a recommendation made by the independent sentencing review” [ref: d271.5/1]. Calum Miller raised two constituent cases and obtained a meeting; the Minister replied, “I will happily meet him to discuss this further” [ref: d271.7/1]. On child‑to‑parent abuse, ministers said the issue would feature in “the upcoming violence against women and girls strategy” [ref: d272.1/1].

### Women and Equalities: Support for trans people and single‑sex services
On the Supreme Court’s For Women Scotland judgment, ministers affirmed protections under the Equality Act 2010 and outlined manifesto commitments, including a trans‑inclusive ban on conversion practices [ref: d272.4/1]. Addressing access to single‑sex services for Gender Recognition Certificate holders, the Minister stated, “biological sex is the means by which single‑sex provision will be delivered” [ref: d272.6/1]. She confirmed work towards a draft Bill to ban conversion practices [ref: d273.0/1], noted that while most organisations followed the law, a “small number… seemed to have misinterpreted” the Equality Act exemptions and that the EHRC would follow up [ref: d273.3/1], and said Government had no advance sight of interim EHRC guidance but would consider the final code when received [ref: d273.6/1].

### Women and Equalities: Violence against women and girls (VAWG)
Ministers reiterated, “We are committed to halving violence against women and girls within the next decade” [ref: d274.2/1], and said improving children’s and adult social care responses is “at the top of the agenda for all of us” [ref: d274.4/1]. The Minister committed to close engagement with the Parole Board on the Sentencing Bill [ref: d274.8/1] and to work with DSIT on online harms, including pornography and platforms such as OnlyFans, within the VAWG strategy [ref: d275.1/1].

### Women and Equalities: Maternity care
Ministers said, “It is unacceptable that women are experiencing poor maternity care” and announced an investigation “to understand the underlying systemic issues” [ref: d275.4/1]. The CQC will monitor Bedford hospitals closely while improvements are implemented, and the national investigation’s terms of reference will be published “shortly” [ref: d275.6/1]. The Minister agreed to raise with colleagues the suggestion of appointing Donna Ockenden [ref: d275.8/1] and said a maternity and neonatal national taskforce, chaired by the Health Secretary, will develop a national plan with expert and family representation [ref: d276.2/1]. He undertook to write urgently on surgical mesh issues [ref: d276.4/1] and noted that Baroness Amos is chairing a rapid investigation of up to 10 trusts by year‑end alongside a system‑wide review to drive national actions [ref: d276.7/1].

### Women and Equalities: Young disabled people and work
Ministers said, “We want young people with disabilities and health impairments to secure good employment… and to fulfil their aspirations” and referenced the “Pathways to Work” Green Paper’s proposed youth phase, with consultation responses under review [ref: d277.2/1]. They highlighted job carving and awaited Sir Charlie Mayfield’s report on employer action [ref: d277.4/1]. A youth guarantee will ensure “all 18 to 21‑year‑olds in England, including disabled young people, can access quality training… or help to find work” [ref: d277.6/1].

### Women and Equalities: Topical questions
Ministers set out cross‑government priorities: “This Government are focused on breaking down barriers to opportunity for everyone” [ref: d278.2/1]. On the motherhood penalty, the Minister called pay gaps “totally unacceptable” and linked expanded 30 hours’ childcare to improving outcomes [ref: d278.4/1]. Other answers covered an LGBT health evidence review led by Dr Michael Brady [ref: d279.0/1], casework follow‑up on wheelchair provision [ref: d279.2/1], and reviewing parental leave to support adoptive families [ref: d279.8/1].

### Prime Minister’s Questions (Engagements)
The Prime Minister announced “the biggest defence deal that Norway has ever placed… a £10 billion investment, securing 15 years of shipbuilding in Scotland and across the rest of the United Kingdom” [ref: d280.8/1]. In exchanges on the economy, he argued the UK had “the highest growth in the G7” and “five interest rate cuts in a row” [ref: d281.5/1]. He stated, “We will not withdraw from the European convention on human rights” while reviewing interpretations to meet current circumstances [ref: d284.4/1]. On Gaza, he condemned restrictions on aid, referring to “a man‑made famine” and urged that “Israel must lift the restrictions” to allow delivery of life‑saving supplies [ref: d285.5/1].

### Point of Order: PCS security staff strike
John McDonnell urged senior management of the House to meet the union representing striking security staff and resolve the dispute [ref: d293.1/1]. The Speaker said it was in everyone’s interest “to work together to resolve matters” and encouraged talks rather than continued strike action [ref: d293.2/1].

### Ten Minute Rule Bill: Methanol Poisoning (Travel Advice)
Laura Trott sought leave to require Government travel advice to include methanol poisoning risks [ref: d294.2/2], arguing current advice is “vague and understated” [ref: d294.2/9] and that “awareness saves lives, but only when Government guidance is accurate, clear and consistent” [ref: d294.2/8]. Leave was granted; the Bill was ordered to be printed and read a Second time on Friday 29 May 2026 (Bill 300) [ref: d294.2/19].

### Opposition Day (10th Allotted Day): Property Taxes
The Opposition motion called on the Government not to introduce, among other measures, an annual property levy on family homes or a land value tax [ref: d297.4/2]. Ministers refused to engage with speculation: “the Government do not respond to speculation in advance of a Budget,” which “will take place on 26 November” [ref: d306.3/1]. The motion was defeated by 98 Ayes to 335 Noes [ref: d336.1/1, d336.1/2].

### Royal Assent
The House was notified of Royal Assent to the Universal Credit Act 2025 and the Armed Forces Commissioner Act 2025 [ref: d314.3/2, d314.3/3].

### Opposition Day: Hospitality Sector
The motion expressed regret at closures, downsizing and job losses in hospitality, citing “an estimated 84,000 job losses over the last 12 months and an average of two site closures per day” and criticising policy choices including reducing business rates relief to 40% and increasing employers’ NICs [ref: d340.2/2, d340.2/3]. The Government argued they had averted a cliff edge by maintaining relief and plan “to introduce permanently lower tax rates for retail, hospitality and leisure properties” from 2026‑27 [ref: d346.1/1, d346.1/7]. The motion was defeated by 158 Ayes to 334 Noes [ref: d404.1/1, d404.1/2].

### Delegated Legislation
The House approved, without division, several statutory instruments, including: the draft Free‑Range Poultrymeat Marketing Standards (Amendment) (England) Regulations 2025; the draft Limited Liability Partnerships (Application and Modification of Company Law) Regulations 2025; the draft Register of People with Significant Control (Amendment) Regulations 2025; the draft Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023 (Consequential, Incidental and Miscellaneous Provisions) Regulations 2025; and the draft Online Safety Act 2023 (Qualifying Worldwide Revenue) Regulations 2025 [ref: d409.1/1, d409.1/2, d409.3/1, d409.3/2, d409.5/1, d409.5/2, d409.7/1, d409.7/2, d409.9/1, d409.9/2].

### Public Petition: Recognition of Palestinian Statehood
Liam Byrne presented a petition whose signatories “request that the House of Commons urge the Government to recognise Palestinian statehood” [ref: d410.1/3]. He framed recognition within efforts to end the conflict, surge aid and release hostages [ref: d410.1/1, d410.1/2].

### Adjournment Debate: Diabetes in Sport
Christopher Bloore highlighted prevalence and risks: “Diabetes affects 12 million people in the UK” and contributes weekly to serious health events, with exercise improving insulin effectiveness and glucose control [ref: d411.2/3]. He cited stigma and access barriers in grassroots settings and urged clearer governing‑body policies. The Minister stressed the preventive role of physical activity, outlined place‑based investments and programmes (e.g., “We Are Undefeatable”), and said, “We expect all national governing bodies to have plans in place to support those with long‑term health conditions,” adding that Sport England will conduct research with Diabetes UK on barriers to activity [ref: d416.2/6]. The House then adjourned [ref: d418.1/4].
