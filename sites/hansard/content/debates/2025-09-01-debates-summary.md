## High-Level Summary
The House held a full day of oral questions and statements focusing on domestic welfare issues, border and asylum reform, the Middle East, and Ukraine. Ministers reported on employment trends and headline welfare reforms, and the Home Secretary set out a new France returns pilot and a package of asylum-system reforms. The Foreign Secretary described the humanitarian crisis in Gaza, announced additional aid and signalled possible recognition of a Palestinian state, while the Defence Secretary announced a major Type 26 frigate export to Norway and reiterated UK military support for Ukraine. The Joint Committee on Human Rights published a report on transnational repression urging new definitions, data collection and victim support measures, and MPs debated a national commemoration for the Lisbon Maru tragedy.

## Detailed Summary
### Work and Pensions — Oral Answers (unemployment, child poverty, PIP and welfare fraud)
Oral questions opened the sitting for the Department for Work and Pensions. Secretary-level answers (Alison McGovern, Liz Kendall, Stephen Timms and others) emphasised recent labour market metrics and programme roll-outs: “The unemployment rate is 4.7%… employment is up by 725,000, to 75.3%, since July 2024” [ref: c1.5/1], and Ministers pointed to a reduction in economic inactivity of “a reduction of 400,000” [ref: c1.5/1].
Parliamentarians probed local claimant rises, youth NEETs (not in education, employment or training) and child poverty. The Department announced expansion of youth trailblazers—“further funding of £45 million for our eight youth guarantee trailblazers” [ref: c6.5/1]—and reiterated that the child poverty strategy will be published in the autumn [ref: c1.10/1]. On disability and PIP (personal independence payment), Stephen Timms set out a co-produced review and a health transformation programme to modernise assessments, saying the review “will cover the assessment for the mobility component” [ref: c5.6/1] and that the health transformation work “will run until 2029” [ref: c7.7/1]. On welfare fraud, Ministers highlighted a package backed by legislation: “the Office for Budget Responsibility has estimated that it will deliver an additional £9.6 billion” [ref: c6.8/1]. Outcomes: ministers committed to publishing the child poverty strategy, to continuing the PIP review and health transformation programme, and to rolling out expanded youth guarantee funding; no divisions were recorded in this section and further implementation details are to be laid out in upcoming policy documents.

### Borders and Asylum — Home Secretary’s statement and subsequent debate
The Home Secretary (Yvette Cooper) made a long statement on asylum and border security describing immediate and planned reforms. She announced a pilot returns agreement with France—“I signed the new treaty with France allowing us, for the first time, to directly return those who arrive on small boats” [ref: c23.2/5]—and said the Government have already removed “35,000 people with no right to be here” [ref: c23.2/4]. She identified the appeals backlog and set out a timetable-driven approach: funding additional sitting days and plans for a new independent appeals body and to “introduce a statutory timeframe of 24 weeks” for appeals [ref: c23.2/10].
The statement also announced temporary suspension of the dedicated refugee family reunion route while new rules are prepared: “we are bringing forward new immigration rules to temporarily suspend new applications under the existing dedicated refugee family reunion route” [ref: c23.2/16]. In debate, Opposition speakers pressed on rising small-boat crossings and local impacts of asylum hotels—one complained that “29,003 illegal immigrants have crossed the English channel” so far this year [ref: c27.1/3]—while the Home Secretary argued the Government had increased returns and saved on accommodation costs. The Minister also gave the operational figure for average appeal waits: “The average appeal time is now 54 weeks” [ref: c32.0/3]. Outcomes and next steps: the France pilot is to begin returns later this month, detention on arrival at Dover for pilot candidates is in place, new immigration rules and a timetable for appeals reform are being progressed, and the Border Security, Asylum and Immigration Bill remains a vehicle for proposed powers; many measures depend on operational rollout and Parliamentary passage.

### Middle East — Foreign Secretary’s statement on Gaza and Iran
The Foreign Secretary (David Lammy) gave a statement describing the humanitarian situation in Gaza and diplomatic action on Iran. He said the UN-backed IPC had confirmed famine in parts of Gaza and described the situation as “famine—famine in Gaza city” [ref: c60.2/2], calling the crisis “not a natural disaster; it is a man-made famine” [ref: c60.2/3]. The Government announced additional support: “I can announce an additional £15 million of aid and medical care for Gaza and the region” [ref: c60.2/5], and listed measures already taken such as restoring funding to UNRWA and suspending some arms exports [ref: c60.2/4].
On Iran, the UK joined France and Germany to trigger the UN Security Council “snapback” mechanism: “On 28 August, the UK, along with France and Germany, triggered the snapback mechanism under UN Security Council resolution 2231” [ref: c60.2/14]; he added that if no deal is reached within 30 days “the sanctions … will come back into force” [ref: c60.2/14]. He also reiterated that recognition of a Palestinian state will be considered “later this month, unless the Israeli Government take substantive steps to end the appalling situation in Gaza” [ref: c60.2/13]. Outcomes and next steps: £15 million in additional aid was announced; the snapback process on Iran has been initiated and may restore JCPOA-era sanctions absent a diplomatic settlement; and a decision on Palestinian recognition was signalled pending developments before UN General Assembly time.

### Middle East — Chamber response and cross-party questioning
Extensive questioning followed the Foreign Secretary’s statement. MPs from across the House expressed anxiety about the humanitarian impact in Gaza, asked for stronger measures such as additional sanctions or arms embargoes, and pressed for increased evacuation and medical assistance for children and students. The Foreign Secretary emphasised multilateral action and that the UK had already taken steps including sanctioning certain Israeli ministers and suspending trade negotiations, and he repeated that “we have stopped the sale of arms to Israel” [ref: c72.1/1]. Several MPs asked whether the Government would apply further targeted measures; the Foreign Secretary said he could not give operational detail from the Dispatch Box but reiterated the UK’s commitment to work with international partners and to pursue legal avenues and evidence-gathering where appropriate [ref: c68.4/1, c68.4/1]. Outcomes: continued diplomatic engagement and additional humanitarian measures were pledged; further sanctions or measures were discussed but not finalised in the Chamber record.

### Ukraine — Defence Secretary’s statement and coalition planning
The Defence Secretary (John Healey) confirmed major industrial and military support: a £10 billion contract supplying Norway with at least five Type 26 frigates—“the biggest British warship deal in our history” [ref: c92.1/2]—and reiterated the UK’s operational support for Ukraine, noting £4.5 billion in military aid for the year: “We are providing £4.5 billion in military aid for Ukraine this year” [ref: c92.1/9]. He outlined coalition planning and pre-positioning for a potential post-ceasefire security architecture and said the UK is co-chairing upcoming multinational defence meetings: “Next week, I will co-chair the 30th UDCG meeting” and host an E5 Defence Ministers summit to step up contributions [ref: c92.1/10].
The Secretary warned of continuing Russian drone and missile pressure—“In July, Russia launched approximately 6,200 one-way attack drones into Ukraine” [ref: c92.1/8]—and paid tribute to Admiral Sir Tony Radakin on his departure. Outcomes and next steps: the Norway Type 26 deal was presented as concluded; further UDCG and coalition ministerial meetings are scheduled to confirm additional military assistance and planning for any ceasefire security arrangements; Ministers emphasised readiness reviews and co-ordination with allies to sustain pressure on Russia.

### Joint Committee on Human Rights — Transnational repression report
The Joint Committee on Human Rights published a report on transnational repression and the committee’s chair (Alex Sobel) presented its key findings. The Committee warned that the phenomenon creates a “chilling effect on entire communities” and that transnational repression is being practised by a number of states, with the Committee highlighting China, Russia and Iran [ref: c110.4/3, c110.4/4]. It noted rising state-threat investigations—“the number of state threat investigations run by MI5 has increased by 48%” [ref: c110.4/5]—and recommended that the Government adopt a formal definition, improve data collection, expand victim reporting and policing responses, and consider a bespoke transnational repression sanctions regime. The report also welcomed the foreign influence registration scheme (FIRS) but recommended that China be placed on FIRS’s enhanced tier: “China’s omission from the enhanced tier risks undermining the credibility and coherence of FIRS” and the Committee “recommends that China be specified under the enhanced tier of FIRS” [ref: c110.4/11]. Outcomes and next steps: the report makes multiple recommendations for Government action (definition, data, hotline, mandatory police training, sanctions review); a formal Government response is not recorded in the transcript excerpt.

### Lisbon Maru Memorial debate
MPs debated commemorating those who died when the Lisbon Maru sank in 1942 en route to Japan, with several Members urging a national memorial in Scotland to reflect local losses and to honour rescuers such as Chinese fishermen. The motion noted the facts of the incident—“On 1 October 1942, an unmarked Japanese freighter called the Lisbon Maru was carrying over 1,800 British and allied prisoners of war” [ref: c115.2/2], and that “the Lisbon Maru was torpedoed by a submarine in the east China sea” [ref: c115.2/3]—and speakers recalled existing commemorations such as the National Memorial Arboretum memorial: “there is a memorial located at the National Memorial Arboretum… which commemorates all those who were taken captive and those who perished” [ref: c117.1/7]. Outcome: the debate concluded with the customary Question and Agreement to adjourn business; Ministers encouraged devolved and local routes for memorial support and highlighted existing memorial activity, and Members asked for consideration of further commemorative support (next steps: “not stated in the transcript” for any specific new national commitment).
