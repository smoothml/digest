## High-Level Summary
The House spent the day on oral questions to Work and Pensions, a Home Office statement on borders and asylum, Foreign Office statements on the Middle East and Iran, and a Defence statement on Ukraine. Ministers outlined labour‑market changes, youth/employment programmes and disability benefit reforms; set out an asylum return pilot with France, a new appeals body and the objective to close asylum hotels; announced £15 million more for Gaza and triggered Iran nuclear ‘snapback’ sanctions; and confirmed a £10 billion frigate export deal and sustained military support for Ukraine. A Joint Committee report warned of transnational repression and urged stronger UK responses. MPs also debated commemorating the Lisbon Maru tragedy, with Government noting existing remembrance and encouraging devolved engagement on any further memorial.

## Detailed Summary
### Work and Pensions: Employment, youth and disability support
Ministers reported headline labour‑market movements and targeted schemes. The Secretary of State said “employment is up by 725,000, to 75.3%, since July 2024” and “inactivity is down … a reduction of 400,000” [ref: c1.5/1]. Youth unemployment and universal credit spikes were raised by Members; the Government pointed to expanded local trailblazers and funding, including “further funding of £45 million for our eight youth guarantee trailblazers” [ref: c6.5/1].

On disability, the Government confirmed that “we will co‑produce our review of the PIP assessment with disabled people” [ref: c5.6/1] and reiterated “The £3.8 billion that we are investing in employment support” for people out of work on health and disability grounds [ref: c5.1/1]. Ministers also confirmed a child poverty strategy is due in the autumn [ref: c22.2/1]. Next steps: PIP review co‑production, continued youth‑guarantee roll‑out and publication of the child poverty strategy.

### Borders and Asylum: treaty with France, appeals reform and hotel closures
The Home Secretary announced a new bilateral returns pilot and a package to overhaul the asylum system. “In August, I signed the new treaty with France allowing us, for the first time, to directly return those who arrive on small boats… The first detentions… took place the next day,” with “the first returns” expected “later this month” [ref: c23.2/5]. She also committed that “every asylum hotel will be closed for good” through controlled reductions in system size [ref: c23.2/17] and identified appeals as a major bottleneck, with “an average wait time already of 54 weeks” [ref: c23.2/10].

Opposition Members cited rising small‑boat crossings—“29,003—illegal immigrants have crossed the English channel” so far this year [ref: c27.1/3]—and pressed for returns numbers and security‑check detail. Outcomes/next steps: pilot returns to France commencing, proposals for an independent appeals body, and further powers in the Border Security, Asylum and Immigration Bill progressing in the other place [ref: c23.2/8].

### Middle East: Gaza humanitarian response, Iran ‘snapback’, and Palestinian recognition
The Foreign Secretary described a worsening humanitarian emergency in Gaza—“the… IPC mechanism confirmed… famine” and “over 300 people have died from malnutrition, including 119 children” since 1 July [ref: c60.2/3]. He announced “an additional £15 million of aid and medical care for Gaza” [ref: c60.2/5] and said the UK is working “to get critically ill and injured children into the UK” for NHS treatment, with first patients expected within weeks [ref: c60.2/6]. On Iran, he confirmed the UK and E3 “triggered the snapback mechanism” on 28 August and warned “Iran’s stockpile of enriched uranium is now 40 times over the limit” [ref: c60.2/14; c60.2/15].

On Palestine, he recalled the UK’s position that it intends “to recognise the state of Palestine later this month” unless substantive Israeli steps are taken [ref: c60.2/10], while noting the UK will assess developments with partners before UNGA [ref: c64.0/5]. Next steps: disbursement of the £15m package, medical evacuations and continued diplomacy ahead of UNGA, alongside Iran snapback implementation if conditions are unmet.

### Ukraine: support, coalition planning and a major export deal
The Defence Secretary announced “a £10 billion contract to supply Norway with at least five Type 26 frigates,” supporting jobs and strengthening NATO’s northern flank [ref: c92.1/2]. On Ukraine, he said the UK is “providing £4.5 billion in military aid for Ukraine this year” and highlighted intensified Russian attacks—“In July, Russia launched approximately 6,200 one‑way attack drones into Ukraine” [ref: c92.1/9; c92.1/8].

He outlined coalition planning for post‑ceasefire security and said he will “co‑chair the 30th UDCG meeting” next week with Germany and NATO’s Secretary‑General [ref: c92.1/10]. Outcomes/next steps: accelerated materiel delivery via the UDCG, further ministerial meetings and development of a ‘coalition of the willing’ plan to help secure any future ceasefire.

### Joint Committee on Human Rights: Transnational Repression report
Reporting to the House, the Committee warned that transnational repression “creates a broader ‘chilling effect’ on entire communities” [ref: c110.4/3], with MI5 state‑threat cases up “by 48%” [ref: c110.4/5]. It recommended adopting a formal UK definition, better data collection, a dedicated victim reporting hotline—“establish a reporting hotline for transnational repression victims” [ref: c110.4/13]—and reconsidering the scope of sanctions and the foreign influence registration scheme, noting “China’s omission from the enhanced tier risks undermining the credibility” of the regime [ref: c110.4/11].

Next steps: Government response to the report’s recommendations on definition, data, victim support and sanctions design.

### Lisbon Maru memorial: commemoration debate
Members considered commemoration of the Lisbon Maru, which on “1 October 1942… was carrying over 1,800 British and allied prisoners of war” when it sank, causing heavy loss of life [ref: c115.2/2]. The debate pressed for a Scottish or UK memorial alongside existing remembrance. The Minister noted “a memorial located at the National Memorial Arboretum” and encouraged devolved engagement on any further memorials [ref: c117.1/7; c117.1/8].

Next steps: local and devolved consideration of additional commemorative options; continued annual remembrance at the Arboretum [ref: c117.1/7].
