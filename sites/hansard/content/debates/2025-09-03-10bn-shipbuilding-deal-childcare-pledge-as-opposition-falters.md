## High-Level Summary
The Commons sat for Women and Equalities questions, Prime Minister’s Questions, a Ten‑Minute Rule Bill, two Opposition Day debates, and later business including a petition and an adjournment debate. Ministers covered domestic abuse identifiers, support for trans people, the violence against women and girls strategy, and maternity service reforms. The Prime Minister highlighted a major Norwegian defence export and new childcare provision, and the Chief Secretary confirmed the Budget date. The House granted leave to a methanol‑poisoning travel advice Bill, rejected both Opposition Day motions on property taxes and the hospitality sector, approved several statutory instruments, and debated diabetes in sport with a Minister agreeing to meet stakeholders.

## Detailed Summary
### Women and Equalities (Oral Questions)
Alex Davies‑Jones said the Government “are implementing a domestic abuse identifier at sentencing” to ensure offenders are consistently recognised across the justice system [ref: d271.5/1], and agreed to meet Calum Miller: “I will happily meet him to discuss this further.” [ref: d271.7/1]. On support for trans people, Bridget Phillipson confirmed work towards a trans‑inclusive ban on conversion practices: “We are committed to bringing forward a draft Bill” [ref: d273.0/1], while noting the Supreme Court ruling on single‑sex provision [ref: d272.6/1]. Jess Phillips reiterated the target to “halving violence against women and girls within the next decade” [ref: d274.2/1]. On maternity, Stephen Kinnock said, “We are establishing the maternity and neonatal national taskforce, which will develop a national plan” [ref: d276.2/1].

### Prime Minister’s Questions
The Prime Minister highlighted “the biggest defence deal that Norway has ever placed… a £10 billion investment” in UK shipbuilding and announced the rollout of 30 hours of funded childcare [ref: d280.8/1]. He defended the fiscal stance, stating that “those fiscal rules are non‑negotiable.” [ref: d281.3/3]. Later, the Chief Secretary to the Treasury told the House that the Chancellor’s Budget “will take place on 26 November” [ref: d306.3/1].

### Ten‑Minute Rule Bill — Methanol poisoning (Travel Advice)
Laura Trott sought leave to bring in a Bill to require official travel advice to include “guidance about the risks of methanol poisoning” [ref: d294.2/2], describing bereaved constituents’ campaign and gaps in current guidance. The House agreed: “Question put and agreed to.” [ref: d294.2/15]. The Bill was presented and read a First time; Second Reading is scheduled for Friday 29 May 2026 [ref: d294.2/19].

### Opposition Day — Property Taxes
The motion cited reports of possible property tax rises and called on the Government to rule out measures such as capital gains tax on primary residences and an annual property levy [ref: d297.4/2]. Responding, the Chief Secretary declined to comment on “speculation” ahead of the Budget and confirmed it “will take place on 26 November” [ref: d306.3/1]. The motion was defeated: “The House divided: Ayes 98, Noes 335.” [ref: d336.1/1].

### Opposition Day — Hospitality Sector
The motion regretted Government choices said to be causing closures and job losses and called for a dedicated strategy [ref: d340.2/2]. Opposition speakers linked job losses to employer national insurance increases, reduced rates relief and proposed employment reforms; UKHospitality was quoted as saying, “More than half of all job losses since October occurring in hospitality…” [ref: d341.7/3]. The Minister said the Government had protected smaller employers by “increasing the employment allowance to £10,500” [ref: d347.2/1] and intends that “from 2026‑27” there will be “permanently lower tax rates for retail, hospitality and leisure properties” with lower rateable values [ref: d346.1/2]. Closing, the Minister said, “I urge the House to reject the motion.” [ref: d402.0/11]. The motion was defeated: “Ayes 158, Noes 334.” [ref: d404.1/1].

### Delegated Legislation and Business without Debate
The House approved several statutory instruments, including: “That the draft Free‑Range Poultrymeat Marketing Standards (Amendment) (England) Regulations 2025… be approved.” [ref: d409.1/1], and company law and transparency regulations [refs: d409.3/1; d409.7/1]. Each was agreed: “Question agreed to.” [refs: d409.1/2; d409.3/2; d409.7/2].

### Petition — Recognition of Palestinian Statehood
Liam Byrne presented a petition urging recognition of Palestinian statehood, saying of the Government’s plan, “They must now follow it through” [ref: d410.1/2]. The petitioners “request that the House of Commons urge the Government to recognise Palestinian statehood.” [ref: d410.1/3].

### Adjournment Debate — Diabetes in Sport
Christopher Bloore highlighted barriers facing people with diabetes in sport, citing stigma, exclusion and positive initiatives such as Chris Bright “setting up the Diabetes Football Community” [ref: d411.2/3]. He called for better policies by sporting bodies. The Minister outlined cross‑Government and Sport England programmes (e.g. Moving Medicine and Buddle) and said, “I am very happy to meet my hon. Friend … and the organisations that he mentioned.” [ref: d416.2/6].
