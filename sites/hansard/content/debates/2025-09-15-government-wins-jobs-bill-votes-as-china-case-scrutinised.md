## High-Level Summary
The Commons day opened with Home Office oral questions on policing, immigration, protests and animal testing. The Security Minister then updated MPs on the Crown Prosecution Service decision not to proceed with charges in a China‑related espionage case, outlining new national security measures and safeguards. The Speaker granted an SO24 emergency debate on the appointment and dismissal of the former UK Ambassador to the United States. In the afternoon and evening, MPs considered and largely rejected Lords amendments to the Employment Rights Bill in a series of divisions, while accepting targeted Government changes. The day concluded with an adjournment debate on council housing, with Ministers setting out funding and policy for expanded social and council homebuilding.

## Detailed Summary
### Home Office Oral Questions
Members questioned Ministers on visible policing and front counters, Channel crossings, neighbourhood policing, shoplifting, antisocial behaviour, human rights in immigration, grooming gangs, and topical issues following protests. Sarah Jones reiterated the Government’s neighbourhood policing plans: “there will be 3,000 more neighbourhood police on the beat by April next year” [ref: d1155.5/1], and described the neighbourhood policing guarantee with “a dedicated named officer in each neighbourhood, guaranteed response times and 3,000 more officers by April 2026” [ref: d1161.0/1]. On channel crossings, the Home Secretary said the National Crime Agency had led “347 disruptions of immigration crime networks—its highest level on record” [ref: d1158.2/1], called the France “one in, one out” arrangement a “landmark agreement”, and said first flights would be “as soon as possible” [ref: d1158.6/1], while rejecting Rwanda as a deterrent: “From the day that agreement was signed to the day it was cancelled, 84,000 people crossed” [ref: d1159.1/2]. 

On animal testing, Dan Jarvis said approvals are granted only when “no alternatives exist” and that “The Government will soon publish a strategy on how we will work towards phasing out the use of animals in science” [ref: d1157.3/1]. On retail crime, Sarah Jones said the Bill will remove the “effective immunity for shop thefts under £200” [ref: d1168.5/1]. On immigration and human rights, the Home Secretary stated: “This Government will legislate to limit the application of article 8 of the European convention on human rights” [ref: d1169.4/1]. On grooming gangs, Ministers confirmed progress towards a national inquiry: “The inquiry will be trauma-informed and time-limited” [ref: d1170.7/1]. Following protests, the Home Secretary condemned violence and stressed unity: “The St George’s cross and the Union Jack belong to us all” [ref: d1171.7/1]. Outcome: no divisions; Ministers set out ongoing and planned actions, including consultations and forthcoming legislation.

### Statement: CPS decision not to proceed in an Official Secrets Act case (China)
Security Minister Dan Jarvis announced that the CPS would not proceed with prosecutions under the Official Secrets Act 1911 in a case involving alleged Chinese espionage in Parliament, emphasising independence: “The decision not to proceed with this prosecution is an independent one for the CPS” [ref: d1181.2/2]. He outlined the modernised framework in the National Security Act 2023, which replaced the 1911 Act and introduced the Foreign Influence Registration Scheme, in force since 1 July [ref: d1181.2/8; d1181.2/9]. He said the Foreign Office had “démarched the chargé d’affaires of the Chinese embassy” [ref: d1181.2/10], and that new protective guidance would be issued to parliamentarians [ref: d1181.2/13]. 

Opposition Members pressed why the case had been dropped and whether any pressure was applied; Jarvis replied he could not speculate on CPS decisions [ref: d1184.0/6]. He said no decision had been taken on placing China in the enhanced tier of FIRS and that national security would guide any decision on a proposed new embassy site [ref: d1184.0/4; d1184.0/5]. He also stated: “Jimmy Lai should be released immediately” [ref: d1193.2/1]. Outcome: no vote; the Government committed to keep tools under review, legislate further on state threats, issue guidance, and continue international co‑operation.

### SO24 emergency debate granted: Ambassador to the United States
David Davis applied for an emergency debate on the appointment process and dismissal of the former UK Ambassador to the US, calling for transparency: “We need to know who knew what, and when” [ref: d1196.2/4]. The Speaker granted the application and scheduled the debate for the next sitting day: “The debate will be held tomorrow, Tuesday 16 September, as the first item of public business… for up to three hours” [ref: d1197.0/1]. Outcome: application agreed; debate scheduled.

### Employment Rights Bill: Consideration of Lords Amendments
Presenting the Bill, the Secretary of State called it “a landmark” and “pro‑worker and pro‑business” [ref: d1200.7/3]. Government changes included extending bereavement leave to cover pregnancy loss before 24 weeks [ref: d1205.2/2], and strengthening non‑disclosure agreement reforms so victims and witnesses can speak about harassment or discrimination, with consultation on secondary legislation [ref: d1207.2/3]. On zero‑hours contracts, Ministers opposed a switch to a right‑to‑request model, arguing the duty should rest with employers because a request model could create “undesirable barriers” for vulnerable workers [ref: d1208.1/1]. Short‑notice cancellation payments will be defined in regulations following consultation; the Bill caps “short notice” at no more than seven days [ref: d1209.1/3]. For special constables’ time off for public duties, the Government replaced a Lords amendment with a statutory commitment to review the section 50 ERA 1996 list and publish findings within 12 months [ref: d1209.1/4]. 

Key decisions by division included: rejecting Lords 1 (right to request guaranteed hours) (Ayes 326, Noes 160) [ref: d1260.3/1–d1260.3/3]; rejecting Lords 7 and 8 (48‑hour short‑notice rule) (Ayes 330–158; 316–172) [ref: d1264.3/1–d1268.1/3]; rejecting Lords 23 and 106–120 (six‑month unfair dismissal qualifying period) (Ayes 329, Noes 163; related amendments disagreed) [ref: d1271.5/1–d1275.1/3]; rejecting Lords 46 and 47 (whistleblowing duty; broader accompaniment right) (Ayes 314–178; 327–164) [ref: d1275.5/1–d1279.3/3]; rejecting Lords 48 and 49 (seasonal work definition; SME consultation duty) (Ayes 328–160; 332–160) [ref: d1283.3/1–d1287.3/3]; rejecting Lords 60 (heritage railways under‑16 volunteering) (Ayes 318, Noes 170) [ref: d1291.1/1–d1291.1/3]; rejecting Lords 61 (retain opt‑in to union political funds) (Ayes 330, Noes 161) [ref: d1294.3/1–d1294.3/3]; and rejecting Lords 62 (retain 50% turnout threshold for strikes) (Ayes 330, Noes 161) [ref: d1298.3/1–d1298.3/3]. Government amendments in lieu on special constables were agreed [ref: d1271.1/2]. Many other Lords amendments were agreed, with Commons financial privilege waived in some cases [ref: d1302.3/8–d1302.3/10]. Next steps: a Reasons Committee was appointed to communicate the Commons’ disagreements to the Lords [ref: d1302.3/11–d1302.3/17].

### Adjournment debate: Provision of council housing
Chris Hinchliff argued that only a major programme of council housing will meet need, noting past commitments—Labour’s mantra should be “council housing, council housing, council housing” [ref: d1308.2/2]—and that “more than 1.3 million households” are on waiting lists [ref: d1308.6/2]. He urged reforms to planning, right to buy and land valuation. The Minister said the Government will deliver the biggest increase in social and affordable housebuilding in a generation, with a 10‑year, £39 billion Affordable Homes Programme and “at least 60%” of homes for social rent [ref: d1315.0/3–d1315.0/4]. He added the programme could deliver “around 300,000 social and affordable homes… with around 180,000 being for social rent” [ref: d1315.0/4], outlined right‑to‑buy reforms including a 35‑year exemption for newly built homes and retention of 100% of receipts [ref: d1315.0/6], and referred to discounted Public Works Loan Board borrowing and rent convergence work [ref: d1315.0/5; d1315.0/7]. Outcome: debate concluded without a division; policies to proceed via forthcoming Budget decisions and legislation.
