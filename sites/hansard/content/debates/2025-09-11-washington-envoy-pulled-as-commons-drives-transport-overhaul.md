## High-Level Summary
The House of Commons focused on transport policy and performance, a high‑profile Urgent Question on the UK Ambassador to the United States, life sciences investment, business scheduling, biosecurity at the border, regional transport inequality, and suicide prevention. Ministers trailed an integrated national transport strategy and progress on the Bus Services (No. 2) Bill, alongside regional investment commitments. The Government confirmed Lord Mandelson’s withdrawal as Ambassador to Washington following new emails about his past links to Jeffrey Epstein. MPs also examined Merck’s cancellation of a £1 billion life sciences facility, heard an EFRA Committee warning on illegal meat imports, debated regional transport inequality, and held a wide‑ranging debate on suicide prevention before an Adjournment on ending male chick culling. No divisions occurred.

## Detailed Summary
### Oral Answers to Questions: Transport
The Secretary of State, Heidi Alexander, said rail performance is stabilising after a period of decline, with passenger numbers “up 7% last year” [ref: b1013.5/1]. On Avanti West Coast she said services would transfer to public ownership when contracts end and “We expect the transfers to have completed by the end of 2027” [ref: b1013.7/1]. On LNER she said December’s timetable will bring “an overall improvement in reliability and capacity” [ref: b1014.1/1], and highlighted that since public control, TransPennine Express has seen “a 75% reduction in cancellations and 42% growth in passenger numbers” [ref: b1014.4/1]. 

On buses, Minister Simon Lightwood promoted the Government’s Bus Services (No. 2) Bill, £1 billion support this year, and the extended £3 fare cap to March 2027 to deliver “more reliable, safe and inclusive routes” [ref: b1014.8/1]. He added the Bill will require staff to “give training to their staff” and allow byelaws to tackle antisocial behaviour and “violence against women and girls in particular” [ref: b1016.3/1]. On transport and growth, the Secretary of State cited “over £92 billion of capital investment in transport infrastructure” [ref: b1016.11/1], committed to legislating on taxi licensing inconsistencies [ref: b1017.1/1], said the proposed Universal Studios development would “prioritise public transport and active travel” [ref: b1018.1/1], and on HS2 land stated, “My first priority is to get HS2 built at the lowest reasonable cost” [ref: b1019.0/1]. In topical questions she cited a new electric car grant that “has already helped over 7,000 drivers to choose an EV” and promised “the first road safety strategy in over a decade” and an integrated transport strategy for England [ref: b1030.4/2], while noting, “No decisions have been made on next year’s rail fares” [ref: b1032.0/1]. No decisions were taken; Ministers offered multiple meetings and updates.

### Urgent Question: UK Ambassador to the United States
Minister Stephen Doughty announced the Prime Minister had asked the Foreign Secretary to withdraw Lord Mandelson as Ambassador to Washington after new emails showed the “depth and extent” of his relationship with Jeffrey Epstein was “materially different from that known at the time of his appointment,” including suggesting Epstein’s first conviction “was wrongful and should be challenged” [ref: b1038.2/3]. He confirmed Mandelson was withdrawn “with immediate effect” [ref: b1041.4/1], and praised “an excellent team… in British embassy Washington” to ensure continuity [ref: b1041.1/1]. 

Members pressed when the Prime Minister knew of the emails and called for publication of vetting papers; the Minister said “we will keep the House updated on these matters” [ref: b1043.2/1] and noted his understanding that Mandelson “is still an employee,” with proper processes to follow [ref: b1046.3/1]. Preparations for the forthcoming state visit continue. No division was sought.

### Urgent Question: Life Sciences Investment (MSD/King’s Cross)
Minister Ian Murray confirmed that Merck (MSD) would not progress its planned £1 billion research centre at King’s Cross, describing it as a commercial decision that was “deeply disappointing” [ref: b1050.2/2]. He stressed that MSD “will continue to be a key investor in the UK” through trials and partnerships [ref: b1050.2/8], and pointed to MSD’s reported need to cut “$3 billion a year from its operating costs” as context [ref: b1052.0/2]. 

He highlighted the £86 billion public R&D settlement and the life sciences sector plan, and the Science, Innovation and Technology Committee announced an urgent session on the sector [ref: b1053.2/1]. No policy changes were announced; the Minister offered meetings with Members and firms [ref: b1053.3/2]. No division.

### Business of the House
Leader of the House Alan Campbell set out business to the conference recess, including consideration of Lords amendments to the Employment Rights Bill and Second Reading of the Sentencing Bill, and business for the week of 13 October (e.g. remaining stages of the Mental Health Bill [Lords] and Sustainable Aviation Fuel Bill, and Second Reading of the Biodiversity Beyond National Jurisdiction Bill) [ref: b1061.3/2–b1061.3/12]. In response to a question on Northern Ireland legacy matters he said, “the Government have given a commitment to bringing forward legislation… we will be saying something shortly” [ref: b1062.0/8]. No division.

### Select Committee Statement: Biosecurity at the Border – Britain’s Illegal Meat Crisis (EFRA)
EFRA Chair Alistair Carmichael described the situation as a “crisis” and warned that 235 tonnes of illegal animal products were seized last year despite low levels of checks [ref: b1083.4/2; b1083.4/4]. The Committee heard of deteriorating hygiene and larger shipments at Dover and raised concerns about funding and facilities; it urged Government to publish quarterly seizure data and model risks [ref: b1083.4/6; b1083.4/9]. One witness called meat smuggling “the biggest threat to food security we have had for a long time” [ref: b1083.4/11]. 

The Committee recommended a DEFRA‑led taskforce and powers/funding for port health authorities to “stop, search and seize” animal products [ref: b1083.4/15–b1083.4/16]. The Government response was not set out in the statement. No division.

### Backbench Business: Regional Transport Inequality
Catherine Atkinson (Derby North) highlighted historic under‑investment in regions such as the east midlands, citing spend of “£368 per person” in 2023‑24 and supporting midland main line electrification beyond Wigston [ref: b1088.3/8; b1089.1/2]. Members across the House raised local disparities in bus, rail and roads; examples included service reliability issues and missing links. 

Minister Simon Lightwood said the Government will publish an integrated national transport strategy, maintain the £3 bus fare cap (“We will extend the £3 bus fare” [ref: b1120.3/6]) and invest £1 billion in buses [ref: b1120.3/6]. He cited £15.6 billion for city mayors and extra local authority resources [ref: b1120.3/7; b1122.1/2]. For the east midlands he pointed to over £2 billion for combined authority transport and over £450 million in local grants, noting per‑head spend of “£561… against an average of £398” [ref: b1122.3/6]. He said further midland main line electrification has paused but “will be kept under review” [ref: b1122.3/7]. The motion was agreed without division [ref: b1124.2/3–b1124.2/4].

### General Debate: Suicide Prevention
Opening the debate, Lee Pitcher (Doncaster East and the Isle of Axholme) said 7,055 people died by suicide in 2023, calling it the leading cause of death for men under 50 and for men and women aged 20–34 [ref: b1125.2/11]. He argued that “Suicide is not inevitable; it is preventable” and called for mandatory first‑responder training, better research into demographic gaps, and rapid roll‑out of community mental health hubs [ref: b1125.2/12; b1125.2/15–b1125.2/19]. 

Replying, the Minister (Dr Ahmed) confirmed investment “up to £120 million to bring the number of mental health emergency departments up to 85” [ref: b1145.1/6], said NHS England has issued “Staying Safe from Suicide” guidance and is launching an e‑learning module [ref: b1145.1/7], and committed that “every school in England will have access to a mental health support team” by the end of the Parliament [ref: b1145.1/8]. He highlighted Online Safety Act protections against suicide/self‑harm content [ref: b1145.1/9–b1145.1/10] and noted mandatory suicide‑awareness training for prison and DWP frontline staff [ref: b1145.1/11]. The House agreed the motion without division [ref: b1148.1/4–b1148.1/6].

### Adjournment Debate: Male Chick Culling
Irene Campbell (North Ayrshire and Arran) said an estimated “around 45 million” male chicks are culled annually in the UK and urged a ban, citing in‑ovo sexing as an alternative and polling support for paying 1p more per egg [ref: b1150.5/2; b1151.1/3]. She noted that several EU countries have legislated against culling [ref: b1150.5/5]. 

Minister Angela Eagle said UK hatcheries use argon gas stunning and that, while regulated, the issue is “principally an ethical issue, rather than a welfare problem,” so viable alternatives must be in place first [ref: b1152.0/4–b1152.0/5]. She cautioned that some bans had led to culling being shifted across borders [ref: b1152.0/6], welcomed “day‑zero sexing technology” [ref: b1153.1/4] and highlighted Government‑funded research into dual‑purpose breeds that may reduce culling while delivering welfare and sustainability benefits [ref: b1153.1/6–b1153.1/9]. No policy change was announced.
