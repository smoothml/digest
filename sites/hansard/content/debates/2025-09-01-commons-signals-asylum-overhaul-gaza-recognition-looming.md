## High-Level Summary
The Commons held Work and Pensions questions on unemployment, youth support and disability benefits, followed by major statements on borders and asylum, the Middle East, and Ukraine. The Home Secretary announced a France returns pilot, appeals reform and a temporary suspension of the refugee family reunion route, alongside plans to end asylum hotel use in an orderly way. The Foreign Secretary detailed the humanitarian crisis in Gaza, announced £15 million in extra aid, set out the intention to recognise a Palestinian state absent substantive Israeli steps, and confirmed triggering UN “snapback” on Iran. The Defence Secretary confirmed a £10 billion Type 26 frigate export to Norway and further UK support for Ukraine. The Joint Committee on Human Rights reported on transnational repression, and MPs debated commemoration of the Lisbon Maru.

## Detailed Summary
### Work and Pensions: Employment, Youth, PIP and Poverty
Alison McGovern reported headline labour market figures: “employment is up by 725,000, to 75.3% since July 2024” [ref: c1.5/1]. She later noted that “in July 2025, there were 768,000 people aged 16 to 24 on universal credit” [ref: c9.7/1]. Members raised local increases in claims and pressed for evidence that programmes are moving people into work.

Ministers highlighted youth guarantee trailblazers and sector partnerships (hospitality and construction), with extra funding: “further funding of £45 million for our eight youth guarantee trailblazers” [ref: c6.5/1] and “investing £80 million in our trailblazer programmes” [ref: c16.0/1]. On disability, Stephen Timms said the health transformation programme will enable “a modern digital service” for PIP and shorter decision times [ref: c7.7/1], and confirmed a co‑produced PIP review covering the mobility component and gateway entitlements such as Motability [ref: c5.6/1]. He also cited “£3.8 billion… in employment support for people out of work on health and disability grounds” [ref: c5.1/1]. Next steps include the PIP assessment review, continued roll‑out/evaluation of trailblazers, and publication of a child poverty strategy in the autumn [ref: c9.4/1].

### Borders and Asylum: Home Secretary’s Statement and Exchange
Yvette Cooper announced a treaty with France enabling direct returns of small‑boat arrivals: “the first detentions… on arrival in Dover took place the next day, and we expect the first returns to begin later this month” [ref: c23.2/5]. She proposed speeding appeals—“the border security Bill will introduce a statutory timeframe of 24 weeks” [ref: c23.2/10]—and a new “independent body to deal with immigration and asylum appeals” [ref: c23.2/11]. She also said, “this week we are… to temporarily suspend new applications under the existing dedicated refugee family reunion route” pending new rules [ref: c23.2/16].

Opposition and backbenchers challenged crossings and hotel use—one cited “29,003” crossings so far this year [ref: c27.1/3]. The Home Secretary said all asylum hotels will close “in a controlled and orderly manner” [ref: c23.2/18], with increased detention and returns capacity, including “a 1,000‑bed expansion at Campsfield and Haslar” [ref: c23.2/11]. Next steps include implementing the France pilot, expanding capacity, progressing the Border Security, Asylum and Immigration Bill, and setting out wider asylum reforms later this year [ref: c35.4/1].

### Middle East: Gaza Humanitarian Crisis, Recognition and Iran ‘Snapback’
David Lammy said a UN‑backed analysis confirmed “famine—famine in Gaza city; famine in its surrounding neighbourhoods” and called it “a man‑made famine” [ref: c60.2/2; c60.2/3]. He announced “an additional £15 million of aid and medical care for Gaza and the region,” including funding for “UK‑Med, whose field hospitals have treated more than 600,000 Gazans” [ref: c60.2/5], and work with WHO to bring critically ill children to the UK, with first patients expected within weeks [ref: c60.2/6].

He reiterated that the UK intends to recognise the state of Palestine “later this month, unless the Israeli Government take substantive steps” and that any Palestinian state should be demilitarised [ref: c60.2/13]. He confirmed that the UK, with France and Germany, had “triggered the snapback mechanism under UN Security Council resolution 2231” on Iran [ref: c60.2/14]. Members pressed for faster aid access, tougher sanctions and clarity on timing; the Foreign Secretary said an “immediate ceasefire” and “unconditional release” of hostages are essential [ref: c60.2/7], and noted the UK had “suspended arms exports that could be used in Gaza” [ref: c60.2/4].

### Ukraine: Type 26 Export and Continued Support
John Healey announced “a £10 billion contract to supply Norway with at least five Type 26 frigates” [ref: c92.1/2], calling it the largest British warship export, strengthening NATO and supporting UK jobs. He confirmed “£4.5 billion in military aid for Ukraine this year” and said the UK’s recent 50‑day drive delivered “nearly 5 million rounds of munitions… and 200 electronic warfare and air defence systems” [ref: c92.1/9].

He outlined coalition planning to secure any ceasefire—“plans to secure the skies and seas, and to train Ukrainian forces” [ref: c92.1/11]—and said the Foreign Secretary would announce further sanctions measures “very soon” [ref: c92.1/6]. Next steps include co‑chairing the next Ukraine Defence Contact Group, hosting an E5 Defence Ministers’ summit, and ongoing coalition planning and deliveries.

### Transnational Repression: Joint Committee on Human Rights Report
Presenting the JCHR report, Alex Sobel described transnational repression as a growing threat, noting “the number of state threat investigations run by MI5 has increased by 48%” [ref: c110.4/5]. Evidence to the Committee identified China, Russia and Iran as principal perpetrators. The report welcomed the foreign influence registration scheme but urged specifying China in its enhanced tier [ref: c110.4/11].

The Committee recommended a formal definition of transnational repression, improved data collection, a dedicated reporting hotline for victims [ref: c110.4/13], mandatory training for police beyond counter‑terrorism units [ref: c110.4/12], and a review of the sanctions framework to consider transnational‑repression‑specific measures [ref: c110.4/16].

### Lisbon Maru Memorial: Commemoration Debate
Members recounted the sinking of the Lisbon Maru on 1 October 1942: “the Lisbon Maru was torpedoed… the POWs were locked in the ship’s hold” and many were killed, while Chinese fishermen “risked their lives to rescue hundreds of survivors” [ref: c115.2/3; c115.2/4]. The debate highlighted the loss of “the 373 Royal Scots who perished” [ref: c115.4/2].

The Government pointed to an existing memorial “at the National Memorial Arboretum in Staffordshire” with annual commemorations [ref: c117.1/7], and encouraged devolved and local bodies to consider further remembrance, noting heritage is devolved in Scotland [ref: c117.1/8].
