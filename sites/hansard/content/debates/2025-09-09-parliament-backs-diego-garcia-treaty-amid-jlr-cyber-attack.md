## High-Level Summary
The House began with Treasury oral questions on long-term infrastructure, employer national insurance and public finances, with Ministers pointing repeatedly to spending‑review allocations and forthcoming Budget measures. An urgent question on the Jaguar Land Rover cyber‑attack set out Government and National Cyber Security Centre support, planned engagement with the company and the prospect of future cyber legislation. The Diego Garcia Military Base and British Indian Ocean Territory Bill received a lengthy Second Reading debate before the House rejected a reasoned amendment and approved the Bill. Without debate, Members agreed two financial services statutory instruments and changes to the Modernisation Committee, and later considered a petition on development near Lutterworth. The day ended with an adjournment debate on improving station accessibility in Dulwich and West Norwood.

## Detailed Summary
### Committee of Selection
The House ordered changes to the Committee of Selection, recording the decision formally as “Ordered,” [ref: c703.2/1]. (The motion named Members to be discharged and added.)

### Treasury: Capital and Infrastructure Funding (Oral Questions)
Answering questions on infrastructure funding and maintenance, the Chief Secretary said the Government will fund “at least £725 billion of infrastructure over the next decade” [ref: c703.9/2]. On the M48 Severn Bridge, he highlighted that National Highways has an allocated capital settlement, including “£24 billion of capital funding… [and] £1 billion to enhance local road networks and create a new structures fund” [ref: c704.0/1]. The Chancellor described the 10‑year national infrastructure strategy as “core to delivering this Government’s growth mission” [ref: c704.7/2]. MPs raised constituency issues (e.g. local rail rolling stock, regional transport integration and Heathrow expansion); Ministers pointed to existing allocations, said they would engage with relevant Departments, and announced no new spending lines during the session.

### Employer National Insurance and Business Taxes
On employer national insurance and support for small firms, the Exchequer Secretary said the Government had increased the employment allowance “from £5,000 to £10,500. That means that 865,000 employers will pay no national insurance contributions at all” [ref: c707.6/1]. He committed to meet UKHospitality and said more detail on support for the sector and business rates reform would come at the Budget: “We will come forward with more details on that in the Budget later this year” [ref: c707.8/2]. Ministers argued that last year’s changes protected the smallest employers and funded public services, while Members expressing concern about a ‘jobs tax’ said the increases were harming hospitality and small business hiring; the Exchequer Secretary noted the NI decision “to raise £20 billion” for services [ref: c708.3/1].

### Agricultural and Business Property Reliefs (Farming)
On reforms to agricultural property relief and business property relief, the Chief Secretary said he had met a wide range of farming bodies, “including the National Farmers Union, the Tenant Farmers Association… NFU Cymru, NFU Scotland and the Farmers Union of Wales” [ref: c710.4/1]. He maintained that the approach from April would “maintain generous tax reliefs within the agricultural property relief and business property relief system, while also raising revenue in a fair way to support the public finances” [ref: c710.8/1]. No policy change was announced; Ministers emphasised ongoing engagement.

### Government Debt Interest and Public Finances
Members asked about debt interest and market confidence. The Chancellor linked fiscal plans to stability, saying, “we published the spending review earlier this year” [ref: c712.5/1], and referred to Office for Budget Responsibility forecasts that “debt is going to fall during the course of this Parliament… [and] the deficit as a share of GDP will fall by 1 percentage point this year” [ref: c712.7/1]. No immediate policy changes were announced; Ministers pointed to the spending review and forthcoming Budget.

### Jaguar Land Rover Cyber‑attack (Urgent Question)
Responding to an urgent question, the Business and Trade Minister said, “The Government and the National Cyber Security Centre will do everything in our power to help resolve this as soon as possible” [ref: c725.3/1]. He confirmed the NCSC “has been engaged since last Wednesday” [ref: c727.4/2], that he would meet JLR’s chief executive, and that the company had invited local MPs to a Q&A on Friday [ref: c725.3/3]. He set out current tools and guidance (including the cyber essentials scheme) and said a cyber security and resilience Bill would be introduced “when parliamentary time allows… soon” [ref: c725.3/5]. He declined to attribute the attack or confirm whether it was state‑sponsored, citing ongoing investigations [ref: c727.4/6]. Next steps include continued NCSC engagement and ministerial meetings.

### Diego Garcia Military Base and British Indian Ocean Territory Bill — Second Reading
Introducing the Bill to implement the UK–Mauritius treaty, the Minister said, “This treaty is indispensable to keeping Britain secure at home and strong abroad” [ref: c740.7/2], arguing it secures long‑term operational control of the UK‑US base and removes legal risks. Opponents challenged the treaty’s cash cost, sovereignty implications, environmental protections and Chagossian rights; a reasoned amendment cited “£34.7 billion” [ref: c754.3/2]. The House divided twice: the reasoned amendment was negatived (Ayes 116, Noes 333) [ref: c832.1/1], and the Bill was read a Second time (Ayes 330, Noes 179) [ref: c835.1/1]. A programme motion then committed the Bill to a Committee of the whole House with specified time limits [ref: c839.2/4; c839.2/6].

### Delegated Legislation and Committee Changes (Business without Debate)
The House approved two financial services statutory instruments—“That the draft Markets in Financial Instruments (Miscellaneous Amendments) Regulations 2025… be approved” and the related consequential regulations [ref: c839.6/1–c839.6/3]—and agreed changes to the Modernisation Committee, including that “Sir Alan Campbell shall be the Chair of the Committee” [ref: c840.1/4].

### Petition: Development Pressures near Lutterworth
Alberto Costa presented a petition from 241 residents on development proposals around Lutterworth. He began, “I rise to present a petition from 241 residents of South Leicestershire” [ref: c840.3/1], and the petition asked the House to urge Government to ensure local planning policies are applied and to protect affordable housing proportions: “The petitioners therefore request that the House of Commons urge the Government to intervene…” [ref: c840.3/6]. The petition was recorded in the Votes and Proceedings.

### Adjournment Debate: Accessibility of Railway Stations in Dulwich and West Norwood
Helen Hayes highlighted significant accessibility gaps across 10 local stations, saying she was “grateful for the opportunity to bring before the House the significant issues that affect my constituents” [ref: c841.2/1], and sought greater prioritisation and pre‑bid feasibility support within the Access for All programme. The Rail Minister noted progress—“To date, we have delivered step‑free access at over 260 stations” [ref: c845.1/4]—and offered to facilitate a meeting on local proposals [ref: c845.1/6]. The adjournment was agreed and the House rose [ref: c848.1/4].
