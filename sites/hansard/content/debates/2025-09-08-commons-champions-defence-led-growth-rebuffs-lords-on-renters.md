## High-Level Summary
MPs quizzed Defence ministers on industry, veterans’ services, NATO spending and recruitment before two urgent questions on protests linked to the proscription of Palestine Action and on the Omar al‑Bayoumi case. Later, Defence Minister Luke Pollard set out a defence industrial strategy centred on skills, SMEs, exports and innovation. The Commons then considered Lords amendments to the Renters’ Rights Bill, rejecting several prominent changes in multiple divisions while accepting limited technical changes. The day concluded with an adjournment debate on targeted support for remote coastal communities and a ministerial response highlighting investment and fair‑funding reforms.

## Detailed Summary
### Oral Answers to Defence Questions
The Defence Secretary, John Healey, trailed the defence industrial strategy, saying it would “make defence an engine for growth in every region and nation of the UK” and put Britain “at the leading edge of innovation within NATO.” [ref: e567.8/2] Ministers stressed SME support as part of the strategy: “the Ministry of Defence will strengthen the defence industrial base… supporting small and medium-sized enterprises” [ref: e581.7/1]. Healey welcomed the Type 26 export to Norway as “the biggest British warship deal ever” [ref: e569.3/1]. 

On NATO spending, Healey said all 32 allies had agreed to increase national security spending to 5% by 2035 and that the UK “played a leading part” in the agreement [ref: e572.3/1]. He described the Diego Garcia settlement as split between the MOD and FCDO, adding, “For defence, our commitment is less than 0.2% of the defence budget.” [ref: e573.1/1] He pledged procurement reform after the Public Accounts Committee branded the system “broken” [ref: e572.7/1]. The Veterans Minister announced £50 million over three years for ‘Valour’ to tackle the “postcode lottery” in support [ref: e569.7/1], and on Northern Ireland legacy said the Government would repeal and replace the Act to secure truth while ensuring “protections are in place… so that they are not subject to lawfare” [ref: e571.2/1, e571.5/1]. Ministers said 33 recommendations from the Atherton report were accepted, with a tri‑service complaints system and a violence against women and girls taskforce [ref: e574.3/1]. On small boats, a minister said Operation Isotrope found naval assets “only made it more dangerous” [ref: e575.2/1]. A defence skills package would “grow, retain and develop” the workforce [ref: e576.2/1]; recruitment and retention were said to be improving following “the largest pay rise in 20 years” [ref: e577.3/2]. On Ukraine, Healey condemned the “worst” air attack since the invasion and said over £1 billion of profits from frozen Russian assets had been put into military aid ahead of hosting the Ukraine Defence Contact Group in London [ref: e580.9/1].

### Urgent Question: Palestine Action—Proscription and Protests
Security Minister Dan Jarvis affirmed the right to protest within the law but distinguished that from support for a proscribed group: “supporting Palestine and supporting a proscribed terrorist organisation are not the same thing.” [ref: e591.3/1] He cited Metropolitan Police figures that “a total of 890 arrests were made” at a central London demonstration, “Most… under section 13 of the Terrorism Act 2000 for displaying articles in support of Palestine Action,” contrasting that with 20,000 who marched peacefully [ref: e591.3/4]. 

Jarvis rejected claims of a ‘chilling effect’, noting “tens of thousands of people came to London to exercise their absolute right to demonstrate” and said the Government had “no plans to amend the existing legislation.” [ref: e595.3/1, e595.3/2] He expressed “absolute abhorrence of antisemitism” [ref: e594.1/1], stressed operational independence of the police, and said the law is applied “without fear or favour” and that “nobody is above the law.” [ref: e596.1/1] MPs pressed for proportionality, data on charging outcomes and resourcing; Jarvis pointed to the role of the independent reviewer of terrorism legislation and said ongoing legal proceedings limited comment on specifics [ref: e595.3/2–e595.3/3].

### Urgent Question: Omar al‑Bayoumi—Arrest and Extradition
Jarvis paid tribute to 9/11 victims: “The attacks on 9/11 were an appalling assault on freedom.” [ref: e610.2/1] He declined to comment on the specific case, citing a long‑standing policy and ongoing US civil proceedings: “It would be inappropriate to comment on an individual case… [we] neither confirm nor deny an arrest for the purpose of an extradition request.” [ref: e610.2/2] He outlined the extradition framework under the Extradition Act 2003 [ref: e610.2/3–e610.2/4]. Responding to calls for scrutiny, he said the Government would look carefully at the issues raised and noted that engagement with the Intelligence and Security Committee was appropriate on sensitive matters [ref: e612.1/2].

### Statement: Defence Industrial Strategy
Defence Minister Luke Pollard announced a plan “supported by £773 million of investment—a plan to make defence an engine for economic growth in every nation and region of our country.” [ref: e615.1/3] Measures include a new office for small business growth and an office of defence exports to bolster SME participation and exports [ref: e615.1/7–e615.1/9]. The strategy creates £250 million defence growth deals (first in Plymouth and South Yorkshire, with more in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland) [ref: e615.1/11], a £182 million investment to establish five defence technical excellence colleges and a defence skills passport [ref: e615.1/12], and commits at least 10% of the equipment budget to novel technologies via UK Defence Innovation backed by £400 million [ref: e615.1/14]. Procurement reforms aim to reduce timescales and improve export licensing after the system was branded “broken” [ref: e615.1/15]. 

Shadow questioning focused on timing, governance and funding—raising delays, the National Armaments Director appointment, whether growth‑deal money was new, and the pace of contracts (e.g., drones, helicopters) [ref: e617.1/4, e617.1/7–e617.1/10]. Pollard replied that an interim National Armaments Director (Andy Start) was in place, the growth‑deal funding was new, “we have signed 900 deals for defence procurement contracts since the election,” and the plan targets British firms and SMEs [ref: e619.0/2–e619.0/3].

### Renters’ Rights Bill—Consideration of Lords Amendments
Housing Minister Matthew Pennycook said the Bill delivers the mandate “to modernise the regulation of our country’s insecure and unjust private rented sector” and must pass “as quickly as possible” [ref: e635.6/1]. He explained the removal of a pet‑damage insurance pathway due to market readiness and opposed a separate pet deposit, arguing such a deposit would cost “over £900” on average and that the existing five‑week cap suffices, with powers available to adjust later [ref: e636.1/5, e636.1/8]. He defended retaining a 12‑month no‑let period after evictions to sell—“It is essential to prevent landlords misusing ground 1A” and the Government remains “firmly committed” to 12 months [ref: e636.1/5]. He rejected widening student and carer possession grounds and keeping a criminal (rather than civil) standard for new enforcement powers [ref: e636.1/3–e636.1/4, e636.1/9]. On service family accommodation, he argued local inspections were “unworkable” but committed the MOD to publish an annual report on standards [ref: e636.1/10, e636.1/12]. He accepted a narrow technical expansion of agricultural grounds with Government amendments in lieu [ref: e636.1/13–e636.1/14]. 

The Opposition criticised elements of the Bill and backed several Lords changes (on pets, students and carers), warning of supply impacts and describing the Bill as “poorly though through and counterproductive” [ref: e640.3/2]. Other Members urged swift commencement and opposed a higher standard of proof and a shorter no‑let period (“Let us leave no loopholes…” [ref: e645.0/4]). The Commons disagreed with the following Lords amendments (division results): 11 (pet deposit) Ayes 398, Noes 93 [ref: e661.1/2–e661.1/3]; 18 (reduce no‑let to six months) Ayes 402, Noes 97 [ref: e665.3/1–e665.3/3]; 19 (shared owners’ exemption) Ayes 336, Noes 158 [ref: e669.1/1–e669.1/3]; 26 (criminal standard of proof) Ayes 404, Noes 98 [ref: e673.3/1–e673.3/3]; 27 (related) disagreed [ref: e673.3/4]; 39 (apply decent homes to service family accommodation) Ayes 325, Noes 171 [ref: e677.3/1–e677.3/3]; 53 (expand student ground 4A) Ayes 401, Noes 96 [ref: e680.3/1–e680.3/3]; 64 (new carer ground) Ayes 335, Noes 160 [ref: e684.1/1–e684.1/3]. The House disagreed with 55–62 but made a Government amendment in lieu adjusting agricultural grounds [ref: e680.3/4–e680.3/5]; several other Lords amendments were agreed, with financial privilege waived for 68 [ref: e684.1/6]. Pennycook confirmed implementation of the new tenancy system “in one stage,” applying to all private tenancies on the commencement date [ref: e660.1/3].

### Adjournment Debate: Support for Remote Coastal Communities
Perran Moon (Camborne, Redruth and Hayle) argued that remote coastal areas face distinct, under‑measured pressures—geographical remoteness, seasonal tourism demand, housing pressures from second homes and short‑term lets, educational isolation, ageing demographics and health inequalities—and called for a dedicated strategy. He noted that “around 8.7 million people—15% of the population—lived in coastal settlements” [ref: e690.2/2], that “Tourism can bring prosperity, but also pressure” [ref: e691.2/1–e691.2/2], and that Cornwall has “the largest supply of short‑term lets… up by 30% since 2019” [ref: e692.2/1–e692.2/2]. He said the “indices of multiple deprivation do not see the full picture” and urged reform and a remote‑coastal strategy [ref: e694.1/4]. 

Local Government Minister Miatta Fahnbulleh acknowledged challenges, describing coastal places as “postcards of national pride” whose beauty can mask reality [ref: e699.0/2]. She cited targeted investment—“£24.7 million via the Camborne town deal” and £12 million via the UKSPF [ref: e700.3/2]—and a £1.5 billion plan for neighbourhoods, with “One in three of those communities… coastal” [ref: e700.3/3–e700.3/4]. On fair funding, she said the review will recognise remoteness and tourist impacts and that services must be designed for local needs. She highlighted flood and coastal defence spending and opportunities in clean energy and defence‑related hubs [ref: e700.3/4–e700.3/6].
