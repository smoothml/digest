## High-Level Summary
The Commons concentrated on justice policy and accountability. During Justice Questions, Ministers set out steps to reduce court backlogs, bolster probation and prison safety, and support victims, while announcing the Public Office (Accountability) Bill (the “Hillsborough law”). An emergency debate examined the appointment and dismissal of the former UK Ambassador to the US, with calls to publish vetting papers and enhance pre‑appointment scrutiny. The Government’s Sentencing Bill won Second Reading after a reasoned amendment was defeated, progressing reforms on community sentencing, tagging, victim protections and managing prison capacity. A Ten Minute Rule Bill to require a child poverty strategy including scrapping the two‑child limit passed First Reading after a division.

## Detailed Summary
### Speaker’s Statement: Clerk of Legislation’s retirement
The Speaker paid tribute to Colin Lee on his retirement after 37 years, thanking him for his “long and distinguished service” [ref: b1317.1/2], and light‑heartedly noted his fondness for Penguin biscuits [ref: b1317.1/3]. No decisions were required.

### Justice Questions: Court backlogs and system capacity
David Lammy said the Government inherited a “record courts backlog” and is funding “a record high allocation of 110,000 Crown court sitting days this year,” while considering reforms from Sir Brian Leveson [ref: b1318.2/2]. He confirmed efficiencies are the focus of Leveson’s second phase, “which goes to the heart” of delays [ref: b1318.7/1], and said Leveson would complete his review by year‑end [ref: b1319.2/1]. He cited a £92 million criminal legal aid increase as “the biggest extension of legal aid…in over a decade” for people harmed by the state [ref: b1319.4/1]. On rape delays, “We must have swifter justice for victims of rape” [ref: b1319.6/1]. He endorsed spreading caseloads between courts, such as Maidstone and Woolwich [ref: b1320.0/1], and agreed to examine concerns about the use of Terrorism Act powers [ref: b1320.2/1]. Lammy said the Crown Court backlog had risen by 77% under the previous Government and pledged to address it [ref: b1320.4/1]. No immediate decisions; next steps included awaiting Leveson’s recommendations and maximising courtroom utilisation.

### Justice Questions: Probation Service support and tagging
Lammy outlined investment of “up to £700 million” this spending review and recruitment of “1,300 trainee probation officers” next year [ref: b1321.3/1]. He promised joint working so early‑release risk assessments “are done” [ref: b1321.5/1] and committed to work with housing colleagues as accommodation on release remains “a big issue” [ref: b1321.7/1]. The Justice Committee Chair cited a reported “shortage of 10,000 probation officers” [ref: b1322.1/2]; Lammy said more recruitment is needed and pointed to the £700m uplift [ref: b1322.2/1]. On tagging delivery, he said of Serco, “We stepped in, and have fined it… I intend to hold Serco to account” [ref: b1322.7/1]. No policy change was announced; recruitment, technology fixes and cross‑government work were highlighted.

### Justice Questions: Court estate – restoration and renewal
Courts Minister Sarah Sackman said capital funding has risen “from £120 million last year to over £148 million for this year” and that new courts are being built while old ones are restored [ref: b1323.3/1]. She confirmed a consultation on Lancaster Crown court’s future [ref: b1323.8/1] and emphasised physical accessibility as part of “access to justice” [ref: b1323.10/1]. No divisions or decisions; the programme and consultations continue.

### Justice Questions: Prison safety
Lammy prioritised staff safety, citing “£40 million to stop the contraband,” rollout of protective body armour in high‑security units and Tasers trials for specialised staff [ref: b1324.2/1]. He said drone counter‑measures announcements are due “over the coming weeks” [ref: b1324.4/1], labelled assaults “entirely unacceptable” and promised further announcements “in the coming days” [ref: b1324.6/1]. On legal protections for prison officers akin to police, he said, “I will certainly be looking closely at this issue” [ref: b1324.9/1] and will consider a whole‑life order proposal for murdering prison officers [ref: b1325.1/1]. No immediate legislative change; commitments to explore enhanced protections and technology upgrades were given.

### Justice Questions: Prison rehabilitation and reoffending
Prisons Minister Jake Richards said the Government is “committed to tackling the root causes of reoffending,” including employment, accommodation and substance misuse treatment [ref: b1325.5/2]. He contrasted prison‑place delivery—“in 14 months we have added 2,500”—with the previous 14 years [ref: b1325.7/1], and undertook to work with health colleagues on continuity of care (e.g., TB treatment) to support rehabilitation [ref: b1326.0/1]. On overall trends, he noted a “long‑term downward trend” but that the UK “remains an outlier,” pointing to measures in the Sentencing Bill and regional employment councils [ref: b1330.4/1]. He agreed to review The Clink Charity situation [ref: b1330.6/1] and said housing post‑release is “a big challenge” to be addressed across Government [ref: b1331.0/1].

### Justice Questions: Support for victims of rape and sexual violence
Victims Minister Alex Davies‑Jones outlined funded support via the national witness service and independent sexual violence advocates, and said the Government is “committed to introducing free, independent legal advisers for victims of adult rape” [ref: b1326.3/1, b1326.5/1]. On delays, she said, “We know that justice delayed is justice denied,” linking backlog reform to the Leveson review [ref: b1327.0/1]. She defended Section 28 recorded testimony as helping victims remain in the process [ref: b1327.2/1], and said the Government is “committed to rolling out more specialist domestic abuse courts” [ref: b1327.4/1]. No votes; commitments centred on legal advice, special measures and specialist courts.

### Justice Questions: Funeral sector regulation
Following the Fuller inquiry, the Minister said the Government “recognise the urgency” of statutory regulation for funeral directors and will give an interim update this year, with a “final response… by summer 2026” [ref: b1328.8/1]. She agreed to meet affected families from Leeds [ref: b1329.1/1]. No legislative instrument was laid; a response timetable was set out.

### Justice Questions: Support for witnesses and jurors
Ministers highlighted support for witnesses via the witness service, which “provides emotional and practical on-the-day support” [ref: b1329.4/1], and undertook to look at support for jurors exposed to distressing evidence [ref: b1329.6/1]. On sentencing concerns after fatal driving cases, the Minister pointed to the Sentencing Bill debate later that day [ref: b1330.1/1].

### Topical Questions (Justice): Hillsborough law and other matters
As Deputy PM and Lord Chancellor, David Lammy announced the Public Office (Accountability) Bill, creating “a new professional and legal duty of candour” and offences for misleading the public [ref: b1331.3/2]. He said, “This law will be their legacy” for Hillsborough and other scandals [ref: b1331.3/3]. Other exchanges covered youth justice neurodiversity [ref: b1331.5/1], foreign national prisoner returns—“gone up 14%” [ref: b1332.0/1]—possible family court reform [ref: b1333.0/1], SEND tribunal delays [ref: b1333.4/1], inquest backlogs with reforms in the Hillsborough law [ref: b1335.1/1], clarification that sharia law “forms no part of the law of England and Wales” [ref: b1336.2/1], and support for tackling bullying and harassment in the legal professions [ref: b1336.9/1]. No decisions; clarifications and commitments were recorded.

### Points of Order
Members raised delayed answers and the handling of major announcements. The Speaker said a three‑month holding answer was “totally unacceptable” [ref: b1339.2/1], and, regarding UN findings on Gaza, said, “I expect the Government to come forward with a statement” [ref: b1340.2/1]. No formal motions resulted; expectations were set on the record.

### Bill Presented: Public Office (Accountability) Bill (‘Hillsborough law’)
The Government presented a Bill to “impose a duty on public authorities and public officials to act with candour” and create related offences and participation provisions in inquiries [ref: b1341.2/2]. It received First Reading and was scheduled for Second Reading on Monday 13 October [ref: b1341.2/3].

### Emergency Debate: Appointment and dismissal of the former UK Ambassador to the US
Under Standing Order No. 24, David Davis led a debate asking “who knew what, and when?” about Lord Mandelson’s appointment and removal [ref: b1342.2/6]. The Chair of the Foreign Affairs Committee, Emily Thornberry, described the Committee’s attempts to scrutinise the appointment, noting the Foreign Secretary’s letter said Cabinet Office propriety and ethics conducted due diligence pre‑announcement, with developed vetting conducted “to the usual standard” after the announcement [ref: b1351.3/9, b1352.3/3]. Opposition speakers called for publication of the “Mandelson‑Epstein files” [ref: b1355.8/3]. Minister Stephen Doughty said the PM acted after “new information showed that the nature and extent of Lord Mandelson’s relationship with Jeffrey Epstein was materially different,” and quoted the PM: “Had I known then what I know now, I’d have never appointed him” [ref: b1384.3/2, b1384.7/2‑b1384.7/3]. He said vetting is independent of Ministers and declined to disclose confidential vetting details [ref: b1386.0/2‑b1386.0/4]. The House agreed the motion that it had considered the appointment process and circumstances [ref: b1390.0/9‑b1390.0/11]. Ministers indicated openness to support Foreign Affairs Committee pre‑scrutiny options [ref: b1384.1/2; b1388.2/1].

### Ten Minute Rule Motion: Child Poverty Strategy (Removal of Two‑Child Limit)
Kirsty Blackman sought leave to bring in a Bill to require a child poverty strategy including removal of the two‑child limit in Universal Credit [ref: b1392.2/2]. She argued the policy is “cruel” and increases poverty; opponents cited cost and fairness to taxpayers. On division, Ayes were 89 and Noes 79 [ref: b1396.1/1]. First Reading was agreed and Second Reading set for Friday 7 November [ref: b1396.1/2, b1396.1/6].

### Sentencing Bill: Second Reading
Introducing the Bill, the Deputy PM said it addresses the risk that “our prisons could run of out places entirely” and aims for “punishment that works” with public protection [ref: b1399.3/4, b1399.3/5]. Key elements include an earned progression model with new release points—“a minimum of one third will be served in prison” for standard determinate sentences, and at least half for more serious cases [ref: b1402.6/3‑b1402.6/4]—plus tougher prison discipline by doubling the maximum additional days to 84 [ref: b1402.6/4]. He stressed, “We are not abolishing short sentences,” setting exceptions where custody remains vital [ref: b1403.1/1‑b1403.1/2], and outlined stricter licences (e.g., bans on pubs or driving) [ref: b1403.1/3] and a presumption to tag on release [ref: b1404.1/2]. Victim‑centred measures include new “restriction zones” to “pin any offender down to a specific location” [ref: b1404.1/14], and a judicial finding of domestic abuse at sentencing [ref: b1406.0/1]. The Shadow’s reasoned amendment warning the Bill would free dangerous offenders was defeated, Ayes 78, Noes 292 [ref: b1457.1/1‑b1457.1/3]. The Bill then passed Second Reading, Ayes 340, Noes 77 [ref: b1460.1/1‑b1460.1/3], and was programmed for Committee of the whole House over two days [ref: b1463.1/5‑b1463.1/10].

### Delegated Legislation: Building Safety Levy (England) Regulations 2025
The draft Regulations were approved without division [ref: b1464.4/1‑b1464.4/2].

### Adjournment Debate: Police professional standards
Ben Maguire raised concerns about complaints handling and public confidence. The Policing Minister noted ongoing standards reforms and said, “We will put the vetting standards on statutory footing through regulations that we expect to lay this year” [ref: b1470.1/1]. She acknowledged improvements at Devon and Cornwall police, now out of “engage,” and said further legislation on suspension for officers under criminal investigation is expected “later this year” [ref: b1470.1/3‑b1470.1/4]. She said a White Paper on police reform is forthcoming and undertook to consider the specific cases raised [ref: b1472.1/2].
