## High-Level Summary
The House held oral questions to Environment, Food and Rural Affairs on climate impacts, water quality and farming support, followed by questions to the Solicitor General on people smuggling, grooming gangs and the criminal justice backlog. The Leader of the House outlined the coming fortnight’s business, and a ministerial statement announced the roll‑out of 30 hours’ funded childcare for children from nine months, alongside new funding for school‑based nurseries. The Commons then considered Lords amendments to the House of Lords (Hereditary Peers) Bill and rejected three key amendments in recorded divisions, agreeing technical amendments on resignation by power of attorney. An Adjournment debate examined the BBC Monitoring service, with Ministers affirming its strategic value and cooperation with the US Open Source Enterprise.

## Detailed Summary
### Climate Change: Food Security
Daniel Zeichner said the Government is responding to climate pressures on farming by flood defence spending and nature‑friendly farming, citing “investing a record £8 billion in flood defences” [ref: a419.5/1]. Matt Western highlighted recent poor harvests and the Bank of England’s view that “extreme weather is now one of the key factors in driving food price inflation” [ref: a419.6/1]. Zeichner also agreed to further engagement with research and biotechnologies, noting, “I have had numerous conversations with leading academics” [ref: a420.2/1]. No decisions were announced; Ministers referred to ongoing work and future updates.

### Water Company Executives and Household Water Bills
The Secretary of State condemned previous years’ executive bonuses and said the Government “have banned the payment of unfair bonuses” and introduced tougher sanctions [ref: a421.0/1]. He said record investment has been secured to upgrade infrastructure, stating “we have secured a record £104 billion to upgrade crumbling pipes” [ref: a421.2/1], alongside ring‑fencing bill‑payers’ money. Members pressed issues including Scottish water performance, to which the Secretary of State replied, “pollution under the SNP in Scotland is even worse than it was under the Tories in England” [ref: a423.3/1]. Ministers said Ofwat is assessing contentious payments and promised stronger regulatory oversight; no votes or statutory changes arose from these exchanges.

### Moorland Protection and Wildfires
Responding to questions following the North York Moors wildfire, DEFRA said it is restoring peatlands, managing grazing and reviewing protections. Daniel Zeichner said the Government has “been consulting on ending locational burning to improve moorland resilience to wildfire” [ref: a424.4/1], and that proposals would be brought forward “shortly” [ref: a424.6/1]. Members paid tribute to firefighters and volunteers. No changes to law were announced; Ministers said consultation responses and cross‑departmental issues (including fire and rescue responsibilities) are being considered.

### Dorset Coast and Bathing Waters: Pollution
On coastal sewage, the Secretary of State said the five‑year investment plan will enable Wessex Water “to cut storm overflow spills by 43%” [ref: a425.4/1]. On bathing waters, the Minister said the Government will “halve sewage water pollution by 2030” [ref: a426.4/1], and a Member highlighted a £50 million upgrade at Southport works to cut overflows [ref: a426.5/1]. Ministers emphasised catchment‑based planning and further regional engagement; no vote was taken.

### Sustainable Farming Incentive (SFI)
Answering on access for small and medium‑sized farms, Daniel Zeichner said funding for environmental land management would rise “by 150%, from £800 million in 2023‑24 to £2 billion by 2028‑29” [ref: a428.2/1]. He acknowledged past scheme issues and said reforms and local advisory support are in development to help smaller farms, adding “we are learning from past SFI iterations” [ref: a428.4/1]. Further detail was promised “shortly”; no firm dates were given on the floor of the House.

### Solicitor General: People Smuggling, Grooming Gangs and Criminal Justice
Lucy Rigby said prosecutions for facilitating illegal entry have risen, with “67% more offences … prosecuted” this year [ref: a433.1/1], and that forthcoming legislation will criminalise the creation of online material facilitating breaches of immigration law. On grooming gangs and CSE, she said “The CPS has significantly increased prosecutions for child sex offences” [ref: a435.4/1], and described measures to improve listings and support for victims. On court backlogs, she cited a CPS “surge team” that “has completed more than 12,000 pre‑charge decisions” [ref: a439.7/1]. No divisions were held on these items; measures include operational resourcing and legislation in train.

### Business of the House
Lucy Powell set out business for the weeks of 8 and 15 September, including “Consideration of Lords amendments to the Renters’ Rights Bill” [ref: a443.2/2] and the Second Reading of further Bills. She also notified the House that the Chancellor “will deliver her Budget statement on Wednesday 26 November” [ref: a443.2/11], and confirmed conference recess dates. Members requested time for debates on a range of issues; Ministers undertook to consider scheduling and arrange ministerial follow‑ups.

### Early Education and Childcare (Ministerial Statement)
Stephen Morgan announced the full roll‑out of “30 hours of funded childcare each week, from when their child turns nine months until they start school”, benefitting “hundreds of thousands of parents” [ref: a477.1/4]. He launched phase 2 of the school‑based nurseries programme “Backed by £45 million” [ref: a477.1/9], and confirmed a phase 3 launch in early 2026 with multi‑year local proposals [ref: a477.1/11]. Opposition speakers welcomed the expansion but warned of cost pressures; one said the national insurance increase “has created a ‘perfect storm’” for providers [ref: a479.1/6]. The Minister said funding distribution will be reviewed and offered to work with Committees and the sector on workforce and sufficiency.

### Point of Order: Rectification Procedure
Clive Lewis apologised for failing to declare an interest when making certain contributions, stating, “I would like to apologise to the House for failing to declare an interest… I am the chair of the British Caribbean Association” [ref: a497.2/1]. The Chair acknowledged the statement and closed the matter for that sitting [ref: a497.3/1].

### Privilege: Charity Commission / Ombudsman Reports
Simon Hoare moved a motion concerning legal action that would prevent the Parliamentary and Health Service Ombudsman laying two reports before the House, stating, “The Charity Commission is bringing legal proceedings deliberately to prevent the laying of two reports before this House” [ref: a498.2/13]. The House agreed the motion, referring the matter to the Committee of Privileges without division [ref: a500.1/2].

### House of Lords (Hereditary Peers) Bill: Consideration and Divisions
The Commons considered Lords amendments. Ministers argued the Bill delivers the manifesto commitment to remove the hereditary route; opponents sought to retain existing hereditaries or create non‑sitting titles. The House rejected Lords amendment 1 (to retain existing hereditary peers) by 336–77 [ref: a547.1/1]; rejected Lords amendment 2 (unsalaried Ministers) by 331–73 [ref: a550.3/1]; and rejected Lords amendment 3 (non‑sitting life peerage) by 338–74 [ref: a554.1/1]. The Commons agreed Lords amendments 4–7 and 9 on resignation via power of attorney [ref: a554.1/5] and appointed a Reasons Committee [ref: a554.1/6]. Next steps: the Commons’ reasons will be communicated to the Lords.

### BBC Monitoring Service (Adjournment Debate)
Julian Lewis set out BBC Monitoring’s history and role in counter‑disinformation, citing the historic “Summary of World Broadcasts” [ref: a558.2/2] and arguing for secure funding and close US cooperation. The Minister affirmed Government support, confirmed that “the service maintains a strong and highly effective information‑sharing partnership with OSE” [ref: a562.0/7], and stressed that while AI tools are being used, “decisions will still be made by people, not by machines” [ref: a562.0/8]. He undertook to write with further detail on funding arrangements [ref: a562.0/6].
