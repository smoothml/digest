## High-Level Summary
The House focused on transport policy, two Urgent Questions, a Select Committee biosecurity warning, and two substantive backbench debates. Ministers took extensive Transport Oral Questions on rail performance, buses, driving tests, ferries, electric vehicles and micromobility, and highlighted forthcoming strategies and funding. An Urgent Question confirmed Lord Mandelson’s withdrawal as ambassador to the United States after new emails emerged; another addressed MSD’s cancellation of a £1 billion life sciences facility. MPs debated regional transport inequality and suicide prevention, with cross‑party contributions and ministerial commitments noted; the day closed with a petition presentation and an adjournment debate on male chick culling.

## Detailed Summary
### Oral Answers — Transport
The session ranged across passenger rail performance, buses, ferries, driving tests, electric vehicles and micromobility. The Secretary of State, Heidi Alexander, said performance was stabilising and that “passenger numbers were up 7% last year.” [ref: b1013.5/1] Charlotte Nichols described Avanti West Coast’s disruption, saying commuters must “plan our weeks around its appalling service and regular last-minute delays and cancellations.” [ref: b1013.6/1] The Secretary of State reiterated engagement with Avanti and Network Rail and the policy of bringing operations into public ownership at the end of contracts: “we will transfer operations to public ownership when contracts end.” [ref: b1013.7/1]

On buses, the Minister emphasised the landmark Bus Services (No. 2) Bill, devolution of powers and investment: “We are also investing £1 billion to support and improve local bus services and keep them affordable.” [ref: b1014.8/1] On driving tests, Ministers said recruitment and overtime had expanded capacity, with “over 10,000 more tests a month now available.” [ref: b1024.2/1] Keir Mather confirmed the Department “will publish the integrated national transport strategy” this year [ref: b1022.6/2], and that trials for e‑scooters have been extended to May 2028 with plans to legislate for micromobility. [ref: b1028.2/1] The Secretary of State also pointed to “over £92 billion of capital investment in transport infrastructure” to underpin growth. [ref: b1016.11/1]

### Urgent Question — UK Ambassador to the United States: Appointment Process
Answering on behalf of the Foreign Secretary, Stephen Doughty told the House that, “in light of additional information in emails written by Peter Mandelson, the Prime Minister has asked the Foreign Secretary to withdraw him as ambassador to the United States… with immediate effect.” [ref: b1038.2/3] He said the emails showed “the depth and extent of Lord Mandelson’s relationship with Jeffrey Epstein is materially different from that known at the time of his appointment.” [ref: b1038.2/3]

Members from across the House pressed for publication of vetting material and clarity on who knew what and when. Ministers pointed to routine vetting procedures and undertook to keep the House updated, including writing on specific employment or process points (e.g. on pay status and the timing of awareness). [ref: b1046.3/1][ref: b1049.7/1] No formal inquiry findings were announced during the exchange; Ministers committed to further correspondence and updates. [ref: b1043.2/1]

### Urgent Question — Life Sciences Investment (MSD decision)
Answering an Urgent Question, Ian Murray said MSD’s decision not to progress its King’s Cross investment was “deeply disappointing” but described it as a commercial decision taken in a global context. [ref: b1050.2/2][ref: b1050.2/3] He stressed ongoing Government backing for UK life sciences, sector funding and R&D commitments, and stated, “MSD will continue to be a key investor in the UK.” [ref: b1050.2/8]

MPs raised questions on pricing, regulation, skills, incentives and cross‑departmental coordination. The Minister committed to engagement with industry, cross‑Government discussions (e.g. with Homes England and DBT), and to working with Select Committees scrutinising the issue. [ref: b1053.3/1][ref: b1055.5/1]

### Select Committee statement — Biosecurity at the Border: Britain’s Illegal Meat Crisis
The Environment, Food and Rural Affairs Committee reported severe risks from illegal meat and dairy imports and highlighted enforcement and facilities shortcomings at ports. Alistair Carmichael said, “Last year, 235 tonnes of illegal animal products were seized at the UK border.” [ref: b1083.4/4] He described deteriorating hygiene in smuggled consignments and the potential role of these products in triggering animal disease outbreaks.

The Committee called for publishing quarterly seizure data and modelling likely imports, and recommended a cross‑government taskforce to co‑ordinate action. [ref: b1083.4/9][ref: b1083.4/15] Members also discussed port health resourcing and the suitability of facilities. As this was a Select Committee statement, no Minister responded in the Chamber at that point; the Committee urged DEFRA, Border Force and PHAs to act on the recommendations.

### Backbench Business — Regional Transport Inequality
Opening the debate, Catherine Atkinson argued that transport spending is uneven and cited the east midlands as the lowest per‑capita recipient: in 2023‑24 it was “£368 per person—just a quarter of the amount for London, and about half the average for England.” [ref: b1088.3/8] Members from coastal, rural and urban constituencies highlighted bus ‘deserts’, station accessibility, rail resilience (e.g., Dawlish), and road bottlenecks, calling for long‑term, devolved funding and Green Book reform.

In reply, the Minister outlined the Government’s approach: a £15.6 billion city‑region transport fund, £1 billion for buses this year, additional local transport grants, devolution and a forthcoming integrated national transport strategy. He said the Government are reviewing the Green Book and moving powers closer to communities. [ref: b1120.3/2][ref: b1122.1/2][ref: b1122.3/5] The House resolved “That this House has considered regional transport inequality.” [ref: b1124.2/4]

### Backbench Business — Suicide Prevention
Lee Pitcher led an emotionally charged debate on suicide prevention, urging early‑years resilience, mandatory first‑responder training, targeted research (e.g. by sex and deprivation), and rapid roll‑out of neighbourhood mental‑health hubs. He noted the scale: “In 2023, 7,055 people across the UK lost their lives to suicide.” [ref: b1125.2/11] He cited evidence that training improves detection — “GPs who complete training are 20% more likely to identify people at risk.” [ref: b1125.2/16]

Responding, the Minister confirmed measures including expanding mental‑health emergency provision and piloting neighbourhood models, stating, “we are investing up to £120 million to bring the number of mental health emergency departments up to 85.” [ref: b1145.1/6] He also referenced guidance for practitioners and work across public services. The House agreed the motion to consider suicide prevention and Members called for continuing cross‑Government action. [ref: b1148.1/6]

### Petition — No Limits Community Café and Hub; Adjournment — Male Chick Culling
Martin Wrigley presented a petition on behalf of constituents urging Government support for inclusive community hubs and long‑term funding (petition reference P003110). [ref: b1148.3/1–8] 

In the adjournment debate, Irene Campbell set out the ethical issues around culling male chicks and the status of alternatives. She said, “It is estimated that 6.5 billion newly hatched male chicks are culled globally every year.” [ref: b1150.5/2] Citing the Animal Welfare Committee’s view, she noted that “chick culling should be banned as soon as reliable, accurate technologies were available.” [ref: b1152.0/5] The Minister observed that the practice poses ethical rather than direct welfare harms and discussed in‑ovo sexing and dual‑purpose breeds, cautioning against unintended consequences (e.g. displacing culling abroad) while reviewing evidence and industry developments. [ref: b1152.0/6][ref: b1153.1/5–8]
