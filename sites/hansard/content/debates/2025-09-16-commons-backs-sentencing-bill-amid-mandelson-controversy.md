## High-Level Summary
The House of Commons sat for Justice oral questions, considered points of order, presented the Public Office (Accountability) Bill (the “Hillsborough law”), held an emergency debate on the appointment and dismissal of Lord Mandelson as Ambassador to the United States, agreed leave for a Ten‑minute Rule Bill on child poverty (removal of the two‑child limit), and gave the Sentencing Bill a Second Reading. Recurrent themes included court and prison capacity, probation resourcing, victims’ protections, policing standards, and ministerial accountability. Formal outcomes included agreement to the emergency debate motion, leave granted for Kirsty Blackman’s Bill (Ayes 89, Noes 79), and passage of the Sentencing Bill at Second Reading (Ayes 340, Noes 77). Delegated legislation on the Building Safety Levy was approved, three petitions were presented, and the House adjourned following a debate on police professional standards.

## Detailed Summary
### Speaker’s Statement: Retirement of the Clerk of Legislation
The Speaker paid tribute to Colin Lee on his retirement after 37 years’ service, highlighting his procedural expertise and modernising work in Select Committees. He added a personal note: “Colin’s favourite biscuits are Penguins, but he could never get the jokes right.” [ref: b1317.1/3] No further action was required.

### Justice Oral Questions: Court backlogs and capacity
The Lord Chancellor, David Lammy, said the Government had funded “a record high allocation of 110,000 Crown court sitting days this year.” [ref: b1318.2/2] Members cited empty courtrooms and trials listed years ahead; Lammy said Sir Brian Leveson’s second-stage review on efficiency would report “by the end of the year.” [ref: b1319.2/1] He also welcomed local initiatives such as case‑sharing between Maidstone and Woolwich: “I look forward to looking more closely at the example of Maidstone and Woolwich.” [ref: b1320.0/1] The session produced commitments, not decisions, including implementing Leveson’s recommendations when received.

### Justice Oral Questions: Probation Service resourcing and early release
Lammy pledged support for staff, stating: “We are determined to back our hard‑working probation staff by investing up to £700 million by the final year of the spending review… We will also recruit 1,300 trainee probation officers in the next year.” [ref: b1321.3/1] He said risk assessment processes would be tightened with the Home Office [ref: b1321.5/1] and committed to cross‑government work so that those leaving prison had housing options: “I commit to working closely  with colleagues in the Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government to ensure that that housing is available.” [ref: b1321.7/1] The Government acknowledged staffing shortages and said allocation plans would be reviewed. No votes occurred.

### Justice Oral Questions: Court estate—restoration and renewal
Courts Minister Sarah Sackman said capital funding had risen “from £120 million last year to over £148 million for this year. From Reading to Blackpool, we are building new courts and restoring old ones.” [ref: b1323.3/1] She invited MPs to raise site‑specific issues (e.g. Prestatyn, Lancaster), noting consultations and prioritisation would guide timelines. [refs: b1323.6/1; b1323.8/1] No binding dates were confirmed.

### Justice Oral Questions: Prison safety
Lammy said staff safety was a priority, with “£40 million to stop the contraband… protective body armour… and trialling the use of Tasers for specialised staff.” [ref: b1324.2/1] He said he would announce drone detection measures “over the coming weeks.” [ref: b1324.4/1] He also condemned assaults on staff and promised further announcements. [ref: b1324.6/1] No decisions were taken in the session.

### Justice Oral Questions: Prison rehabilitation
Prisons Minister Jake Richards said rehabilitation policies focused on employment, housing and substance misuse support: “That means investing in services that turn offenders away from a life of crime… That includes services in employment, accommodation and substance misuse treatment.” [ref: b1325.5/2] He underlined that prison capacity was being expanded while the Sentencing Bill aims to reduce reoffending. [ref: b1325.7/1] He also committed to joint work with Health on uninterrupted care for infectious diseases in prisons. [ref: b1326.0/1]

### Justice Oral Questions: Support for victims of rape and sexual violence
Victims Minister Alex Davies‑Jones listed funded court‑based support including the national witness service and independent sexual violence advocates. [ref: b1326.3/1] She said the Government is “committed to introducing free, independent legal advisers for victims of adult rape across England and Wales,” with more details to follow. [ref: b1326.5/1] On special measures, she told the Justice Committee she had discussed “how important section 28 is to vulnerable witnesses and victims.” [ref: b1327.2/1] She also said the Government would work on specialist domestic abuse courts consistent with Leveson’s recommendations. [ref: b1327.4/1]

### Justice Oral Questions: Funeral sector regulation; support for witnesses and jurors
On the Fuller inquiry, Davies‑Jones said the Government would provide “an interim update on progress this year, and a final response will be given by summer 2026.” [ref: b1328.8/1] On jurors facing distressing cases, she said: “There is support available for jurors… If the hon. Member wants to write to me, I will happily look into this more.” [ref: b1329.6/1] Witness support via section 28 and witness services was reiterated. [ref: b1329.4/1]

### Topical Questions: Public Office (Accountability) Bill and deportations
Lammy introduced the Public Office (Accountability) Bill, which he said would “create a new professional and legal duty of candour, placing public servants under a duty to act with honesty and integrity at all times.” [ref: b1331.3/2] On foreign national offenders, he stated: “returns under this Government have gone up 14%” and that reforms in the Sentencing Bill would aid deportation at sentencing. [refs: b1332.0/1; b1332.2/1]

### Points of Order: Ministerial accountability and statements
Members complained about delayed written answers (UEA dental school), announcement of Typhoon deployments outside the House, and sought a statement on a UN report concerning the Occupied Palestinian Territories. The Speaker said he had “no indication that Ministers intend to come to the House to make a statement,” but expected one on the House’s return or for an urgent question to be sought. [refs: b1339.1/1; b1339.4/1; b1340.0/1] On a Sizewell C contingent liability, he referred Members to relevant Committees. [ref: b1340.4/1]

### Bill Presented: Public Office (Accountability) Bill (Hillsborough law)
Secretary David Lammy presented a Bill “to impose a duty on public authorities and public officials to act with candour, transparency and frankness… [and] create an offence… who mislead the public.” [ref: b1341.2/2] First Reading was agreed; Second Reading was scheduled for Monday 13 October, with explanatory notes to be printed. [ref: b1341.2/3]

### Emergency Debate: Ambassador to the United States (Lord Mandelson)
David Davis raised concerns over suitability, vetting and accountability, asking “who knew what, and when?” and calling for documents and scrutiny. [ref: b1342.2/6] The Foreign Affairs Committee Chair Emily Thornberry said the Committee had repeatedly requested to question Lord Mandelson and urged transparency. [ref: b1351.3/5] Minister Stephen Doughty said “the Prime Minister took this decision after new information showed that the nature and extent of Lord Mandelson’s relationship with Jeffrey Epstein was materially different from what was known at the time of his appointment,” and confirmed that “James Roscoe… has been put in place as the chargé d’affaires.” [refs: b1384.3/2; b1384.7/3] The House resolved: “That this House has considered the appointment process and the circumstances leading to the dismissal of the former United Kingdom Ambassador to the United States, Lord Mandelson.” [ref: b1390.0/11]

### Ten‑minute Rule Bill: Child Poverty Strategy (Removal of Two‑Child Limit)
Kirsty Blackman sought leave to bring in a Bill requiring a child poverty strategy including removal of the two‑child cap, arguing: “The two-child cap is cruel, and it must be scrapped now.” [ref: b1392.2/7] The House granted leave in a Division: “The House divided: Ayes 89, Noes 79.” [ref: b1396.1/1] The Bill was read a First time; Second Reading was set for Friday 7 November. [ref: b1396.1/6]

### Sentencing Bill: Second Reading
Opening Second Reading as Lord Chancellor, David Lammy said the Bill restores the purpose of sentencing—“punishment that works”—and implements the independent review’s recommendations to reduce reoffending via earned progression, tougher licence conditions, and expanded community sanctions. [ref: b1399.5/3] Robert Jenrick opposed the Bill, warning: “Up to 43,000 criminals will avoid jail every year as a result of this plan.” [ref: b1408.1/3] The House rejected his reasoned amendment (Ayes 78, Noes 292) [ref: b1457.1/1] and approved Second Reading (Ayes 340, Noes 77) [ref: b1460.1/1]. A programme order and money resolution were then agreed. [refs: b1463.1/1–6; b1463.3/1–5]

### Delegated legislation and Petitions
The House approved the Building Safety Levy (England) Regulations 2025. [ref: b1464.4/1–2] Petitions were presented on: Plan for Neighbourhoods funding (Birmingham Hodge Hill and Solihull North) [ref: b1464.6/2]; road safety action plans near primary schools in Suffolk Coastal [ref: b1464.8/3]; and peatland protection in planning [ref: b1465.1/3].

### Adjournment Debate: Police—Professional Standards
Ben Maguire (North Cornwall) highlighted constituent cases involving Devon & Cornwall Police professional standards decisions, including a collision investigation dispute, and asked whether judicial review was the only recourse. He said: “is that really the only recourse for our constituents in this kind of situation?” [ref: b1466.2/18] Policing Minister Sarah Jones said she was “a week into my new role as Policing Minister” and outlined ongoing reforms, including putting vetting standards “on statutory footing” and strengthening misconduct systems in the Crime and Policing Bill. [refs: b1469.1/1; b1469.1/6] The House then adjourned. [ref: b1472.1/3]
