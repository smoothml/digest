## High-Level Summary
The Commons held Treasury Questions on infrastructure, tax and growth, followed by an Urgent Question on the Jaguar Land Rover cyber‑attack. Ministers reiterated long‑term infrastructure and public service investment plans, discussed employer National Insurance, farming reliefs, debt interest and planning reform, and outlined financial services reforms and the Better Futures Fund. The House then debated and passed the Second Reading of the Diego Garcia Military Base and British Indian Ocean Territory Bill, defeating a reasoned amendment before agreeing a programme motion. Two financial services statutory instruments were approved, committee membership changes were ordered, a constituency petition was presented, and the day concluded with an adjournment debate on rail station accessibility in Dulwich and West Norwood.

## Detailed Summary
### Business before Questions: Committee of Selection
The House ordered changes to the Committee of Selection, discharging three Members and adding three others. The order stated: "That Chris Elmore, Samantha Dixon and Jeff Smith be discharged from the Committee of Selection and Lilian Greenwood, Nesil Caliskan and Gen Kitchen be added." [ref: c703.2/2] No debate or division was held.

### Treasury Questions: Critical Infrastructure Funding (maintenance and repair)
Chief Secretary to the Treasury James Murray said the Government would fund "at least £725 billion of infrastructure over the next decade," including roads, flood defences and long‑term maintenance budgets for public service estates [ref: c703.9/2]. On the M48 Severn Bridge, he said £24 billion has been allocated to National Highways between 2026‑27 and 2029‑30, including a new structures fund for key bridges, flyovers and tunnels [ref: c704.0/1]. Members raised local cases on rail and road investment; the Minister pointed to existing allocations and offered to engage. No decisions were taken.

### Treasury Questions: 10‑Year Infrastructure Strategy and Economic Growth
The Chancellor, Rachel Reeves, described the 10‑year national infrastructure strategy as "core to delivering this Government’s growth mission" and reiterated the planned investment of at least £725 billion [ref: c704.7/2]. She highlighted support for Greater Manchester’s Bee Network, aiming for "zero‑emission public transport by 2030" and the purchase of 1,000 electric buses [ref: c705.0/1]. Addressing claims of a new charge on construction, she said, "The Government are currently consulting on a landfill tax" [ref: c707.0/1]. Members pressed for local transport schemes; the Chancellor committed to consider proposals and emphasised private investment leverage. No votes occurred.

### Treasury Questions: Employer National Insurance Contributions and Growth
Exchequer Secretary Dan Tomlinson said smaller firms were protected by raising the Employment Allowance to £10,500 so that "865,000 employers will pay no national insurance contributions at all" [ref: c707.6/1]. He linked last year’s NIC rise to public service funding, stating "waiting lists have fallen pretty much every single month since the election" [ref: c708.3/1]. Opposition and Liberal Democrat Members raised pressures on hospitality—one citing that "vacancies among small businesses alone have dropped by 18%" [ref: c709.5/1]—while the Minister pointed to support via business rates reforms and recent interest rate cuts reducing borrowing costs [ref: c709.1/1]. No new policy was announced; further detail was deferred to the Budget.

### Treasury Questions: Agricultural and Business Property Relief – impact on farming
Chief Secretary James Murray said Ministers had met widely with farming bodies and "continue to believe that the approach we have set out is the right one" [ref: c710.4/1]. Responding to criticism, he said a CenTax report "validates the Government’s position" that the reforms meet objectives [ref: c711.1/1]. Calls for a rethink or a family farm test were noted; Murray said options had been considered but the chosen approach balances revenue raising with "generous reliefs" [ref: c711.4/1]. No changes to policy were indicated.

### Treasury Questions: Government Debt Interest Payments
The Chancellor said the Government had restored stability, citing "five interest rate cuts" and that the UK was the fastest‑growing G7 economy in the first half of the year [ref: c711.8/1]. On borrowing costs comparisons, she said, "The spread on our gilts over the central bank rate is lower in the UK than it is in Greece" [ref: c712.3/1], and added that the OBR forecasts debt will fall over the Parliament [ref: c712.7/1]. Opposition Members argued UK borrowing costs were high and urged welfare savings; Reeves defended prior tax rises and pointed to universal credit reforms underway [ref: c721.1/1]. No votes were held.

### Treasury Questions: Planning Reform and Growth
The Chancellor said the Planning and Infrastructure Bill faces "more than 600 amendments" in the Lords and asserted that the Government "back the builders" [ref: c714.3/1]. On skills, she said "new construction colleges have started opening" and apprenticeships have been reformed to widen access, including removing some entry requirements [ref: c715.2/1]. Responding to claims of a "builders tax", she reiterated that a consultation is in train and urged support for wider reforms [ref: c716.0/1]. No immediate policy changes were announced.

### Treasury Questions: Better Futures Fund
James Murray confirmed the Better Futures Fund as the "largest social outcomes partnership fund in the world" targeting up to 200,000 vulnerable children and young people [ref: c716.4/1]. He said the Department for Culture, Media and Sport will co‑design the fund with departments, mayors and local partners to target need and impact [ref: c716.6/1; c717.1/1]. On adoption support, he noted confirmation of the fund’s extension and ongoing work with the Department for Education [ref: c717.3/1]. Timelines remained unchanged.

### Treasury Questions: Zero‑based Review of Tax Reliefs
Asked about a zero‑based review before the Autumn Budget 2025, Dan Tomlinson said HMRC has produced costings for around 350 reliefs and detailed analysis of the 38 largest non‑structural reliefs [ref: c718.2/1]. He added that about "800 of the 1,200 reliefs" define the scope of taxes and ensure fairness and simplicity [ref: c718.4/1]. He declined to pre‑empt Budget measures.

### Treasury Questions: Financial Services Reform
Economic Secretary Lucy Rigby outlined the Government’s financial services growth and competitiveness strategy, describing the "Leeds reforms" as the most wide‑ranging package in a decade [ref: c718.7/1]. She highlighted improved SME access to finance, including increasing the British Business Bank’s capacity to £25.6 billion and a new business growth service [ref: c719.0/1]. She agreed to meet on issues around market liquidity and stamp duty [ref: c719.2/1]. No policy changes were announced.

### Treasury Topical Questions
Opening topicals, the Chancellor said the UK was "the fastest‑growing economy in the G7 in the first half of this year" [ref: c720.2/1]. On taxing wealth, she listed Budget measures: "we got rid of the non‑dom tax status" and changed the treatment of carried interest, alongside VAT and business rates on private school fees [ref: c720.4/1]. Other exchanges covered support for pubs—"permanently lower tax rates for retail, hospitality and leisure" [ref: c721.5/1]—international rail links—"working closely with the German and Swiss Governments" [ref: c722.1/1]—the ongoing landfill tax consultation [ref: c723.1/1], and that a child poverty taskforce will report later this year [ref: c723.3/1].

### Urgent Question: Jaguar Land Rover cyber‑attack
Business Minister Chris Bryant said the Government and the National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) are "engaging with JLR on a daily basis" and that the NCSC "has been working with Jaguar Land Rover since last Wednesday" [ref: c725.3/1; c725.3/4]. He noted investigative sensitivities—"there is a limit to what I can say" [ref: c725.3/2]—outlined guidance and tools available to businesses, and said the Government "will introduce the cyber‑security and resilience Bill" to raise standards in critical and essential services [ref: c725.3/5]. He promised cross‑Government support—"we will bend over backwards"—and urged against ransom payments: "We do not recommend people paying ransoms in any circumstances" [ref: c732.1/1; c733.0/1]. No attribution of the attack’s origin was given.

### Diego Garcia Military Base and British Indian Ocean Territory Bill: Second Reading
Defence Minister Luke Pollard introduced the Bill to implement a UK‑Mauritius treaty, saying it "guarantees the continued UK operational control of Diego Garcia for the next 99 years and beyond" [ref: c739.3/2]. He argued that without the treaty, international rulings could further undermine the base’s operations, including through potential UNCLOS proceedings with legally binding effects [ref: c741.1/3; c741.3/2]. The Government said the treaty secures unrestricted operations—control over movements and the electromagnetic spectrum and a 24‑nautical‑mile buffer—and confirmed "we do not need to undertake pre‑notification" of actions to Mauritius [ref: c743.3/2; c744.3/1]. Costs were presented as an average of £101 million per year, "rigorously calculated" using Green Book methods, with a comparison to France’s Djibouti base at €85 million annually [ref: c748.1/2; c749.3/1].

Opposition parties tabled a reasoned amendment opposing ceding sovereignty, challenging costs (arguing £34.7‑£35 billion), raising concerns on operational constraints, environmental protection and Chagossian rights. Priti Patel said, "We ... stand against Labour’s £35 billion Chagos surrender deal" [ref: c754.3/3], and warned of risks to the Marine Protected Area, citing Mauritian ministerial remarks about issuing fishing licences [ref: c765.3/5]. The Liberal Democrat spokesperson said there had been an "abject failure ... properly to consult the Chagossian people" and raised concerns about the position beyond 99 years [ref: c769.1/7; c769.1/8]. Pollard confirmed Chagossians will be able to visit but not reside on Diego Garcia, and supported working with Mauritius on a new marine protected area [ref: c746.4/4; c753.7/2].

Outcome: The reasoned amendment was defeated (Ayes 116, Noes 333) [ref: c832.1/2]. The Bill received a Second Reading (Ayes 330, Noes 179) [ref: c835.1/1‑c835.1/3]. Next steps: Committee of the whole House, with proceedings to conclude four hours after commencement; Consideration and Third Reading to conclude within five hours thereafter [ref: c839.2/6‑c839.2/8].

### Delegated Legislation: Financial Services regulations
Without division, the House approved two instruments: the draft Markets in Financial Instruments (Miscellaneous Amendments) Regulations 2025, and the draft Financial Services and Markets Act 2023 (Capital Buffers and Macro‑prudential Measures) (Consequential Amendments) Regulations 2025 [ref: c839.6/1‑c839.6/3].

### Modernisation Committee membership
The House ordered changes to the Modernisation Committee: Lucy Powell was discharged and Sir Alan Campbell added, and "Sir Alan Campbell shall be the Chair of the Committee." [ref: c840.1/3‑c840.1/4]

### Public Petition: Developments near Lutterworth and surrounding villages
Alberto Costa presented a petition opposing several local developments, arguing they "threaten the character of our historic Lutterworth market town and our wonderful Leicestershire villages" and requesting Government intervention to uphold planning policies and affordable housing commitments [ref: c840.3/3‑c840.3/7]. No debate or decision followed.

### Adjournment Debate: Accessibility of railway stations in Dulwich and West Norwood
Helen Hayes said that out of 10 local stations, "Only three—Herne Hill, East Dulwich and West Norwood—are accessible" and urged reforms to Access for All and better pre‑bid support to improve access [ref: c841.2/3; c843.1/2]. Transport Minister Simon Lightwood said over 260 stations now have step‑free access, with "over 1,500 smaller‑scale improvements" delivered [ref: c845.1/3‑c845.1/4], and that "Network Rail completed feasibility work on 50 stations" for potential future investment [ref: c846.1/1]. He said broader plans on accessible travel would come "later this year ... as part of our integrated national transport strategy" [ref: c846.1/4].
