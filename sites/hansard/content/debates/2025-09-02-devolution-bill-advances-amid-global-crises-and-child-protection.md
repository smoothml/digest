## High-Level Summary
The Commons concentrated on foreign affairs and domestic policy, heard two substantive ministerial statements, and advanced major devolution legislation. Foreign Office questions ranged across ODA allocations, Sudan, Gaza humanitarian aid and ceasefire efforts, the Chagos treaty, Georgia’s democracy, recognition of Palestine, and sanctions on Russia. Ministers then outlined the Government’s immediate response to Liberty Speciality Steels entering liquidation and announced a new national policing operation and statutory inquiry framework on group‑based child sexual exploitation. The English Devolution and Community Empowerment Bill passed Second Reading after a reasoned amendment was defeated, and later business included a Ten Minute Rule Bill on infant allergy guidance and an Adjournment Debate on large‑scale solar schemes in Newark.

## Detailed Summary
### Business before Questions: General Cemetery Bill [Lords]
The House concluded formalities before Questions. The General Cemetery Bill [Lords] was read a Second time: “Bill read a Second time.” [ref: d121.2/1]. No debate is recorded in the transcript.

### Speaker’s Statement: Potential impediment to a PHSO report
The Speaker granted precedence to a privilege matter regarding a legal case that could prevent the Parliamentary and Health Service Ombudsman laying a report. He emphasised, “The free flow of information to Parliament is essential to its work” [ref: d121.4/2] and informed the House that a motion could be debated on Thursday, to be taken after any urgent questions or statements and before Government business [ref: d121.4/2].

### FCDO Oral Questions: ODA, Sudan, Gaza, Chagos, Georgia, Gaza students/ceasefire, Palestine recognition, Russia sanctions, and topical issues
Official Development Assistance: Ministers said they remain committed to development while modernising delivery, with detailed allocations to follow “in due course… informed by impact assessments” [ref: d122.3/1, d122.6/1]. Stephen Doughty said, “we must ensure that every pound delivers for the UK taxpayer, as well as the people we support” [ref: d121.10/1].

Sudan: The Foreign Secretary called Sudan “its worst humanitarian crisis on record. Over 30 million people need aid” [ref: d123.3/1], backed a pause in fighting to let aid in, and said he would lead on the issue heading into UNGA [ref: d123.5/1].

Gaza humanitarian aid: Ministers announced a further £15 million of aid, taking total UK funding to £75 million this year [ref: d124.10/1]. The Minister cited the IPC report—“500,000 people in Gaza are facing famine” [ref: d125.1/1]—criticised the Gaza Humanitarian Foundation mechanism—“The GHF is not working” [ref: d125.5/1]—and referred to earlier actions including “the suspension of arms that could be used in Gaza” [ref: d125.1/1]. A further £3 million via UNFPA for maternal health needs was confirmed [ref: d126.3/1]. On legal risk assessments, he said Ministers apply the long‑standing “real risk” test [ref: d127.0/1].

Chagos Islands: Ministers defended the treaty and costings: “The net present value of payments under the treaty is £3.4 billion” [ref: d127.5/1], calling £30 billion claims “inaccurate and misleading” [ref: d128.4/1]. Environmental protection will be supported alongside Mauritius’s ambition for a marine protected area [ref: d128.1/1].

Georgia: Ministers expressed deep concern about democratic backsliding—“We are deeply concerned about the situation in Georgia” [ref: d128.7/1]—noted sanctions already imposed, and suspended programme support to the Georgian Government [ref: d129.1/1, d129.3/1].

Gaza: evacuation of scholarship students and ceasefire/journalists: The Government said, “We are expecting nine Chevening students to start their courses soon” [ref: d130.3/1], noting departures depend on Israeli permissions [ref: d130.5/1]. An “immediate ceasefire is our overwhelming priority” [ref: d131.6/1], they condemned violence against journalists and called for investigations [ref: d132.0/1]. The Government also highlighted sanctions on “Ben‑Gvir and Smotrich” over incitement of settler violence [ref: d132.4/1].

Recognition of a Palestinian state: The Government said they would recognise a Palestinian state at UNGA in September unless Israel takes substantive steps towards a sustainable peace [ref: d134.2/1], adding “Hamas must never be rewarded” [ref: d134.6/1].

Russia sanctions: Since taking office, the Government have sanctioned “more than 680 individual entities and ships” [ref: d134.9/1] and are working with partners on measures including the oil price cap and Russian sovereign assets [ref: d135.1/1].

Topical: Notable points included confirmation of a forthcoming vote on the Chagos Bill—“there is a Second Reading next week, and of course there will be a vote” [ref: d136.4/1]—the China approach of “co‑operate” where possible and “challenge where we must” [ref: d136.9/1], and an update on polio eradication—“We have helped 600,000 children” [ref: d141.7/1]. No divisions occurred during Questions; Ministers undertook to provide further updates and meetings on several issues (e.g., Sudan, scholarship cases, Syria).

### Ministerial Statement: Speciality Steel UK (Liberty) insolvency
The Business Minister reported the High Court’s winding‑up order against Liberty Speciality Steels on 21 August, with the official receiver (OR) appointed. She assured there would be “no immediate changes to the current operation of the business… employee payroll processed within 24 hours” [ref: d145.1/3]. She noted the director is “under investigation by the Serious Fraud Office” [ref: d145.1/6] and said pre‑insolvency support was ruled out given risks and “hidden costs” [ref: d145.1/9]. The Government will seek private investment and “pursue every option to keep steelmaking” in affected regions [ref: d145.1/15].

In questions, she said it is too early to estimate OR costs but salaries are being paid: “I cannot give… the actual numbers on that” now [ref: d149.3/2]. She highlighted measures on procurement, energy costs (raising the super‑charger relief from 60% to 90%), trade protections, and a forthcoming steel strategy [ref: d149.3/4]. Progress on US tariff talks was cited, and she explained energy relief could have been worth about £1.5 million at previous production levels [ref: d150.1/1‑d150.1/2]. MPs raised apprenticeships (the OR is seeking alternative placements) [ref: d152.0/1], pensions (to be clarified once records are established) [ref: d155.3/1], possible consolidation options [ref: d156.1/1], and defence procurement of UK steel [ref: d156.5/1]. Outcome: OR stabilisation funding approved; the Government committed to support workers, seek buyers, and publish a steel strategy later this year.

### Ministerial Statement: Group‑based Child Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (Casey recommendations)
The Home Office accepted all 12 recommendations from Baroness Casey’s audit. Jess Phillips announced: “Operation Beaconport has been established” under National Crime Agency oversight with policing partners [ref: d160.2/4]. Following a review request, “1,273 cases have now been identified for formal review” and 216 rape‑related cases will be accelerated [ref: d160.2/6]. The tackling organised exploitation (TOEX) programme is being expanded with £426,000 of new funding and national roll‑out of tools [ref: d160.2/9‑d160.2/10]. The Government will require ethnicity and nationality data collection for CSEA suspects [ref: d160.2/11]. A statutory national inquiry will be victim‑centred, run for “two to three years” [ref: d160.2/16], with a chair to be appointed shortly and terms of reference consulted on [ref: d160.2/14‑d160.2/16].

In exchanges, the Minister said “no local authority area can turn the inquiry down” and “no stone will be left unturned” [ref: d165.0/5]. She confirmed the NCA will investigate matters concerning South Yorkshire Police under IOPC direction [ref: d160.2/7]. Next steps: chair appointment and publication of draft terms of reference; further updates promised.

### Bills Presented and Ten‑Minute Rule Motion
Sentencing Bill: Presented and given First Reading; Second Reading set for the next day [ref: d176.4/1‑d176.4/3].

Vehicle Registration Marks (Misuse and Offences) Bill: Presented under the Ten Minute Rule; First Reading agreed and Second Reading set for 12 September [ref: d176.6/2‑d176.6/3].

Babies and Infants (Allergy Guidance) Bill (Ten Minute Rule): Becky Gittins sought a review of infant feeding and allergy guidance “relating to the feeding and weaning of babies and infants” [ref: d177.2/2], noting “The UK has some of the highest rates of allergic conditions in the world” [ref: d177.2/5] and clarifying that this does not contradict breastfeeding advice [ref: d177.2/9]. Leave was granted; First Reading ordered and Second Reading set for 12 September [ref: d177.2/17‑d177.2/21].

### English Devolution and Community Empowerment Bill: Second Reading
The Deputy Prime Minister introduced what she termed “the biggest transfer of power in a generation” [ref: d180.3/4], aiming to make devolution the default, strengthen mayors, create strategic authorities, and empower communities (including a community right to buy and banning upwards‑only commercial rent reviews) [ref: d186.5/5]. The Bill would restore the supplementary vote for mayoral and PCC elections and bar mayors from sitting as MPs [ref: d186.0/1]. It also proposes a Local Audit Office and requires councils using committee systems to move to a leader‑and‑cabinet model [ref: d186.5/4‑d186.5/5].

The Shadow Secretary of State moved a reasoned amendment, calling the Bill “a blatant power grab” and arguing it would centralise control and raise local costs [ref: d188.0/3‑d188.0/4]. Decisions: The reasoned amendment was defeated by 367 to 167 [ref: d251.1/1‑d251.1/3]. The Bill was read a Second time by 365 to 164 [ref: d255.1/1‑d255.1/3]. A programme motion committed the Bill to a Public Bill Committee until 12 November 2025 [ref: d259.1/6], and Money and Ways and Means resolutions were agreed [ref: d259.3/3‑d259.3/6; d259.5/2‑d259.5/5]. Next steps: Public Bill Committee scrutiny then Report and Third Reading.

### Delegated Legislation approvals
Without division the House approved instruments on: disclosure of adult social care data [ref: d260.4/1]; extending electrical safety standards to the social rented sector and prescribing hazards in social housing [ref: d260.6/1‑d260.6/2]; removal of prisoners for deportation [ref: d260.8/1]; amending the definition of smaller authority for local audit [ref: d260.10/1]; and a hovercraft enactments amendment [ref: d260.12/1‑d260.12/2].

### Public Petition: GP practice in Burton, Dorset
Christopher Chope MP presented a petition seeking to reopen Burton’s GP surgery, asking the House to urge the Government to overrule the Dorset ICB’s refusal. Petitioners “request that the House of Commons urge the Government to overrule the Dorset Integrated Care Board’s decision” [ref: d260.14/5]. The petition was recorded [ref: d260.14/7].

### Adjournment Debate: Solar development in Newark
Robert Jenrick MP opposed three very large solar farm proposals, saying together they would “cover at least 10,000 acres of land” [ref: d262.2/2]. He argued it was not nimbyism—“This is not about nimbyism” [ref: d262.6/3]—raising concerns about food security, cumulative impact, and rural character.

The Minister defended solar as vital to achieving “clean power by 2030” [ref: d267.0/2], stressed rigorous planning and community engagement to ensure “only the right projects get the green light” [ref: d267.0/4], and noted even ambitious deployment would use “up to 0.4% of UK land” [ref: d267.0/6‑d267.0/7]. She described rooftop expansion, including GB Energy’s plan for “200 schools and… 200 NHS sites” [ref: d268.1/5], signalled reforms to grid planning and more local decision‑making on mid‑size schemes, and undertook to write on detailed points [ref: d268.1/4‑d267.0/5]. No decision was taken.
