## High-Level Summary
The House sat for a full day covering a Speaker’s opening statement, Defence oral questions, two urgent questions (on the proscription of Palestine Action and on Omar al‑Bayoumi), a major Defence Industrial Strategy statement, extended consideration of Lords amendments to the Renters’ Rights Bill (with multiple divisions), a petitions presentation and an adjournment debate on remote coastal communities. Ministers previewed and later published the Defence Industrial Strategy with investment, skills and innovation measures, and reaffirmed NATO and domestic spending trajectories. On Palestine Action, Ministers defended proscription and policing decisions while declining legislative changes during ongoing legal proceedings. The Commons disagreed with several significant Lords amendments to the Renters’ Rights Bill and appointed a committee to draw up reasons. The day concluded with calls for tailored support for coastal areas and ministerial commitments to work with Members on funding and services.

## Detailed Summary
### Speaker’s Statement
The Speaker, Sir Lindsay Hoyle, reported his recess engagements, including international parliamentary diplomacy: “I had the honour of attending the sixth World Conference of Speakers of Parliament” [ref: e567.1/1]. No decisions arose; the statement introduced the day’s business.

### Oral Questions: Defence (innovation, industry, veterans, spending, cadets)
The Defence Secretary, John Healey, said the Government’s plan would be set out later that day: “Our defence industrial strategy, launched later today, will meet that challenge” [ref: e567.8/2]. Ministers highlighted measures to broaden supply‑chain participation, including an SME support centre and a commitment to “ringfence £400 million of direct defence investment that will go to SMEs” [ref: e568.3/1]. On NATO trajectories, the Secretary noted that “all 32 NATO nations agreed to step up and increase spending on national security to 5% by 2035” [ref: e572.3/1]. The Veterans Minister announced new funding to re‑shape support pathways: the Government are “investing £50 million over three years to set up Valour” [ref: e569.7/1]. On youth engagement, Ministers said they will “increase the number of existing cadet forces by 30% by 2030 with £70 million of funding” [ref: e578.6/1]. The Government also confirmed a 10‑year defence investment plan would be “published this autumn” [ref: e583.3/1].

### Urgent Question: Palestine Action — proscription and protests
Answering an urgent question, the Security Minister, Dan Jarvis, stated the weekend policing outcomes and rationale for proscription. He told the House: “The Metropolitan police has confirmed that a total of 890 arrests were made at a demonstration in central London on Saturday” [ref: e591.3/4], and cited the independent reviewer Jonathan Hall KC: “There is no way ordinary criminal law would be effective against funding, training and recruitment” [ref: e593.3/6]. Mr Speaker earlier noted an active judicial review and said, “I have decided to grant a waiver” to allow debate [ref: e591.1/1]. Jarvis said the Government “do not currently have any plans to amend the existing legislation” while criminal and judicial proceedings continue [ref: e595.3/2]. No changes were agreed; Ministers committed to continue engagement with the independent reviewer and support operational policing decisions.

### Urgent Question: Omar al‑Bayoumi — arrest and extradition
In response to the right hon. David Davis, the Security Minister said it was “inappropriate to comment on an individual case” [ref: e610.2/2]. He added that “there are legal proceedings ongoing in the United States” limiting further detail at this time [ref: e611.1/2]. He undertook to examine recent reporting and noted engagement with the Intelligence and Security Committee could follow, but announced no new operational commitments pending proceedings.

### Statement: Defence Industrial Strategy
Defence Minister Luke Pollard published the Defence Industrial Strategy, describing a “plan supported by £773 million of investment” [ref: e615.1/3]. Priorities include raising defence spending to “2.6% of GDP by 2027, and 3.5% by 2035” [ref: e615.1/4], regional defence growth deals worth “£250 million” [ref: e615.1/11], innovation funding where “UK Defence Innovation is backed by £400 million of ringfenced investment” and a pledge to spend “at least 10% of our equipment budget on novel technologies” [ref: e615.1/14]. A skills package will establish five defence technical excellence colleges [ref: e615.1/12] and improve SME access and exports. The 10‑year Defence Investment Plan will follow later in the year [ref: e583.3/1].

### Consideration of Lords Amendments: Renters’ Rights Bill
Introducing the Government’s case, the Housing Minister argued against several Lords amendments; for example, on pet deposits he said: “Requiring a further three weeks’ deposit would cost the average tenant in England over £900” [ref: e636.1/8]. After extended debate, the Commons rejected a series of peers’ changes in recorded divisions: Lords amendment 11 (pet deposit) disagreed to: “Ayes 398, Noes 93” [ref: e661.1/1]; Lords amendment 18 (no‑let period) disagreed to: “Ayes 402, Noes 97” [ref: e665.3/1]; Lords amendment 19 (shared owners) disagreed to: “Ayes 336, Noes 158” [ref: e669.1/1]; Lords amendment 26 (standard of proof) disagreed to: “Ayes 404, Noes 98” [ref: e673.3/1]; Lords amendment 39 (decent homes for service families) disagreed to: “Ayes 325, Noes 171” [ref: e677.3/1]; Lords amendment 53 (student ground) disagreed to: “Ayes 401, Noes 96” [ref: e680.3/1]; and Lords amendment 64 (carers ground) disagreed to: “Ayes 335, Noes 160” [ref: e684.1/1]. The Government accepted narrower changes in lieu on agricultural grounds [ref: e680.3/5] and appointed a committee to draw up reasons for disagreement with a set of Lords amendments [ref: e684.1/6‑e684.1/11].

### Petition: Disabled Bus Passes
Tom Gordon presented a petition asking that disabled bus passes be valid at all times, including peaks, noting that people “live with their disabilities 24/7” (paraphrase) and reading the petition text. The petition was recorded as [P003099] [ref: e688.0/8]. No immediate Government decision was taken in Chamber.

### Adjournment Debate: Remote Coastal Communities
Perran Moon highlighted distinct pressures on remote coastal areas, including housing impacts from second homes and holiday lets; he said Cornwall “had the largest supply of short‑term lets in the country…around 24,000 properties, which is up by 30% since 2019” [ref: e692.2/2], and urged a dedicated strategy and fairer funding. The new Under‑Secretary, Miatta Fahnbulleh, outlined support and reforms, including redirecting “around £2 billion of existing funding to the places and communities that need it the most” (local government finance) [ref: e700.3/2], and referenced coastal resilience and flooding investment: “a record £2.65 billion over this year and last year for the construction and maintenance of flood schemes” [ref: e700.3/4]. She committed to work with Members across the House on programmes and funding [ref: e700.3/7].
