## High-Level Summary
The Commons focused on Work and Pensions questions (unemployment, youth support, disability benefits, fraud, and child poverty), followed by statements on borders and asylum reform, the Middle East and Iran, and support for Ukraine. The Home Secretary announced a UK‑France small‑boats returns pilot, an overhaul of asylum appeals, and a temporary suspension of the dedicated refugee family reunion route. The Foreign Secretary confirmed UN‑verified famine in Gaza, £15 million more UK aid, intent to recognise a Palestinian state (absent substantive Israeli steps), and UN “snapback” sanctions action on Iran. The Defence Secretary reaffirmed backing for Ukraine, unveiled a £10 billion Type 26 frigate export to Norway, and trailed allied meetings to step up support. The House also heard a Joint Committee statement on transnational repression, a petition on bank services in Moffat, and an adjournment debate on a Lisbon Maru memorial.

## Detailed Summary
### Oral Answers to Questions: Work and Pensions
Ministers reported current headline labour market figures and recent movements, with Alison McGovern stating: “The unemployment rate is 4.7%” [ref: c1.5/1], “employment is up by 725,000” [ref: c1.5/1], and there has been “a reduction of 400,000” in inactivity [ref: c1.5/1]. On youth support, the Government highlighted trailblazers and local partnerships: “Our Get Britain Working trailblazer in the north-east is already helping” [ref: c3.6/1], and in rural areas they are “introducing measures such as mobile jobcentres” [ref: c4.0/1].

On benefits and fraud, Stephen Timms cited the OBR’s March forecast that incapacity and disability benefits spending would be “£90.7 billion in 2029-30” [ref: c4.8/1]. He said the PIP review would be co-produced and cover mobility-related gateways such as Motability: “the mobility component… which leads on to the Motability scheme” [ref: c5.6/1]. The Government’s fraud package was said to “deliver an additional £9.6 billion of savings over the next five years” [ref: c6.8/1]. PIP processes will be modernised via a “health transformation programme” [ref: c7.5/1] to create “a process that is simpler and easier to understand” [ref: c7.7/1]. On poverty, Ministers trailed a child poverty strategy “coming out in the autumn” [ref: c9.4/1] and an “expansion of free school meals to all children in households claiming universal credit” [ref: c10.6/1]. Pension credit drives continue, with a “lower backlog… than we inherited” [ref: c12.1/1], and winter fuel changes will be “automatic” for most via PAYE [ref: c12.6/2]. Statutory sick pay reforms will “improve eligibility for 1.3 million” by removing the waiting period [ref: c22.0/1]. Opposition spokespeople challenged increases in youth unemployment and queried costs; Ministers pointed to new funding—“£45 million for our eight youth guarantee trailblazers” [ref: c6.5/1], including “up to another £5 million” in the West of England [ref: c11.5/1]. No decisions were taken; ministers signalled strategies and reforms later this year.

### Statement: Borders and Asylum
The Home Secretary set out measures to “strengthen our border security and the next steps in our reforms to the asylum system” [ref: c23.2/1]. She announced a treaty with France “allowing us, for the first time, to directly return those who arrive on small boats,” with “first detentions… took place the next day” and “the first returns to begin later this month” on a pilot basis [ref: c23.2/5]. She pressed for the Border Security, Asylum and Immigration Bill, including “counter-terrorism powers against smuggler gangs” and powers to seize phones [ref: c23.2/8].

Structural changes include a “statutory timeframe of 24 weeks” for appeals [ref: c23.2/10] and creating “a new independent body to deal with immigration and asylum appeals” able to surge capacity [ref: c23.2/11]. To manage pressures, the Government will “temporarily suspend new applications under the existing dedicated refugee family reunion route” pending a new framework [ref: c23.2/16], and pledged that “every asylum hotel will be closed for good” as numbers are reduced and accommodation reconfigured [ref: c23.2/17]. The Home Office will issue “expedited visas” for “critically ill and injured children” from Gaza and for students [ref: c23.2/20]. In exchanges, the Home Secretary said prior freezes on asylum decisions drove backlogs and reiterated plans to “ban sex offenders from the asylum system altogether” via the Bill [ref: c29.0/6]. She said monthly updates would be provided on the France pilot’s progress [ref: c42.5/1].

### Statement: Middle East (Occupied Palestinian Territories) and Iran
The Foreign Secretary said UN-backed analysis confirmed “famine—famine in Gaza city” spreading [ref: c60.2/2], condemned obstructions to aid, and announced “an additional £15 million of aid and medical care” for Gaza and the region [ref: c60.2/5]. He listed recent UK actions: “We restored funding to the United Nations Relief and Works Agency” and “We suspended arms exports that could be used in Gaza” [ref: c60.2/4]. The UK will work with WHO to “get critically ill and injured children into the UK” for NHS treatment and support scholarship students [ref: c60.2/6].

He reiterated the call for “an immediate ceasefire” with the unconditional release of hostages [ref: c60.2/7], criticised settlement expansion (including E1), and said the UK intends to “recognise the state of Palestine later this month,” unless there are substantive steps towards a sustainable peace [ref: c60.2/10]. On Iran, the UK (with France and Germany) has “triggered the snapback mechanism” under UNSCR 2231, so sanctions “will come back into force” if there is no agreement within 30 days [ref: c60.2/14]. In questioning, he emphasised using established UN channels for aid—“UNRWA… and the World Food Programme” [ref: c64.0/3]—and argued that “Recognition is rooted in the principle of a two-state solution, which Hamas rejects” [ref: c60.2/13].

### Statement: Ukraine
The Defence Secretary announced “a £10 billion contract to supply Norway with at least five Type 26 frigates” [ref: c92.1/2], calling it the biggest British warship export deal, and said, “A secure Europe needs a strong Ukraine” [ref: c92.1/3]. He condemned recent Russian strikes that killed civilians and “damaged a British Government building” housing the British Council in Kyiv [ref: c92.1/5]. The UK will provide “£4.5 billion in military aid for Ukraine this year” and, in a recent 50‑day push, delivered munitions, drones and air‑defence/EW systems [ref: c92.1/9].

Looking ahead, the UK will “co-chair the 30th UDCG meeting” with Germany and host an E5 Defence Ministers’ summit [ref: c92.1/10], while leading a “coalition of the willing” planning to secure the “skies and seas” and train Ukraine after any ceasefire [ref: c92.1/11]. He welcomed US consideration of “very serious economic sanctions on Russia,” and said the Foreign Secretary “will have more to say on similar UK action very soon” [ref: c92.1/6]. Questioners pressed on readiness and the shape of security guarantees; the Secretary of State said details depend on any peace agreement, with planning and resourcing under way.

### Select Committee Statement: Transnational Repression in the UK (Joint Committee on Human Rights)
Presenting the Committee’s report, Members said transnational repression is “a serious and under-recognised threat… creating a broader ‘chilling effect’ on entire communities” [ref: c110.4/3], noting state threat investigations had “increased by 48%” [ref: c110.4/5]. The report highlighted cases involving China, Russia and Iran and recommended: adopting a formal UK definition and data collection; considering adding China to the enhanced tier of FIRS; making police training mandatory; establishing a victim reporting hotline; and reviewing sanctions tools for transnational repression [ref: c110.4/10, c110.4/11, c110.4/12, c110.4/13, c110.4/16]. The Government was asked to consider and respond.

### Petition: Bank Facilities in Moffat
David Mundell presented a petition opposing the closure of the Bank of Scotland’s Moffat branch, citing impacts on residents and businesses. A local petition had “garnered over 3,000 signatures” [ref: c114.2/6]. It asks the House to urge action to keep the branch open and ensure access to cash. The petition was formally presented; no decision was required at this stage.

### Adjournment Debate: Lisbon Maru Memorial
Kirsteen Sullivan called for Government support for a memorial to the 1942 sinking of the Lisbon Maru, which carried “over 1,800 British and allied prisoners of war” [ref: c115.2/2]. She highlighted that “Chinese fishermen… risked their lives to rescue hundreds of survivors” [ref: c115.2/4]. The Government expressed condolences and noted that Japan has “acknowledged and shown contrition” [ref: c117.1/6]. Ministers pointed to “a memorial located at the National Memorial Arboretum” [ref: c117.1/7] and noted that “Heritage is a devolved policy area in Scotland,” encouraging consideration by the Scottish Government [ref: c117.1/8]. No new UK funding was announced.
