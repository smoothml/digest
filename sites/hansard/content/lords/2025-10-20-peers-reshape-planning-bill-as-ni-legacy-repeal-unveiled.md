## High-Level Summary
The House of Lords held oral questions on HIV testing and care, touring musicians’ CITES certificates, GDP per capita, and healthcare inequalities, before moving to Report on the Planning and Infrastructure Bill. Peers restored a duty on Ministers to respond to parliamentary recommendations on national policy statements and accepted changes enabling third‑party delivery of water infrastructure as nationally significant projects, while adding heritage and community safeguards. The Government reverted to a clearer acceptance test for DCO applications, created regulation‑making powers to protect defence seismic arrays while facilitating onshore wind, and accepted affirmative procedure for the electricity bill discount scheme. An amendment to curb large solar projects on best and most versatile farmland was defeated. The Government also announced legislation to repeal and replace the Northern Ireland legacy Act, removing immunity and outlining safeguards for veterans.

## Detailed Summary
### Leave of Absence
Lord McFall of Alcluith told the House: "I seek leave of absence from your Lordships’ House" [ref: a469.1/1], explaining he would be away from Tuesday 21 October until Thursday 23 October.

### Oral Question: HIV testing and medical care
Baroness Merron reported progress and funding: "HIV testing rates in sexual health services increased last year by 3%" [ref: a469.7/1]; "we invested £27 million in HIV opt-out A&E testing" [ref: a469.7/1]; and the action plan "will be out before the end of the year" and "will include HIV testing and care actions" [ref: a469.7/1]. Lord Black proposed wider access to PrEP and dedicated caseworkers; the Minister replied, "I will certainly take those very helpful proposals back" and noted NICE’s recommendation on "the use of cabotegravir" [ref: a470.0/1]. She endorsed opt‑out emergency department testing—"We have 79 emergency departments in the programme"—and said lessons would inform any expansion [ref: a471.1/1]. She confirmed the plan would target under‑tested groups—"a focus on HIV testing" and on "groups that are less likely to be tested" [ref: a471.3/1]—and undertook to write on pharmacy information [ref: a471.5/1]. She said the Government would keep engaging the devolved Administrations [ref: a472.0/1] and that UKHSA supports local delivery while focusing on "equality of access and outcome" [ref: a472.2/1].

### Oral Question: Musical instrument certificates (CITES) and touring musicians
Baroness Hayman of Ullock said the Government "have not yet fully assessed the impact" of introducing a fee for musical instrument certificates and would use evidence from a consultation that "will close on 23 October" [ref: a472.5/1]. She stressed "no decisions as yet on fees have been made" and that MICs are currently "the only CITES permit or certificate available to applicants at no cost", with fairness and proportionality under consideration [ref: a473.2/1, a473.2/2]. She confirmed ongoing engagement with DCMS and the European Commission, recalling the UK‑EU summit had "recognised the value of travel and cultural and artistic exchanges" [ref: a473.4/1], undertook to write with further details [ref: a475.0/1], and affirmed Cabinet Office involvement [ref: a475.8/1].

### Oral Question: GDP per capita and contributing factors
Lord Livermore said "GDP per capita has risen by 0.9% over the past year" and was the "second fastest in the G7", compared with "a fall of 0.1% during the previous Parliament" [ref: a476.2/1]. He rejected claims recent policies explained quarterly changes and stated the OBR had been clear Brexit "permanently reduced the size of our economy by 4%" [ref: a476.4/1]. On migration, he said the OBR is assessing impacts and the Government would "review the visa offer for highly talented individuals" and "work towards an ambitious youth mobility scheme with the EU" [ref: a477.1/1]. He reaffirmed the growth mission—"the fastest-growing economy in the G7"—and cited OBR forecasts of "GDP per capita to rise by 5.6% over this Parliament" [ref: a477.3/1]. He agreed on retaining high earners [ref: a478.0/1], pointed to recent real wage growth [ref: a478.2/1], noted the ONS is reviewing workforce data [ref: a478.4/1], and emphasised productivity gains through investment [ref: a478.6/1]. He outlined OBR estimates of capital spending’s impact of "0.14% over five years, 0.43% over 10 years and 1.4% in the long term" [ref: a479.1/1].

### Oral Question: Healthcare provision inequalities
Baroness Merron linked the Darzi report to the Government’s "10-year plan" to tackle inequalities [ref: a479.5/1]. She called women’s poor hysteroscopy experiences "unacceptable" and said updates to the Women’s Health Strategy will address pain relief [ref: a479.7/1]. On learning disabilities, she said disparities are "unacceptable" and cited Core20PLUS5 to target high‑risk groups [ref: a480.1/1]. She said improvements would come "in the course of the next 10 years" rather than "month-by-month" [ref: a480.3/2], highlighted stark life‑expectancy gaps (Wokingham vs Barnsley) [ref: a481.0/1], and stressed local flexibility within a strategic approach [ref: a481.0/2]. She addressed rural service pressures and digital inclusion [ref: a481.2/2, a481.4/1], and assured attention to unpaid carers [ref: a482.1/1].

### Legislation and motions
The Sustainable Aviation Fuel Bill was read a first time and ordered to be printed [ref: a482.2/1]. The House approved the Building Safety Levy (England) Regulations 2025 [ref: a482.5/4] and the Companies (Directors’ Report) (Payment Reporting) Regulations 2025 [ref: a482.7/4].

### Commons Urgent Question answered in the Lords: Official Secrets Act case witness statements
Baroness Anderson said a 1 September meeting concerned "the management of the court case" and that "no Box note existed" for the Prime Minister [ref: a484.0/1]. She pledged that both the Joint Committee and the ISC would receive "full co-operation and support" [ref: a484.2/1], emphasised CPS independence [ref: a484.4/1], and said invitees would be expected to attend [ref: a485.1/1]. She explained multiple government witness statements and described them as setting out "our position on China" [ref: a485.3/1]. She outlined proactive guidance and support for those targeted, including by MI5 and GCHQ [ref: a486.0/1], noted that the Official Secrets Act was updated by the National Security Act after issues with defining an "enemy" [ref: a486.4/1], and affirmed that China "poses threats to UK security" [ref: a486.6/1].

### Planning and Infrastructure Bill – Purpose clause debate
Baroness Pinnock proposed a purpose clause to balance accelerating homes and infrastructure, improving processes, nature recovery and community acceptability [ref: a487.4/2]. Lord Fuller criticised the Bill’s centralisation [ref: a488.0/2]. Baroness Taylor said the Bill supports "building 1.5 million homes" and "fast-tracking 150" major decisions this Parliament [ref: a491.0/2], arguing a purpose clause "would create ambiguity rather than clarity" [ref: a491.0/11]. The amendment was withdrawn [ref: a493.0/3].

### Planning Bill – NSIP cumulative impacts
Baroness McIntosh sought to mandate cumulative impact assessments for NSIPs. Baroness Taylor said these are already addressed: strategic environmental assessments for national policy statements must include cumulative effects [ref: a497.1/3], and project‑level environmental impact assessments are required before consent can be granted [ref: a497.1/4]. The amendment was withdrawn [ref: a498.0/3].

### Planning Bill – National policy statements: restoring parliamentary reply duty
Baroness Coffey’s Amendment 3, to retain a requirement for the Government to reply to parliamentary resolutions or Select Committee recommendations on NPSs, was agreed (Ayes 235, Noes 164) [ref: a502.0/2]. She argued that when Parliament makes recommendations, "the least we can expect is that the Government will respond" [ref: a499.0/2]. Ministers defended a streamlined update route with consultation and a 21‑day laying period, during which the Commons can block amendments [ref: a501.0/4, a501.0/5].

### Planning Bill – Water projects as NSIPs with third‑party delivery; heritage and community safeguards
Government Amendment 4 enables "third-party providers appointed by water undertakers" to deliver reservoirs, water transfers and desalination plants via the NSIP route [ref: a506.0/2]. The House agreed Lord Lansley’s amendments to ensure such providers are designated infrastructure providers under the 2013 regulations [ref: a522.2/4]. Two safeguards were added: Lord Parkinson’s Amendment 7A requiring heritage consent regimes to continue to apply to reservoir NSIPs passed (Ayes 216, Noes 175) [ref: a522.5/3], despite the Government’s view that existing Planning Act provisions already protect heritage [ref: a506.0/8-11]. Baroness Scott of Bybrook’s Amendment 7B—requiring residents be notified and able to make representations before projects demolishing more than 20 homes proceed as NSIPs—also passed (Ayes 227, Noes 168) [ref: a526.2/2], although Ministers noted qualifying reservoirs are automatically NSIPs and outlined affected persons’ existing rights in examinations [ref: a506.0/13-16]. The Minister said a consultation to improve reservoir safety regulation is imminent [ref: a506.0/19-21].

### Planning Bill – Pre‑application consultation
Baroness Pinnock proposed to retain statutory pre‑application consultation and to require guidance to reflect principles of meaningful engagement. The Government argued statutory requirements have become "overly rigid" and encourage tick‑box behaviour, preferring proportionate engagement under guidance [ref: a532.0/2]. Baroness Taylor said, "we all agree that we need to speed the system up" and warned the amendments would reinstate problems [ref: a532.0/5]. Amendment 12 to embed consultation principles in guidance was defeated (Ayes 61, Noes 154) [ref: a536.2/2].

### Planning Bill – Acceptance stage for DCOs
Responding to sector concerns, Ministers "restore[d] the original, clear test for acceptance"—that applications be of a "satisfactory standard"—and removed powers to delay acceptance for minor changes [ref: a538.2/8, a541.1/7]. They also required publication of reasons for any non‑acceptance decision [ref: a541.1/5]. The House agreed these changes [ref: a540.1/2, a541.1/9].

### Planning Bill – Regulators and net zero duties for low‑carbon NSIPs
Lord Ravensdale’s Amendment 20A to place duties on relevant authorities when engaging on low‑carbon NSIPs was withdrawn. The Minister pointed to strengthened national policy statements and a new duty on public bodies to have regard to guidance, adding that communications on fast‑tracked strategic policy statements for regulators would come "very soon"—"days, not weeks or months" [ref: a545.0/7-9].

### Planning Bill – Environmental Delivery Plans (EDPs) and NSIP decisions
Baroness Coffey sought to require the Secretary of State to consider EDPs when determining NSIPs. The Minister said that, where a promoter opts to pay the levy, "the making of that commitment discharges the relevant environmental obligation" and EDPs themselves do not need to be considered in consent decisions [ref: a549.0/2-3]. The amendment was withdrawn [ref: a549.1/2].

### Northern Ireland Troubles: Government Statement and Q&A
Baroness Anderson announced legislation "to repeal and replace the legacy Act" and a remedial order to "remove the previous Government’s much‑criticised immunity scheme" [ref: a549.3/4, a549.3/10-11]. She outlined safeguards for veterans, including that they "will not be forced to travel to or around Northern Ireland" and may "seek anonymity" [ref: a549.3/8-9]. She promised full cooperation with inquiries [ref: a555.0/4], said nine inquests would immediately restart and up to 24 would be assessed by the Solicitor‑General [ref: a555.0/10], and expected Ireland’s interstate case to fall away once the Bill receives Royal Assent [ref: a555.0/12]. She said the reformed commission’s governance would be strengthened with two co‑directors of investigations [ref: a559.1/2], confirmed no expectation of appointing a co‑director from the Republic of Ireland [ref: a560.1/1], and stressed a victim‑centred approach and "fairness under" the law [ref: a560.3/2, a561.1/3].

### Planning Bill – Whistleblowing on NSIPs
Baroness Kramer proposed making the National Infrastructure and Service Transformation Authority a whistleblowing channel for NSIPs. The Government responded, "we do not share the view that there is evidence of whistleblowing being a current, widespread concern within the NSIP regime" and noted more prescribed persons "can already be prescribed" under existing law [ref: a566.0/2-3]. The amendment was withdrawn [ref: a566.1/4].

### Planning Bill – DCOs for critical national priority projects: confirmation by Act
Viscount Hanworth proposed confirming certain DCOs via an Act of Parliament, ousting judicial review. The Government said this would "shield decisions from judicial review even where they were unlawful" and risk constitutional confrontation, instead working with the judiciary on faster timetables for NSIP cases [ref: a574.1/3, a574.1/6-7]. The amendment was withdrawn [ref: a575.0/2].

### Planning Bill – Grid connections for small renewables and local area energy plans
Baroness Bennett’s amendment to prioritise support for sub‑10 MW connection applicants was withdrawn after Ministers pointed to Ofgem’s planned obligations and incentives on networks to improve service for smaller customers [ref: a579.0/2-3]. On local area energy plans, the Government said relevant research and guidance work is under way with local government, including tools due by January 2026, and did not see a need for primary legislation [ref: a579.0/6-7].

### Planning Bill – Long duration electricity storage (LDES) fire safety
Lord Fuller’s proposal to mandate local fire authority consultation and fees for LDES installations was withdrawn. Ministers noted that the HSE regulates safety and that "Defra will conclude its industry consultation shortly" on bringing BESS within environmental permitting, and that Minister Shanks "hosted a round table today" on battery safety [ref: a585.1/4, a585.1/6-7]. A meeting was scheduled to continue discussions [ref: a586.0/1-4].

### Planning Bill – Consumer benefit scheme: affirmative procedure
The Government accepted the Delegated Powers Committee’s recommendation so that all regulations implementing the electricity bill discount scheme are subject to the affirmative procedure: "The Government welcome the recommendation ... and ... accept its suggestion" [ref: a587.2/1-2]. The Opposition welcomed the change [ref: a588.0/3].

### Planning Bill – Forestry estate renewables and Forestry Commission duties
Amendments confined Clause 28 (renewables on the public forest estate) to England and clarified that Secretary of State consent can be required only for projects above set capacities—"5 megawatts" for wind and "50 megawatts" for other technologies—adjustable by regulation [ref: a595.2/14]. Ministers said the thresholds were based on current data on "the typical scale, visual footprint and land use" and can be updated [ref: a595.0/2]. An amendment to place explicit climate and biodiversity duties on the Forestry Commission was debated; Ministers argued existing duties already require regard to biodiversity and climate and said wider updates to the 1967 Act may be considered via a future vehicle [ref: a589.0/7-11].

### Planning Bill – Seismic arrays and onshore wind
A Government amendment was agreed to empower regulations for safeguarding defence seismic arrays (notably Eskdalemuir) while enabling onshore wind. It would "empower the Secretary of State to make regulations ... relating to planning permissions for wind generating stations that may affect the functioning of a relevant seismic array system" [ref: a600.0/1-4].

### Planning Bill – Solar on best and most versatile agricultural land
Lord Fuller’s amendment to prohibit the NSIP route for large‑scale solar on grade 1, 2 or 3a land was defeated (Ayes 32, Noes 57) [ref: a618.2/1]. Ministers said, "We must find the right balance between food security and energy security" and noted monitoring of solar projects "from application to operation" [ref: a616.0/2, a617.1/1].
