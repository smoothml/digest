## High-Level Summary
The House of Lords questioned Ministers on progress towards ending new HIV transmissions in England by 2030, the management and future of British Steel following nationalisation, executions and human-rights abuses in Iran, and the retention of China expertise in government. It agreed a Business of the House motion to bring forward committee-stage sittings for the Health Bill and considered a Commons urgent-question answer on alleged access to UK parliamentarians’ data. The principal debate was on the Autumn Budget 2026, covering fiscal policy, household support, growth, defence, welfare, housing, devolution, energy, entrepreneurship and public services. The Budget motion was agreed and the House adjourned at 6.07 pm; no division numbers were stated in the transcript.

## Detailed Summary
### HIV: ending new transmissions by 2030
Lord Black of Brentwood asked what progress had been made towards ending new HIV transmissions in England by 2030. Baroness Hyde of Bemerton reported provisional falls in new diagnoses in 2025 compared with 2024: 7% among gay and bisexual men, 15% among heterosexual men and 22% among heterosexual women. She said that the ring-fenced public health grant supported open-access sexual-health services, including pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP), and that local authorities could procure online and postal PrEP. The transcript does not record a specific commitment to expand PrEP through community pharmacies. She described the £9 million, three-year retention and re-engagement initiative for people diagnosed with HIV who were not currently retained in specialist care; detailed national plans were to follow. Directly relevant statements included “new HIV diagnoses continuing to fall” and “Detailed plans for that national retention and re-engagement initiative will be set out in due course”.

Members also raised opt-out testing in accident and emergency departments, geographical access to testing, inequalities affecting Black African heterosexual people, school-based sex education, community testing and stigma. On continued funding and expansion of accident and emergency testing, the Minister referred to the 2027 spending review and said she would write on the remainder of the question. She did not pre-empt the outcome of the ongoing NHS app testing pilot. She also pointed to the fourth priority of the HIV action plan, “thrive”, which was intended to address stigma and improve quality of life for people living with HIV. The date of the Foreign Secretary’s last discussion with Iranian officials is not relevant to this topic; no equivalent unresolved outcome was stated beyond the commitments above.

### British Steel: nationalisation and transformation
Lord Fox asked what British Steel had changed since nationalisation in relation to products and markets, operating costs, raw materials and investment. Baroness Lloyd of Effra said that the new board, led by Alan Lovell, was working with management to stabilise the business, understand its cost base and develop a transformation plan. She said that management was reviewing material expenditure and investing in health and safety and blast-furnace maintenance to support safer and more cost-effective operations. The transcript does not provide specific changes to products or markets, financial-loss forecasts, or financial targets. The Minister said that the plan would be published once agreed, subject to commercial confidentiality: “the board will bring forward a plan for transformation” and “We have undertaken to publish the key elements of the transformation plan once it is agreed, subject to elements which are commercially confidential”.

Members also asked about raw-material supplies, public procurement, private investment and the wider business environment. The Minister said the transformation plan would address the company’s competitiveness, markets and supplies, and highlighted updated procurement guidance so that UK-made steel was routinely considered in public projects. She did not state the Government’s anticipated losses for the next two years; the longer-term requirements for public funding and private investment remained linked to the board’s transformation plan. The Minister also identified health and safety and carefully managed investment as priorities during the transition.

### Iran: executions of dissidents and opposition activists
Lord McCabe asked when the Foreign Secretary had last discussed executions with Iranian officials. Baroness Winterton of Doncaster condemned Iran’s use of the death penalty against protesters and opposition activists and called for an immediate moratorium. The date of the Foreign Secretary’s last such discussion was not stated in the transcript. Members raised reports of executions, threats against lawyers, the case of Mohammad Javad Vafaei Sani, women facing execution, the treatment of religious minorities and safe routes to the UK for people at risk. The Minister undertook to raise threats against lawyers in the UK’s national statement to the United Nations General Assembly Third Committee and said she would look at the case of Vafaei Sani: “Iran must immediately establish a moratorium on executions” and “Iran should cease reprisals against lawyers representing protesters”.

On Iran’s election to the UN Committee for Programme and Coordination, the Minister said that the UK did not comment on secret ballots but stated: “We strongly regret that Iran has been elected to the UN Committee for Programme and Coordination”. She said the Government regularly raised the treatment of Iranian women and girls in international forums. In response to concerns about Iranian Christians and other religious minorities, she said she would take away the point about safe and legal routes; no specific route or policy change was announced in the transcript.

### China: UK Government expertise
Lord Davidson of Glen Clova asked how the Government would maintain access to China expertise in light of reports of experts leaving government. Baroness Winterton of Doncaster referred to the cross-government China capability programme, which had trained more than 1,000 people from departments, agencies and public bodies in the previous year, and to a China cadre with more than 800 members. She also cited research analysts covering China, advanced Mandarin qualifications among Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office staff, and a China pathway in the diplomatic Fast Stream. The Minister said the Government had “an extensive and integrated pool of China expertise”.

Members questioned recruitment, institutional memory, staffing reductions and the balance between security and economic engagement with China. The Minister did not provide figures on recruitment of graduates in Chinese studies or on Arabic-language personnel. She rejected the suggestion that the Government’s approach to China was a strategic misstep, describing it as a long-term approach rooted in UK and global interests. She referred to China, including Hong Kong, as equivalent to the UK’s third-largest trading partner in goods and said that Keir Starmer’s visit to China had secured £2.2 billion in export deals and £2.3 billion in market access. She said overseas posts were being protected and that the Government considered their China expertise and training resources adequate.

### Business of the House: Health Bill committee stage
Baroness Smith of Basildon moved a motion to dispense with Standing Order 38(1) on Tuesday 17 November and Wednesday 25 November, enabling committee stage of the Health Bill to begin before oral questions. She said the proposal followed discussions through the usual channels and was intended to provide additional scrutiny time while avoiding very late sittings. She described it as “a proportionate and sensible plan”. The motion was agreed. No further next step beyond the planned committee-stage sittings was stated in the transcript.

### China: alleged breach of UK parliamentarians’ data
The House considered an answer given in the House of Commons on 10 November to an urgent question concerning alleged activity involving UK parliamentarians’ phone records and financial information. Lord Cameron of Lochiel asked why affected parliamentarians had not been briefed. Lord Hanson of Flint said he could not comment on what the Government knew or when because an FBI investigation and an Interpol arrest warrant were ongoing, but said that confidential briefings could be arranged for affected Members: “The Government will not comment for security reasons on what we knew and when”.

Members also sought a wider risk-management framework for relations with China, clarification of parliamentary procedures and privilege, possible extension of the Foreign Influence Registration Scheme (FIRS), and assurances that parliamentarians and the Speakers would be warned about threats. The Minister said Home Office officials were discussing the security implications with the parliamentary authorities and would examine lessons from the case. He said the Government would publish the first annual FIRS report shortly but would not comment publicly in advance on extending the scheme to additional countries. He also said officials would work with the Lord Speaker and the Speaker of the House of Commons on protecting the integrity of the democratic process. No date for any briefing, conclusion of the investigations or extension of FIRS was stated in the transcript.

### Autumn Budget 2026: fiscal strategy, household support and the economy
Lord Pitt-Watson opened the take-note debate by presenting the Budget as part of a wider programme that included a forthcoming 10-year plan and next year’s spending review. He said it complied with the fiscal rules, funded defence and offered support to households and businesses, including a £100 energy payment for eligible households in council tax bands A to D in England, extensions to VAT and fuel-duty measures, and business-rates support. He described the purpose as providing “breathing space for families and businesses”. Supporters emphasised fiscal discipline, public investment, devolution, the National Wealth Fund, the British Business Bank, housing, homelessness, prevention and innovation. Lord Pitt-Watson later said that the Government intended to spend 2.7% of GDP on defence in 2027, 3% in 2030 and 3.5% in 2035.

Opposition and other Members argued that the Budget relied on temporary measures and deferred difficult decisions on debt, welfare, defence, taxation, energy, housing and growth. Baroness Neville-Rolfe described it as “a Budget of short-term fixes and promises of action tomorrow”, while Lord Bridges of Headley argued that “our finances are not resilient but fragile” and questioned how the defence increase would be funded. Other contributions addressed farmers and nature, high-street business rates, self-employed taxation, science and technology scale-ups, artificial intelligence, youth unemployment and public-service prevention. Four maiden speeches were delivered, in chronological order, by Baroness Ussher, Baroness Aglionby, Lord Neri and Baroness Kitching of Penistone, covering economic policy, farming and environmental land management, music and creative industries, and local government. Lord Pitt-Watson said welfare reform would be informed by the Milburn and Timms reviews and offered further discussion or written responses on detailed matters, including quantitative easing and quantitative tightening. He said he believed that some of the £1.5 billion for social and affordable housing could be available to housing associations. The motion was agreed and the House adjourned at 6.07 pm.
