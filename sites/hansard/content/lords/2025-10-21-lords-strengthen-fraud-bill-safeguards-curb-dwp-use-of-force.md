## High-Level Summary
The House of Lords undertook Oral Questions on housing delivery, rail rolling‑stock plans, Erasmus+ association and prison education, and heard a Private Notice Question on the Amazon Web Services outage. Peers then considered the Public Authorities (Fraud, Error and Recovery) Bill on Report, agreeing amendments to tighten eligibility‑verification safeguards and to restrict DWP investigators’ use of force to property only. An amendment to expand the independent review of eligibility verification costs and impacts was also agreed, while a bid to compel disclosure to an independent reviewer on another clause tied and therefore fell. The Deprivation of Citizenship Orders (Effect during Appeal) Bill completed Lords stages. Ministers confirmed a government response on Carer’s Allowance overpayments will be published before year‑end and clarified that new DWP recovery powers will not apply to people currently on benefits.

## Detailed Summary
### Oral Question: New Homes Target
Lord Young of Cookham asked about progress towards the target of 1.5 million new homes. Baroness Taylor of Stevenage reaffirmed the commitment and said “our bold planning reforms alone will drive housebuilding in the UK to its highest rate in 40 years” [ref: d621.2/1]. On London, she said the Government is working with the Mayor/GLA and that “London will be allocated up to 30% of the £39 billion social and affordable homes programme” (about £11.7bn) [ref: d621.4/1]. 
She said modern methods of construction could contribute [ref: d622.1/1], reiterated a brownfield‑first approach—“We have been very clear that we take a brownfield-first approach” [ref: d622.3/1]—but asked authorities unable to meet needs to review the green belt, prioritising low‑quality grey belt [ref: d622.3/1]. Local authorities were described as “key partners” in delivery, with support via planning fee changes and “a £46 million package of support to help train and build capacity” [ref: d623.0/2; d623.4/1]. She added new homes are expected to have PV panels “wherever possible” and a Future Homes Standard will be published later this year [ref: d623.2/1].

### Oral Question: Great British Railways – Rolling Stock Strategy
Lord Hendy of Richmond Hill said a long‑term rolling stock and infrastructure strategy is being developed “to give certainty to the manufacturing and assembly market” and to support carbon‑friendly traction and accessibility [ref: d624.4/1]. Engagement with EUROFIMA is under way and, if appropriate after due diligence, the UK “would aim to accede by the end of 2026” [ref: d624.6/1]. 
On South Western Railway’s new fleet, he said “as of today, 23 are now in service” out of 90, with further introduction as driver training progresses [ref: d625.1/1]. He expects to publish the strategy “next summer” and noted three live procurements [ref: d625.3/1]. He highlighted a procurement of “464 vehicles for Northern” to replace an elderly diesel fleet [ref: d626.2/2], confirmed decarbonisation via bi/tri‑mode technology [ref: d627.2/1], and emphasised staffing, particularly reliable Sunday services [ref: d627.4/1].

### Oral Question: Erasmus+ Association
Baroness Anderson of Stoke‑on‑Trent said the UK has agreed to work towards association to Erasmus+ on mutually agreed financial terms, with negotiations under way [ref: d628.1/1]. She said association in 2027–28 is being considered, “although timelines are extremely tight” and “we are moving at pace” [ref: d628.3/1]. She added the aim is to be part of the next EU multi‑annual financial framework—“we therefore hope to be part of the full MFF going forward” [ref: d629.0/1]. 
She confirmed continued commitment to the Turing, Taith and SEEP schemes, seeking “no gap” in opportunities [ref: d630.0/1], and said the Government will “seek to ensure the fullest opportunities, including in respect of staffing” such as teacher development [ref: d629.2/1].

### Oral Question: Education in Prisons
Lord Timpson said, despite pressures, “The education budget has not been cut—in fact, it has increased by 3%,” though some prisons face reduced delivery due to rising costs [ref: d631.2/1]. He cited progress: “10% more prisoners participated in education compared to last year” and a reading strategy in every prison, while emphasising skills and work opportunities [ref: d631.4/1]. 
Peers raised peer‑mentoring (Shannon Trust), procurement, and comments about an “oversupply”. The Minister referred to population changes (e.g., Manchester) and the importance of skills for employment on release [ref: d633.0/1]. He noted churn harms continuity—“the average number of days a woman is there is 46” at Eastwood Park—making stability a priority [ref: d633.5/1]. On AI/digital, he said, “Digital learning is important in prisons” and mentioned in‑cell Launchpad devices and other tech initiatives [ref: d634.2/1].

### Private Notice Question: Amazon Web Services Outage
Lord Leong said the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology is leading the Government response. “There is no evidence that this was caused by any malicious activity,” with AWS citing a DNS configuration issue [ref: d635.3/1]. Departments choose suppliers, but the Government is seeking to “diversify the UK’s cloud ecosystem” and improve resilience, with NCSC guidance and business continuity planning [ref: d635.3/1]. 
Costs are still being assessed; resilience measures include treating major cloud providers as part of critical national infrastructure and consulting on enhanced incident reporting [ref: d636.2/1–2]. He listed impacted departments as “HMRC, the Home Office, the DVLA and the DWP” [ref: d637.1/1] and said a cybersecurity and resilience Bill is expected “very soon, before the end of the year” [ref: d638.4/1].

### Legislation and Motions: First Readings and Approvals
The Diego Garcia Military Base and British Indian Ocean Territory Bill was brought from the Commons, read a first time and ordered to be printed [ref: d639.0/1]. The House approved the Aviation Safety (Amendment) Regulations 2025 [ref: d639.3/4]. The Animal Welfare (Import of Dogs, Cats and Ferrets) Bill’s order of commitment was discharged by agreement [ref: d639.6/1–2].

### Commons Urgent Question: Alleged Spying Case – Home Office Involvement (Answer repeated)
Lord Hanson of Flint said that “Ministers were informed after the DPP had made his decision and shortly before reporting restrictions were lifted” and he came to Parliament immediately [ref: d641.0/1]. He reiterated there was no ministerial interference—“the CPS has confirmed that it came under no outside pressure” [ref: d639.8/4–6]—and confirmed full co‑operation with ISC and Joint Committee inquiries [ref: d642.3/1]. He affirmed the presumption of innocence for the two men whose case was discontinued: “they are not guilty of any offence” [ref: d644.1/1]. He also noted the Chinese embassy planning application will follow the statutory planning process [ref: d642.1/2].

### Public Authorities (Fraud, Error and Recovery) Bill – Report (2nd Day): Oversight and PSFA Governance
Baroness Finn moved amendments to strengthen ministerial and parliamentary oversight of investigatory and enforcement powers, including ministerial authorisation for particularly intrusive actions and maintaining a register laid annually before Parliament. The Government opposed them, warning they “would give dishonest Ministers the power to block investigations that were politically inconvenient” [ref: d647.1/5] and could expose investigators [ref: d647.1/13]. The House nevertheless agreed Amendment 27 (Ayes 270, Noes 160) and Amendment 28 [ref: d650.0/6; d654.1/3]. 
Government amendments then enabled the Public Sector Fraud Authority to be established by affirmative regulations and allowed the transfer of functions to another public authority, so the enforcement unit can be placed at arm’s length from Ministers without creating a new ALB [ref: d655.1/2; d655.2/4].

### Public Authorities (Fraud, Error and Recovery) Bill – Report (2nd Day): Scotland and Eligibility Verification (EVM) Safeguards
To align with devolution, the Government re‑framed social security information powers so Scottish Ministers can access equivalent powers and confirmed new recovery methods will not apply to devolved benefits [ref: d661.0/2; d663.0/4]. On the eligibility verification mechanism for bank data, Ministers added safeguards: EVNs may be issued only where “necessary and proportionate” [ref: d678.1/4]; clarified EVNs are only for “assisting in identifying” incorrect payments [ref: d678.1/2]; and extended banks’ internal review window to 14 days [ref: d692.1/2]. 
The House also agreed Amendment 52, making clear that “The existence of an eligibility indicator alone does not constitute reasonable grounds for suspicion” and requiring human review before intrusive action [ref: d679.3/2; d688.1/1]. Amendment 67 expanded the scope of the independent review to include costs and unintended consequences (Ayes 253, Noes 153) [ref: d693.2/1].

### Public Authorities (Fraud, Error and Recovery) Bill – Report (2nd Day): DWP Powers of Entry and Reviewer Access to Information
Peers debated new DWP powers of entry, search and seizure. The House agreed Amendment 76 to limit authorised investigators’ use of reasonable force to “items and property, not against persons”, preserving police powers (Ayes 212, Noes 144) [ref: d710.1/2; d710.4/1]. The Minister said DWP intends that “reasonable force will be used only against things, not people” and that officers will have equivalent training and court‑authorised warrants [ref: d706.2/1; d706.2/3]. 
On independent reviewer access to information under Clause 89, an amendment to change “may” to “must” tied 138–138 and, under Standing Orders, was disagreed [ref: d721.0/1–2].

### Deprivation of Citizenship Orders (Effect during Appeal) Bill – Committee and Third Reading
An amendment to protect the citizenship of children born during a parent’s appeal was withdrawn. The Minister confirmed that “where a child already holds British citizenship, the subsequent deprivation of a parent’s citizenship does not change that” [ref: d724.0/2]. The Bill was then read a third time and passed [ref: d726.3/3].

### Public Authorities (Fraud, Error and Recovery) Bill – Report (2nd Day) (continued): Recovery Powers, Carer’s Allowance, Overpayments, ‘Sickfluencers’, Scotland and Commencement
Ministers clarified that the new DWP recovery methods (e.g., direct deduction orders and driving disqualification) cannot be used where a person “is entitled to and in receipt of a relevant benefit”, and any order must be suspended or revoked if benefits start [ref: d731.1/2–3]. Banks may inform customers about account‑information notices once a three‑month prohibition has expired [ref: d732.1/3]. 
On Carer’s Allowance overpayments, Ministers committed: “we will publish both the report … and the Government’s response to it before the end of this year” [ref: d737.0/3]; a blanket pause on recoveries was not accepted. A proposal to require affordability consultation and a six‑year limit for recovery by benefit deductions was not pressed; Ministers cited existing caps (the fair repayment rate at 15%) and hardship safeguards [ref: d745.0/9–10]. An offence targeting online ‘sickfluencers’ was withdrawn after Ministers noted existing offences already criminalise facilitating fraud (Fraud Act 2006; Serious Crime Act 2007) [ref: d752.0/2]. Scottish provisions were adjusted to treat certain clauses as pre‑commencement for devolution [ref: d753.2/2]. Commencement powers were made flexible to start provisions on different days “in different areas” for operational readiness [ref: d754.1/2].
