## High-Level Summary
The House of Lords held a substantial Report day on the Planning and Infrastructure Bill, alongside Oral Questions and a Home Office Statement. Peers focused on climate and biodiversity duties in planning, small‑developer burdens, and the new Nature Restoration Fund (NRF) and Environmental Delivery Plans (EDPs). The House voted to restrict EDPs to nutrient neutrality, water quality, water resource and air quality, while proposals on gambling outlet controls and special treatment for SMEs in planning were defeated. Oral Questions covered an EU youth mobility scheme, tackling corruption using public funds in poorer countries, water sector reform, antisemitism in universities, and emergency aid for Jamaica after Hurricane Melissa. A Statement on an erroneous prisoner release announced immediate safeguards and an independent investigation.

## Detailed Summary
### Planning Bill – Climate and biodiversity duties in planning (Amendments 114, 121F, 206)
Lord Ravensdale sought a statutory duty for Ministers and planning authorities to give “special regard” to climate mitigation and adaptation across planning to upweight climate in decisions, arguing it would “effectively prioritise or upweight climate within the planning system” [ref: a1322.0/2]. The Minister said existing statutes and the National Planning Policy Framework already embed climate considerations and warned that a new legal duty could trigger litigation: “A legal obligation to give special regard to climate change across the planning system risks opening many decisions to potential legal challenges” [ref: a1328.0/8]. She invited engagement on upcoming NPPF consultation [ref: a1328.0/6]. Lord Ravensdale withdrew the amendment [ref: a1331.0/5]. Linked debates: Lord Blencathra highlighted extensive biodiversity duties under the Environment Act 2021, while the Minister said a new biodiversity duty would duplicate existing law and policy [ref: a1328.0/11-16].

### Planning – Habitats and strategic environmental assessment at plan stage (Amendments 115–116)
Baroness Willis proposed requiring Habitats Regulations assessments and strategic environmental assessments at local plan/spatial strategy stage to steer development away from sensitive sites. The Government responded that habitats assessments are already required where relevant and that project‑level EIAs at plan‑making would lack sufficient detail: “This would require a depth of information about a specific development proposal that simply would not be available at the plan-making stage” [ref: a1335.0/3]. She withdrew the amendment, stressing work should be done earlier to speed decisions [ref: a1336.0/2, a1336.0/4].

### Gambling outlets – cumulative impact assessments (Amendment 117)
Lord Foster sought to enable licensing authorities to adopt cumulative impact assessments (CIAs) for gambling and require planning authorities to have regard to them, citing Government pledges to introduce CIAs “when parliamentary time allows” [ref: a1337.0/12-14]. Ministers agreed on CIAs in principle but said this Planning Bill is “not a licensing Bill” and warned of conflicts between planning and licensing if used here [ref: a1346.0/3; a1347.0/1]. The amendment was defeated (Ayes 97, Noes 128) [ref: a1349.2/2]. The Government said they would introduce gambling CIAs via “a suitable vehicle” [ref: a1346.2/1].

### SME developers – proposed planning duty (Amendment 119)
Baroness Neville‑Rolfe proposed a statutory ‘have regard’ duty on public bodies to consider SME difficulties in the planning system. The Minister argued it would impose new burdens and routes for legal challenge, preferring targeted reforms such as a ‘medium site’ definition and streamlined requirements [ref: a1347.4/5-7]. The House rejected the amendment (Ayes 133, Noes 188) [ref: a1352.1/2].

### Nature Restoration Fund/EDPs – limiting scope (Amendment 130)
Baroness Willis warned Part 3 adds a “further layer of potential bureaucracy” and sought to limit EDPs to nutrient neutrality, water quality, water resource and air quality [ref: a1359.0/2; a1359.0/4]. The Government said “the status quo is not working” and that EDPs would be targeted plans required to “materially outweigh the negative effect of development” [ref: a1383.4/2; a1383.4/4]. The House agreed Amendment 130 (Ayes 260, Noes 141), restricting EDPs to those specified issues [ref: a1388.3/2].

### Evidence, reporting and monitoring of EDPs (Amendments 131, 137, 151, 152, 156, 157, 174)
Peers pressed for explicit duties to use the best available evidence and for more reporting. Ministers said government changes already require NE and the Secretary of State to take account of “the best available scientific evidence” and that Natural England will publish annual NRF reports alongside mid‑point and end‑point reports for each EDP [ref: a1395.0/1-3]. Proposals to report on local economic impacts were rejected as disproportionate [ref: a1395.0/3-4]. The amendments were withdrawn or not moved.

### Technical/cross‑border changes and Ramsar protections
Government amendments clarified cross‑border consultation with Wales/Scotland for EDPs and aligned marine definitions [ref: a1396.2/1-6]. They also put Ramsar sites on a statutory footing so EDPs can address impacts on internationally important wetlands; Ministers said this offers clarity and continues existing protections: this “will place the existing policy protections for Ramsar sites on a statutory footing” [ref: a1396.2/6-7]. The House agreed the amendments [ref: a1399.0/4].

### Securing conservation measures; invasive species; swift bricks
A proposal to require Section 106 obligations for all EDP conservation measures was withdrawn after the Government argued NE needs flexibility and many measures will not require planning permission [ref: a1402.0/4-6]. Lord Roborough’s amendment to mandate eradication of invasive non‑native species where they threaten an EDP feature was resisted; Ministers said control can be a conservation measure but should be decided case‑by‑case to achieve best outcomes [ref: a1411.0/3-4]. On swift bricks, Ministers favoured planning policy over building regulations, noting that building regulations “were not designed to apply to the protection of wildlife” and promising consultation via the NPPF [ref: a1411.0/7-9].

### NRF/EDPs – levy, procurement, entry and CPOs
Ministers said making the nature restoration levy mandatory would be used only in “limited, exceptional circumstances” (e.g., to ensure deliverability of an EDP), with consultation and a duty to ensure viability so it does not render development “economically unviable” [ref: a1457.0/2; a1457.0/6]. Lord Curry’s amendment to require NE to favour private delivery and competitive procurement was defeated (Ayes 52, Noes 113) [ref: a1461.2/2]. The Earl of Caithness’s bid to extend NE’s entry notice to 21 days for farmers/statutory undertakers was rejected (Ayes 50, Noes 115) [ref: a1464.2/1]. On compulsory purchase, Ministers confirmed that where land acquired for an EDP becomes genuinely surplus, Crichel Down rules on offering it back apply [ref: a1466.3/1-2].

### Sustainable drainage systems (SuDS) and sewer connections (Amendments 197–198)
Baroness McIntosh urged immediate commencement of Schedule 3 to the Flood and Water Management Act 2010 (SuDS) and linking sewer connections to SuDS compliance. The Government highlighted updated NPPF policy now requiring SuDS for developments “that have drainage implications” and stated: “There is no automatic right to connect to the sewer system” as sewerage companies can refuse on technical grounds [ref: a1475.0/3-4]. She withdrew the amendments after debate on timing and forthcoming consultations [ref: a1476.2/1-2].

### Heritage trees
A new “heritage tree preservation order” regime was proposed to protect exceptional trees. Ministers argued that existing TPO powers and planning policy already protect ancient and veteran trees and that a new category risks confusion and burden: “the amendment does not … offer sufficient additional protection to justify its implementation” [ref: a1480.1/4]. The amendment was withdrawn.

### Nuclear power station consenting (Amendment 202)
A proposal to let the Secretary of State disregard certain environmental requirements for large nuclear DCOs and cap EIA size was withdrawn. The Government said such exemptions “would create uncertainty for developers and erode public support” and pointed to the forthcoming nuclear NPS (EN‑7) and flexibility in existing law [ref: a1485.1/2-3].

### Youth Mobility Scheme with the EU – Oral Question
Ministers said the UK is negotiating a “balanced youth experience scheme” with visas, place caps and time limits, adding they hope to “bring forward responses … in the coming months” [ref: a1366.3/1; a1366.5/1]. Members asked about language learning, Erasmus/Turing and access for disadvantaged youth; the Government cited the Turing scheme’s “over 35,000 opportunities” and said 61% of English participants are from underprivileged backgrounds [ref: a1368.0/1; a1369.0/1].

### Corruption: low‑ and middle‑income countries – Oral Question
The Government reaffirmed their commitment to reduce illicit finance harms and hold those responsible to account, citing the NCA’s international corruption units and plans for a new anti‑corruption strategy [ref: a1370.0/1]. On proposals for an international anti‑corruption court, Ministers were “supportive of the ambition” but noted “geopolitical challenges”, awaiting a draft treaty later this year [ref: a1370.5/1]. Responses covered sanctions on illicit gold, asset recovery, overseas territories’ transparency, and frozen assets, with Ministers emphasising acting “within the law” [ref: a1372.1/1].

### Water companies: private ownership – Oral Question
Asked about privatisation’s social costs, Ministers favoured tougher regulation over nationalisation, estimating renationalisation would cost “at least £100 billion” [ref: a1373.4/1]. They cited new measures (blocking bonuses; potential prison terms for cover‑ups) and plans for a single water regulator following the Cunliffe review [ref: a1373.4/1; a1374.4/1].

### Antisemitism: universities – Oral Question
Ministers condemned antisemitic intimidation of staff and students as “not free speech; it is harassment and it is abhorrent” [ref: a1376.3/1]. They highlighted the new OfS condition E6 on harassment and intimidation and stressed that protest is “not a right to disrupt, intimidate or harass” [ref: a1376.5/1; a1379.2/1], alongside support for chaplaincy and security training [ref: a1377.1/1].

### Jamaica: Hurricane Melissa – Private Notice Question
The UK announced up to £2.5 million in emergency aid and deployed crisis teams, with HMS “Trent” in the region, and committed to work through regional co‑ordination and parametric insurance to speed recovery [ref: a1379.5/1; a1380.0/2; a1381.0/1]. Ministers stressed Jamaica’s leadership and the UK’s readiness to assist as assessments develop [ref: a1382.1/1].

### Prisoner Release Checks – Statement
Following the erroneous release of Hadush Kebatu from HMP Chelmsford, the Home Secretary’s Statement said, “This was a mistake that should not have happened” [ref: a1443.1/2]. Immediate measures include strengthened release checklists and making duty governor presence compulsory for early removals, with an independent investigation led by Dame Lynne Owens [ref: a1443.1/6-8]. In Lords exchanges, Ministers said a £500 payment to facilitate deportation was an operational decision “far cheaper” than delay, and that the inquiry is expected to report within eight weeks [ref: a1449.0/7; a1452.3/1].
