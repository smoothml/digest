## High-Level Summary
The House of Lords concluded the second day of Report on the Planning and Infrastructure Bill, amending the Bill on heritage, legal challenge timing and Natural England advice, and approving powers to mandate accessibility at electric vehicle charge points. Peers tightened scrutiny of the forthcoming national scheme of delegation by requiring the first regulations to be affirmative, and defeated a government bid to restrict councils from refusing planning permission while call‑in is considered. Other planning measures (including on cross‑pavement EV charging, lane rental, post‑flood resilience and affordable housing delivery) were withdrawn following ministerial assurances. Oral questions covered drink‑ and drug‑driving limits, duty‑free rules for Belfast‑EU flights, open‑access rail capacity, and a delay to the International Maritime Organization’s net‑zero framework; later, the Government outlined a Post‑16 Education and Skills White Paper introducing “V‑levels”, a youth guarantee, and index‑linked university fees.

## Detailed Summary
### Clause 33 – Temporary possession for highways (Amendment 47)
Lord Moylan queried whether Clause 33 created a new power and the distinction between temporary "possession" and "occupation", warning of legal uncertainty and lack of guidance; he indicated he would seek a vote: "I will seek to test the opinion of the House." [ref: a760.0/1] Lord Hendy of Richmond Hill said the measure clarifies implied temporary possession powers already used and explained why “possession” matters for safe delivery, stating: "It is not a new power; it is an implied right to take land temporarily that already exists and is already used, but the Government’s Bill makes it explicit." [ref: a759.3/4] He cautioned that removing the term would "risk introducing legal uncertainty" [ref: a759.3/6]. Outcome: a division could not proceed as tellers were not appointed, and the amendment was disagreed. [ref: a760.0/2, a760.0/3]

### Heritage consents and disapplication – Clause 41 replaced (Amendments 48–51)
Baroness Pinnock withdrew her amendment after ministers tabled a full replacement of Clause 41. Lord Parkinson said Clause 41 had "caused the most concern among heritage groups" and welcomed that ministers had "listened to that chorus of disapproval" [ref: a762.1/1, a762.1/2]. Baroness Taylor of Stevenage said there would be “no loss of heritage protection”, introducing deemed listed building consent powers aligned with deemed planning permission and retaining the “special regard”/“special attention” duties [ref: a764.1/3, a764.1/5- a764.1/6]. Outcome: Government Amendment 49 (replacing Clause 41) agreed, and linked Amendment 51 (removing Schedule 2) agreed. [ref: a767.0/2, a767.2/3]

### EV charging: cross‑pavement solutions, parking impacts and accessibility (Amendments 52–55, 57)
Baroness Pidgeon sought to treat cross‑pavement charging as public charge points and to extend permitted development. Lord Hendy said the Government will "launch a consultation on introducing permitted development" and will write to local authorities to highlight delivery options, but that Section 50 street works licences must remain in place for safety while the market is nascent [ref: a775.0/2, a775.0/5, a775.0/3- a775.0/4]. Lord Moylan’s amendments on parking impact assessments and post‑installation reviews were resisted as burdensome and duplicative of existing traffic regulation procedures [ref: a775.0/7- a775.0/11], and were not moved [ref: a778.0/3]. Lord Borwick’s amendment to create enabling powers to mandate accessibility standards under the Automated and Electric Vehicles Act was pressed; he said "fewer than 3% of public electric vehicle charging points are considered safe and reliable for drivers with accessibility needs" [ref: a769.0/2]. The Minister acknowledged slow adoption of PAS 1899 and said a review "will be published shortly", but could not accept the amendment [ref: a775.0/15- a775.0/17]. Outcome: the House agreed Amendment 55 by 228–113. [ref: a779.0/2, a779.2/1]

### Low‑hazard reservoirs: review and deregulation (Amendment 56)
Baroness McIntosh of Pickering sought an assessment of the impact of the current regulatory regime and proposals to deregulate low‑hazard reservoirs to facilitate construction, citing farmers’ water needs after recent summers: "it is extremely important that this review is brought forward" [ref: a781.2/1]. Outcome: Agreed by division, Ayes 173, Noes 120. [ref: a781.2/2, a782.1/1]

### National Lane Rental Scheme (Amendment 58)
Lord Jamieson argued for a national, simpler lane‑rental regime to cut disruptive roadworks. Lord Hendy said lane rental is effective but "not suitable for every area or every road", with devolving approval to mayoral authorities under consultation and ring‑fenced revenues from 2026 [ref: a786.2/2- a786.2/4]. Outcome: Withdrawn. [ref: a787.0/2]

### Water infrastructure project licences (Amendment 58A)
Baroness Coffey proposed removing the “size and complexity” test for specified infrastructure projects to broaden competitive delivery. Earl Russell and Baroness Taylor of Stevenage pointed to a pending review and a forthcoming White Paper, with Baroness Taylor saying the Government will “address our proposals… in the White Paper that will be published shortly” [ref: a788.0/6, a789.1/2]. Outcome: Withdrawn. [ref: a790.0/2]

### Planning fees, enforcement costs and surcharges (Amendments 59–61)
Baroness Thornhill sought a statutory duty of proportionality for planning fees, arguing SMEs pay more per home and the Bill lacks a clear legal safeguard [ref: a790.3/3- a790.3/6]. Baroness Taylor replied that proportionality already exists, a local variation model with consultation is planned, and the surcharge for statutory consultees will be set so that "its income does not exceed" aggregate relevant costs [ref: a794.0/2- a794.0/4, a794.0/9]. Outcome: Amendments withdrawn or not moved. [ref: a797.0/3, a797.0/7]

### Mandatory training: climate and biodiversity content (Amendment 62)
Lord Hampton (for Baroness Boycott) sought science‑based training requirements on climate, biodiversity and ecological surveying for planning committee members and officers. Baroness Taylor said she was "very sympathetic" but that specifying content should be reserved for secondary legislation and guidance, not primary legislation [ref: a804.0/2- a804.0/4]. Outcome: Withdrawn. [ref: a804.1/3]

### Oral Question: Drink‑drive limit
Baroness Hayter asked whether the Government plan to lower the drink‑drive limit. Lord Hendy said the forthcoming road safety strategy will consider motoring offences including drink‑driving and that ministers are "deeply considering concerns raised by campaigners and bereaved families" [ref: a805.2/1]. Baroness Hayter urged reduction to 50 mg, noting a fifth of road deaths are due to drink‑driving [ref: a805.3/2]. The Minister also highlighted work on drug‑driving enforcement and detection [ref: a806.1/1]. No decision was announced.

### Oral Question: Duty‑free rules for Belfast‑EU flights
Baroness Hoey pressed for parity with the rest of the UK. Treasury Minister Lord Livermore said that allowing duty‑free on such routes would require checks and infrastructure inconsistent with the Windsor Framework, stating: "The enforcement controls required for this would run counter to the Windsor Framework" [ref: a808.2/1] and, more generally, "If you have duty-free, you have to have allowances… checks… border infrastructure" [ref: a809.3/1]. No policy change was announced.

### Oral Question: Open access rail services
Lord Snape asked about the impact on rail capacity. Lord Hendy said that while open access can provide connectivity and choice, main lines are "heavily constrained" with most extra train paths declined by the regulator, and noted that current charging gives open access operators advantages, with changes proposed in the forthcoming railways Bill [ref: a811.7/1, a812.0/2]. He said Great British Railways would manage the national timetable with appeals to the independent regulator [ref: a813.3/2].

### Oral Question: International Maritime Organization net‑zero framework
Lord Hendy said the UK is "disappointed" by the delay to 2026 in adopting the IMO framework, and will work to secure adoption next year to give industry certainty—potentially taking effect in 2029 [ref: a814.6/2]. He confirmed the UK will continue active engagement with IMO states and industry: "The simple answer to the noble Lord is yes." [ref: a815.1/1]

### Commons Urgent Question (repeated): Maccabi Tel Aviv away fans ban
Lord Parkinson asked when the Government were first aware of the safety advisory group’s intention to bar away fans. Baroness Twycross said the Home Office, via the UK Football Policing Unit, was involved in the risk assessment, and that the ban was confirmed "only last Thursday", after which ministers engaged with local partners [ref: a819.1/1]. She stressed operational independence but criticised outcomes that exclude fans, noting the wider context of rising antisemitism; she added that the club later refused an allocation [ref: a818.2/5, a820.0/1].

### Commons Urgent Question (repeated): National inquiry into grooming gangs
Lord Hanson said the Government are at "the very late stages" of appointing a chair and that the statutory inquiry will remain "laser-focused on grooming gangs", including ethnicity and religion, with a three‑year limit [ref: a823.1/1, a824.0/2- a824.0/4]. He emphasised victim‑survivor engagement, with the NWG Network facilitating meetings with prospective chairs [ref: a823.1/1- a823.1/2].

### Delegation of planning decisions – scrutiny and ministerial directions
Baroness Coffey’s Amendment 62A (preventing officers approving schemes outside plans or below density thresholds) was withdrawn. Lord Lansley’s Amendment 63 to require that the first regulations establishing a national scheme of delegation are subject to the affirmative procedure was agreed, 246–169. [ref: a836.2/2, a836.4/1] Government Amendment 64 to enable holding directions restricting refusals while a call‑in is considered was opposed as centralising and premature and was rejected by 236–163. [ref: a853.2/1] Earlier, the Minister had described the change as addressing an anomaly in current call‑in processes [ref: a839.2/1- a839.2/4].

### Special regard to heritage on deemed permissions (Amendment 66)
A government amendment applying the “special regard” duty for listed buildings (and the conservation area duty where relevant) when granting deemed planning permission—e.g., under Transport and Works Act orders—was agreed. [ref: a856.1/3]

### Extending planning implementation periods during legal challenges (Amendment 67)
The Government adopted a tiered extension to prevent planning permissions and listed building consents expiring during litigation: +1 year when permission to bring proceedings is granted; +1 year if permission to appeal to the Court of Appeal is granted; and +2 years if permission to appeal to the Supreme Court is granted [ref: a860.0/4]. Lord Banner welcomed the change and therefore did not press his alternative amendment [ref: a862.0/2- a862.0/3]. Outcome: Agreed. [ref: a865.0/2]

### Natural England planning advice: discretion and operational statement (Amendment 68)
The Government empowered Natural England to prioritise higher‑risk/higher‑opportunity advice requests on Town and Country Planning Act casework, and to publish an operational statement on how such requests will be handled [ref: a866.0/3]. Supporters cited a heavy caseload—over 32,000 planning consultations in 2023–24, nearly half low‑risk [ref: a871.1/3- a871.1/7]. Critics warned about reduced local ecological capacity and called for wider consultation and alignment with a forthcoming statutory consultees review [ref: a868.0/2- a868.0/5]. Outcome: Agreed. [ref: a878.3/3]

### Mediation and ADR in planning (Amendment 69)
Earl Russell (for Lord Murray) proposed guidance requiring promotion of mediation across key planning stages. Baroness Taylor said the National Planning Policy Framework already encourages proactive engagement (pre‑application, planning performance agreements), the Scottish approach is non‑mandatory, and a statutory duty risks adding complexity [ref: a885.0/2- a885.0/6]. Outcome: Withdrawn. [ref: a887.0/5]

### Post‑16 Education and Skills White Paper – Statement
The Government set out reforms to align skills with the industrial strategy: a new vocational “V‑levels” pathway alongside A‑ and T‑levels, a youth guarantee for 16–17‑year‑olds, Skills England, and index‑linking of university fee caps while reintroducing targeted maintenance grants funded by a levy on international students’ fees. The Secretary of State said “transformation is what we need” to meet AI, green energy and technological change [ref: a887.2/2- a887.2/3], aimed for “two‑thirds of our young people to get into high‑level learning” [ref: a887.2/11], confirmed fees will increase with inflation for two years [ref: a887.2/13], and announced parity for technical routes: “V‑levels… sitting proudly alongside A‑levels and T‑levels” [ref: a887.2/10].

### Property flood resilience and ‘agent‑of‑change’ (Amendments 70–71)
Baroness McIntosh of Pickering pressed for mandatory property flood resilience measures where building on higher‑risk flood areas. The Government cited existing building regulations promoting resilient construction and strong NPPF flood policies; she withdrew [ref: a907.0/3, a909.0/2]. On the ‘agent‑of‑change’ principle to protect existing venues from impacts of new residential development, peers urged a statutory footing; the Government preferred to use the NPPF and ongoing reviews, and the amendment was withdrawn [ref: a915.1/1- a915.1/3, a915.2/2].

### Affordable housing delivery (Amendment 72)
Lord Best sought to ensure that affordable housing required by planning consents is delivered, with at least 20% social rent. The Government pointed to strengthened viability guidance and enforcement of Section 106 obligations, noting upcoming work on national decision‑making policies; the amendment was withdrawn [ref: a921.1/3- a921.1/4, a922.1/3].

### Asylum accommodation and planning permission (Amendments 73–75, 87E)
Baroness Scott of Bybrook proposed requiring planning permission for repurposing hotels and houses in multiple occupation as asylum accommodation, to secure local consultation. The Government said the priority is to end hotel use "in a controlled and orderly way" and that restricting accommodation types would hinder that [ref: a926.0/3- a926.0/7]. Amendments 73 and 74 were defeated on division; subsequent related amendments were not moved. The House then adjourned at 10:29 pm. [ref: a929.6/1, a932.2/1, a932.2/2, a934.2/3]
