## High-Level Summary
The House of Lords began with the introductions of Baron Leunig and Baroness Ussher, followed by questions on fracture liaison services, Diego Garcia, industrial electricity prices and motorway fuel prices. Members then agreed the order of consideration for the Armed Forces Bill. The main business was Second Reading of the Health Bill, which proposes abolishing NHS England, expanding integrated care board responsibilities, creating a single patient record, and changing patient-safety and patient-voice arrangements. The Bill was read a second time and committed to a Committee of the Whole House; an amendment seeking a statement on Commons scrutiny was withdrawn before the motion was agreed.

## Detailed Summary
### Introductions
Timothy Charles Leunig was introduced as Baron Leunig, of Surbiton in the Royal Borough of Kingston upon Thames, made the solemn affirmation and signed an undertaking to abide by the Code of Conduct, supported by Baroness Kramer and Lord Pack. The transcript records that he “was introduced and made the solemn affirmation”. Katharine Anne Ussher was introduced as Baroness Ussher, of Burnley in the county of Lancashire, took the oath and signed the undertaking, supported by Lord Khan of Burnley and Baroness Hodge of Barking; she “was introduced and took the oath”. No substantive debate or further decision was recorded.

### Fracture liaison services
Lord Black of Brentwood asked how many new fracture liaison services (FLS) had opened in England since July 2024. Baroness Merron said the latest 2025 analysis showed an increase from 73 to 78 since 2024, but the database did not provide monthly figures: “we are unable to provide information on how many new services have opened since July 2024”. Lord Black and other Members questioned the pace of progress towards universal coverage by 2030, and asked about funding, implementation planning, milestones and accountability.

Baroness Merron said the commitment remained in place and described a whole-pathway approach covering prevention, diagnosis and treatment. She cited investment in 20 new DEXA scanners and said the Department of Health and Social Care was working with NHS England to keep secondary fracture prevention a priority. Baroness Hayter said she would table an amendment requiring a plan, while Baroness Merron said further details would be shared “in due course”. No implementation plan, funding arrangement or date for its publication was stated in the transcript.

### Diego Garcia Military Base and British Indian Ocean Territory Bill
Lord Bellingham asked whether the Diego Garcia Military Base and British Indian Ocean Territory Bill would be reintroduced. Baroness Winterton of Doncaster said Diego Garcia was strategically important and that the Government were continuing discussions with Mauritius and the United States. She said, “We will return to the Bill in due course”. Lord Bellingham also questioned the legal basis for claimed international-court risks and argued that the treaty should be abandoned.

Other Members asked about the security of the base and the rights and involvement of Chagossians. Baroness Winterton said the Government would continue engaging with the Chagossian community through the Chagossian Contact Group. She also said that “The agreement is the only viable path to resettlement on the archipelago”, and that eligible UK-based Chagossians could obtain Mauritian citizenship and participate in any future resettlement programme. No decision to reintroduce or abandon the Bill was announced.

### Industrial electricity prices and British manufacturing
Lord Blencathra asked about the effect of industrial electricity prices on the international competitiveness of British manufacturing and argued that the Government’s energy policy was harming industry. Lord Leong accepted that high prices could affect competitiveness but defended the Government’s support measures. He said that “from 2027 more than 10,000 businesses will be eligible for the British industrial competitiveness scheme”, with savings of up to 25% on electricity bills, and referred to the British industrial supercharger.

Members also raised network infrastructure, renewable-energy curtailment, support for horticulture, gas security, small nuclear reactors and the longer-term competitiveness of British industry. Lord Leong said renewable generation and clean-power investment would reduce exposure to volatile fossil-fuel markets: “Our transition to clean power will strengthen energy security and reduce exposure to volatile fossil fuel markets”. He said eligibility for the relevant support schemes would be reviewed later in the year. No further decision was recorded.

### Motorway service-station petrol and diesel prices
Lord Moylan asked about petrol and diesel prices at motorway service stations. Baroness Curran acknowledged that prices were typically higher there and referred to the Competition and Markets Authority’s finding that competition was weaker than elsewhere. She pointed to the fuel-finder open-data scheme, which the department estimated could save consumers £10.4 billion over 10 years. Members questioned whether an app was practical while driving, whether a proposed fuel-duty increase should be cancelled, and whether the Competition and Markets Authority needed stronger powers.

Further questions covered Russian-origin fuel, supply diversification, refinery capacity, carbon taxes, electric-vehicle charging and grid infrastructure. Baroness Curran said the UK’s ban on Russian oil and oil products applied irrespective of how they entered the UK, and that the Government were working with G7 partners to diversify supplies. She also referred to “the creation of ‘Great British Grid’” to help address grid-connection problems. The Government’s position was to keep the relevant measures under review; no further decision was recorded.

### Armed Forces Bill: order of consideration
Lord Coaker moved a motion setting out the order in which the amendments for the Report stage of the Armed Forces Bill were to be marshalled and considered. The order covered the clauses, schedules and title specified in the motion. The House agreed the motion: “Motion agreed”. The transcript records no division.

### Health Bill: Second Reading
Baroness Merron introduced the Health Bill as legislation intended both to improve the timeliness of NHS treatment and to reform the health service. Its principal measures discussed were the legal framework for a single patient record, the abolition of NHS England, greater responsibility for integrated care boards (ICBs), changes to patient-safety arrangements, and new arrangements for patient experience and public voice. The Minister said, “The Bill establishes the legal foundations for the single patient record”, and argued that transferring functions from NHS England would clarify ministerial accountability while enabling more local decision-making. She also confirmed the Government’s intention to introduce an amendment establishing a statutory maternity and neonatal commissioner.

Members differed over whether the proposed reorganisation would reduce duplication and improve accountability or create disruption, cost and uncertainty. Supporters including Lord Hunt of Kings Heath, Baroness Coffey, Baroness Neville-Rolfe and Baroness Nargund supported greater direct ministerial accountability and reductions in bureaucracy. Critics including Earl Howe, Lord Lansley, Lord Stevens, Baroness Pidgeon and Lord Scriven questioned the concentration of powers, the financial case and the risk of diverting leadership capacity from patient care; Earl Howe said, “The forces created by the Bill therefore pull in two opposing directions”. Baroness Merron said savings of up to £1 billion a year were expected by the end of the Parliament, building over time, and that the Government would publish further updates and revised impact assessments.

The single patient record received substantial support in principle, but Members sought safeguards covering confidentiality, sensitive information, consent, interoperability, access by carers and private-sector data. Baroness Merron said access would be restricted to authorised users with an audit trail, and that secondary uses such as planning and research would require a separate legal basis. Members also questioned the proposed abolition of Healthwatch and the transfer of the Health Services Safety Investigations Body’s functions to the Care Quality Commission, arguing that patient voice and safety investigations required independence. The Government committed to further engagement on both matters; Baroness Merron described Healthwatch as “an area for further work” and said the Government would continue listening on HSSIB. Other issues included health inequalities, social care, children’s services, mental health, unpaid carers, maternity care, specialist commissioning, workforce planning, continuity of care, disability rights, cancer services, prevention and the role of independent providers. The Government said a single national maternity and neonatal action plan would be published by the end of the year and confirmed that the mental health investment standard would continue. The Bill was read a second time: “Bill read a second time”. Further scrutiny is expected in Committee.

### Health Bill: commitment and Commons-scrutiny amendment
Baroness Merron moved that the Health Bill be committed to a Committee of the Whole House and set out the proposed order for consideration of its clauses and schedules. Lord Rooker proposed delaying Committee stage until the Minister provided a statement identifying provisions added in the Commons that had not been considered on the floor of that House. He said the amendment sought “a good audit trail” because programme motions could limit scrutiny and late amendments might not have been debated fully.

Baroness Merron described the Commons stages, including 17 Public Bill Committee sittings over nine days and a two-day Report stage. Lord Rooker remained dissatisfied but withdrew the amendment rather than pressing it to a vote: “Amendment to the Motion withdrawn”. The commitment and order-of-consideration motion was then agreed. The House adjourned at 10.44 pm.
