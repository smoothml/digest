## High-Level Summary
The House of Lords held oral questions on the Western Sahara conflict, national security risks to undersea cables, non‑crime hate incidents, and child poverty and ‘no recourse to public funds’. Peers then considered numerous Report‑stage amendments to the Planning and Infrastructure Bill, agreeing changes to require chalk stream protections in spatial strategies, to prioritise brownfield/compact growth in spatial development strategies, and to prevent demolition of Assets of Community Value via permitted development. Other planning amendments (e.g. distributed water storage, playing fields, proportionality in planning, and overlapping permissions following Hillside) were defeated. The Lords also debated a Commons Statement on reviewing the Airports National Policy Statement for Heathrow, and considered two Commons Urgent Questions. Royal Assent was notified for five Acts.

## Detailed Summary
### Western Sahara conflict: alleged Iranian support for Polisario
Lord Godson asked about the UK’s assessment of alleged Iranian support to the Polisario Front and coordination with allies. Baroness Chapman of Darlington replied the UK “has not seen evidence of Iranian support for the Polisario Front” and is monitoring Iranian activity [ref: b1045.2/1]. She said a UK Minister met the Polisario “in the context of the declaration that we signed to recognise Morocco’s intentions in Western Sahara” and called the approach “the right, pragmatic way forward” [ref: b1045.4/1]. She agreed the UK will proceed “with pragmatism … and … with full transparency” [ref: b1046.1/1], noted an upcoming UN Security Council decision and a regional “window” for progress [ref: b1047.0/1], and confirmed that while the UK abhors Iran’s destabilising activities, it has no specific evidence in this case [ref: b1047.2/1]. Outcome: no policy change; continued monitoring and engagement.

### Undersea cables: national security threat
Viscount Stansgate queried the threat to undersea cables. Lord Coaker said the Government keeps the threat “under close review”, with MoD monitoring, Royal Navy patrols, maritime patrol aircraft and the multi‑role ocean surveillance programme; after the strategic defence review, the Royal Navy will play a new coordinating role [ref: b1048.3/1]. He confirmed a seabed warfare strategy: “We certainly should develop one, and we are developing one” [ref: b1048.5/1]. Cooperation with Ireland, Norway and Germany was highlighted, including German P‑8 Poseidon deployments to work with UK aircraft at Lossiemouth [ref: b1049.0/1, b1049.2/1, b1049.4/1]. On preparedness, he said national exercise plans exist and the average cable repair time is “eight days”, with rerouting available [ref: b1050.1/1, b1051.2/1]. Outcome: No division; Ministers outlined surveillance, international cooperation, cyber coordination, and planned public‑private exercises.

### Non‑crime hate incidents (NCHIs)
Lord Strasburger asked if measures on NCHIs are being reviewed. Lord Hanson of Flint said the College of Policing and NPCC are “undertaking a review … working closely with the Home Office” [ref: b1051.6/1]. He would not pre‑empt the outcome, noting, “Again, I genuinely do not wish to pre‑empt the review” and that it must balance intelligence value and focusing on real crime [ref: b1052.2/1, b1052.0/1]. He reminded the House that NCHIs were codified in 2023 and declined to abolish them now: “The short answer is no” [ref: b1053.0/1, b1054.0/1]. Outcome: Review due “shortly”; no immediate policy change.

### Child poverty strategy and migrant families with NRPF
Baroness Lister asked about the impact of ‘no recourse to public funds’ (NRPF) on child poverty. Lord Hanson of Flint said a 10‑year child poverty strategy will be published later this year and that “children whose families have no recourse to public funds will be included” in scope [ref: b1055.2/1]. He listed existing support and reiterated child poverty is central to breaking barriers to opportunity [ref: b1055.4/1]. On refugee children’s health and extended ILR routes, he answered “yes” to health being paramount [ref: b1056.0/1], would not pre‑empt Healthy Start consultation outcomes [ref: b1056.2/1], and said migration control must not mean children suffer: the aim is to “protect and develop those children” [ref: b1056.4/1]. Outcome: Strategy promised; no immediate policy changes.

### Conduct Committee: appointment of external members
The House agreed to appoint Alexandra Marks CBE and Sandra Paul as external members of the Conduct Committee, replacing Cindy Butts and Andrea Coomber, following an open competition chaired by Baroness Manningham‑Buller [ref: b1057.1/2, b1057.2/1]. Outcome: Motion agreed without division [ref: b1057.2/2].

### Alleged spying case: role of the Attorney General’s Office (Commons UQ, discussed in Lords)
Peers questioned the Attorney General’s role following the collapse of an Official Secrets Act prosecution. Lord Hermer said “politicians play no role whatever in prosecutions” and confirmed he was informed (not consulted) after the DPP’s evidential decision, per the framework agreement [ref: b1058.1/1]. Consent to prosecute had previously been given under the former Government [ref: b1059.1/1], with the earlier Commons Answer noting the date 3 April 2024 [ref: b1057.4/4]. On the meaning of “enemy”, he cited Roussev and said the test depends on the relevant period; between 2021‑2023 the Government’s official position was that China was not an enemy, posing evidential difficulties [ref: b1059.3/1]. He quoted the then Foreign Secretary’s Mansion House speech to illustrate the Government’s stance at that time [ref: b1060.3/2‑b1060.3/5]. Outcome: No decision required by the Lords; senior officials to give evidence to the Joint Committee on the National Security Strategy as previously stated [ref: b1057.4/5].

### Fishing and Coastal Growth Fund (Commons UQ, discussed in Lords)
Peers examined the £360 million fund and its devolution. Baroness Hayman of Ullock said allocations use the Barnett formula and devolved Governments have “full flexibility to target this funding” to their fishing and coastal communities [ref: b1062.1/1]. She noted SPS (sanitary and phytosanitary) negotiations with the EU are “due to start shortly” and the aim is “a really good deal” [ref: b1062.3/1]. On sustainability, she said quotas must be set on a sustainable basis and better data are needed [ref: b1063.0/1]. Outcome: Fund confirmed as devolved; SPS talks forthcoming; no votes.

### Planning Bill: Gardens Trust and networks of green/blue spaces
Lord Inglewood proposed making the Gardens Trust a statutory consultee. Supporters stressed its efficiency: “it was consulted 1,842 times last year” and responded within deadlines 99% of the time, with objections in only 6.6% of cases [ref: b1067.0/4]. The Government said they will consult on reforming statutory consultation (including impacts of removing the Trust’s role), with Historic England retaining roles for higher‑graded sites, and will explore amenity society notification models; registered parks and gardens remain protected under the NPPF [ref: b1085.0/4, b1085.0/5]. The amendment was withdrawn [ref: b1090.0/4].
Peers sought to require spatial development strategies (SDSs) to include networks of green/blue spaces; Ministers said the NPPF already supports healthy communities and SDSs “may specify infrastructure that promotes or improves the social or environmental well‑being of an area”, with a Defra access strategy forthcoming [ref: b1085.0/10, b1085.0/11]. No amendment agreed.

### Planning Bill: distributed water storage
Baroness Jones of Moulsecoomb’s amendment to require planning authorities to secure distributed rainwater/greywater storage and reuse was defeated on division (Ayes 84, Noes 185) [ref: b1091.4/1]. She argued to capture storm‑ and surface water that otherwise is “totally gone” [ref: b1091.0/1]. Outcome: Amendment defeated.

### Planning Bill: Environmental Delivery Plans and the Nature Restoration Fund (NRF)
Baroness Coffey withdrew an amendment to require decision‑makers to take Environmental Delivery Plans (EDPs) into account. Lord Banner’s Amendments 163A/163B, to allow EDP requests after development has commenced and to guide Natural England via Secretary of State policy, were backed by the Government to maximise NRF scope for large/complex schemes [ref: b1096.1/3, b1096.1/4]. The Minister said post‑commencement access to the NRF would not “skirt” planning procedures: “all of the normal things that apply to planning permission would still apply” [ref: b1096.1/4]. Outcome: Government support indicated; Coffey amendment withdrawn.

### Planning Bill: demolition of Assets of Community Value (ACV)
Baroness Coffey’s amendment to ensure buildings designated as ACVs “cannot be demolished through permitted development rights” was agreed on division (Ayes 243, Noes 157) [ref: b1102.3/1, b1103.1/1]. Outcome: Amendment agreed; PDR constrained where ACV status exists.

### Planning Bill: consulting water and sewerage undertakers on DCOs
Baroness McIntosh of Pickering sought to make water/sewerage undertakers statutory consultees for development consent orders (DCOs). The Government resisted, noting Clause 4 repeals statutory pre‑application consultation (Section 42) and that guidance will set expectations for engagement; statutory bodies will still be notified at acceptance and can make representations [ref: b1107.2/1, b1107.2/4, b1107.2/5]. Outcome: Amendment withdrawn.

### Planning Bill: Responsible Actors Scheme and building safety remediation
Baroness Pinnock’s amendment to block major planning permissions for Responsible Actors Scheme members until all unsafe blocks are remediated (including sub‑11m) was defeated (Ayes 83, Noes 157) [ref: b1116.1/1]. The Government argued it would “stop the largest housebuilders … building new homes” and delay remediation, citing over 50 developer contracts (~£4.7 billion) and a proportionate PAS 9980 approach [ref: b1113.0/3, b1113.0/5, b1113.0/6]. Outcome: Amendment defeated; Government to bring forward a remediation Bill separately.

### Planning Bill: SDS areas and national parks
Lord Lucas proposed extending powers to establish SDS boards to areas including national parks. Ministers said SDS areas already encompass national parks and the Secretary of State can establish boards covering them; national park authorities will not sit on boards but will play an “important role” and continue to prepare local plans [ref: b1119.1/1, b1119.1/2]. Outcome: Amendment withdrawn.

### Planning Bill: accessible housing and older people
Lord Best’s amendments to mandate Part M4(2) accessible/adaptable (and some M4(3) wheelchair) standards and to require SDSs to reflect older people’s housing needs were withdrawn. The Government said local authorities can already apply enhanced standards via planning where justified by evidence, and SDSs must be consistent with national policy, which addresses older/disabled needs; the forthcoming national housing strategy and updated NPPF will cover this [ref: b1127.0/3, b1127.0/4, b1127.0/11]. Outcome: No change; existing policy tools cited.

### Heathrow expansion: Airports National Policy Statement review (Commons Statement, Lords debate)
A Commons Statement announced a review of the Airports National Policy Statement (ANPS), aiming for a decision this Parliament and “flights taking off on a new runway in 2035” [ref: b1131.3/3]. Four tests were set—economic growth, air quality, noise, and alignment with climate obligations—and the Climate Change Committee will be asked for advice [ref: b1131.3/5]. In the Lords, Ministers said the tests “will have to be met in their entirety” and pledged to work with the judiciary to speed judicial reviews; the CAA will review regulation to incentivise cost control [ref: b1137.0/6, b1131.3/9, b1131.3/10]. Peers queried financeability, noise and surface access, including rail links. Outcome: No decision yet; review proceeding with enabling reforms flagged in the Bill.

### Planning Bill: SDS content on employment land
Lord Lansley sought to require SDSs to specify amounts of employment/industrial/logistics/commercial development. The Government said this is already covered by Section 12D(1), which requires SDS policies on strategic land use, and that the forthcoming NPPF will set explicit expectations [ref: b1146.2/2, b1146.2/3]. Outcome: Amendment withdrawn.

### Planning Bill: chalk streams in spatial development strategies
The Bishop of Norwich’s amendment requiring SDSs to list chalk streams, set protective measures and assign responsibilities was agreed on division (Ayes 196, Noes 137) [ref: b1156.5/1]. He called chalk streams “a very special type of river” and “globally rare”, urging protection now [ref: b1149.0/1, b1149.0/3]. The Government cited local nature recovery strategies and water sector reforms/investment, but peers pressed for statutory planning weight [ref: b1153.1/4, b1153.1/10]. Outcome: Amendment agreed; SDSs must include chalk stream provisions.

### Planning Bill: brownfield‑first and compact growth duty
Baroness Scott of Bybrook’s amendment requiring SDSs to prioritise brownfield land and urban densification, and to minimise travel distances between homes, jobs and services, was agreed on division (Ayes 166, Noes 139) [ref: b1159.4/1]. She argued statutory provision is needed as current measures “are not working” [ref: b1159.2/1]. Outcome: Amendment agreed; brownfield/compact growth to be built into SDSs.

### Planning Bill: SDS continuity after local government reorganisation
Lord Lansley proposed keeping SDSs in force for five years following organisational changes. The Government pointed to powers for tailored transitional provisions in regulations (new Section 12T) and cautioned against restricting authorities’ ability to respond to policy changes [ref: b1163.2/2]. Outcome: Amendment withdrawn.

### Planning Bill: neighbourhood priorities and neighbourhood plans
Lord Lansley sought to commence neighbourhood priorities statements and bring earlier levelling‑up provisions into force now. The Government said commencement should align with wider plan‑making reforms, and that neighbourhood groups can already contribute to local plans; they resisted giving the SoS powers to vary neighbourhood plans in DCO decisions, noting existing processes (local impact reports, representations) [ref: b1168.0/2, b1168.0/4, b1168.0/8, b1168.0/10]. Outcome: No change; commencement to align with broader reforms.

### Planning Bill: flooding tests and mapping
Baroness McIntosh of Pickering proposed to place sequential and exception flood tests on a statutory footing and to require Strategic Flood Risk Assessments to use the latest Environment Agency mapping. The Government said the tests are embedded in the NPPF (with guidance updated last month) and EA flood maps were updated in 2024/25 with ongoing quarterly/annual updates; a forthcoming consultation on planning reforms will consider flood risk management [ref: b1174.1/1, b1174.1/3]. Outcome: Amendments withdrawn.

### Planning Bill: cultural assets and heritage processes
The Earl of Clancarty sought to extend the Assets of Community Value regime to ‘assets of cultural value’. The Government said the forthcoming English Devolution and Community Empowerment Bill will expand ACV definitions (including cultural/economic assets) and introduce a community right to buy, with guidance highlighting cultural assets [ref: b1181.4/6, b1181.4/7, b1181.4/8]. On heritage processes, Ministers said they will consult on national decision‑making policies for heritage and consider how listed building consent orders can streamline works while protecting historic features; switching national orders to the negative procedure was not accepted [ref: b1181.4/9, b1181.4/11]. Outcome: Cultural assets amendment withdrawn; heritage consultations ahead.

### Planning Bill: proportionality in planning
Lord Banner’s amendment to embed a statutory ‘principle of proportionality’ across planning decision‑making was defeated (Ayes 46, Noes 133) [ref: b1185.1/1]. He argued it would “facilitate more focused decision‑making and more effective public participation” [ref: b1184.1/2]. Outcome: Amendment defeated.

### Planning Bill: overlapping permissions (Hillside)
Lord Banner’s amendments to address the Supreme Court’s Hillside ruling on overlapping permissions (and to enable regulations) were defeated (Ayes 49, Noes 110) [ref: b1194.1/1]. The Government set out a two‑step approach: “we will implement the provisions from the Levelling‑up and Regeneration Act for a new, more comprehensive route to vary planning permissions—Section 73B” and “explore with the sector the merits of putting drop‑in permissions on a statutory footing” [ref: b1192.3/5, b1192.3/6]. Outcome: Amendments defeated; Government to proceed via Section 73B and explore further legislation.

### Planning Bill: chief planner in local authorities
Lord Lansley’s amendment to require every local planning authority to appoint a qualified chief planner was withdrawn. The Government preferred local discretion given varying authority sizes/functions, while committing to “keep this issue under review” during planning reforms [ref: b1198.2/2, b1198.2/3]. Outcome: No change; matter under review.

### Planning Bill: preservation of playing fields
Lord Addington’s amendment imposing a statutory duty on LPAs to preserve playing fields/pitches was defeated (Ayes 24, Noes 93) [ref: b1200.1/1]. Ministers pointed to strong existing NPPF protections—“playing fields can be built upon only if they are no longer needed … if equivalent or better provision will be delivered elsewhere, or if … alternative sports and recreational provision … outweigh[s] the loss” [ref: b1085.0/18]—and forthcoming design guidance updates [ref: b1085.0/19]. Outcome: Amendment defeated.

### Royal Assent
Royal Assent was notified for five Acts: the Bus Services Act; Deprivation of Citizenship Orders (Effect during Appeal) Act; Renters’ Rights Act; Absent Voting (Elections in Scotland and Wales) Act; and General Cemetery Act [ref: b1131.1/1‑b1131.1/5].
