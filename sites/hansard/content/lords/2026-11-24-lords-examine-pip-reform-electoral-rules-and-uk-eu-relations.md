## High-Level Summary
The House of Lords questioned the Government on VAT compliance, civil society’s role in supporting young people, the autism strategy and Technical Capability Notices, before debating the UK-EU summit statement. Committee proceedings on the Representation of the People Bill covered overseas electors, registration and duplicate entries, voter identification, participation, electoral data, returning-officer payments and the City of London franchise. The House also considered the Timms Review of Personal Independence Payment (PIP), with a White Paper planned for spring and legislation to follow where required. The transcript records several amendments being withdrawn or not moved and Amendment 74 being agreed; it records no divisions or numerical votes.

## Detailed Summary
### Timms Review of Personal Independence Payment
Baroness Sherlock repeated the Statement on the final Timms Review of Personal Independence Payment (PIP). The review concluded that the current system was “no longer fit for purpose” and recommended fundamental reform of assessment, support for additional costs and access to personalised services. The Government agreed to two specific recommendations as a first step: a fast-track route for people with serious, well-evidenced conditions, including a distinct route for people at the end of life, and no repeated award reviews for people with lifelong or progressive conditions whose needs were unlikely to improve. A White Paper was planned for spring, followed by legislation where required. The Statement said: “The review reaches the view that we need a full system reform”.

Baroness Spielman questioned the review’s financial sustainability, eligibility criteria and lack of costings, while Lord Addington welcomed the proposed treatment of lifelong conditions but sought more detail about PIP’s interaction with other support. Baroness Sherlock said the Government wanted to reduce the benefits bill without crude cuts and would consider the Timms and Milburn reviews together. She confirmed that detailed eligibility rules, the effects on existing claimants and the financial consequences had not been settled. Continued engagement with disabled people and representative organisations was promised, with the White Paper as the next stated step.
