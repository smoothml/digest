## High-Level Summary
The sitting covered business rates and high-street regeneration, Chelsea Football Club sale proceeds for humanitarian purposes in Ukraine, domestic high-assay low-enriched uranium capacity, and drug policy and enforcement. The House also agreed procedural and delegated-legislation motions, including the order of commitment for the Youth Justice Board Bill. The main substantive business was the fourth day of Committee on the Health Bill, which addressed work and health, dentistry, urgent and emergency care, climate duties, NHS digital procurement, public health and specialised commissioning; several amendments were withdrawn or not moved, and the clauses and Schedule 1 considered were agreed. A Commons Statement was repeated on strengthened mobile-phone guidance in schools from September 2027 and a consultation on education-technology safety certification.

## Detailed Summary
### High Streets Strategy: business rates
Lord Pitkeathley of Camden Town asked whether business-rates liability would be transferred from occupiers to property owners. Lord Pitt-Watson said the Government were not considering the proposal because the evidence suggested it would be administratively cumbersome and could disrupt funding for local services: "the Government are not considering this proposal at this time". Further questions concerned a commercial landowner levy, the categorisation of cultural venues, town-centre regeneration, recording studios, residential tenancy clauses affecting start-ups, and taxation and regulation. The Minister highlighted Budget reliefs, funding for community assets and vacant properties, hospitality zones, and support for business improvement districts. He offered to write about recording studios and said he would consider specific categorisation or tenancy problems; no change to rates liability was announced. He cited a 20% reduction for pubs and venues and said 70,000 high-street venues would no longer be liable for rates.

### Chelsea Football Club sale proceeds and frozen Russian assets
Baroness D'Souza asked about using the proceeds of Chelsea Football Club’s sale to support humanitarian purposes in Ukraine. Lord Pitt-Watson said ministers continued to engage across government, that a legal route had been established, and that the Government were prepared to go to court if necessary to enforce Roman Abramovich’s undertaking. He confirmed: "Those funds have not yet been released". Questions addressed the delay, proposed claims that £1.4 billion represented loans, the Foundation for the Victims of Conflict, possible legal costs and whether court proceedings should begin. The Minister said the Charity Commission was reviewing the foundation’s application and that the Government had written to its sponsor. He declined to discuss possible litigation strategy or provide a timetable. On wider Russian sovereign assets, he said the UK and G7 remained committed to keeping them immobilised until Russia ceased its war and paid for the damage.

### Capenhurst high-assay low-enriched uranium facility
Members linked the facility to small modular reactors, the proposed replacement for Wylfa, medical radioisotopes, supply-chain development, British manufacturing and intellectual property. The Minister said she had been assured that Capenhurst would not delay the Wylfa programme and offered to write on specific issues, including supply-chain work and intellectual property. She committed to supporting a domestic supply chain and said the Government’s ambition was for 70% of small modular reactor supply-chain products to be British built.

### Drug prohibition, criminalisation and drug-related deaths
Other questions covered prison-leaver overdoses, sophisticated trafficking methods, drug-driving, specialist drug courts, education, street dealing and online ketamine sales. The Minister emphasised action against organised crime, citing 189 tonnes of class A drugs seized by the National Crime Agency in 2025-26, and referred to approximately £3.4 billion for drug and alcohol treatment over three years. He agreed to reflect on police practice in drug-driving enforcement and referred to neighbourhood policing and online enforcement. No review of prohibition was announced.

### Procedural and delegated-legislation motions
The House agreed the order of consideration motion for the Railways Bill and approved the Terrorism Prevention and Investigation Measures Act 2011 (Continuation) Order 2026, the Tobacco and Vapes Act 2026 display regulations, the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 cryptoassets regulations, and the International Claims Commission for Ukraine (Immunities and Privileges) Order 2026. The motions were considered in Grand Committee where stated. The order of commitment for the Youth Justice Board for England and Wales (Restoration of Powers and Responsibilities) Bill [HL] was discharged after Lord Oates said: "no amendments have been set down to this Bill". The motion was agreed.

### Health Bill Committee: work, health and return to employment
Baroness Hyde of Bemerton referred to existing programmes, including WorkWell, the Keep Britain Working programme and fit-note reforms. She said the national rollout of WorkWell could support up to 250,000 people with health conditions to stay in or return to work, backed by up to £259 million over three years. The Government did not consider an additional statutory duty or review necessary, but offered meetings with officials. Amendment 48 was withdrawn. The separate disposition of Amendment 279QF is not stated in the transcript.

### Health Bill Committee: dentistry and equitable access
Baroness Merron said Amendment 49 was unnecessary because existing duties to provide a comprehensive health service and reduce inequalities already covered dentistry. She reported 50 additional dental-school places, recruitment incentives, nearly 3 million additional NHS treatments in 2025-26 and 4 million urgent appointments. She said that "the annual dental statistics help to identify problems and inform policy development". She agreed to write on the Chief Dental Officer’s framework and on infrastructure and capital funding. Amendment 49 was withdrawn; the dispositions of Amendments 213 and 216 are not stated in the transcript.

### Health Bill Committee: ambulance access, minimum waits and corridor care
Baroness Merron said ambulance funding already reflected age, deprivation and longer rural journeys, and reported an improvement in the category 2 mean response time. She opposed a new statutory admission time limit, referring to £140 million for urgent and emergency-care schemes and to work on capacity and patient flow. She said there was no national policy supporting blanket minimum waits, although local commissioners could use capacity measures subject to clinical triage and an 18-week ceiling; the Government did not plan to publish which integrated care boards used such measures. She undertook to ensure that concerns about inaccurate corridor-care data were examined and said the Government remained committed to eliminating corridor care for children before the end of the Parliament. Amendments 50 and 72 were withdrawn, and Amendment 51 was not moved. The dispositions of Amendments 219 and 229 are not stated in the transcript.

### Health Bill Committee: climate change and NHS resilience
Baroness Hyde said the Climate Change Act and Environment Act already applied to the Secretary of State, while existing duties and green plans would remain for integrated care boards, NHS trusts and foundation trusts. She said the Secretary of State would issue guidance and that adverse-weather planning would continue. She confirmed that "information on annual NHS emissions will continue to be published in the DHSC’s annual report and accounts". She also said NHS carbon-footprint emissions had fallen by 17%. Amendment 52 was withdrawn; the disposition of Amendment 248 is not stated in the transcript.

### Health Bill Committee: NHS digital procurement and data sovereignty
Baroness Merron said the federated data platform contract was under review and that any decision would be announced formally, but she could not give a date. She said existing procurement, data-protection and cyber-security safeguards were sufficient, and stated: "I am not able to commit to an NHS digital sovereignty strategy, nor additional legislation". She nevertheless confirmed that procurement of the single patient record would have to address resilience, UK jurisdiction and NHS control. She rejected further annual reporting, blanket NHS ownership of connector intellectual property and a complete prohibition on tightly controlled supplier access to identifiable data. Amendment 53 was withdrawn. The wider debate was adjourned for the dinner break and later resumed.

### Health Bill Committee: public health, vaccination, antimicrobial resistance and smoking
Baroness Hyde said existing statutory powers and cross-government programmes made a new public-health duty unnecessary, and that NHS England and the United Kingdom Health Security Agency already published vaccination data. She said antimicrobial resistance was covered by the 2024-2029 national action plan and that stop-smoking services received at least £153 million annually through the public-health grant. In response to Baroness Cass, she agreed to write on a wider preparedness approach to disinformation. Amendment 53A was withdrawn. The dispositions of the other amendments in this group are not stated in the transcript.

### Health Bill Committee: specialised commissioning, continuing healthcare and patient involvement
Baroness Merron said regulations on nationally commissioned services would require parliamentary approval and that the relevant list would be available by Report: "yes, the list will be available by Report". She promised information on the seven offices for pan-ICB commissioning, said binding national standards and performance assessments would apply, and said the Government were gathering data on regional IVF provision. She said continuing-healthcare eligibility already considered the nature, intensity, complexity and unpredictability of needs and was not dependent on terminal status. Amendment 54 was withdrawn; Amendments 55 to 58, 59 and 60 were not moved; Clauses 12 to 14, Schedule 1 and Clause 15 were agreed, and Amendment 65 was not moved.

### Statement: mobile phones and education technology in schools
Members welcomed the phone-free objective but questioned the timetable, implementation costs, arrangements for young carers and pupils with special educational needs and disabilities, coverage of school trips, and the scope of edtech certification. The Minister said schools should not require families to buy devices and confirmed that assistive technology would remain supported. She said educational effectiveness would be examined through a £24 million national edtech test-bed programme rather than assessed identically to safety certification. She said she would consider involving health experts and would return to the question of school trips if necessary.
