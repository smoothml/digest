## High-Level Summary
The House of Lords held four oral questions on maternity mortality (MBRRACE‑UK), Jobcentre staffing, exclusions of autistic pupils, and the planning process for a proposed Chinese Embassy at the Old Royal Mint. The House agreed to suspend Baroness D’Souza for eight weeks following a Conduct Committee report and approved Procedure and Standing Orders changes. The Public Authorities (Fraud, Error and Recovery) Bill completed Third Reading and was returned to the Commons with amendments. Peers then held a take‑note debate on the Steel Industry (Special Measures) Act 2025, including two maiden ministerial speeches, focusing on Scunthorpe, trade measures, energy costs and the forthcoming steel strategy. The Renters’ Rights Bill was returned from the Commons agreed to.

## Detailed Summary
### Introduction: The Lord Bishop of Portsmouth
Jonathan Hugh, Lord Bishop of Portsmouth, was introduced, took the oath, was supported by the Bishops of Lincoln and Leicester, and signed an undertaking to abide by the Code of Conduct [ref: a935.0/1].

### MBRRACE‑UK Report 2025 – Oral Question
Baroness Merron said that although maternal mortality had “slightly decreased recently”, the report showed “further urgent action is needed,” noting an independent investigation into NHS maternity and neonatal services with interim findings due in December and a maternal mortality care bundle planned for next month [ref: a935.4/1]. In response to Baroness Berger on late maternal deaths and deprivation, she said maternal mental health is an urgent priority and that “Identified pathways, as she describes, are part of the solution” [ref: a935.6/1].
Peers raised racial disparities; Baroness Merron said it is “unacceptable that, in 2025, black women are twice as likely to die in childbirth,” and highlighted proposed interventions on blood clots developed with MBRRACE [ref: a936.1/1]. She assured Lord Kirkhope that inquiries would act “at pace,” and noted the Secretary of State chairs the maternity and neonatal task force [ref: a936.3/1]. On interagency working, she said the Government is “not waiting” for reviews, citing rollout of perinatal mental health services and a move to a “single patient record” to improve safety [ref: a936.5/1]. She undertook to write on ethnicity data in negligence claims [ref: a937.1/1], and pointed to data initiatives including an “inequalities dashboard.” She confirmed the care bundle is “to be published next month and actioned next year” [ref: a937.5/1, a937.3/1].

### Jobcentres: Staffing Levels – Oral Question
Baroness Sherlock said DWP monitors demand and has workforce planning to ensure “the right people in the right place at the right time” [ref: a938.4/1]. She acknowledged concerns about appointments but emphasised tailoring: “consistency is not the same as uniformity… we are exploring ways to adapt the length, frequency and channels for appointments” [ref: a938.6/2].
Peers pressed for local flexibility and support for disabled claimants. Baroness Sherlock said “one size does not fit all” and outlined local “trailblazers,” youth hubs, mobile vans, work in libraries and in GP settings [ref: a939.1/1, a940.3/1, a940.1/1]. On staffing numbers, she said modelling “pretty much matches the demand we are predicting” and that the department is “happy that we have the right numbers at the moment,” while prioritising if demand rises [ref: a941.1/1]. The Bishop of Leicester quoted a BBC report that with 10‑minute appointments “you’re just being a benefits policeman” [ref: a938.5/2]; the Minister said DWP is investing in a work coach academy and AI tools [ref: a939.3/2].

### Autistic Children: State Schools – Oral Question
Baroness Smith of Malvern said “we expect schools to support pupils with special educational needs and disabilities or mental health needs appropriately,” considering early intervention before any exclusion and trusting head teachers to use exclusion “when necessary” [ref: a941.5/1]. She agreed with Lord Touhig on the need for early intervention and staff training and urged multi‑agency assessment before deciding to exclude [ref: a942.1/1].
Responding to Baroness Browning, she stressed personalised approaches and said support should not require a medical diagnosis, adding “there must be an individualised approach, including a plan where necessary” [ref: a942.3/1]. On data about unlawful exclusions, she restated schools’ legal duties and undertook to write on data collection [ref: a943.3/1]. She noted that differences in how autism may present in girls “will need to be considered” in teacher training [ref: a944.0/1], and balanced inclusion with order: “it is sometimes necessary to remove children from the classroom, or even from the school” after support options are considered [ref: a944.2/1].

### Chinese Embassy: Planning Decision – Oral Question
Baroness Taylor of Stevenage stressed the quasi‑judicial process for the called‑in application for a Chinese Embassy at the Old Royal Mint; decisions will be taken “based on evidence and planning rules” by the Planning Minister and are “independent of the rest of government” [ref: a944.5/1]. She would not prejudge the outcome, saying all relevant planning considerations will be taken into account [ref: a945.1/1].
On material considerations and timing, she said: “Those documents will be released alongside the decision of the Minister by 10 December” [ref: a945.3/1]. She confirmed a “reference back letter” sought further information on consolidation of diplomatic premises, site security and redacted drawings, calling such referrals routine [ref: a947.2/1]. Asked about Cabinet views and national security, she said the decision “will be taken on material planning grounds by the Planning Minister” [ref: a947.8/1]. On delay, she cited the need to give parties time to respond to detailed representations [ref: a948.0/1].

### Conduct Committee report and suspension of Baroness D’Souza – Motions agreed
Lord Kakkar summarised the findings that Baroness D’Souza wrote to the Metropolitan Police about speeding offences on House of Lords paper, indicating that the loss of her licence would affect attendance, and accepted she breached the Code. The committee deemed it a “serious breach of the Code of Conduct” and said such behaviour “risks undermining public confidence” [ref: a948.3/4]. The House agreed the report without debate [ref: a948.4/2]. A further motion suspended Baroness D’Souza for eight weeks and confirmed the conduct occurred after the 2015 Act came into force; it was agreed [ref: a949.1/2, a949.1/3].

### Procedure and Privileges Committee: Fourth Report – Motion to Agree; Standing Orders amendments – Motions to Agree
Lord Gardiner outlined three changes to the leave of absence scheme: a five‑day window for the Sub‑Committee to review applications; a six‑month sessional review to encourage return or retirement; and a Standing Order change allowing the Sub‑Committee to grant or withhold leave for extended absences [ref: a949.4/3, a949.4/4, a949.4/5]. He also proposed a technical amendment to disapply fixed date requirements in certain Private Business Standing Orders for hybrid Bills [ref: a949.4/6]. The report was agreed [ref: a949.4/8]. The House then agreed associated amendments to Standing Orders (public and private) to give effect to the changes [ref: a950.1/5, a950.1/6, a950.3/5, a950.3/6].

### Public Authorities (Fraud, Error and Recovery) Bill – Third Reading
Baroness Sherlock said the Government is “committed to safeguarding public money and tackling fraud and waste,” with “Tens of billions of pounds … being lost to public sector fraud,” and the Bill expected to “deliver £1.5 billion of benefits over the next five years” [ref: a951.4/1]. Viscount Younger cited PSFA estimates that fraud and error cost the taxpayer “£55 billion to £81 billion” in 2023‑24 and praised cross‑House work to improve safeguards [ref: a952.0/1, a952.0/3].
Lord Vaux welcomed improved drafting and engagement while hoping to resolve remaining issues before ping‑pong [ref: a954.0/2, a954.0/4]. The Bishop of Leicester noted assurances about reviewing overpayment communications [ref: a954.1/2]. The Bill passed and was returned to the Commons with amendments [ref: a956.0/1, a956.0/3].

### Steel Industry (Special Measures) Act 2025 – Motion to Take Note (including two maiden ministerial speeches)
In her maiden speech, Baroness Lloyd of Effra defended intervention to keep Scunthorpe operating: “we could not and would not let the fires in the blast furnaces be extinguished,” and said roughly “£270 million” had been made available as working capital, with almost £4 million spent on safety [ref: a957.0/8, a957.0/9]. She highlighted that the UK “is the only country in the world to benefit from a preferential 25% rate on steel and aluminium exports to the US,” EU proposals to replace steel safeguards, electricity cost reliefs, a procurement notice to consider UK steel, and the intention to publish a steel strategy and review options for primary steelmaking [ref: a957.0/11, a957.0/13, a957.0/14, a957.0/15, a957.0/16].
Peers broadly welcomed avoiding closure but pressed for the overdue steel strategy, lower energy costs and clarity on trade measures and ownership. Lord Hunt asked: “Where is the plan?” and quoted that “the message is the same: high energy prices are holding back growth” [ref: a962.0/5, a962.0/15]. Others urged a regional approach and better use of scrap; Lord Eccles noted “we produce a lot of scrap” and stressed understanding users [ref: a980.0/9, a980.0/6]. Replying in his maiden speech, Lord Stockwood said actions under the Act “do not constitute nationalisation” but a “temporary, targeted intervention,” and that long‑term solutions require significant investment [ref: a995.0/13, a995.0/14]. He said the UK would defend its industry while engaging on EU/US measures, is consulting on deepening the British Industry Supercharger, and has pressed partners at the global forum on excess capacity [ref: a999.1/4, a999.1/2, a999.1/3]. He confirmed the UK Steel Council met in January, April and July, with the next meeting on 4 November, and that the Government is in “constructive dialogue with Jingye” [ref: a999.1/9, a999.1/10]. The motion was agreed [ref: a999.1/13].

### Renters’ Rights Bill – Returned from the Commons
The Renters’ Rights Bill was returned from the Commons agreed to [ref: a1002.0/1].
